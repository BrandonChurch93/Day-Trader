# Independent audit of the crypto replay rig

I found no future-bar price leaking into the daily or hourly orders I traced. I did find a shuffle test that cuts trade counts roughly in half, inconsistent measurement windows, an overfitting estimate sensitive to an implementation choice, and trade counts inflated by tiny floating-point balances. The independent replays agree with the daily equity outcomes and with the hourly Sharpe and growth when I use the rig's first-day normalization. The measured changes below do not establish that any hourly rule passes all five marks or justify trading real money.

MEASURED means I ran a check or tested against the supplied files. STATED means I traced the code or written plan but did not run that specific case. UNKNOWN means the provided evidence could not establish the answer. File lines refer to the untouched rig unless specified otherwise.

- **Cost arithmetic:** A buy spends traded notional times (1+c); sale proceeds are notional times (1-c). I checked the fractional after-fee equations on both sides and independently matched BTC P1. Buy-and-hold is charged a single entry on the second bar and has no sale fee unless sold; no terminal liquidation is in the plan. Fee plus 5 bps gives 7/30/43/85/95 bps per side as registered. The first hour/day baseline omissions concern measurement, not whether the engine debits trading fees.
- **Standard metrics:** Equity is cash plus position at the bar close. `metrics` correctly computes simple returns, 365-day CAGR and Sharpe/volatility with sample standard deviation, cash rate zero, and minimum decline versus the running equity peak on the supplied series. Hourly headline ratios use hour-23 marks to make daily returns; they do not measure intraday peak-to-trough drawdown.
- **Statistics:** Deflated Sharpe has the expected per-period Sharpe ratio, (T-1), sample dispersion across trials, skew, raw rather than excess kurtosis, and the expected-best null construction from [Bailey and López de Prado](https://davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf). Replay 01 counts 18 single-asset runs and 2 portfolios; Replay 03 adds 16 and reruns the earlier 20 at the same fee; X2 retains 20 and repeats the two portfolio specifications. CSCV otherwise evaluates all 12,870 complementary 8-of-16 splits and ranks the in-sample winner by the out-of-sample Sharpe. Block bootstrap uses the same time indices for rule and hold, 30 consecutive daily returns per block, 2,000 moving-block draws, seed 7, and the 5th/50th/95th percentiles of annualized Sharpe differences. This gives a conditional empirical range, not proof the strategy works.
- **Dates and price coverage:** Base uses the amended Bitstamp ETH source and 2018-03-05 start, with BTC from 2013-01-01. Daily input through 2026-11-24 had no gaps or duplicates: 5,578 BTC rows including warm-up (33 zero volume) and 3,388 ETH (none zero volume). Hourly data through 2026-11-24 23:00 had no gaps between first and last actual timestamps or duplicate rows: 121,824 BTC and 81,296 ETH, with 242 and 35 zero-volume rows across full data. ETH's first hour was 2017-08-16 16:00 UTC. The cutoff excluded in-progress Nov 25 bars. BTC S1 and ETH S1 choose hour 01 if present, first later that UTC day if not, then daily open; day-late shifts the order one extra daily bar. No next-bar close is used to set a fill.
- **X2 protocol:** The compiled series use the archive's daily partition through 2026-09-30, then Kraken API days 2026-10-01 through 2026-11-24. They have 4,798 BTC days and 4,128 ETH days after gap filling, no duplicate dates. Starts (BTC 2014-05-01; ETH 2018-03-05 and 2016-03-01) and date cutoffs match. The builder records median and maximum close differences; the downstream `build_doc12b.py:15–25` lists changes in either direction even though `results_01x2.json` has no explicit verdict diff. The draft's policy of treating disagreement as a fail is a downstream interpretation, not a merged Boolean in the runner.
- **Engine fixtures:** I ran `python -B fixtures.py`; all checks printed PASS, including the explicit 20% round trip, fee math, buy-and-hold, intentionally peeking negative control, seeded random trading losing approximately its costs, no apparent leverage, and determinism. This verifies current fixtures, not that fixtures must pass before every run. I made no edits to the rig and ran no git commands.

## Unverified real-world assumptions

Clock-edge execution is the model's explicit next-open convention. Using a bar's close to enter at the boundary of the next bar is not an array look-ahead, but access, order submission latency, fill quality, and 5-bp slippage at another venue are UNKNOWN from historical candles. The daily S1 sensitivity checks delayed access; Replay 03 has no comparable intrahour execution-latency sensitivity. The stored files allow internal checks of dates and consistency, not proof of original market-data provenance or actual execution.
