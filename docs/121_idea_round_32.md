### 1.1 Fallen-angel corporate bond downgrade month-end forced selling (FAIL)

- Published source (opened): [Cass Business School / Invesco PowerShares (Sep 2016)](https://www.bayes.citystgeorges.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0020/362207/fallen-angels-investment-opportunity-cass-business-school-invesco-powershares-sep-2016.pdf).
- Stated effect size and sample: STATED sample of 534 bonds downgraded from investment grade to high yield from January 2006 to February 2016. Average bond prices fell 2.4% from T-24 to T and a further 1.0% to T+7, then recovered 2.5% from T+7 to at least T+30 ("This is the sort of 'V-shaped' price reaction that DeBondt and Thaler found in their work and which can be seen as a confirmation of the overreaction hypothesis, and/or with the forced selling of both active and indexed bond funds.").
- Instrument and US retail access: Individual OTC corporate bonds ($1,000 to $2,000 par increments, 50 to 150 bps odd-lot spreads) are unreachable on a $500 Alpaca cash equity account (`AGENTS.md`). The liquid US retail proxies are fallen-angel ETFs (`ANGL`, `FALN`) paired against broad high yield (`HYG`).
- Event count, cost, and power screen: STATED 12 month-end index rebalances per year (120 over 10 years), ~10 bps round-trip ETF cost. Because newly downgraded bonds represent only ~1% to 5% of `ANGL` or `FALN` weight in a typical month, a +2.5% single-bond rebound dilutes to ~+5 to +15 bps at the ETF level (~+5 bps net after 10 bps RT) against ~100 bps monthly `ANGL` minus `HYG` tracking noise: `120 * (5 / 100)^2 = 0.30` (far below 4). Fails the screen.

### 1.3 Dividend capture around CEF year-end special distributions (FAIL)

- Published source (opened): [Blaz, Poitras, and Chung (2006, International Journal of Finance)](https://www.sfu.ca/~poitras/IJF_blaz+GP_chung_06.pdf).
- Stated effect size and sample: STATED 307 US closed-end bond funds (101 taxable, 96 national muni, 110 state muni) and 27,506 ex-dividend observations from January 1988 to December 2000. The paper states: "Tests for abnormal volume of transactions on the ex-date and cum-date also provided evidence of short-term dividend capture trading for a small number of high dividend payout fully taxable bond funds. (These funds typically paid quarterly dividends as opposed to the monthly dividend payments for other funds.) No evidence was found for short-term trading of closed-end funds with monthly payouts, which compose the bulk of closed-end bond funds, including all of the municipal bond funds."
- Instrument, cost, and power screen: US-listed CEFs via Alpaca cash (~40 bps RT). No published study documents a positive post-declaration or ex-date capture edge on CEF year-end excise-tax specials (which are frequently paid in stock by default, diluting NAV and creating tax drag; corporate special dividends are already queued in Row 74 / Plan 43 with 443 events). ### 1.4 Discount mean reversion after activist Schedule 13D filings in CEFs (QUEUE ROW 102, COUNT-FIRST)

- Published source (opened): [Bradley, Brav, Goldstein, and Jiang (2008/2010, Journal of Financial Economics)](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4118/activist_arb.pdf).
- Stated effect size and sample: STATED 142 US closed-end funds (1988 to 2002) and 127 open-ending attempt events (1988 to 2003). The paper states: "We show that open-ending attempts reduce the discount of the targeted funds by more than 10 percentage points on average (considering both the successful and unsuccessful attempts)." Raw average discounts fell from 19.82% at year t-1 to 14.46% in the attempt year, 8.42% at t+1, and 5.61% at t+3 (matched-fund excess discount narrowed by -5.27 pp in the attempt year and -10.78 pp at t+1, or ~5.5 pp additional narrowing after the attempt year).
- What is UNKNOWN: The paper does not tabulate an exact daily `[+1, +60]` post-13D-filing abnormal return after SEC EDGAR acceptance; part of the narrowing occurs ahead of the filing.
- Instrument and US retail access: US-listed CEFs via Alpaca cash account (long-only, fractional shares, zero commissions, ~40 bps RT spread estimate); SEC EDGAR `SC 13D` and `SC 13D/A` filings are free and timestamped to the second.
- Event count and power screen: STATED rough count of ~15 to 35 initial activist CEF `SC 13D` filings per year (150 to 350 over 10 years; raising event count via `SC 13D/A` proxy-fight or tender-demand escalations pushes N toward 300+). If post-filing discount narrowing captures +150 to +200 bps gross (+110 to +160 bps net after 40 bps RT) against 600 bps peer-hedged CEF noise, a 10-year sample of N = 200 gives `200 * (110 / 600)^2 = 6.7` to `200 * (160 / 600)^2 = 14.2` (at N = 100 and +110 bps net, 3.4, close to 4). Queued as Row 102 count-first (EDGAR CEF 13D census only, zero price access).

## Direction 2: High-Count Event Families with Public Timestamps

### 2.1 Earnings-date advancements (QUEUE ROW 101, SOURCE-FIRST)

- Published sources (opened): [Johnson and So (2016/2018, JFQA)](https://www.wallstreethorizon.com/upload/SoandJohnsonTimeWillTellUpdatedOct2016.pdf) and [RavenPack / Wall Street Horizon (Oct 2022)](https://www.wallstreethorizon.com/blog/5-Research-Findings-on-Earnings-Date-Timing-That-Affect-Trading).
- Stated effect size and sample: STATED 18,959 unique firm-quarter calendar revisions (2006 to 2013, ~2,370 revisions/year) in Johnson and So (2016/2018). The paper states: "Advancers, on average, outperform the market by approximately 138 basis points and delayers underperform by 130 basis points" and "The reported returns are calculated over the month following the calendar revision from r+1 to r+21." Table 3 reports equal-weighted advancers at +1.384% market-adjusted (+1.627% four-factor) and value-weighted advancers at +1.261% market-adjusted (+1.624% four-factor). RavenPack (2022) reports 8.0% annualized (IR 0.8) for Mid/Large-Cap and 19.7% annualized (IR 1.2) for Small-Cap at 1-day aggregation and states: "Both Mid/Large- and Small-Caps long-only strategies decay more slowly than short-only, and advance events have higher momentum than delay events."
- Instrument and US retail access: Liquid US-listed common stocks via Alpaca cash account (long-only, fractional shares for $500, zero commissions, ~10 to 20 bps RT spread on Mid/Large-Cap and ~30 to 50 bps on Small-Cap).
- Event count and power screen: STATED ~400 to 900 advancers per year (4,000 to 9,000 over 10 years). Using a conservative N = 2,000 effective non-overlapping observations across 10 years, +138 bps gross minus 20 bps RT = +118 bps net against 800 bps 21-day idiosyncratic noise gives `2000 * (118 / 800)^2 = 43.5` (well above 8). Even halving the published gross edge for post-2018 decay to +69 bps (+49 bps net) gives `2000 * (49 / 800)^2 = 7.5` (clears 4; at N = 4,000, 15.0 > 8).
- Key data blocker (UNKNOWN): Both studies used Wall Street Horizon (paid institutional database). Queued as Row 101 source-first and count-first: check whether a lawful free timestamped source of advance earnings-date announcements exists (such as SEC Form 8-K Item 7.01/8.01 press releases or a forward logger of public issuer notices) before pulling any prices.

### 2.2 FDA calendar dates: PDUFA pre-decision run-up (FAIL)

Median D-30 to D-1 run-up is +0.43% for PDUFA decisions (-0.07% for readouts, -0.03% for conferences). The study states: "No systematic pre-catalyst run-up. The 'buy the run-up' folklore that dominates biotech retail is not visible in the data. Individual names run; the population does not."

Net edge is ~+3 bps (+43 bps minus 40 bps) against ~1,800 bps (18%) 30-day biotech return noise across 1,600 10-year events: `1600 * (3 / 1800)^2 = 0.004` (far below 4). Fails the screen.

### 2.3 IPO lock-up expiries (FAIL)

- Published source (opened): [Bradley, Jordan, Roten, and Yi (2001, Journal of Financial Research)](https://www.uky.edu/~icrote2/venture.pdf).
- Stated effect size and sample: STATED 2,529 US IPOs (1988 to 1997). Event-day abnormal return is -0.74% and 5-day CAR (-2 to +2) is -1.61%. The paper states: "As illustrated, there is no tendency for the prices to rebound shortly following lockup expiration. Instead, there appears to be a permanent decline of about 2 percent, virtually all of which occurs in the days surrounding the expiration of the lockup."
- Instrument, cost, and power screen: US recent IPO stocks (~100 to 200/yr). The effect is short-only (unreachable on a $500 cash account and subject to elevated IPO borrow fees); long-only post-expiry rebound has net edge <= 0 and power screen 0.00. Fails the screen.

### 2.4 Overnight SEOs and S-3 shelf takedowns (FAIL)

- Published source (opened): [Gustafson (2018, JFQA)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/price-pressure-and-overnight-seasoned-equity-offerings/DE87F9B6B394706A5371AABDB87B2227) and prior repo record Queue row 59 (`docs/QUEUE.md`, citing Huang and Qian 2024).
- Stated effect size and sample: STATED Gustafson (2018) states: "non-overnight issuers experience a 2.5% pre-issue stock-price decline that reverses within 7 days. This post-issue reversal is increasing in SEO offer size and bigger following large pre-issue price declines. In contrast, returns following overnight offerings are less positive and unrelated to SEO offer size or pre-issue returns." Row 59 already documented that 2008 to 2021 post-SEO 10-day returns turned negative (-0.29% mean, -2.15% median) and SEC EDGAR 424B5 pricing supplements arrive 1 to 2 business days after pricing in 12 of 14 sampled deals.
- Instrument, cost, and power screen: US common stocks via Alpaca cash (~30 bps RT). Retail accounts cannot receive institutional offering allocations, and secondary-market post-offer returns are <= 0 net. Power screen 0.00. Fails the screen.

## Direction 3: US-Reachable Crypto Triggers (Kraken Pro Margin and Coinbase CFM)

### 3.1 FOMC and CPI scheduled macro announcements on Bitcoin (FAIL)

- Published source (opened): [Nazaruk (Kyiv School of Economics, May 2026)](https://kse.ua/kse-media/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/illia-nazaruk_268722_assignsubmission_file_nazaruk_final_thesis.pdf).
- Stated effect size and sample: STATED 62 CPI releases and 42 FOMC announcements (104 total, January 2020 to May 2025). The study states: "Bitcoin's volatility rises around U.S. macro announcements, while returns show no consistent drift." and "For returns, H2 is generally not supported: across specifications the average CAR does not differ significantly from zero on announcement days, and pre- or post-window CARs are also mostly insignificant." Two-day pre/post CARs are -0.06% / -0.30% for CPI and -0.64% / +0.13% for FOMC (all p > 0.47).
- Instrument, cost, and power screen: Coinbase CFM `BIP` (5.76 bps fixed clearing + 1 tick floor per `docs/research/120_round_31_cfm_futures_cost_rescreen.md`, before UNKNOWN spread and variable commission) or `IBIT`. About 20 events per year (200 over 10 years), net directional edge <= 0 against ~350 bps 2-day BTC noise. Power screen 0.00. Fails the screen.

### 3.2 Cryptocurrency protocol hard forks and scheduled upgrades (FAIL)

- Published source (opened): [arXiv 2307.11718 (The Forking Effect, 2023)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.11718).
- Stated effect size and sample: STATED 93 Bitcoin forks (85 analyzed, 22 hard forks) from 2014 to 2020. The paper states: "As we can observe in Table 8, hard fork events do not impact bitcoin's returns, a result in line with our initial investigation from Section 4." Table 8 reports hard-fork return mean delta = -0.0033 (-0.33%, p = 0.287).
- Instrument, cost, and power screen: Coinbase CFM 24 contracts (5.76 to 20.66 bps floor on BTC/ETH; 10.83 bps median per `docs/research/120_round_31_cfm_futures_cost_rescreen.md`) or Kraken Pro 27 spot margin pairs (40 to 80 bps per side per `docs/research/98_kraken_and_coinbase_short_access.md`). Few major contentious hard forks occur per year post-2021 (~3 to 10/yr across 24 coins) and published directional edge is <= 0. Power screen 0.00. Fails the screen.
