# Working with the owner

How Brandon wants to be worked with. Every line is OWNER unless marked TRANSLATED.

## Putting a decision to him

- Brainstorm, then recommend one option and give the reason. Let him pick.
- Lock decisions one at a time. Decisions first, details second.
- Say plainly what he is approving and what he is not approving at each step.
- End a proposal with one clear, simple statement of exactly what you recommend, so he never agrees to something he does not fully understand.
- Tell him plainly any decision you made that changes money, scope or the roster, and name the one constant to change if he disagrees.
- He wants a partner, not agreement. If his idea is weaker than another option, say so and say why.

## Changing things

- Propose the change, wait for a yes, then make it. This holds for any deliverable he is iterating on, and most of all for big changes.
- When he writes in a working thread he is steering. Apply it, log it and carry on. Do not stop to confirm.
- A change sent to a running build is queued for the next phase boundary unless he says it must happen now. Do not drop the work in hand to pivot.

## How to write to him

- Lists, not information buried in paragraphs.
- Plain words.
- No em dashes in copy, documents or designs made for him. Use a period, a comma or an interpunct (·).
- No hype and no sales voice.
- Do not predict how long work will take. Agent time estimates are unreliable and they do not change the project. Scope the work start to finish instead.

## Giving him steps

- Give only the steps needed right now, as a plain numbered list of exact instructions.
- No "if this happens do that" branches. If there is more than one route, hold the others for the next message.
- Name which terminal to use and exactly what to paste. He works in VS Code and uses its integrated terminal.
- Keep setup steps few and pasteable. He is often on a phone or tablet.
- If you have the access to do a step yourself, do it. Do not hand him output to copy and paste back.
- When he has to download files, give him one command that proves every file is present.

## Prompts he will send to another agent

- Direct. Open with the decision or the request.
- No thanks and no praise.
- For a small fix, only the fix. No file-reading preamble and no restating of settled rules. Save the full ceremony for a full build.

## While work runs

- Never leave him looking at a silent run. The status rhythm is in `02_how_we_build.md` under "Showing the work".
- Show unfinished and bad output. He would rather correct drift in the first hour than find it twenty hours later.
- When a build teaches something, say what it was and propose the change to the method.

## In his name

- Anything sent, posted or submitted in his name gets his yes first.
- TRANSLATED: in this repo that includes every record in `docs/decisions/` that says the owner decided something. Write "owner decision" only for what he actually said, with the date.

## What still stops for him

`AGENTS.md` lists it: connecting a live account, creating or widening an API key, moving money, raising a risk limit, and any change to `AGENTS.md`. Nothing in this folder loosens that list.
