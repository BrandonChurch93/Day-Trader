## Detailed candidate evaluations

### Rank 1 (QUEUE Row 104): Equity CEF periodic and special distribution discount narrowing

- Published source opened: [Day, Li, and Xu (University of Oklahoma Center for Financial Studies working paper)](https://www.ou.edu/dam/price/Finance/CFS/paper/pdf/92107.pdf), "Dividend Distributions and Closed-End Fund Discounts".
- Exact quotes from source: "Our sample consists of 124 domestic and international equity funds for the ten-year sample period from 1993 to 2002." "Our sample includes 2364 distribution events, of which 2300 represent taxable distributions, including 1692 ordinary dividend distributions and 608 capital gains distributions." "As predicted, the aggregate discount shown in Panel A of Figure 1 declines during the event window surrounding dividend distributions, with the cross-sectional average discount reaching its maximum of 10.1% roughly two weeks before the ex-dividend week." "Panel A of Table 2 reports that the average discount then falls to 9.3% during the ex-dividend week, and continues to drop for two weeks following the ex-dividend week to a low of 8.6%." "A comparison of these plots shows that the distribution-week decline in the discount associated with special dividend distributions is more than twice as large as the decline associated with ordinary dividend distributions."
- Stated effect size and sample: Across all 124 equity CEFs (2,364 events, 1993 to 2002, STATED), average discount narrows by 1.506 percentage points (150.6 bps) from week `t-2` (10.09%) to week `t+2` (8.59%), including 78 bps from `t-2` to `t=0` (9.31%). For the 46 US equity CEFs, average discount narrows by 0.909 percentage points (90.9 bps) from `t-2` (8.45%) to `t+2` (7.54%), and 65 bps from `t-2` to `t=0`. For US funds with special distributions, average discount narrows by 1.645 percentage points (164.5 bps) from `t-2` (14.85%) to `t+2` (13.21%), including 143 bps from `t-2` to `t=0` (13.42%) and 114 bps from `t-2` to `t-1` (13.71%, prior to ex-date).
- Mechanism and counterparty: Taxable retail investors avoid buying closed-end funds with embedded unrealized capital-gains tax overhang just before a distribution (selling or delaying purchases into `t-2`, widening the discount). Once the distribution is declared and paid (clearing the contingent tax liability), investors re-enter and the discount narrows. This is distinct from Round 32's dropped monthly-CEF ex-day capture (Blaz et al. 2006) because it targets the pre-to-post tax-overhang cycle in quarterly, annual, and special equity CEF distributions.
- 10-year event count: STATED 2,364 events across 124 equity CEFs in 10 years (~236/yr). Accounting for December clustering across funds, estimated effective independent 2-to-4-week fund-windows are 400 to 600 across all equity CEFs, or 150 to 250 on US special/year-end distributions alone (UNKNOWN for 2016 to 2026 until counted).
- US retail $500 fit: US-listed equity CEFs trade on NYSE/Nasdaq at roughly $8 to $25 per share (20 to 60 whole shares on $500), $0 commission, STATED/modeled 40 bps round-trip spread (20 bps/side, matching Replay 48/53). Selling at `t-1` close avoids receiving a taxable distribution on a taxable account.
- Power screen (Doc 117): Conservative US-all-46 (`N = 400`, +90.9 bps gross - 40 bps RT = +50.9 bps net, 450 bps UNKNOWN peer-hedged 4-week SD) gives `400 * (50.9 / 450)^2 = 5.12` (clears 4). All 124 equity CEFs (`N = 600`, +110.6 bps net, 450 bps SD) gives `36.2` (clears 8). US special distributions 2-week hold `t-2` to `t=0` (`N = 150`, +143 bps gross - 40 bps RT = +103 bps net, 320 bps UNKNOWN 2-week peer-hedged SD) gives `150 * (103 / 320)^2 = 15.5` (clears 8). Note: if post-2002 decay halved the gross US-special edge to +71.5 bps (+31.5 bps net), the screen on specials alone would fall to `1.45` (fails 4).
- Pre-price closure gate: Count-first audit of 2016 to 2026 US equity CEF quarterly, annual, and special distributions from free SEC N-CSR/N-CEN filings and Alpaca corporate-action dates (zero prices read). Close before prices if modern monthly managed-distribution policies (MDPs) leave fewer than 150 non-clustered quarterly/annual/special events, or if stock-default distribution elections prevent clean cash-return accounting.

### Rank 2 (QUEUE Row 105): Unpredicted and special US stock ex-dividend day short-window capture (1-day hold)

- Published sources opened: [Deakin University (2015)](https://www.deakin.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/425119/InternationalEvidence.pdf), "Can Dividend Schedules Predict Abnormal Returns? International Evidence"; [Chowdhury and Sonaer (2015/2016, Journal of Economics and Finance)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12197-015-9317-7), "Ex-dividend day abnormal returns for special dividends"; and [Eugster, Ducret, Isakov, and Weisskopf (2021, International Review of Finance, PMC8447032)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8447032/) (Western Europe reference only).
- Exact quotes from sources: From Deakin (2015): "We find similar results to Hartzmark and Solomon (2013) for the US, who from 1927 to 2009 find a 37 basis point monthly difference between predicted dividend months and other months for both all companies and for only dividend payers." "After removing the ex-day return there are only nine countries where the premium is significant. Interestingly, the premium is no longer significant in the US." In Table 8, United States ex-day return is "0.291**" (0.291%) for "Paid and predicted" and "0.565**" (0.565%) for "Paid not predicted". From Chowdhury and Sonaer (2015): "Based on a sample of large special dividends, we find that price drop on ex-dividend day is significantly less than the dividend amount. Furthermore, we show that ex-dividend day returns are positive and hence, are not fully arbitraged away. Our tests indicate that tax hypothesis explains some portion of ex-dividend day abnormal returns even for large special dividends, whereas the support for the short-term trading hypothesis is weak." From Eugster et al. (2021, non-US reference): "Buying a stock paying a large dividend five (one) days before the ex-date and selling it at the ex-day opening, would have provided an average abnormal return of 3.43% (2.09%) during the COVID period, while only 1.8% (1.34%) in regular times."
- Stated effect size and sample: US ex-dividend-day abnormal return is +29.1 bps (p < 0.01) for predicted regular dividends and +56.5 bps (p < 0.01) for unpredicted dividends (STATED, Deakin 2015 Table 8). Chowdhury and Sonaer (2015) confirm positive ex-day abnormal returns on large US special dividends, though the open Springer abstract does not print the exact percentage (UNKNOWN).
- Mechanism and counterparty: Taxable retail holders value a dollar of dividend below a dollar of capital gain and face transaction/shorting frictions, causing the ex-day price drop to fall short of the cash dividend. Unlike Replay 60's 20-day declaration drift, holding only from `t-1` close to `t=0` close (1 trading day) cuts paired market/stock noise by roughly `sqrt(20) = 4.47x`.
- 10-year event count: Replay 60 already MEASURED 339 placed whole-share $500 US special-dividend pairs (443 EDGAR-eligible) over 10 years; adding unpredicted dividends puts `N` at 339 to 400+ over 10 years.
- US retail $500 fit: Whole shares on $500 already confirmed feasible in Replay 60; $0 commission; STATED/modeled round-trip spread 20 to 30 bps on liquid small/mid-cap payers. Critical tax caveat: a 1-day hold across ex-date makes the dividend non-qualified ordinary income in a US taxable account (taxed at ordinary rates while the ex-day drop is a short-term capital loss), whereas a Roth/Traditional IRA has 0% tax drag.
- Power screen (Doc 117): At `N = 400`, +56.5 bps gross - 20 bps RT = +36.5 bps net, 200 bps UNKNOWN 1-day paired SD vs IWM: `400 * (36.5 / 200)^2 = 13.3` (clears 8). Conservative (`N = 339`, +26.5 bps net after 30 bps RT, 220 bps SD): `339 * (26.5 / 220)^2 = 4.92` (clears 4). Regular predicted dividends (+29.1 bps gross - 20 bps RT = +9.1 bps net) fail on power unless `N` exceeds 2,000 and spreads stay below 10 bps.
- Pre-price closure gate: (1) Confirm account tax status (taxable ordinary-dividend drag vs IRA) before testing; (2) measure real closing bid-ask spreads on `t-1` and `t=0` for special-dividend ex-dates (close if median RT spread exceeds 35 bps); (3) verify ex-dates and exact per-share special cash amounts from primary issuer/exchange notices rather than unverified vendor fields.

### Rank 4 (DROP): Bitcoin Deribit options expiry intraday V-shaped gamma reversal (08:00 to 10:00 UTC)

- Published source opened: [Weiss, Gaudiosi, Zhou, and Webb (Sept 2026, Finance Research Letters)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1544612326008688), "Bitcoin option expiration, gamma exposure, and intraday price reversals".
- Exact quotes from source: "We source high-frequency transaction data on Bitcoin options from the Deribit exchange for the period from 1 January 2021 to 31 December 2023." "This table reports coefficients and t-statistics for two OLS regression specifications on Bitcoin spot index log returns (reported in % form) one hour before expiration (Return Pre) and two hours after expiration (Return Post) over the full sample (n=1058):" "Deribit determines the settlement price of expiring options using the average value of the Deribit Bitcoin Index over the final 30 min before the 8:00 am UTC expiry, that is, from 7:30 am to 8:00 am UTC." "The estimates in Column (2) imply that, on days with both high open interest and negative gamma exposure, pre-expiration returns are approximately 0.17% lower than on other days." "As reported in Table 2, the price move from 7:00 am to 8:00 am fully reverses by 10:00 am under these conditions." (Table 1 post-expiry 8 to 10 a.m. UTC return SD is `1.209%`.)
- Mechanism and counterparty: Option market makers hedging net short gamma into the 07:30 to 08:00 UTC Deribit settlement window push spot down by ~17.1 bps on high-OI negative-gamma days; after expiry at 08:00 UTC, forced hedging ends and spot rebounds over 08:00 to 10:00 UTC.
- 10-year event count: Top-decile open interest and negative gamma (~5% of 365 daily expiries) yields roughly 18 events/yr, or `N = 180` over 10 years (STATED from paper's top-decile definition).
- US retail $500 fit: `IBIT` is closed at 08:00 to 10:00 UTC (03:00 to 05:00 a.m. ET). Coinbase Advanced Intro spot (100 to 180 bps RT) and Kraken Pro spot (80 to 160 bps RT) exceed the +17.1 bps gross move by 5x to 10x. Coinbase CFM `BIP` (0.01 BTC, ~$850 to $900 notional) requires >$500 notional / margin and costs 5.76 bps fixed-clearing+1-tick floor before variable commission and real spread (~10 bps realistic RT, STATED Doc 120). Requires live Deribit strike-level OI and gamma modeling at 07:00 UTC.
- Power screen (Doc 117): On US retail spot, net edge is negative (screen `0.00`). Even on CFM `BIP` at 10 bps RT (+7.1 bps net vs 120.9 bps STATED SD, `N = 180`): `180 * (7.1 / 120.9)^2 = 0.62` (and even at the impossible 5.76 bps zero-commission floor, `1.58`, still < 4).
- Pre-price closure reason: Dropped on US retail costs and Doc 117 power (0.00 spot / 0.62 `BIP` < 4).
