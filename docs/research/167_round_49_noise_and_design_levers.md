## Short Answer

- STATED: No single prompt tweak eliminates LLM trading noise, because even at `T=0` with a fixed seed, a local LLM still shows `4.3%` SD over three months on one stock (`GOOGL`) in [TradingAgents Reproducibility](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3800973.3801029), and frontier models show `0.36` to `0.88` within-model pairwise action agreement across repeated runs at `T=0` on daily BTC in [AlphaForgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18481).
- STATED (NON-TRADING ANALOGY ONLY): On 562 static 4-option MMLU trivia/knowledge questions (not trading returns), same-model binary correctness errors are correlated at `rho = 0.85` (`N_eff` about `1.1` independent voters across 5 samples), whereas combining five distinct model families lowers binary error correlation to `rho = 0.38` (`N_eff = 2.0`) in [Correlation limits the reliability of ensemble decision making with large language models](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44564-026-00040-z). Whether this correlation gap transfers to continuous weekly equity portfolio returns — where all models see the same stocks and share market-factor covariance — is UNKNOWN.
- STATED (WITH SCOPE LIMITS): No controlled multi-seed study proves that a discrete position grid plus fixed executor takes run-to-run variance to near zero. [AlphaForgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18481) tests LLM-generated Python strategy code (not a discrete grid) on pre-cutoff 2021–2025 data: re-running identical code has zero variance by definition, while fresh code generations still have `0.02` to `0.06` intra-query Sharpe SD. [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) shows structured workflows beat autonomous mode on 5-seed mean Sharpe but prints no per-mode SD, and [Macro-to-Tilt Ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08283) runs one cached `T=0` call per date with no repeated-seed SD. Execution inertia / lower rebalance cadence show strong net-return gains ([Can Blindfolded LLMs Still Trade?](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17692) via RL policy smoothing; [ChatGPT in Systematic Investing](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26228) via single-run monthly vs weekly rebalancing), but neither isolates multi-seed return SD of a pure LLM desk.
- STATED (WITH CAVEATS): Across cost-aware ablations (Part B), free-text reflective memory is null or negative ([CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3), [On the Role of Tools](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper196.pdf)), pairwise debate hurts Sharpe and widens variance ([Market Regime Council](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24490v2)), and deterministic risk overlays cut drawdowns while trimming average return ([Representation Signatures](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28850), [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3)). Positive post-cost Sharpe effects appear for technical-indicator tools in larger portfolios (20 to 50 stocks, though `+0.13` when removed at 10 stocks, in [Toward Expert Investment Teams](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23330), and null in [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3)), slower rebalancing in a single-run proprietary-data preprint ([ChatGPT in Systematic Investing](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26228)), and RL execution smoothing plus GNN/LLM features in a 20-seed GNN+RL pipeline ([Can Blindfolded LLMs Still Trade?](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17692)).

## Part A: What Reduces Run-to-Run Noise Without Changing Average Behavior

### Temperature and Deterministic Decoding

- STATED: In [TradingAgents Reproducibility](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3800973.3801029) (ACM IC-AIF 2026 proceedings, no code/data link; single stock `GOOGL`, three-month period May to July 2025, post-cutoff, 10 runs per setting), lowering local `Qwen3:30B` temperature from `T=1` (C2) to `T=0` with random seed (C3) cut three-month cumulative return SD from `9.0%` to `5.5%` (an arithmetic `38.9%` SD reduction, not printed as a percentage in the paper), and fixing `seed=42` at `T=0` (C4) cut SD further to `4.3%`, with zero extra API cost: "Reducing temperature to 0 (C3) decreases the standard deviation to 5.5%, demonstrating that lower temperature constrains financial variability." Caveat on mean invariance: mean returns were `18.1%` (C2), `21.3%` (C3), and `16.8%` (C4); the paper's `p > 0.26` t-test is reported in its discussion of the `T=1` runs (GPT-4o C1 vs Qwen3:30B C2), not as a proof of mean invariance across C2/C3/C4, and 10 runs (`SEM` `1.5%` to `2.8%`) lack power to rule out a 3 to 4 pp mean shift.
- STATED: In [TradingAgents Reproducibility](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3800973.3801029), full zero variance required locking all four local sampling controls (`T=0, seed=42, top_k=1, top_p=0`, C5), which jumped mean return to `28.2%` and hides seed dependence rather than removing it: "Only the fully deterministic configuration (T = 0, seed = 42, top_k = 1, top_p = 0) achieves zero entropy and zero variance, but its 28.2% return reflects the specific choice of seed = 42 and constitutes a form of cherry-picking."
- STATED: In [AlphaForgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18481) (arXiv preprint listing KDD 2026, code/data promised upon acceptance; Appendix C BTC daily, Jan 1, 2025 to Jan 1, 2026, 5 runs per model, zero cost), direct per-bar trading remained unstable at `T=0` across six frontier models, with within-model pairwise action agreement across repeated runs ranging from `0.36` to `0.48` (`gemini-3-pro-preview`) up to `88.2%` (`gpt-5.2`): "Even with temperature=0 (deterministic decoding), LLMs generate completely different trading action sequences across runs on identical market data, exhibiting no consistency in decision-making patterns."
- STATED (WEAK / OVERSTATED AS DISPERSION EVIDENCE): In [Evaluating "Thinking" LLMs for Stock Prediction](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08608) (arXiv preprint, no code URL, no public data, no numerical cost rates printed, NIFTY Indian equities "2019–present" walk-forward with no cutoff check or exact end date, single-run / run count unstated), a calibrated direct LLM (`gpt-4o-mini`, `B=0`) achieved `+1.471` net Sharpe versus `-5.667` (raw calibrated `gpt-5`, `B=512`) and `-0.426` (calibrated+blended `gpt-5`), while plain Ridge regression beat both at `+4.156`. The abstract asserts: "TLLM outputs exhibit higher dispersion date–by–date than direct LLMs, increasing error bars and turnover." However, the paper does not report any multi-run or repeated-seed variance statistic to back that dispersion claim.

### Majority Vote or Averaging Across k Parallel Sessions

- STATED (NON-TRADING BINARY QA): In [Correlation limits the reliability of ensemble decision making with large language models](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44564-026-00040-z) (*Discover Informatics*, Springer open-access, Nov 2026, code/prompts/responses released in ESM 1–3; 562 four-option MMLU questions), `rho` is defined strictly as the correlation across questions between two agents' **binary correctness indicators** (`1` right, `0` wrong — i.e., binary error correlation, not vote similarity and not portfolio returns). Under exchangeable binary votes, averaging `N` votes gives variance proportional to `1 + (N - 1) * rho` divided by `N`, so `N_eff = N / [1 + (N - 1) * rho]` never exceeds `1 / rho`: "The effective number of independent voters, a variance-based effective sample size, is capped by the reciprocal of the correlation: a hundred agents correlated at 0.3 have the variance of about three independent ones."
- STATED (NON-TRADING BINARY QA): In [Correlation limits the reliability of ensemble decision making with large language models](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44564-026-00040-z), five temperature samples (`T=0.8`) or five persona prompts (`T=0`) of `gpt-4.1-mini` both measured binary error correlation `rho = 0.85` (`95% CI 0.81` to `0.89`), yielding `N_eff` of about `1.1` independent voters at `5x` token cost and zero accuracy gain over the best single member: "The two cosmetic ensembles are almost indistinguishable: temperature samples and personas of the base model are both correlated at 0.85 (95% interval 0.81 to 0.89), each ensemble is worth about 1.1 independent voters, and its plurality vote is no more accurate than its best member (0.843 against 0.843 for temperature, 0.838 against 0.847 for personas)."
- STATED (NON-TRADING BINARY QA): In the same study ([Correlation limits the reliability of ensemble decision making with large language models](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44564-026-00040-z)), five models from different developers (`gpt-4.1-mini`, `Mistral-Large-3`, `DeepSeek-V3.2`, `Kimi-K2.5`, `grok-4.3`; note the last two reason internally) cut binary error correlation to `rho = 0.38` (`95% CI 0.30` to `0.45`, pairwise `0.23` to `0.48`), raising `N_eff` to `2.0` and plurality accuracy to `0.923` (`+4.5 pp` above average member, though still below the single best member `Kimi-K2.5` at `0.940`): "The family ensemble differs in kind. Its correlation is 0.38 (0.30 to 0.45), with pairwise values between 0.23 and 0.48; its effective size is 2.0; and its plurality vote reaches 0.923, 8.4 points above the base model and 4.5 above the average member."
- UNKNOWN: No controlled post-cutoff trading study directly measures `N_eff` or return correlation `rho` of averaging `k` parallel equity-portfolio sessions (same-model vs cross-family) on weekly portfolio returns. Because equity portfolios share market-factor exposure and price-tape covariance across all models, MMLU binary-error correlations (`0.85` vs `0.38`) do not automatically transfer to weekly return correlation.

### Structured Output With Fixed Position Grids

- STATED: In [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) (arXiv preprint, artifacts available only on request; ~72 S&P 100 equities, 26 bi-weekly rounds June 2025 to June 2026, post-cutoff, LOW cost tier, 5 seeds), structured workflow mode outperformed unconstrained autonomous mode on every reliable model tested (`deepseek-chat-v3.1`: Sharpe `2.99` vs `1.67`; `claude-haiku-4.5`: `3.21` vs `1.92`; `gpt-5-mini`: `2.62` vs `1.76`; `gemini-2.5-flash-lite`: `2.31` vs `1.75`), with zero constraint violations and zero style drift across all 13 configurations; per-mode SD is UNKNOWN because Table 5 prints 5-seed means without per-mode SD.
- STATED: In [Macro-to-Tilt Ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08283) (arXiv preprint, replication materials available only on request; 15 US commodity-related ETFs, 124 weekly rounds October 2023 to February 2026, `gpt-4o-mini` at `T=0`, API cost `~$0.002` per weekly observation, `0` to `30 bp` one-way costs), feeding LLM JSON tilt signals in `[-2, +2]` (note: the five-level discrete integer grid `{-2, -1, 0, +1, +2}` is specified for the Rule Agent; LLM tilts are described as in `[-2, +2]`) into a shared deterministic inverse-volatility/equal-weight allocator with `0.30` single-name cap and `0.50` weekly turnover cap kept annual return and Sharpe tightly clustered across different prompt personas — Hawkish (`7.74%`, Sharpe `0.57`), Dovish (`7.61%`, Sharpe `0.56`), Debate (`7.69%`, Sharpe `0.57`), and deterministic Rule (`7.11%`, Sharpe `0.53`) agents: "To ensure consistency and comparability across results, all strategies share the same data pipeline and the same portfolio construction engine." Caveat: outputs were cached from a single `T=0` pass per date, so **zero repeated-seed run-to-run SD was measured**.
- UNKNOWN: Neither study reports a paired repeated-seed SD comparison of a discrete tilt grid (`{-2, -1, 0, +1, +2}`) against free-form dollar sizing on the same model and dates.

### Fewer Decisions Per Week (Rebalance Cadence)

- STATED: In [ChatGPT in Systematic Investing](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26228) (arXiv preprint, no code, proprietary internal Finreon equity dataset; S&P 500 equities, post-cutoff test January 2024 to March 2025, 312 trading days, `gpt-4o-mini` October 2023 cutoff, `2 bp` transaction costs, single run), cutting decision frequency from weekly to monthly raised out-of-sample Sharpe from roughly `0.7` to `1.1` (optimized Table 3 OOS Sharpe `1.06` vs baseline `0.79`): "Panel (a) shows that the LLM-enhanced strategy performs remarkably better at a monthly rebalancing frequency than at a weekly one, with Sharpe ratios of approximately 1.1 and 0.7, respectively." (Note: the ~75% API-call reduction is arithmetic from weekly to monthly cadence, not a figure stated in the paper.)
- UNKNOWN: [ChatGPT in Systematic Investing](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26228) did not report repeated-seed return SD for weekly versus monthly cadence, so the exact run-to-run SD reduction from fewer decisions per week remains unmeasured in a multi-seed equity benchmark.

### Turnover Caps and Execution Inertia

- STATED: In [Can Blindfolded LLMs Still Trade?](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17692) (arXiv preprint, no code link, datasets promised upon publication; point-in-time anonymized S&P 500 stocks, 145 trading days Jan 2 to Aug 1, 2025 OOS, `10 bp` per unit turnover, 20 seeds), replacing naive equal-weight top-20 GNN rebalancing with a cost-aware RL execution policy (using inertia smoothing `rebalance_eta = 0.10` in main 2025 OOS and `0.08` in extended 2024–2025 OOS) cut daily turnover from `139%/day` to `1.7%/day` and turned net Sharpe from `-1.17` to `+1.08 ± 0.31` (full system `1.40 ± 0.22`): "What if we simply invest equal weight in the top 20 GNN scores? Turnover reaches 139%/day, and after cost deduction, Sharpe collapses to -1.17. The RL policy suppresses turnover to 1.7%/day while achieving Sharpe 1.08±0.31." Caveat: this is a learned RL execution policy over GNN scores, not a pure LLM desk with a simple fixed weight-change deadband.
- STATED: In [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) (5 seeds, 52 weeks), removing both cost-modeling and systematic execution (`execution-naive` cluster) increased realized trading friction by `18%` while leaving Sharpe inside the `±0.42` noise band (`ΔSharpe +0.19`, range `[+0.04, +0.44]`, cumulative return `+23.3%`, turnover `~0.18`); removing cost-modeling alone (`no-cost-modeling`) raised realized friction by `10%` with `ΔSharpe +0.37` (`[+0.36, +0.39]`).
- UNKNOWN: No controlled multi-seed study directly isolates the change in same-model run-to-run weekly return SD from adding a hard turnover cap or weight-change deadband alone.

### Rule Layer That Executes the Model's Stated Thresholds or Code

- STATED: In [AlphaForgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18481) (arXiv preprint listing KDD 2026, no code/data released yet; 7 single assets: BTC, ETH, AAPL, GOOGL, MSFT, NVDA, TSLA; 270 Stage-2 structured queries; 5 generations per query at `T=0` and `T=0.7`), moving the LLM from per-bar trading decisions to emitting deterministic Python strategy code makes re-executing identical generated code deterministic (zero execution variance), held intra-query Sharpe SD across five fresh code generations to `0.02` to `0.06` (typically `0.04`, an order of magnitude below inter-query SD of `0.26` to `0.28`), and kept mean Sharpe difference between `T=0` and `T=0.7` below `0.008` (`0.001` to `0.008`) across all six models: "Under the code-generation paradigm, the backtest execution is fully deterministic: given the same generated code and the same market data, the output metrics are identical with zero variance."
- STATED: In [AlphaForgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18481), Stage-2 code-execution Sharpe ratios ranged from `0.415` to `0.628` across the six models (`0.329` to `0.449` on 633 Stage-1 real-world queries) while cutting inference from one LLM call per bar to one call per strategy, though the paper's 2021 to 2025/2026 code-generation window is pre-cutoff (not look-ahead clean), tests single-asset binary invest/cash code rather than a multi-stock discrete tilt grid, and has conflicting friction notes (`10^-3` in Sec 4.1/5 vs `0` in Appendix E).

### Committee and Debate Designs

- STATED: In [Market Regime Council](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24490v2) (accepted NeurIPS 2026, open-source code/demo data on GitHub; 13 crypto assets, 1,037 trading days March 2023 to December 2025, no cutoff check, 5 seeds, `0 bp` main Table 2/3 with `5`/`10`/`30 bp` sensitivity — `10 bp` 5-seed MRC SR `1.23 ± 0.06`), pairwise Socratic debate between specialist agents degraded Sharpe relative to the best individual specialist (`1.34 ± 0.25`, `1.10 ± 0.28`, and `0.98 ± 0.17` vs `1.54 ± 0.22`) AND preserved or amplified inter-seed variance (CR SD up to `±95.2%`, SR SD up to `±0.28` vs `±0.05` to `±0.22` for solo specialists): "Stage 2 pairwise coalitions consistently underperform the best Stage 1 individual portfolio on SR (1.34, 1.10, and 0.98 versus 1.54) while preserving or amplifying inter-seed variance, with A1 ⊕ A3 CR variance reaching ±95.2%, and offer no compensating improvement in MDD."
- STATED: In [Market Regime Council](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24490v2), replacing free-form debate with numerical Shapley-weighted coalition aggregation cut 5-seed Sharpe SD to `±0.07`, cut max drawdown by `13.4 pp` (`34.1%` vs `47.5%`), and achieved the only statistically significant IR gain (`0.47 ± 0.20`, `p < 0.05`) at a cost of `0.03` Sharpe (`1.51` vs `1.54`): "The full MRC system records SR = 1.51 and MDD = 34.1%, reducing MDD by 13.4 percentage points relative to A1 alone at a cost of only 0.03 in SR, tightening seed-level SR variance to ±0.07, and producing the only statistically significant IR advantage in the table (p<0.05)."
- STATED: In [Multi-Agent Debate for Explainable Trading](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29701) (arXiv preprint, no public code URL or data repo; Table 3 subset of 3 quarterly equity scenarios 2022–2023 out of 35 total scenarios, possible training-corpus overlap, zero transaction costs, `N = 2` to `3` runs per Table 3 row), 3-agent enriched debate lowered drawdown (`5.33%` vs `9.73%`) and raised Sharpe (`0.76 ± 0.48` vs `0.35 ± 0.17`) while WIDENING return SEM (`±1.74%` vs `±0.99%`), and 4-agent 5-round debate turned returns negative (`-0.27%`, Sharpe `-0.05`): "The 4-agent 5-round debate produces negative returns (-0.27%) despite maintaining stable per-round CRIT scores (ρ̄ ≈ 0.83–0.85, B.4), illustrating that extended debate can degrade performance through over-convergence."
- STATED: In [Macro-to-Tilt Ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08283) (124 weekly rounds, `0` to `30 bp` costs, single cached `T=0` pass), adding a Debate Agent changed Sharpe by `-0.0037` vs Hawkish (`p = 0.769`), `+0.0056` vs Dovish (`p = 0.163`), and `+0.001` vs the Hawkish/Dovish arithmetic average (no p-value reported) — a clean null at extra token cost.

## Part B: Design Levers in Controlled Tests (Positives, Nulls, and Negatives)

### Memory and Reflection

- STATED (NULL / NEGATIVE): In [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) (~72 US equities, post-cutoff 2025 to 2026, LOW costs, 5 seeds), removing memory raised Sharpe by `+0.52` (range `[+0.24, +0.72]`), removing reflection raised Sharpe by `+0.09` (`[-0.16, +0.39]`), and removing both (`no-learning`) raised Sharpe by `+0.53` (`[+0.12, +0.78]`, cumulative return `+29.6%`): "Every module-off ΔSharpe is positive (means +0.09…+0.66): removal never cleanly hurts, which is mechanistically implausible."
- STATED (NEGATIVE): In [On the Role of Tools in Agentic LLM-Based Trading Systems](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper196.pdf) (CLEF 2026 Working Notes / CEUR Workshop Proceedings, no code or data URL; TSLA and BTC, chronological walk-forward August 2024 to January 2026, `ChatGPT-4.1-nano`, run count and transaction costs unstated), adding 5-day or 10-day rolling reflective memory degraded TSLA Sharpe from `-0.76` to `-1.14` (5d) and `-0.85` (10d) and left BTC Sharpe negative (`-0.27` and `-0.04` vs `-0.29`): "Contrary to our hypothesis, the introduction of reflective memory consistently deteriorates performance across both assets."
- STATED (TINY POSITIVE ON SYNTHETIC ONLY): In [Representation Signatures and Risk-Feedback Alignment](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28850) (arXiv preprint, derived artifacts in TradeArena repo; 3 synthetic assets, seeds 3, 7, 11, with simulated slippage), a mechanical memory-aware exposure overlay (cutting exposure after recent drawdowns or rejected orders) slightly improved return (`0.427` vs `0.423`) and Sharpe (`7.878` vs `7.696`), but this is a synthetic risk-feedback throttle rather than free-text LLM reflection.

### Tool Access to Quantitative Indicators

- STATED (POSITIVE AT 20–50 STOCKS, NULL/NEGATIVE AT 10 STOCKS): In [Toward Expert Investment Teams](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23330) (arXiv preprint, code promised upon acceptance, no dataset released; TOPIX 100 Japanese equities, September 2023 to November 2025 with `GPT-4o` paper-stated August 2023 cutoff, `10 bp` one-way cost, 50 independent trials, monthly rebalance), removing the fine-grained Technical indicator agent caused statistically significant Sharpe drops for portfolio sizes 20, 30, 40, and 50 (`-0.42`, `-0.40`, `-0.56`, `-0.66`, all `p < 0.0001`), **but raised Sharpe at 10 stocks (`+0.13`, not significant)**, whereas removing the Quantitative, Qualitative, News, or Macro agents improved Sharpe across all tested sizes (`+0.12` to `+0.50`): "In the fine-grained setting (Table 2a), however, the 'w/o Technical' condition shows predominantly negative differences, especially for larger portfolio sizes. This implies that the Technical Agent provides particularly strong predictive signals." (Note: the paper's `< 0.002` SD across 50 trials is for text-embedding cosine similarity between agent outputs, not return or Sharpe SD.)
- STATED (MIXED / NULL): In [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) (post-cutoff, 5 seeds, LOW costs), removing `advanced-quant` tools changed Sharpe by `+0.16` (range `[-0.06, +0.32]`, inside the `±0.42` noise band). In [On the Role of Tools](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper196.pdf), replacing standard technical-indicator tools with a random-forest ML signal improved BTC Sharpe (`-0.29` to `+0.15`, and `-0.30` to `+0.40` with enhanced news) and TSLA with naive news (`-0.76` to `-0.45`, `+0.31` SR) but hurt TSLA with enhanced news (`-0.13` to `-0.37`, `-0.24` SR).
- STATED (POSITIVE IN GNN+RL PIPELINE, ANONYMIZED, COSTED, 20 SEEDS): In [Can Blindfolded LLMs Still Trade?](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17692) (point-in-time anonymized S&P 500, Jan–Aug 2025 OOS, `10 bp` cost, 20 seeds), keeping LLM-interpreted technical+news features in a GNN+RL pipeline beat RAW technical indicators alone (`Sharpe 1.40` vs `1.14 ± 0.02`, `p < 10^-4`), though the graph structure contributed three times more (`-0.78` SR, `p < 0.001`) than the LLM features (`-0.26` SR): "If we remove LLM features and use only RAW technical indicators (passed directly to the GNN without SBERT encoding), Sharpe drops from 1.40 to 1.14±0.02 (Δ = -0.26, p < 10^-4, 20 seeds)."

### Role Split (Scout and Boss / Multi-Agent vs Single-Agent)

- STATED (NULL / NEGATIVE): In [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) (post-cutoff, 5 seeds, LOW costs), stripping the entire multi-module analyst and learning stack down to `bare-workflow` (mandate + cost-aware execution only) matched or beat the full multi-agent stack on returns (`+27.3%`, Sharpe `2.52` vs `2.26`, `ΔSharpe +0.26`, range `[-0.40, +0.66]`, IR vs SPY `-0.00`): "On this benchmark and period, the agentic scaffolding contributes little to Sharpe, while the axes register what returns hide (Table 7)."
- STATED (NEGATIVE ON EXTRA ROLES): In [Toward Expert Investment Teams](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23330) (post-cutoff, `10 bp` cost, 50 trials), removing any non-technical specialist role (Quantitative, Qualitative, News, Macro) in the fine-grained multi-agent team raised Sharpe by `+0.12` to `+0.50`, showing that extra analyst roles injected noise or redundancy: "Overall, most ablation settings show positive differences relative to the full-agent configuration, indicating that most individual agents may introduce noise or redundant signals."
- UNKNOWN: No controlled post-cutoff, cost-included, multi-seed stock study shows that splitting a price scout from a boss improves net return over a single model seeing the same structured price table directly.

### Longer Horizon (Weekly vs Monthly vs Daily)

- STATED (POSITIVE, SINGLE-RUN PREPRINT): In [ChatGPT in Systematic Investing](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26228) (S&P 500, post-cutoff 2024 to 2025, `2 bp` cost, single run, proprietary data), monthly rebalancing beat weekly rebalancing by roughly `+0.4` Sharpe (`~1.1` vs `~0.7`) because lower turnover preserved net returns.
- UNKNOWN: No multi-seed post-cutoff study isolates holding-period length (1 week vs 4 weeks) on the same LLM stock desk with repeated-run variance reported.

### Risk Overlays (Position Caps, Drawdown Gates, Vol Scaling)

- STATED (CUTS DRAWDOWN, LOWERS AVERAGE RETURN, SYNTHETIC ONLY): In [Representation Signatures and Risk-Feedback Alignment](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28850) (120 heterogeneous synthetic markets with slippage and fills), enabling the risk-aware overlay versus the no-risk ablation lowered average return by `-0.045` (`95% CI [-0.059, -0.030]`, `p < 10^-9`) while cutting max drawdown (`+0.015`, `p < 10^-11`, `80%` win rate), raising fill rate (`+0.200`, `p < 10^-12`), and cutting rejected orders by `38.2` per market (separately, tightening the max-position cap from `50%` to `35%` to `20%` cut 3-seed synthetic return from `0.459` to `0.423` to `0.243` and drawdown from `-0.026` to `-0.025` to `-0.018`): "Compared with the no-risk ablation, the risk-aware agent has lower return on average (-0.045, p<10^-9) but materially better drawdown, much higher fill rate, and 38.2 fewer rejected orders per market."
- STATED (CUTS DRAWDOWN, SLIGHT SHARPE TRADE-OFF, UNQUANTIFIED): In [ChatGPT in Systematic Investing](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26228) (S&P 500, post-cutoff 2024 to 2025, `2 bp` cost), enforcing a `15%` single-stock weight cap improved risk management at an unquantified slight Sharpe cost: "Importantly, applying diversification constraints to portfolio weights reduces the Sharpe ratio slightly, but results in substantial improvements in terms of risk-management."
- STATED (INFERRED DRAWDOWN PROTECTION): In [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) (post-cutoff, 5 seeds, LOW costs), hard position caps and the staged risk-constraint layer held max drawdown between `-3.2%` and `-4.7%` across all 13 module ablations (versus `-8.9%` for SPY), though the constraint layer was not ablated alone: "The attribution is by elimination, since the constraint layer is not ablated alone."

## Three Concrete, Cheap Changes to Our Desk Tests (Queued as Rows 122 to 124)

Each proposal targets the INFERRED `91.4 bp` weekly repeat-gap SD proxy in `docs/research/163_tm09_power_count.md` before spending judged weeks on TM09, treating the outside papers as motivation rather than proved equity-desk SD reductions.

1. **Row 122: Deterministic code executor on a fixed five-level tilt grid (`{-2, -1, 0, +1, +2}`) at `T=0` with a `2 pp` turnover deadband.** Motivated by [AlphaForgeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18481) (code generation, `0.02`–`0.06` intra-query SR SD), [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) (structured > autonomous mean SR, no per-mode SD), [Macro-to-Tilt Ablation](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.08283) (shared allocator on `[-2, +2]` tilts, no multi-seed SD), and [Can Blindfolded LLMs Still Trade?](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.17692) (RL execution smoothing); multi-seed return SD of a discrete tilt grid is UNKNOWN in published equity tests. What we would measure: same-model weekly repeat-gap SD (`P1` minus `P2`), weekly turnover, and mean weekly net return versus the inferred `91.4 bp` baseline. Count needed: INFERENCE in `docs/research/163_tm09_power_count.md`, **7 unused post-cutoff price-only stock weeks** (28 boss sessions across 2 bosses x 2 repeats) bound the repeat-gap SD within a factor of `2` at `90%` confidence (`14` weeks / `56` sessions for a factor of `1.5`), testing whether SD falls below `45 bp` a week.
2. **Row 123: Cross-family averaging (one `T=0` session per distinct non-reasoning model family) instead of `k` repeats of one model.** Motivated by the non-trading MMLU binary-error correlation gap (`rho = 0.85` same-model vs `0.38` cross-family, `N_eff` `1.1` vs `2.0`) in [Discover Informatics 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44564-026-00040-z) and single-stock `T=0` SD reduction in [TradingAgents Reproducibility](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3800973.3801029) (note: [Evaluating "Thinking" LLMs](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08608) is an uncoded pre-cutoff preprint with no quantified multi-run dispersion). Because transfer from MMLU binary errors to weekly equity returns is UNKNOWN, this test measures pairwise weekly return correlation `rho` within-model (`P1` vs `P2`) versus across model families (`Boss A` vs `Boss B`), implied `N_eff`, and the weekly SD of the cross-family average versus the same-model average. Count needed: **7 to 14 unused post-cutoff price-only stock weeks** (reuses the same 28 to 56 boss sessions as Row 122 if run on the same weeks with 2 to 4 direct model families).
3. **Row 124: Single weekly wake with pre-computed technical indicator tools and deterministic caps, keeping reflection and debate off.** Motivated by [ChatGPT in Systematic Investing](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26228) (single-run uncoded preprint: monthly `~1.1` vs weekly `~0.7` SR), [Toward Expert Investment Teams](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23330) (uncoded preprint: removing technical agent hurt at 20–50 stocks, `+0.13` at 10 stocks), [CLQT](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29771v3) (memory/reflection null-to-negative; `advanced-quant` null), [On the Role of Tools](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper196.pdf) (reflective memory negative; no costs/run count), and [Market Regime Council](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24490v2) (pairwise debate harmful). What we would measure: weekly repeat-gap SD (`P1` minus `P2`), weekly turnover, and mean weekly net return for a single-wake weekly schedule (with pre-computed local technical indicators and hard single-name caps, no intra-week wakes, no reflection, no debate) versus the multi-wake TM09 schedule. Count needed: **7 unused post-cutoff price-only stock weeks** (28 boss sessions, cutting API calls roughly `80%` relative to five daily wakes per week) to measure whether weekly repeat-gap SD drops by at least `25%` without degrading average net return.
