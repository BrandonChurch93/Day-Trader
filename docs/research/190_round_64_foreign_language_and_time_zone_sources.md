## Idea 1A: Foreign Crypto Exchange Listing Notices (Coinone, Korbit, OKX, Bitget, KuCoin, HTX, MEXC)

- Relation to prior repo work: Extends Replay 20 (`docs/research/71_replay_20_upbit_listing_short.md`), Round 15 (`docs/research/85_round_15_crypto_scheduled_events.md`), Round 28 (`docs/research/115_round_28_us_reachable_crypto_forced_flows.md`), and Queue row 48 (Plan 28). What is different now is checking non-Upbit, non-Bithumb exchanges whose APIs actually open from this machine (Coinone, OKX, Bitget, KuCoin, HTX).
- What it is and who is on the other side: Buying a US-tradable coin immediately when a foreign exchange posts a new spot or perpetual listing notice, or shorting the post-listing fade after retail buyers chase the headline. On the long side, fast bots already buy within seconds and sell into slower readers. On the short fade side, domestic retail buyers on the newly listing exchange (especially Korean won accounts) overpay for newly accessible tokens and hold through the unwind.
- Why an AI agent can do it and why a fund skips it: An agent can poll Korean, Chinese, Japanese, and English exchange notice APIs every few seconds around the clock without sleep. Professional crypto desks already run low-latency announcement scrapers on offshore venues with zero-fee maker tiers and deep perpetual liquidity; they do not skip this trade, they dominate the first seconds of it.
- Evidence: In the [CoinSight exchange announcement study](https://getcoinsight.com/en/research/exchange-announcement-study) (non-peer-reviewed industry data study, Aug 2022 to Nov 2026, 2,844 coin-events across 1,843 notices on Binance, Bybit, OKX, Upbit, and Bithumb, priced from 3 minutes after publication with no fees or slippage deducted), median post-listing returns from +3 minutes are negative across every exchange (`STATED`): OKX spot listings (n=75) return +0.2% at 1h, -2.1% at 24h, and -8.9% at 7d; Bybit spot listings (n=110) return -0.2% at 1h, -1.9% at 24h, and -8.5% at 7d; OKX perpetual listings (n=336) return +0.1% at 1h, -0.7% at 24h, and -3.9% at 7d; Upbit KRW listings (n=165) return -0.6% at 1h, -10.3% at 24h, and -18.2% at 7d. In the [newcoinlistings.com two-year tracker](https://newcoinlistings.com/listing-effect) (non-peer-reviewed live database, exact start date not stated, gross returns from announcement or start of trading), median 24h and 7d returns are negative on OKX (-5.2% 24h / -15.1% 7d, n=56), Gate (-6.3% / -13.4%, n=89), HTX (-1.4% / -4.8%, n=129), MEXC (-4.4% / -13.1%, n=73), and KuCoin (-4.9% 24h, n=3) (`STATED`). No audited peer-reviewed study shows positive net returns for slow US-spot buyers after +3 minutes (`UNKNOWN`).
- US $500 to $2,000 account feasibility and dollar math: Long spot trades on Coinbase Advanced (1.0% to 2.5% round-trip with spread, `MEASURED` in `docs/research/180_round_57_cost_as_the_binding_limit.md`), Kraken Pro US (0.80% to 2.0% round-trip), or Binance.US (0% maker / 2 bp taker fee plus live spread under Measurement 132) face negative gross medians after +3 minutes, yielding negative expected dollars a year. Shorting the post-listing fade (as in Replay 20) is blocked for a $500 to $2,000 Idaho account because US-legal retail shorting covers only about 30 to 35 larger-cap non-BTC/ETH coins across Kraken Pro US margin (`:BTNL`, 25 to 28 USD pairs, 40/80 bps per side plus 4h rollover) and Coinbase Financial Markets (24 perpetual-style futures) (`MEASURED` in `docs/research/115_round_28_us_reachable_crypto_forced_flows.md` and `docs/STATUS.md`). In Plan 28, only 1 of 205 Upbit KRW listings in 2024 to 2026 had a US-reachable retail short contract at notice time (`MEASURED` in `docs/STATUS.md`).
- Data availability, timestamps, and machine access: Coinone (`created_at`), OKX (`pTime`, `businessPTime`), Bitget (`cTime`), KuCoin (`cTime`), and HTX open with HTTP 200 JSON from this machine (`MEASURED`). As documented in Round 18 (`docs/research/88_round_18_point_in_time_feeds.md`), exchange APIs can silently edit or delete past notices without a revision log (`MEASURED`).

## Idea 1B: Foreign Crypto Exchange Delisting, Warning, and Deposit/Withdrawal Suspension Notices

- Relation to prior repo work: Extends Queue row 72 (desk exit rule on major exchange delisting or monitoring tag), Queue row 79 / Plan 45 (Binance/Upbit/Bithumb cautions closed at feasibility due to HTTP 403), and Round 28 (`docs/research/115_round_28_us_reachable_crypto_forced_flows.md`). What is different now is using open-API foreign venues (Coinone, OKX, Bitget, KuCoin, HTX) that return HTTP 200 JSON with timestamps from this machine.
- What it is and who is on the other side: Monitoring foreign exchange APIs for delisting notices, investment-warning tags, and unplanned deposit or withdrawal suspensions on coins that also trade on US spot venues (Coinbase, Kraken, Binance.US), either to short the coin or to trigger an immediate protective exit on an existing long spot position (such as Plan 75 / E10). The losing side is slow spot holders who do not read non-English exchange notices and sell days later into thinning order books.
- Why an AI agent can do it and why a fund skips it: An agent can parse Korean, Chinese, and English notice titles across five open APIs 24 hours a day and match tickers to a US spot portfolio. Funds already short Binance and OKX delistings on offshore perpetuals, but they do not bother running a protective exit overlay on a $500 US spot account.
- Evidence: In the [CoinSight exchange announcement study](https://getcoinsight.com/en/research/exchange-announcement-study) (non-peer-reviewed, Aug 2022 to Nov 2026, priced from +3 minutes, gross of fees), median post-notice moves are sharply negative (`STATED`): OKX delistings (n=65) drop -1.4% at 1h, -5.8% at 24h, and -11.7% at 7d (only 9% up after 24h); Upbit caution/monitoring tags (n=46) drop -1.2% at 1h, -5.2% at 24h, and -13.6% at 7d; all-exchange delistings (n=395) drop -6.8% at 24h and -16.2% at 7d. Pair or margin removals have almost no effect (+0.1% at 24h, n=635, `STATED`). Separate peer-reviewed or audited studies of deposit/withdrawal suspension notices alone were not found (`UNKNOWN`).
- US $500 to $2,000 account feasibility and dollar math: Standalone shorting is not feasible on a $500 to $2,000 US account because Round 28 (`docs/research/115_round_28_us_reachable_crypto_forced_flows.md`) measured 0 full delistings across the 29 US-shortable coins in 2024 to 2026 (`MEASURED`). As a protective exit filter for a long-only US spot account holding coins on Coinbase, Kraken, or Binance.US, the account can always sell what it already owns at normal spot fees; dollar value depends entirely on how often held US-spot coins receive a foreign delisting or caution notice (`UNKNOWN` until counted).
- Data availability, timestamps, and machine access: Coinone (`created_at`), OKX (`pTime`), Bitget (`cTime`), KuCoin (`cTime`), and HTX all returned HTTP 200 JSON from this machine (`MEASURED`). Historical archives can be edited or pruned without a log (`MEASURED` in `docs/research/88_round_18_point_in_time_feeds.md`), so forward logging is cleaner than backfilling.
- Independent trades a year: Korea's five exchanges delisted 430 altcoins from 2022 through July 2026, including 157 on Coinone (~34/yr) (`STATED` in [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/08/31/korean-crypto-exchanges-delisted-430-of-1236-altcoins-since)), and OKX/Bitget/KuCoin/HTX issue dozens more delistings and suspension notices per year. How many hit US-spot-tradable coins (especially the narrower Plan 75 / E10 universe) is `UNKNOWN` and may be under 30/yr.
- Strongest reason it is false or already gone: Most delisted or warned coins on Coinone, OKX, Bitget, KuCoin, and HTX are tail altcoins never listed on Coinbase, Kraken, or Binance.US, and when a US-listed coin is hit, the initial drop may already finish inside the first 1 to 3 minutes before a spot sell fills.
- Verdict: `COUNT FIRST (no prices)` for the US-spot protective exit filter (extending Queue row 72 to open APIs); `DROP` for standalone shorting.

## Idea 2B: Japan JVCEA Green List Approved-Coin Additions

- Relation to prior repo work: `NEW`.
- What it is and who is on the other side: Buying a coin when Japan's self-regulatory body, the Japan Virtual and Crypto Assets Exchange Association (JVCEA), adds it to the Green List, allowing other Japanese exchanges to list it without full screening. The hoped-for counterparty would be slower Japanese retail buyers once more domestic venues list the token.

No peer-reviewed or industry event study showing post-addition abnormal returns after JVCEA Green List updates was found (`UNKNOWN`).
- US $500 to $2,000 account feasibility and dollar math: US spot venues list most large-cap coins that reach three Japanese exchanges, so spot execution would be possible at 0.04% to 2.5% round-trip depending on venue, but with no measured positive edge, expected dollar return is `UNKNOWN` (likely negative after costs).
- Data availability, timestamps, and machine access: The [JVCEA documents page](https://jvcea.or.jp/about/document/) opens with HTTP 200 HTML from this machine (`MEASURED`), but shows no token count, no latest update timestamp, and no historical revision log (`STATED`). ## Idea 3: Foreign Corporate Filings (HKEXnews, TDnet, DART, MOPS, MAYA, UK RNS) Traded in US Pre-Market or Overnight on ADRs

- Relation to prior repo work: `NEW` (prior rounds covered US SEC EDGAR filings such as Form 4, 8-K, 13D/G, and S-3, not non-US exchange portals for foreign private issuers).
- What it is and who is on the other side: Reading earnings results and material notices published in Hong Kong, Tokyo, Seoul, Taipei, Tel Aviv, or Europe during US nighttime hours, then buying or shorting the US-listed ADR or dual-listed share in US overnight (8:00 PM to 4:00 AM ET) or pre-market (4:00 AM to 9:30 AM ET) sessions before US regular-hours traders react. For this to work, US ADR liquidity providers would have to leave stale quotes sitting in pre-market while the home-market stock already moved.
- Why an AI agent can do it and why a fund skips it: An agent can read Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Hebrew, and European filings at 3:00 AM Mountain time without fatigue. Professional cross-border arbitrage desks and market makers already link Tokyo, Hong Kong, London, and Tel Aviv order books directly to US ADR quotes in milliseconds; they do not leave free money on the table.
- Evidence: In a peer-reviewed study of 374 NYSE and Nasdaq ADRs from 37 countries over 1991 to 2023, [Hoang and Vo (2024, Australian Journal of Management)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/03128962241286085?download=true) find that ADR opening prices are significantly more efficient than matched US stocks (opening-price efficiency 0.96 vs 0.89, t=10.43), that 50% of ADR daily return is already incorporated overnight (vs 21% for matched US stocks, t=134.97), and that the home-market price is quickly handed off to the US open whenever the home market trades (`STATED`). On multi-day drift, a non-peer-reviewed preprint by [X.M. (2026, Research Square)](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10584858/v1) studying 1,506 earnings announcements from Jan 2021 to Dec 2024 (including 38 non-US ADR issuers / 399 announcements) finds that after ChatGPT's release in Nov 2022, post-earnings continuation drift fell significantly for non-US ADRs (-0.0205, t=-3.37), indicating that AI text processing is already compressing foreign-issuer filing drift (`STATED`). No study shows net profits after extended-hours spreads for a slow retail ADR reader (`UNKNOWN`).
- US $500 to $2,000 account feasibility and dollar math: A small US retail account can place commission-free extended-hours limit orders on Alpaca from 4:00 AM to 8:00 PM ET and overnight limit orders from 8:00 PM to 4:00 AM ET (`STATED` in [Alpaca Orders documentation](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca)). However, Alpaca requires limit orders only, warns of wider spreads and partial or missed fills in extended hours, and restricts shorting on a $500 to $2,000 cash or sub-$2,000 margin account (`STATED`). Even a 50 to 150 bp extended-hours half-spread on thin ADRs would erase any residual drift, yielding negative expected dollars a year.
- Data availability, timestamps, and machine access: HKEXnews, TDnet, DART web, MOPS, MAYA web, UK RNS (Investegate), and Euronext Live returned HTTP 200 HTML from this machine, while KIND and MAYA API returned 403, OpenDART returned 502, and EDINET v2 API requires a key (`MEASURED`). Most of these portals serve session-based HTML rather than clean free historical archives with exact UTC publication seconds (`MEASURED`/`UNKNOWN`).
- Independent trades a year: Hundreds of quarterly earnings and material notices per year across ~370+ NYSE/Nasdaq ADRs (`STATED` in [Hoang and Vo](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/03128962241286085?download=true)), so event count is well above 30/yr, but the move occurs in the home market before US execution.
- Strongest reason it is false or already gone: The underlying shares trade in the home market (Hong Kong, Tokyo, London, Tel Aviv) while the US is closed, so cross-border arbitrageurs reprice the ADR immediately on home-market ticks, and AI readers have already compressed post-announcement ADR drift since late 2022.
- Verdict: `DROP`.

- What it is and who is on the other side: Shorting (or buying on successful appeals/cleared cases) US-listed multinationals or Chinese ADRs when the European Commission (DG COMP), UK Competition and Markets Authority (CMA), or China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) announces an antitrust investigation, dawn raid, or infringement decision.
- Why an AI agent can do it and why a fund skips it: An agent can poll UK CMA's JSON API and EU/Chinese competition portals continuously in local languages. Event-driven funds already have wire alerts and legal analysts covering every major competition case on large-cap US names.
- Evidence: In a peer-reviewed study of 253 companies across 118 EU antitrust cases from 1974 to 2004, [Günster and van Dijk (2016, International Review of Law and Economics)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/irlaec/v46y2016icp20-33.html) found abnormal stock returns of almost -5% around dawn raids, -2% around final decisions, and up to +4% around successful appeals (gross daily returns, no trading costs) (`STATED`). In a working paper covering 39 antitrust events from 2010 to 2022, [Huang, Yin, Zhang, and Zhao (2022)](https://www.ninayin.org/uploads/8/7/8/6/8786548/huang_yin_zhang_zhao_2022.12.15_r_r.pdf) found a -1.45% CAAR over [-1, +1] for 35 EU/US platform investigation starts (p<0.05) and -13.92% for China's SAMR probe of Alibaba (n=1) (`STATED`).
- US $500 to $2,000 account feasibility and dollar math: Most antitrust actions are negative events requiring shorting, which is restricted or unavailable on a $500 to $2,000 cash or small margin account. Even at 10 events/yr and a hypothetical 1% net capture on $500, gross profit would be $50/yr before borrow fees.
- Data availability, timestamps, and machine access: UK CMA's search JSON API (`https://www.gov.uk/api/search.json`) and EU DG COMP's web search both returned HTTP 200 from this machine (`MEASURED`). However, dawn raids are typically broken by press reports or company statements before appearing on regulator case pages.
- Independent trades a year: Well under 30 surprise events a year on US-listed stocks (Günster and van Dijk's entire 30-year EU dataset had 118 cases across all European and foreign firms, or ~4 cases/yr, `STATED`).
- Strongest reason it is false or already gone: Event frequency on US-listed companies is far below 30 a year, the primary move is negative (requiring shorting), and financial wires break dawn raids and fines before agency portals update.
- Verdict: `DROP`.

Business Science and Applied Management)](https://epublications.marquette.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1332&context=mgmt_fac) found a Day 0 mean abnormal return of -1.34% and a 3-day CAR of -1.87% (gross daily returns, no costs) (`STATED`). - Strongest reason it is false or already gone: Most EU Safety Gate alerts involve unlisted or immaterial consumer items, major recalls and UPC injunctions are disclosed via company press releases that move prices immediately, and the UPC portal blocks this machine (`HTTP 403`).

## Idea 5: Crypto Time-Zone Gaps (Asian Business Hours News vs US Venue Adjustment)

- Relation to prior repo work: Directly overlaps Replay 19 (`docs/research/64_replay_19_monday_asia.md`, Monday Asia 24h BTC/ETH trend FAILED at -53.88% net), Round 21 (`docs/research/93_round_21_small_coin_events.md`, Binance/Bybit-to-US-spot ms lead-lag ~300ms and Korean volume spikes skipped), and Round 30 (`docs/research/119_round_30_high_count_crypto_power_screen.md`, Kraken 12h daytime/overnight reversal and non-Plan-58 time-of-day windows skipped). What is different now is checking recent 2026 empirical evidence on whether Asian-hours information still creates a slow-adjusting gap on US crypto venues.
- What it is and who is on the other side: Trading BTC, ETH, or US-listed altcoins on Coinbase, Kraken, or Binance.US based on Asian-hours news or price moves under the hypothesis that US venues adjust with a delay when New York wakes up.
- Why an AI agent can do it and why a fund skips it: An agent can stay awake during 00:00 to 09:00 UTC. Market makers and cross-exchange arbitrageurs already run 24/7 bots linking Binance, OKX, Upbit, Coinbase, and Kraken within hundreds of milliseconds, so no multi-minute or multi-hour price gap survives between Asian and US venues.
- Evidence: In a peer-reviewed study of 87,672 hourly Kraken XBT/USD bars from 2016 to 2025, [Aldhahi (2026, Journal of Risk and Financial Management)](https://www.mdpi.com/1911-8074/19/9/707) shows that Bitcoin's intraday volatility has migrated strongly toward US equity hours: the 13:00 to 21:59 UTC window accounted for 50.6% of daily realized variance in 2022 to 2025 (up from 38.4% in 2016 to 2018), the calmest hour of the day is 05:00 UTC (Asian close / pre-Europe), and NYSE weekday holidays cut the US-hours variance share by 13.9 percentage points (t=3.81, p<0.001) (`STATED`). In a working paper using 5-minute Binance and Coinbase data across 17 US daylight-saving transitions from 2018 through spring 2026, [Wang (2026, SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7526338) confirms that BTC and ETH spot activity shifts with New York local time (+36.3% higher activity in the NY-aligned window, p=0.0078) (`STATED`). Neither study finds a tradable directional delay from Asian hours into US venues (`STATED`).
- US $500 to $2,000 account feasibility and dollar math: Executable on Coinbase, Kraken, or Binance.US, but prior repo Replay 19 lost -53.88% net (`MEASURED`) and Round 21 showed cross-venue lead-lag collapses above 0.12% round-trip cost (`STATED` in repo record).
- Data availability, timestamps, and machine access: Hourly and minute bars on Coinbase, Kraken, and Binance.US are free and timestamped in UTC (`MEASURED` in repo record).
- Independent trades a year: Hundreds of Asian sessions a year, but without an edge, high frequency only compounds spread and fee drag.
- Strongest reason it is false or already gone: Crypto trades 24/7 with automated cross-venue arbitrage closing price differences in under a second, while volatility and price discovery have shifted toward New York equity hours rather than lagging Asian hours.
- Verdict: `DROP`.
