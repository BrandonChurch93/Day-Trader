# 51. Buying single stocks to hold for weeks or months: the reading, checked, with the funds measured

Status: one helper wrote the reading on 2026-11-25 and a second opened every source: 26 claims confirmed, 8 wrong as first written, none not found. The builder then measured the real-money funds from our own price feed. Written 2026-11-25 18:51 UTC.

Why: the owner, 2026-11-25 (`docs/DECISIONS.md`): day trading belongs to small volatile coins; big stocks he would hold for weeks or months; he wants both explored.

## In plain words

- Most of the well-known ways to pick stocks to hold for weeks or months have a fund that runs them with real money, and most of those funds did not beat SPY. The table below is our own measurement.
- The clearest loser is the best-known one: buying breakouts in fast-growing leaders. The fund built on that list made +41.2% in almost 11 years while SPY made +321.1%.
- One fund is clearly ahead: SPMO, which holds the large stocks that rose most over the past year. It is ahead of SPY over 11, 5 and 3 years, but only in 6 of 11 calendar years, and a similar fund from another firm (MTUM) is behind. So the rule's details matter, and the lead is not clear of chance (t = 1.01 in the published check).
- The scout's top idea, buying a stock the day it gaps up on big news, rested on a test with a fault: it bought at the day's open using facts only known at the close. And 5% of its trades made 80% of its gain. It is not evidence.
- One result depends on the end date and on a figure we could not match. The fund that buys stocks dropped from an index (NIXT) was behind SPY from its start to 2026-09-30 by our feed (+36.5% against +42.6%) and ahead by 2026-11-24 (+46.6% against +34.4%). The reading has it ahead at 2026-09-30 (16.1% a year against 11.3% for the index, from a fund report). Our feed agrees on the fund's figure and not on the index's: SPY made about 19% a year over those dates. Cause UNKNOWN. Until it is traced, the claim that this fund beat the market over its first two years is not trusted.

## What this changes for our queue

- Dropped: breakouts in leaders as a plain rule, nearness to the 52-week high, momentum by industry, quality plus momentum screens, buybacks.
- Added to the queue as row 20: the plain large-stock momentum rule that SPMO follows, as a fixed monthly rule against SPY on a list of stocks fixed in time. It is the one method here with a real-money lead, and it is the yardstick our AI stock pickers should have to beat.
- Folded into row 17 (drift after earnings): the gap-up on earnings news, entered at the next open only.
- Folded into row 15 (insider buying): buying by several insiders at once in small companies far below their high. STATED: the published first-day gain of 4.13% is mostly an overnight jump nobody could buy (3.27 of it), and the costs in such small stocks are 1.7 to 3% a round trip.
- Kept as ideas, not tests: spin-offs (few events a year) and stocks dropped from an index (the holding time is years).
