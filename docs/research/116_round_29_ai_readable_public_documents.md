## Queued candidate: Row 97 (US corporate forward stock split announcements via SEC Form 8-K)

Why an AI reader helps: SEC EDGAR has no dedicated form code for forward stock splits. Standard keyword searches mix corporate forward splits with reverse stock splits, ETF/ETN splits, recapitalizations, and share-count mentions. An AI reader parsing timestamped `Form 8-K` acceptance dates and exhibits can cleanly separate genuine domestic exchange-listed corporate forward stock splits (ratio >= 2-for-1), extract the exact split ratio, record date, and ex-date, and exclude ETFs, OTC issuers, and reverse splits.

Count from free lawful source (2016 to 2025): MEASURED via SEC EDGAR EFTS (`2016-01-01` to `2025-12-31`): `2,048` Form 8-K filings match `"forward stock split"`, and `10,000+` Form 8-K filings match `"stock split"` excluding `"reverse stock split"` and `"reverse share split"`. Even after deduplicating to unique domestic exchange-listed corporate forward stock splits (excluding ETFs/ETNs and OTC), the 10-year docket is expected to exceed 150 to 300+ dated events (well above the 30-event floor). This count is a rough upper bound from the SEC full-text search answered on 2026-11-26; that source is on the project's stopped list and will not be used for the roster, so the real count for Row 97 must come from Form 8-K filings reached by CIK and form index (Item 5.03 or 8.01 with split language, read by the AI reader).

Published effect vs 2x round-trip spread: STATED across academic and institutional studies:

- [Ikenberry, Rankine, and Stice (1996, Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/what-do-stock-splits-really-signal/46D1B882F218BBED33C37FA9C4ED1709): "We observe significant post-split excess returns of 7.93 percent in the first year and 12.15 percent in the first three years for a sample of 1,275 two-for-one stock splits. These excess returns follow an announcement return of 3.38 percent, indicating that the market underreacts to split announcements."
- [Chen et al. (NCCU, 9,693 US stock splits 1984 to 2012)](https://ah.lib.nccu.edu.tw/bitstream/140.119/128724/1/354.pdf): "We first document that post-split drift mainly exists in the first three months and is positively associated with post-split standardized unexpected earnings (SUE)." And: "Based on 9693 stock splits during the period 1984-2012, we find that the post-split abnormal returns are significantly positive, with approximately 40 (22) basis points per month for 12 months in the equal- (value-) weighted portfolios using the WLS regressions, after controlling for size, book-to-market, momentum, and liquidity."
- [University of Texas at Austin / CICF (2016)](http://www.cicfconf.org/sites/default/files/paper_344.pdf): "The post-announcement drift in the U.S. continues for 12 months after the announcement, and continues to hold after 2000, i.e., after the anomaly was identified in the academic literature."
- [Bank of America Global Research via Business Insider (February 2025)](https://www.businessinsider.com/stock-split-candidates-buy-rated-picks-where-to-invest-bofa-2025-2): "After a split is announced, stocks have notched 25% total returns in the subsequent 12 months, compared to 12% for the broad index" and "Splits in 2024 are on track to outpace historical norms, with the average stock up 17% in the 6 months after their split."

Spread and US retail reachability: On liquid exchange-listed splitting equities (typically mid- and large-cap winners), retail round-trip spread plus slippage is roughly 10 to 30 bps ($0 commission on Alpaca, Fidelity, or Schwab). Published post-announcement excess drift (+1.2% EW over 3 months to +4.8% EW over 12 months in Chen et al., and +13% vs S&P 500 over 12 months in Bank of America 2025) is 4x to 40x the round-trip spread. Key $300 to $500 constraint to verify at count-first feasibility: many splitting stocks trade above $500 per share before the split, so a whole-share $500 account either needs fractional shares at announcement (`AD+1`) or entry on the post-split ex-date (`Ex+1`) once the share price drops into the $50 to $200 range.
