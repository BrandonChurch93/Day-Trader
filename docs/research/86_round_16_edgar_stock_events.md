## Small-Account Cost Floor ($500, Whole Shares, Long Only)

STATED: US retail equity commissions are $0 at standard retail brokers (such as Alpaca, Fidelity, and Schwab), and regulatory sell-side fees are under 0.01% per round trip. The binding friction on a $500 whole-share account is the bid-ask spread plus whole-share sizing lumps:

- Liquid mid-cap and large-cap stocks (market cap above $1 billion): roughly 0.04% to 0.15% round-trip spread (STATED).
- Small-cap exchange-listed stocks ($200 million to $1 billion): roughly 0.20% to 0.60% round-trip spread (STATED).
- Microcap and OTC stocks (below $200 million): roughly 1.00% to 3.00% or more round-trip spread, which can absorb a 1% to 2% drift (STATED).
- Whole-share constraint on $500: stocks trading above $500 cannot be purchased; stocks between $150 and $499 allow only 1 to 3 shares and create lumpy position sizing; stocks between $5 and $100 allow clean sizing across 2 to 5 concurrent positions (STATED).
- Because the day-0 filing jump is priced before a retail trader can enter at the next tradable bar, a long-only candidate needs a documented post-filing drift above 1.0% to 1.5% over days to weeks, outside microcaps, to clear costs.

## 1. Special Dividend Declarations (Post-Announcement Drift)

- Mechanism and who trades: Boards declare one-time special cash dividends when surplus cash exceeds near-term investment needs, signaling unexpected cash-flow strength or balance-sheet discipline (STATED). Because special dividends are irregular and frequently declared by smaller, less-followed firms outside regular earnings releases, institutional attention and analyst revisions lag the initial declaration, producing gradual post-announcement buying over the following month (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence: MEASURED: In a 2026 working paper, Hyun Ahn ([SSRN 7249419](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7249419) and [preprint draft](https://github.com/Crane-lupert/us-special-dividend-recurring-drift-xs/blob/main/paper/SD-01-SSRN-preprint-draft.md)) examines 5,246 US special-dividend declarations from 2003 to 2024 plus 7,986 declarations from 1962 to 2002: "I extend the window and document that declaring firms continue to earn abnormal returns of about $1.2\%$ over the following month (trading days $+2$ to $+20$; declaration-month clustered $t = 6.03$) on 5,246 U.S. declarations from 2003 to 2024 --- and that the same drift, at nearly the same magnitude, is present in the 7,986 declarations from 1962 to 2002 that no prior study examined: an anomaly hiding in plain sight for six decades." MEASURED: The same study reports that first-time special dividends drive a much larger drift and that the effect is independent of earnings drift and factor exposure ([preprint draft](https://github.com/Crane-lupert/us-special-dividend-recurring-drift-xs/blob/main/paper/SD-01-SSRN-preprint-draft.md)): "First-time specials drift roughly twice as much as specials the firm has declared repeatedly" and "a calendar-time portfolio earns a five-factor-plus-momentum alpha of $14.7\%$ per year ($t = 3.90$) during the drift window and none afterward, with unchanged loadings --- and it holds against size- and momentum-matched control firms." MEASURED: On trading costs, the author states ([preprint draft](https://github.com/Crane-lupert/us-special-dividend-recurring-drift-xs/blob/main/paper/SD-01-SSRN-preprint-draft.md)): "It survives realistic bid-ask and price-impact costs outside microcaps."
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: PARTIAL (STATED). Form 8-K has no dedicated mandatory item code for cash dividends; however, many issuers furnish special-dividend press releases under Item 7.01 (Regulation FD) or Item 8.01 (Other Events) with an exact EDGAR acceptance timestamp, as noted in the [SEC Investor Bulletin on Form 8-K](https://www.investor.gov/sites/default/files/readan8k.pdf): "Actual examples of 8-Ks filed under this item address a wide range of topics, such as announcements of dividends, quarterly sales figures, or other business developments." Recent SEC filings confirm this exact pattern, such as [Flowco Holdings Form 8-K (August 10, 2026)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2035149/000119312526342622/floc-20260810.htm): "On August 10, 2026, Flowco Holdings Inc. (the "Company") issued a press release stating the Company has declared a one-time special cash dividend of $0.14 per share of Class A common stock, payable on August 31, 2026, to holders of record as of the close of business on August 21, 2026 (the "Special Dividend")." A point-in-time list can be built free from EDGAR EFTS full-text search on Form 8-K (`"special cash dividend"` or `"special dividend"`), though issuers that announce only via press release without an 8-K would be missed (coverage percentage is UNKNOWN until counted).
- Expected events per year: MEASURED: 5,246 US declarations over 22 years (2003 to 2024) equals roughly 238 events per year across all US equities; filtering out microcaps and recurring payers leaves an estimated 80 to 120 candidate events per year (STATED).
- Cost against effect: +1.20% overall (and roughly +2.0% to +2.4% on first-time special dividends) over trading days +2 to +20 versus 0.15% to 0.50% small/mid-cap round-trip spread leaves a clear net margin on $500 whole shares, provided microcaps and stocks priced above $250 are excluded.
- Verdict: WORTH A TEST. First step (no returns): run a free EDGAR EFTS count of Form 8-K special-dividend declarations from 2016 to 2026 to measure annual event counts, share-price eligibility under $500, and non-microcap liquidity.

## 2. Form NT 10-K / NT 10-Q (Late Filings) and Form 8-K Item 4.02 (Restatements) as Avoid

- Mechanism and who trades: Filing Form 12b-25 (`NT 10-K` or `NT 10-Q`) notifies the SEC that a company cannot file its financial statements on time, while Form 8-K Item 4.02 discloses that previously issued financial statements can no longer be relied upon (STATED). Institutional investors, auditors, and credit analysts gradually re-price accounting risk, covenant stress, and deteriorating operations over subsequent weeks and months as the full scope of errors emerges (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence:
- MEASURED (NT 10-K / NT 10-Q): Eli Bartov and Yaniv Konchitchki ([Accounting Horizons, 2017](http://aaahq.org/portals/0/newsroom/sec%20filings%20article%20-%20ah%20dec%202017.pdf) and [AAII summary](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/delaying-sec-filings-leads-to-negative-returns)) study 2,100 Form NT filings (2000 to 2008) and report: "We find that, on average, abnormal returns for both NT 10-Q and NT 10-K filers continue to drift downward during the post-filing months." Their table reports post-NT abnormal drift of -4.93% overall (`p < 0.0001`), -4.89% for NT 10-K (`p = 0.0025`), and -4.96% for NT 10-Q (`p = 0.0001`), and the AAII summary notes: "The additional drop is 13%, on average, over the following 180 trading days."
- MEASURED (NT 10-K / NT 10-Q independent confirmation): S&P Capital IQ Quantamental Research ([Late to File study](https://www.spglobal.com/content/dam/spglobal/mi/en/documents/general/sp-capital-iq-quantamental-late-to-file-the-costs-of-delayed-10-q-and-10-k-company-filings.pdf)) confirms: "Late filers continue to underperform after NT filing. Abnormal returns over the next one week, one month, three months and one year are -0.62%, -1.76%, -4.44% and -7.90% respectively (all returns significant at the 1% level)."
- MEASURED (Form 8-K Item 4.02): J. Schroeder and Peter N. Posch ([SSRN 5118253, 2025/2026](https://algoseek.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/ssrn-5118253.pdf) and [sec-api.io summary](https://sec-api.io/resources/stock-price-reactions-to-item-4-02-disclosures-in-sec-form-8-k-filings)) analyze 8,006 Item 4.02 filings from 2004 to 2023: "On day +1, the mean CAR is -0.87% for the full sample and -1.10% when excluding 2021, indicating an immediate adverse investor response. This effect deepens over time, reaching -1.54% and -2.00%, respectively, by day +20." The summary adds: "Median CAR values register at -0.5% one day post-disclosure and -2.6% at 20 days, with all results statistically significant at the 0.1% level." Earlier work by Alina Lerman and Joshua Livnat ([The New Form 8-K Disclosures, NYU Stern](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jlivnat/f8k%20current.pdf)) similarly reports Item 4.02 post-filing drifts of -1.04% (30d), -1.61% (60d), and -2.34% (90d), all significant at 1%.
- MEASURED (Post-resolution check): Audit Analytics ([Long-Term Capital Market Consequences of Financial Restatements](https://blog.auditanalytics.com/long-term-capital-market-consequences-of-financial-restatements/), 966 resolved Item 4.02 restatements, 2005 to 2016) finds no bounce in the first 30 days after restated financials are finally filed and only +1.07% in days 31 to 60: "no statistically significant abnormal returns were identified in the first 30-days window following the date of the restated financials. However, significant positive abnormal returns of 1.07% were identified during the second 30-day window (days 31 to 60)." That is too small and slow to trade long.
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: YES (100% free and exact). `NT 10-K` and `NT 10-Q` are explicit form types in EDGAR `master.idx` and `submissions` JSON, and Item `4.02` is an explicit structured item field on Form 8-K filings with exact SEC acceptance timestamps (STATED).
- Expected events per year: Roughly 400 Item 4.02 filings per year (8,006 over 20 years) plus several hundred `NT 10-K` / `NT 10-Q` filings per year (MEASURED/STATED).
- Cost against effect: Because this is a long-only account, we cannot short these names; however, avoiding entry and exiting any held position immediately on an `NT 10-K`, `NT 10-Q`, or `8-K Item 4.02` filing avoids a documented -1.7% to -4.9% multi-week downward drift for the cost of one normal exit spread (0.15% to 0.50%).
- Verdict: DESK RULE CANDIDATE (AVOID / EXIT). Record as a standing equity desk rule ("do not buy, and exit any held stock at the next regular session upon an SEC `NT 10-K`, `NT 10-Q`, or `8-K Item 4.02` filing"), analogous to the crypto delisting desk rule in Queue row 72.

## 3. S&P SmallCap 600 Additions

- Mechanism and who trades: When S&P Dow Jones Indices announces that a stock outside the S&P 1500 will be added directly to the S&P SmallCap 600, passive ETFs and index mutual funds tracking the SmallCap 600 must buy shares by the effective date (typically 3 to 5 trading days later), creating concentrated demand in smaller, less liquid stocks (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence: MEASURED: While Robin Greenwood and Marco Sammon ([The Disappearing Index Effect, Journal of Finance, 2025](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=65745) and [HBS working paper](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/23-025_563e45c6-df92-4d9c-ae05-608d4d0acab1.pdf)) show that the S&P 500 addition effect collapsed from 7.4% in the 1990s to 0.3% in the 2010s, they explicitly find that S&P SmallCap 600 direct additions did NOT vanish: "The effect of being added to the SmallCap hasn't changed much over the past 30 years, consistently hovering around 6%, but the effect of being deleted from the SmallCap increased and then shrunk, from -6.7% in the 1990s to -24.6% in the 2000s and finally -12.2% in the 2010s." MEASURED: For the post-announcement-to-effective window specifically (`AD+1` through `CD=0`), earlier work by Charles Cuny ([WUSTL study](http://apps.olin.wustl.edu/faculty/cuny/cjcuny%20400-600%20changes.pdf)) reports: "the corresponding number for the 105 stocks promoted to the S&P SmallCap 600 index with more than one day's notice is +2.4%." A recent Erasmus University thesis ([The Influence of Firm Size and ETFs on the Index Inclusion Effect](https://thesis.eur.nl/pub/66997/Master-Thesis-Final-Version.pdf), N = 326) reports +4.05% at `AD+1` and +3.73% over `(+1, +6)`, though `AD+1` includes the overnight announcement gap.
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: NO (STATED). S&P SmallCap 600 additions are announced via S&P Dow Jones Indices press releases (typically at 5:15 PM ET on PRNewswire), not via a mandatory SEC EDGAR filing. Historical S&P DJI press releases can be collected from public news archives, but not from EDGAR index files.
- Expected events per year: Roughly 40 to 60 direct additions per year from outside the S&P 1500 (STATED).
- Cost against effect: SmallCap 600 additions are liquid exchange-listed small caps (0.15% to 0.40% spread). The key unknown is how much of the 6% move happens overnight between the 5:15 PM ET press release and the next morning's regular-session open (`AD+1` open) versus between `AD+1` open and effective-date close (`ED` close).
- Verdict: WATCH. Strong persistence relative to the S&P 500, but it lacks an EDGAR timestamp and requires a free S&P press-release timestamp check plus an `AD+1 open` to `ED close` measurability check before considering a test.

## 4. Spin-Off Completions (Form 10 and When-Issued Dates)

- Prior coverage: Already tracked in Queue row 58 (`docs/research/76_round_11_larger_moves.md`), which noted that classic spin-off outperformance occurs over 12 to 24 months rather than days to weeks.
- Mechanism and who trades: When a large-cap parent distributes shares of a smaller subsidiary to existing shareholders, index funds and mandate-constrained institutions are forced to sell the unwanted small-cap spin-off shares during the first few weeks of regular-way trading, temporarily depressing the price below fundamental value (STATED).
- New published evidence (2024 to 2025): MEASURED: A Copenhagen Business School thesis ([Spin-off Performance: An Unrelenting Anomaly](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/spin-off-performance-an-unrelenting-anomaly/), 2000 to 2022, North America and Western Europe) quantifies the exact short-window forced-selling dip: "This paper finds evidence that spin-offs initially exhibit negative abnormal returns following temporary supply and demand pressures resulting in a negative cumulated average abnormal return of 1.7% after 14 trading days, significant at the 1% level. However, we find that spinoffs yield positive cumulative average abnormal returns of 12.9% after 12 months, 19.7% after 24 months, and 28.5% after 36 months of trading, with all estimates being statistically significant at the 1% level." MEASURED: A 2025 thesis by Albert Thorsbrink and Malcolm Tornqvist ([Spin-Offs: Beyond the Breakup and Across Borders](https://research.cbs.dk/en/studentProjects/spin-offs-beyond-the-breakup-and-across-borders/), 558 spin-offs, 1998 to 2022) similarly finds negative short-term abnormal returns and long-term subsidiary outperformance.
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: PARTIAL (STATED). Spin-off registration statements (`Form 10-12B` / `10-12G`) are free in EDGAR `master.idx`, but exact distribution and when-issued dates must be parsed from subsequent amendments and parent Form 8-K Item 8.01 notices.
- Expected events per year: Roughly 15 to 25 completed US corporate spin-offs per year (STATED).
- Cost against effect: Entering after the 14-day forced-selling dip avoids the -1.7% selling pressure, but published positive abnormal returns accrue over 12 to 36 months rather than days to weeks, and 15 to 25 events per year is thin for fast-turnover testing.
- Verdict: KEEP IN QUEUE ROW 58 (WATCH). Note the new 14-day forced-selling dip evidence in row 58, but do not promote to a fast-money test without independent evidence of a >1% rebound over a short post-dip window (such as days +15 to +45).

## 5. Form 144 Filings and IPO Lock-Up Expirations as Avoid

- Mechanism and who trades: IPO lock-up agreements (typically 180 days after the IPO) bar insiders and venture-capital investors from selling; expiration releases a sudden supply of shares (STATED). Form 144 notifies the SEC of an affiliate's intent to sell restricted or control securities under Rule 144 (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence:
- MEASURED (IPO lock-ups): Laura Casares Field and Gordon Hanka ([The Expiration of IPO Share Lockups, Journal of Finance, 2001](https://pubsonline.informs.org/servlet/linkout?suffix=B26&dbid=16&doi=10.1287/mnsc.2024.06121&key=10.1111/0022-1082.00334), 1,948 US IPOs, 1988 to 1997) report: "When lockups expire, we find a permanent 40 percent increase in average trading volume, and a statistically prominent three-day abnormal return of -1.5 percent." A later study on short selling around lock-ups ([Journal of Banking & Finance / CSULB](https://web.csulb.edu/colleges/cob/intranet/vita/pdfsubmissions/3062e-1-s2.0-s0378426617302339-main.pdf)) clarifies that the negative return is concentrated in VC-backed IPOs: "In contrast to the negative abnormal return on the lockup expiration date for VC-backed IPOs, the abnormal return on the lockup expiration date is not negative for either PE-backed or other IPO firms." An MIT Sloan paper ([Short-sales Constraints and Aftermarket IPO Pricing](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Short-sales%20Constraints%20and%20Aftermarket%20IPO%20Pricing.pdf)) reports -10.1% for small, constrained IPOs versus roughly zero for large IPOs.
- MEASURED (Form 144): Electronic filing of Form 144 on EDGAR became mandatory only on April 13, 2023 ([SEC Form 144 Electronic Filing](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/filer-information/form-144-resources-filing-electronically)): "Compliance with the requirements took effect on April 13, 2023." A 2026 empirical paper on Form 144 and Form 4 reporting inversions ([arXiv:2602.17890](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17890)) finds only a 14.49 bps abnormal return (`p = 0.021`) in large caps and statistically insignificant drift (`t = 1.03`) for aborted filings.
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: YES for IPO lock-ups via Form `424B4` full-text search (as demonstrated in [Equibles, 2026](https://equibles.com/research/who-is-actually-locked-up-after-an-ipo), 824 filings from 2024 to 2026); YES for Form 144 from April 13, 2023 onward only (too short for a 10-year historical test).
- Expected events per year: Roughly 100 to 150 US IPO lock-up expirations per year (fewer VC-backed); thousands of Form 144 filings per year since April 2023 (STATED).
- Cost against effect: As an avoid filter, skipping purchases of VC-backed IPOs during days -5 to +5 around the 180-day lock-up date has near-zero cost, while Form 144 alone has too small an effect (<0.2%) and too short an EDGAR history.
- Verdict: WATCH (LOW-PRIORITY AVOID FILTER). Keep IPO lock-up expiry as a lightweight avoid rule for VC-backed IPOs only; drop standalone Form 144.

## 6. FDA Decision Dates (PDUFA) Run-Up

- Mechanism and who trades: Small biotechnology firms disclose target FDA Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA) decision dates months in advance; retail and momentum traders often buy ahead of the binary approval decision and sell before the announcement ("buy the rumor, sell the news") (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence: MEASURED: Large-sample tracker evidence shows that the unconditional pre-PDUFA run-up over the final 30 days is very small and fades into decision day. Across 1,661 PDUFA events (2020 to 2026), [pdufa.bio](https://www.pdufa.bio/research/readout-reaction) reports a median D-30 to D-1 run-up of only +0.43% (`FDA decisions (PDUFA) | 1,661 | +0.43%`). Across 1,134 decisions from 2020 to 2026 ([pdufa.bio](https://www.pdufa.bio/)), the site reports: "The median stock rose 17.3% at its best point in the 120 trading days before the decision, but was only 4.4% higher on the day before it. In other words the typical run-up was largely given back before the answer arrived." BioRadar ([102 PDUFA decisions, May 2025 to November 2026](https://bioradar.io/how-biotech-stocks-move-around-fda-decisions/)) reports: "On average, biotech stocks in this dataset climbed +1.9% in the 30 days before their PDUFA date (56% rose during the run-up window), then gave back ground: -1.8% on the decision day and -3.6% five days later." Smaller academic-style studies that report larger run-ups (such as +7.6% in `[-20, -2]` in [lukahere007, 234 approvals, 2015 to 2024](https://github.com/lukahere007/biotech-fda-event-study)) condition on ex-post approved drugs, introducing look-ahead bias (STATED).
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: NO (STATED). As [pdufa.bio](https://www.pdufa.bio/) notes: "a date marked announced has no public FDA record of its own." Companies mention PDUFA dates in unstructured 8-K, 10-Q, or press-release text, and dates frequently shift due to 3-month extensions, Advisory Committee meetings, or early Complete Response Letters, making a clean free point-in-time EDGAR calendar error-prone.
- Expected events per year: Roughly 150 to 190 PDUFA decisions per year across US-listed biopharma (MEASURED: 111 to 188 per year in 2020 to 2026 on [pdufa.bio](https://www.pdufa.bio/runup-by-year)).
- Cost against effect: An unconditional 30-day median run-up of +0.43% does not clear small-cap biotech bid-ask spreads (0.30% to 1.00% round trip) and exposes a $500 account to sudden 30% to 80% overnight gaps from surprise interim safety halts, early CRLs, or dilutive offerings.
- Verdict: DROP. Unconditional 30-day run-up is below 1%, point-in-time EDGAR extraction is noisy, and binary gap risk is severe on $500.

## 7. Schedule 13D Activist Filings (Post-Filing Drift)

- Mechanism and who trades: An investor acquiring more than 5% of a public company with activist intent must file Schedule 13D on EDGAR (STATED). The activist's own accumulation and market leakage drive a large price jump before and on the filing date; the question here is whether a retail trader buying AFTER the public filing timestamp earns a positive drift over days to weeks.
- Published evidence and persistence: MEASURED: Classic academic studies show the gain happens before and on the filing date, with flat returns afterward. Alon Brav, Wei Jiang, Frank Partnoy, and Randall Thomas ([Hedge Fund Activism, Duke, 2013](https://people.duke.edu/~brav/HFactivism_SEPTEMBER_2013.pdf)) describe the `(-20, +20)` abnormal return of ~5.0% as occurring into the "filing date, and roughly flat afterward." Pierre Collin-Dufresne and Vyacheslav Fos ([Netspar working paper](https://www.netspar.nl/assets/uploads/037_Collin.pdf)) confirm: "Specifically, there is no trend in the abnormal return after the filing date." Robin Greenwood and Michael Schor ([HBS Working Paper 08-004](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/08-004.pdf)) show that longer-term post-13D gains come from ex-post takeovers: "Abnormal returns surrounding investor activism are high for the subset of targets that are acquired ex-post, but not detectably different from zero for targets that remain independent a year after the initial activist request." A recent EDGAR Schedule 13D event-study replication ([fortifiedai/edgar-13d-event-study](https://github.com/fortifiedai/edgar-13d-event-study/blob/main/README.md)) measures Pre (-10d) at +3.22% (`t = 5.88`), Reaction (0, +1) at +1.34% (`t = 4.86`), and Post (+20d) at -0.60% mean (-1.29% median, `t = -1.17`): "Short answer from this sample: the abnormal return is concentrated before and at the filing. There is no significant post-filing drift to trade."
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: YES (`SC 13D` is an explicit EDGAR form type with exact acceptance timestamps) (STATED).
- Expected events per year: Roughly 200 to 350 initial Schedule 13D filings per year (STATED).
- Cost against effect: Zero to negative post-filing 20-day drift (-0.60% mean, -1.29% median) fails before costs.
- Verdict: DROP. The edge is entirely pre-filing accumulation and the filing-day jump; post-filing drift over days to weeks does not exist.

## 8. Share Buyback and Accelerated Share Repurchase (ASR) Announcements

- Mechanism and who trades: Companies announce open-market share repurchase (OMR) authorizations or accelerated share repurchase (ASR) contracts with investment banks, signaling undervaluation or returning excess cash (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence:
- MEASURED (Open-market buybacks): While 1990s studies reported multi-year drift, Leonce Bargeron, Alice Bonaime, and Shawn Thomas ([Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis, 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/timing-and-source-of-longrun-returns-following-repurchases/3A0AA39EF30BB41483794352CDB40BAC), 3,089 US programs) show there is no gradual post-announcement drift: "Returns between program authorization and completion announcements are indistinguishable from 0. Abnormal returns occur only after completion announcements. Long-run returns are largely attributable to announcement returns at subsequent authorizations and takeover attempts; that is, anomalous post-authorization returns are not persistent drifts but rather step functions." Two Sigma ([Share Buybacks: A Brief Investigation](https://www.twosigma.com/wp-content/uploads/SV-06_19_v8.pdf), Russell 3000, 1998 to 2019) finds only 0.5% to 0.75% in the announcement month and less than 0.5% cumulative abnormal return from pre-selloff to six months post-announcement: "the total CAAR from the beginning of the sell-off to six months after the announcement is less than 0.5%". Inmoo Lee, Yuen Jung Park, and Neil Pearson ([KAIST, 2019](https://www.business.kaist.ac.kr/faculty/inmoo/Articles/RepurchasesHaveChanged_June2019_final.pdf), US OMRs 1994 to 2014) confirm: "Using recent U.S. data, we find that the long-horizon abnormal returns following repurchase announcements made after 2001 are much smaller than those following earlier announcements."
- MEASURED (ASRs): Allen Michel, Jacob Oded, and Israel Shaked ([The Case of Accelerated Stock Repurchases](https://www.tau.ac.il/sites/coller-english.tau.ac.il/files/RP_169_Oded.pdf)) and Teague ([Charlotte, 2017](https://belkcollegeofbusiness.charlotte.edu/ichiang1/wp-content/uploads/sites/863/2014/09/2017S_Teague.pdf), 716 ASRs, 2004 to 2015) report a positive 3-to-5-day announcement reaction (+1.3% to +1.95%) followed by negative post-announcement drift: "They also report a negative post-announcement drift of 8.5% in the nine months following an ASR announcement, contrary to the positive long-run abnormal returns found in the OMR literature."
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: PARTIAL (STATED). Buyback authorizations and ASRs are often furnished on Form 8-K Item 7.01 or 8.01 (and ASR contracts sometimes under Item 1.01), requiring full-text EFTS filtering.
- Expected events per year: Roughly 400 to 600 US buyback and ASR announcements per year (STATED).
- Cost against effect: Post-announcement drift over days to weeks is indistinguishable from zero for US OMRs and negative for ASRs.
- Verdict: DROP. No reliable post-filing drift above 1% in recent US data.

## 9. Form 8-K Item 1.01 and Item 2.01 Deals

- Mechanism and who trades: Form 8-K Item 1.01 reports entry into a material definitive agreement outside the ordinary course of business (within 4 business days), while Item 2.01 reports the subsequent closing of a significant acquisition or disposition (STATED; [SEC Form 8-K Final Rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/33-8400.pdf): "Typically, a company will report its entry into a material definitive agreement to acquire or dispose of assets under Item 1.01, and then later disclose the closing of the acquisition or disposition transaction under Item 2.01.").
- Published evidence and persistence: MEASURED: Alina Lerman and Joshua Livnat ([The New Form 8-K Disclosures, Review of Accounting Studies / NYU Stern](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jlivnat/f8k%20current.pdf), 176,693 Form 8-K filings from 2004 to 2007) show that Item 1.01 is the second most common item (34,997 filings, 27.9% of all 8-Ks), mixing credit-line amendments, leases, debt agreements, and M&A, and produces a post-filing drift of only +0.52% (30 days), +0.41% (60 days), and +0.74% (90 days). Item 2.01 (2,508 filings, 2.0%) is a lagging confirmation of deals already priced at signing under Item 1.01 (STATED). (Large customer or government contract awards are already isolated in Queue row 13.)
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: YES (exact 8-K item tags in EDGAR submissions and EFTS) (STATED).
- Expected events per year: Over 10,000 Item 1.01 filings and roughly 700 Item 2.01 filings per year (STATED).
- Cost against effect: Unconditional Item 1.01 post-filing drift (+0.52% over 30 days) is below the 1% threshold and does not justify small-cap spreads without heavy NLP filtering.
- Verdict: DROP. Too heterogeneous and below 1% unconditional post-filing drift.

## 10. Merger Arbitrage on Small Cash Deals After Definitive Agreement

- Mechanism and who trades: After a definitive cash acquisition agreement is signed, target shares trade at a discount ("merger spread") to the cash offer price until closing, compensating arbitrageurs for deal-break risk, regulatory delay, and time value of money (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence: MEASURED: Federal Reserve Bank of New York Staff Report 761 ([Merger Options and Risk Arbitrage](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr761.pdf), 2,856 cash deals) reports: "In cash deals, the probability of success is 78%, the average deal duration is 3.7 months, the average offer size is $743 million, and the average offer premium is 40% relative to the target stock price one week before the announcement" and "statistically significant monthly alphas of 40 to 50 basis points are similar across the various merger arbitrage return series." MEASURED: Malcolm Baker and Serkan Savasoglu ([Limited Arbitrage in Mergers and Acquisitions, HBS](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/arbitrage_af05900b-acd4-44db-8210-70a9fbc3cf6c.pdf), 1,901 deals, 1981 to 1996) report 0.6% to 0.9% per month, while later US cash-deal studies show compression (Glans and Vo, 2013, 2000 to 2012 US cash deals, alpha -0.1% cited in [CBS thesis](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/60730644/106248_Thesis.pdf)). Recent US public M&A data from 2016 to 2024 ([Xiya Wang, NYU Shanghai, 2026](https://cdn.shanghai.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/xiya_wang_thesis_nyush_honors_2026.pdf)) confirms: "realized merger arbitrage performance is shaped by both time to completion and downside deal risk, but that failure risk is the more powerful and consistent determinant."
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: PARTIAL (STATED). Definitive agreements appear in Form 8-K Item 1.01, `DEFM14A`, and `SC TO-T`, but accurately extracting cash-only terms, per-share offer price, contingencies, and deal-termination amendments from raw EDGAR HTML requires fragile text parsing.
- Expected events per year: Roughly 100 to 150 US public cash M&A deals per year (STATED).
- Cost against effect: Even at 40 to 50 bps/month (~1.5% to 1.8% over an average 3.7-month tie-up), a single failed cash deal drops 20% to 40% overnight. On a $500 whole-share account, holding 1 to 4 deals at a time cannot diversify that left tail. (Going-private `SC 13E-3` filings are already covered separately in Plan 38 / Queue row 62.)
- Verdict: DROP. Unacceptable left-tail concentration risk on $500, compressed post-2000 cash-deal alphas, and multi-month capital lock-up.

## 11. Bankruptcy Emergence (Post-Chapter 11 Relisted Equities)

- Mechanism and who trades: When a firm emerges from Chapter 11 reorganization, former bondholders receive new equity; institutions restricted from holding equities historically sold shares into an illiquid market, creating initial underpricing (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence: MEASURED: Early work by Allan Eberhart, Edward Altman, and Reena Aggarwal ([Journal of Finance, 1999](https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/finance/docs/WP/1996/pdf/wpa96002.pdf), 131 firms, 1980 to 1993) found +22.8% to +29.6% over 200 trading days. However, modern evidence shows the effect has vanished. Yan Yang ([The Disappeared Outperformance of Post-reorg Equity, SSRN 3906039](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3906039), 1992 to 2019) and Wei Jiang, Wei Wang, and Yan Yang ([Duke FinReg Blog, 2021](https://sites.duke.edu/thefinregblog/2021/09/17/has-post-reorg-equity-outperformed-in-the-last-three-decades/) and [CFA Institute, 2026](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/blogs/enterprising-investor/2026/cleaner-balance-sheet-does-not-guarantee-returns)) report: "the median post-reorganization stock earned roughly zero abnormal return and that the outperformance disappeared in the 2010s." Yang (SSRN 3906039) explains: "The positive post-emergence earnings announcement returns disappear in the most recent decade, and stocks of firms have better initial valuation after emergence."
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: PARTIAL (STATED). Emergence is disclosed across Form 8-K Item 1.03 / 8.01 and Form 10, but new-equity relisting dates and ticker changes are notoriously messy in free feeds.
- Expected events per year: Roughly 5 to 15 relisted US Chapter 11 emergences per year (MEASURED: 93 firms over 12 years from 2008 to 2019 in [Verdad](https://verdadcap.com/archive/ymo55pidxxqf34wns523ph1vkvp0e8)).
- Cost against effect: Median abnormal return is zero, the historical anomaly disappeared in the 2010s, the horizon was 200 days (not days to weeks), and newly relisted shares carry wide spreads.
- Verdict: DROP. Anomaly disappeared in the 2010s, median is zero, and annual sample count is tiny.

## 12. Uplistings from OTC to Nasdaq or NYSE

- Mechanism and who trades: Companies migrating from OTC markets to Nasdaq or NYSE gain institutional eligibility and lower trading costs, attracting pre-listing anticipation buying followed by post-listing profit-taking and equity-offering dilution (STATED).
- Published evidence and persistence: MEASURED: The long-standing "post-listing puzzle" shows positive returns before listing and NEGATIVE returns after listing. John McConnell and Gary Sanger ([Purdue / Journal of Finance](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/stock-exchange-listings-firm-value.pdf) and [The Puzzle in Post-Listing Common Stock Returns](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/the-puzzle-in-postlisting-common-stock-returns.pdf)) report: "Newly listed firms lose, on average, approximately 3 percent of their value, relative to the market model benchmark, over the three-week period following listing" and "On average, stocks that became listed on a major exchange have experienced negative returns over the four- to six-week period following listing". MEASURED: An SEC Division of Economic and Risk Analysis white paper ([Outcomes of Investing in OTC Stocks](https://www.sec.gov/files/White_OutcomesOTCinvesting.pdf), citing Bruggemann et al., 2016) confirms: "even when OTC stocks transition to a national securities exchange, they subsequently average a 4% annualized return, which lags behind the returns of other securities listed on a national exchange." Ryan Davis, Todd Griffith, Brian Roseman, and Serhat Yildiz ([The Financial Review, 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/fire.12266), 190 OTC uplistings, 2011 to 2016) document dramatic liquidity gains (spreads fall 143 bps and dollar volume rises 80.21%), not durable post-listing outperformance.
- Free EDGAR point-in-time feasibility: PARTIAL (STATED). Form `8-A12B` registers shares on a national exchange right before trading begins, and many uplistings pair with an `S-1` equity offering; however, the pre-listing run-up occurs while the stock still trades OTC (outside standard SIP exchange feeds and with wide OTC spreads).
- Expected events per year: Roughly 30 to 60 OTC-to-exchange uplistings per year (MEASURED: 190 over 5 years in Davis et al., 2021).
- Cost against effect: Buying after exchange listing earns negative average abnormal returns (-3% over 3 weeks); buying pre-listing requires OTC execution with 2% to 5% spreads and failed-uplist risk.
- Verdict: DROP. Post-listing returns are negative and pre-listing OTC shares do not fit an exchange-listed $500 account.
