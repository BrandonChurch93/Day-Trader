# Idea Round 47: Can a $500 US Account Earn the Spread with Small Resting Limit Orders?

Date: 2026-11-26 16:21 UTC
Status: COMPLETE (price-blind, sources only, no data pulled by us; `docs/QUEUE.md` untouched per lead instruction).

## Verdict

None of the five candidate families stands on published evidence as a verified positive-realized-spread strategy for a $500 US retail account. Wherever US lit-exchange or live crypto order-book realized spreads have been measured after adverse selection, non-collocated resting limit orders lose money or fail to cover inventory and maker-fee costs: US stock exchanges average -0.34 bp 5-minute realized spread across all stocks (MEASURED), least-liquid stocks show -20.37 bp lower realized spread than S&P 500 stocks after inventory-cost controls (MEASURED), small-cap realized spreads decay 34.6% to 44.4% within one second (MEASURED), and live crypto maker orders suffer negative 1-second markouts across every queue state before paying 30 to 100 bp round-trip US retail maker fees (MEASURED / STATED). For closed-end funds, preferred stocks, baby bonds, and thin ETFs, published US realized-spread studies for slow retail limit orders do not exist (UNKNOWN), while wide quoted spreads alone are not evidence of profit and a $500 equity order is an unprotected odd lot under Regulation NMS.

## 1. Evidence For and Against Small Resting Limit Orders After Adverse Selection

### Retail Limit-Order Studies (Linnainmaa 2010 and Later)

- [Linnainmaa (2005 / 2010 JF working paper, UCLA)](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/LimitOrder.pdf), MEASURED on the 30 most actively traded stocks on the Helsinki Exchanges from September 18, 1998 to October 23, 2001: household limit orders lose money on average because informed counterparties pick them off. Inside-the-spread household limit orders earned -0.04% same-day and -0.07% one-week total (splitting into -0.26% same-day when triggered by an institution versus +0.25% when triggered by another household); outside-the-spread household limit orders lost -0.26% same-day and -0.63% one-week. Around 586 pre-scheduled earnings announcements, stale household limit orders executed in the first five minutes lost -1.58% same-day, -2.92% one-week, and -4.06% two-week, and those filled in the first two minutes lost -2.5% (t = -2.5). Verbatim quotes: "the cross-sectional average same-day return on orders executed during the [0, 120s) interval is -2.5% (with a t-value of -2.5)." "Investors (on average) gain less than the half-spread from using limit orders. This is the risk of adverse selection." "In our risk-neutral model, these losses offset all the gains (because Eq. A3 holds with equality)."
- Barrot, Kaniel, and Sraer (2016, Journal of Financial Economics) and Kelley and Tetlock (2013, Journal of Finance), STATED via published abstracts: while aggregate retail limit-order imbalances are contrarian and predict short-term liquidity-provision rebounds, individual retail investors fail to capture those gains because they suffer negative returns on the day of execution (picking-off adverse selection) and hold too long after liquidity-provision returns dissipate.

### US Lit-Exchange vs Wholesaler Realized Spreads by Liquidity Tier

- [The Retail Execution Quality Landscape (AFA working paper)](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=13580), MEASURED from January 2019 through March 2022 across SEC Rule 605 reports for 14 US stock exchanges and 8 largest wholesalers (nearly 2.9 trillion traded shares, non-ETF stocks, round lots >= 100 shares, 5-minute realized-spread horizon):
- Verbatim quotes: "In a comprehensive sample of all U.S. equities traded from January 2019 through March 2022, we carefully compare the benefits of wholesaler and exchange executions..." "Rule 605 requires that price impacts and realized spreads are reported at the 5-minute horizon." "We calculate the price impact from the 605 reports as the effective spread minus the realized spread."
- Across all stocks on US exchanges (where a resting retail limit order actually sits), quoted spread is 48.67 bp, effective spread is 46.98 bp, 5-minute price impact is 47.32 bp, and 5-minute realized spread is -0.34 bp (MEASURED). By contrast, wholesalers earn +16.53 bp realized spread (quoted 64.92 bp, effective 49.06 bp, price impact 32.53 bp) ONLY because brokers route segmented retail market orders off-exchange rather than letting them hit public exchange books.
- By liquidity tier (Table 5, MEASURED): on exchanges, S&P 500 5-minute realized spread is -1.23 bp and Tercile 3 (least liquid stocks) is +4.65 bp (wholesalers: +1.21 bp S&P 500, +33.09 bp Tercile 3). After controlling for volume as an inventory-cost proxy (Table 10, MEASURED), Tercile 3 realized spreads flip sharply negative relative to S&P 500 (-20.374 bp). Verbatim quote: "What at face value appeared to be excessively large realized spreads of 39.17 bps for Tercile 3 stocks relatively to S&P 500 stocks in column [1] are insufficient to compensate for incremental inventory costs based on columns [3] which shows that realized spreads are 20.37 bps lower."

### Small-Cap Realized Spread Decay at Sub-Second Horizons

- [Tick Size Pilot small-cap liquidity study (Stanford Law / SSRN 3076257)](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/SSRN-id3076257.pdf), MEASURED on 1,087 binding small-cap stocks from November 7, 2016 to June 30, 2017: even when the SEC artificially quintupled tick sizes to $0.05 (nearly doubling quoted spreads from 30 bp to 67 to 71 bp and effective spreads from 23 bp to 51 to 55 bp), realized spreads collapsed within milliseconds to minutes.
- Measured realized spreads in control stocks decayed from 20.2 bp at 1 microsecond to 12.2 bp at 1 second and 5.1 bp at 5 minutes; in Test Group 1 (nickel tick), realized spread fell from 50.5 bp at 1 microsecond to 33.0 bp at 1 second (a 34.6% drop, with 60% of 1-second price impact occurring in the first millisecond) and 20.0 bp at 5 minutes (another 39.6% drop).
- Verbatim quotes: "We demonstrate that realized spreads decay quickly within the initial microseconds of a trade. The effect reduces the subsidy offered by wider tick sizes, particularly for non-HFT market makers." "To capture the wider spread, liquidity providers need to be faster than human market makers are capable of being and indeed probably even collocated, highlighting that any movement to a nickel quoting environment is likely to favor HFT market making."

### Overnight US Equities (Extended Hours)

- [Lim (April 2026, SSRN 6610883)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=6610883), MEASURED on 30 actively traded symbols across 24 matched sessions from September 2025 through March 2026 comparing Blue Ocean ATS (8:00 p.m. to 4:00 a.m. ET) to NASDAQ regular hours: "Effective spreads overnight average 7 cents per share higher, and the 10-second price impact component averages 2.4 cents per share higher" and "Only about one-third of the overnight spread premium reflects adverse selection; the balance reflects wider quoted spreads in a single-venue continuous market operating without the competitive liquidity provision of regular hours." For ETFs specifically: "exchange-traded funds exhibit larger price-impact premia consistent with the loss of underlying-basket arbitrage when the underlying market is closed." Realized spread is not printed directly (UNKNOWN).

### Closed-End Funds, Preferreds, Baby Bonds, and Thin ETFs

- [SEC Staff Background Paper on Thinly Traded Securities (2019)](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/policy/2019/thinly-traded-securities-tm-background-paper.pdf) reports quoted/bid-ask spreads ONLY (MEASURED): corporate stocks with ADV <= 50,000 had "a median bid-ask spread of 234 basis points versus a median of 36 basis points for corporate common stocks overall" and median "~100 trades" per day; thinly traded ETPs (60% of 2,147 US-listed products, ADV <= 50,000) had "an average bid-ask spread of 33 basis points versus 8 basis points for the more actively traded ETPs." The SEC paper prints zero realized-spread figures (UNKNOWN) and does not cover closed-end funds or preferred stocks.
- No directly opened modern US empirical study measures post-adverse-selection realized spreads for resting retail limit orders in US closed-end funds, preferred stocks, $25-par baby bonds, or thin US ETFs (all UNKNOWN). A non-US DMM study ([Santiago Stock Exchange 2011 to 2013](https://microstructure.exchange/papers/Paper_DMM.pdf)) measured 66 bp effective half-spread, 16 bp 5-minute price impact, and 50 bp realized spread on 160 Chilean stocks, which does not transfer to US Regulation NMS markets.

### US-Reachable Crypto Venues at Maker Fees

- [Ait-Sahalia et al. (2025, arXiv:2502.18625)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625), MEASURED in two live trading experiments on Binance USDT-margined Bitcoin perpetuals (February 12 to 19, 2024: 232,897 minimum-sized maker orders submitted, 127,051 filled; August 21 to 31, 2024: 172,800 submitted, 95,998 filled):
- Verbatim quotes: "We document a fundamental trade-off: a negative correlation between maker fill likelihood and post-fill returns." "In summary, maker orders experience negative instantaneous price drift and negative expected price drift over longer time scales, amounting to adverse selection."
- Across all 16 queue-size and queue-position cells, 1-second post-fill markout (before fees) was negative, ranging from -0.058 bp (front of large near-side queue) to -1.157 bp (back of large two-sided queue). Five-second terminal-state expected return was -0.8 bp gross (-0.3 bp net of a 0.5 bp maker rebate).
- An unfiltered naive passive maker strategy lost almost 60% in three days and seven hours (Sharpe -109.0, mean -0.4307 bp per round trip net of a 0.5 bp maker rebate, average hold 22.56 seconds). Their best logistic-regression maker strategy earned +0.71 bp per round trip assuming a +0.5 bp maker rebate (327 round trips/day, p = 0.0160, "likely not scalable"). Against US retail spot maker fees of 30 bp (Alpaca), 80 bp (Kraken Pro), or ~100 bp (Coinbase Advanced) round trip, even that best-case gross edge is deeply negative.
- No directly opened study documents positive net realized spreads for retail-tier maker orders on Coinbase Advanced, Kraken Pro, or Alpaca Crypto (UNKNOWN).

### SEC Reg NMS Minimum Price Increments (Rule 612 and 2025/2026 Delays)

- Current enforceable tick size ([SEC Release No. 34-101070](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-101070.pdf) and [SEC Exemptive Order 34-105656, June 11, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2026/34-105656.pdf), STATED): Regulation NMS Rule 612 sets a $0.01 minimum pricing increment for NMS stock quotes and orders priced at or above $1.00 ($0.0001 below $1.00). On September 18, 2024, the SEC amended Rule 612 to add a $0.005 half-penny tick size for stocks with a Time-Weighted Average Quoted Spread of $0.015 or less, originally set for November 2025. That compliance date was extended first on October 31, 2025 to November 2026, and then on June 11, 2026 to November 2027.
- Verbatim quote ([SEC Release No. 34-105656](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2026/34-105656.pdf)): "Accordingly, IT IS HEREBY ORDERED, pursuant to section 36(a)(1) of the Exchange Act and Rules 610(f) and 612(d) of Regulation NMS, that the Commission grants exemptive relief, as set forth in this order, from compliance with Rules 600(b)(89)(i)(F), 610(c), and 612, as amended in the Adopting Release until the first business day of November 2027."
- Impact on wide-spread candidates: as of November 26, 2026, $0.01 remains the enforceable tick size for all NMS stocks >= $1.00; even if $0.005 takes effect in November 2027, wide-spread securities (TWAQS > $0.015) remain at $0.01.

### SEC Reg NMS Round-Lot and Odd-Lot Display Rules (Effective November 2025 and May 2026)

- Tiered round-lot definition ([SEC Release No. 34-101070](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-101070.pdf), STATED): "Rule 600(b)(93), as adopted by the MDI Rules, defines a round lot for NMS stocks that have an average closing price on the primary listing exchange during the prior calendar month of: (1) $250.00 or less per share as 100 shares; (2) $250.01 to $1,000.00 per share as 40 shares; (3) $1,000.01 to $10,000.00 per share as 10 shares; and (4) $10,000.01 or more per share as 1 share." "The round lot definition will be implemented on the first business day of November 2025." For a $500 account, every order in any security priced above $5.00 per share is an odd lot (< 100 shares).
- Odd-lot SIP display (BOLO) ([SEC Release No. 34-105259](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/iex/2026/34-105259.pdf), STATED): "Thus, starting on May 1, 2026, the SIPs will begin disseminating the BOLO and the best odd-lot bid and offer priced at or better than the NBBO from each exchange and FINRA." "Best odd-lot order to buy is the highest-priced odd-lot order to buy that is priced higher than the NBB, and Best odd-lot order to sell is the lowest-priced odd-lot order to sell that is priced lower than the NBO." Depth-of-book odd-lot quotes under Rule 600(b)(69)(ii) were deferred until May 2028.
- No NBBO-setting or trade-through protection ([SEC Release No. 34-101070](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-101070.pdf), STATED): "Unlike orders in the round lot sizes adopted pursuant to the MDI Rules, odd-lots are not 'protected quotations.'" "Pursuant to Regulation NMS, bids and offers can only be in round lot sizes, therefore, the NBBO can only be reflected in round lot sizes." "Orders that remain odd-lots under the new definitions are not bids and offers and therefore do not independently contribute to establishing the NBBO." "Rule 611 currently only applies to round lots." An odd-lot bid from a $500 account can now be seen via BOLO if priced better than NBB (since May 1, 2026), but it still does not set the NBBO and other venues may legally trade through it.

## 4. Data Required for a Fair Test and Conservative Fill Rule

What data exist for a fair quote-and-trade test:

- Historical consolidated quotes (NBBO, plus post-May 1, 2026 BOLO odd-lot quotes) and tick-by-tick consolidated trades with nanosecond or millisecond timestamps and trade/quote condition codes (needed to exclude off-exchange TRF sub-penny prints, out-of-sequence reports, and auctions).
- [Alpaca Market Data API](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api) and [Alpaca Market Data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq) (STATED): historical CTA/UTP SIP data "Since 2016" are queryable on the Free Basic plan provided the query end timestamp is at least 15 minutes old ("For historical queries, the `end` parameter must be at least 15 minutes old to query SIP data without a subscription"); real-time SIP requires Algo Trader Plus at $99/month.
- [Databento US Equities](https://databento.com/catalog/us-equities) (STATED): 8+ years of historical L1/L2/L3 US equities data across 45 venues (including `EQUS.SIP`, `TBBO`, and direct exchange feeds such as NYSE Pillar and Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH) on usage-based pricing from $0.40 to $0.50/GB.

Whether a conservative fill rule (fill only when a later trade prints through the limit by at least one tick, and count missed entries as missed winners) can be applied:

- It can be applied mechanically to historical SIP quotes and trades, but three structural flaws limit what it can prove for a $500 odd-lot account:
- First, odd-lot non-protection: because a $500 order is an odd lot with no Rule 611 trade-through protection, a trade printing one tick through the limit on a different exchange or an off-exchange TRF does not guarantee that a resting odd-lot order on one venue would have filled. Filtering strictly to lit-exchange prints on the order's venue is required, and even then queue position is unknown in L1 SIP data.
- Second, pre-May 2026 odd-lot blindness: historical SIP quotes before May 1, 2026 omit all odd-lot quotes, so any backtest prior to May 2026 cannot see whether competing odd-lot orders sat ahead of our price inside the NBBO.
- Third, conservative adverse-selection bias vs missed-winner accounting: requiring a 1-tick trade-through intentionally forces every simulated fill to occur only when price moves against the resting order (which is appropriately conservative for testing survival against adverse selection), while missed entries that never print through produce zero PnL rather than a gain (useful as an opportunity-cost diagnostic against a passive-midpoint benchmark, not a direct PnL loss).

## 5. Most Likely Way Each Candidate Fools Us

- Closed-end funds: wide quoted spreads (50 to 200+ bp) look like easy passive income on daily bars or midpoint quotes, but a $500 resting odd-lot bid gets ignored when benign retail orders route to wholesalers off-exchange and gets filled disproportionately when NAV drops or an informed seller sweeps the lit book (leaving an unhedged inventory loss that exceeds the spread).
- Preferred stocks and $25-par baby bonds: quoting inside a 50 to 150 bp spread looks profitable on paper, but with 1,000 to 5,000 shares/day and frequent zero-trade days, a filled resting bid cannot reliably exit at the ask; forcing an exit crosses the wide spread, while holding exposes a $500 position to rate, credit, or call-announcement gaps.
- Small and thin US ETFs: wide quoted spreads (33 bp average) reflect AP hedging costs and stale-quote risk rather than free rent; because professional APs and HFTs price the ETF continuously off the live underlying basket, any static retail limit order is a free option that gets picked off within milliseconds whenever the underlying basket moves.
- Thin small-cap US stocks: headline wide quoted spreads (234 bp median for ADV <= 50,000) and positive wholesaler realized spreads (+33.09 bp on Tercile 3) fool backtesters into thinking lit-exchange resting orders earn the same spread, when in fact exchanges absorb the toxic residual flow (+4.65 bp before inventory costs, -20.37 bp relative to S&P 500 after inventory costs) and 35% to 44% of realized spread decays within one second.
- US-reachable spot coins at maker fees: zero-fee or rebate-tier academic papers (such as +0.71 bp with a +0.5 bp rebate) and tight 1-tick books hide the fact that naive maker fills suffer negative 1-second markouts across every queue state, while US retail accounts pay 30 to 100 bp round-trip maker fees on Alpaca, Kraken Pro, and Coinbase Advanced.
