# Idea Round 47: Can a $500 US Account Earn the Spread with Small Resting Limit Orders?

Date: 2026-11-26 16:30 UTC (revised from 16:21 UTC after fact-checker review)
Status: COMPLETE (price-blind, sources only, no data pulled by us; `docs/QUEUE.md` untouched per lead instruction).

## Verdict

None of the five candidate families stands on published evidence as a verified positive-realized-spread strategy for a $500 US retail account. Wherever US lit-exchange or live crypto order-book realized spreads have been measured after adverse selection, non-collocated resting limit orders lose money or fail to cover inventory and maker-fee costs: US stock exchanges average -0.34 bp 5-minute full realized spread across non-ETF stocks (MEASURED, round lots >= 100 shares), wholesaler Tercile 3 realized spreads flip to -20.37 bp relative to S&P 500 names after inventory-cost controls in a wholesaler-only regression (MEASURED), small-cap realized spreads decay 34.6% to 44.4% within one second (MEASURED), and live crypto maker orders suffer negative 1-second markouts across every queue state and negative pre-rebate round-trip returns (-0.29 bp even for the best ML selector, -1.43 bp naive) before paying 30 to 80 bp round-trip US retail maker fees on Alpaca Crypto and Kraken Pro (MEASURED / STATED; Coinbase Advanced numeric Intro rate UNKNOWN without sign-in). Published US ETF studies do measure positive aggregate 5-minute realized spreads (+6.11 bp on exchanges across 3,241 ETFs; 3.19 bp mean / 1.23 bp median across 105 equity ETFs; 15.04 bp mean / 7.04 bp median across 113 corporate bond ETFs), but those aggregate figures are dominated by fast professional market makers and round lots, and none isolates a slow $500 odd-lot order. For closed-end funds, preferred stocks, and baby bonds, published US realized-spread studies do not exist (UNKNOWN), while wide quoted spreads alone are not evidence of profit and a $500 equity order is an unprotected odd lot under Regulation NMS. The ranking and verdict remain unchanged after the ETF and Table 10 corrections.

## 1. Evidence For and Against Small Resting Limit Orders After Adverse Selection

### Retail Limit-Order Studies (Linnainmaa 2010 and Later)

- [Linnainmaa (2005 / 2010 JF working paper, UCLA)](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/finance/LimitOrder.pdf), MEASURED on the 30 most actively traded stocks on the Helsinki Exchanges from September 18, 1998 to October 23, 2001: household limit orders lose money on average because informed counterparties pick them off. Inside-the-spread household limit orders earned -0.04% same-day and -0.07% one-week total (splitting into -0.26% same-day when triggered by an institution versus +0.25% when triggered by another household); outside-the-spread household limit orders lost -0.26% same-day and -0.63% one-week. Around 586 pre-scheduled earnings announcements, stale household limit orders executed in the first five minutes lost -1.58% same-day, -2.92% one-week, and -4.06% two-week, and those filled in the first two minutes lost -2.5% (t = -2.5). Verbatim quotes: "the cross-sectional average same-day return on orders executed during the [0, 120s) interval is -2.5% (with a t-value of -2.5)." "Investors (on average) gain less than the half-spread from using limit orders. This is the risk of adverse selection." "In our risk-neutral model, these losses offset all the gains (because Eq. A3 holds with equality)."
- Barrot, Kaniel, and Sraer (2016, Journal of Financial Economics) and Kelley and Tetlock (2013, Journal of Finance), STATED via published abstracts: while aggregate retail limit-order imbalances are contrarian and predict short-term liquidity-provision rebounds, individual retail investors fail to capture those gains because they suffer negative returns on the day of execution (picking-off adverse selection) and hold too long after liquidity-provision returns dissipate.

### US Lit-Exchange vs Wholesaler Realized Spreads by Liquidity Tier

- [The Retail Execution Quality Landscape (AFA working paper)](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=13580), MEASURED from January 2019 through March 2022 across SEC Rule 605 reports for 14 US stock exchanges and 8 largest wholesalers (nearly 2.9 trillion traded shares, 8,165 ordinary non-ETF stocks in the main tables, round lots >= 100 shares only, 5-minute horizon). All spreads in this paper are full spreads (twice the signed distance from the midpoint).
- Verbatim quotes: "The data cover the period from January 2019 through March 2022" "To assess trading costs actually incurred by liquidity demanders, Rule 605 data contain the effective spread computed as twice the signed difference between the traded price and the midquote (the average of the best offer and the best bid) at the time of the trade." "The second component, the realized spread is the difference between the effective spread and the price impact." "Rule 605 requires that price impacts and realized spreads are reported at the 5-minute horizon." "Rule 605 data exclude odd lots, so our analysis is restricted to the orders of 100 shares or more."
- Across all non-ETF stocks on US exchanges (where a resting retail limit order actually sits), full quoted spread is 48.67 bp, effective spread is 46.98 bp, 5-minute price impact is 47.32 bp, and 5-minute realized spread is -0.34 bp (MEASURED). By contrast, wholesalers earn +16.53 bp realized spread (quoted 64.92 bp, effective 49.06 bp, price impact 32.53 bp) ONLY because brokers route segmented retail market orders off-exchange rather than letting them hit public exchange books.
- By liquidity tier (Table 5, MEASURED): on exchanges, S&P 500 5-minute full realized spread is -1.23 bp and Tercile 3 (least liquid stocks) is +4.65 bp (wholesalers: +1.21 bp S&P 500, +33.09 bp Tercile 3). In a wholesaler-only panel regression controlling for volume as an inventory-cost proxy (Table 10, column [3], MEASURED; not an exchange or market-wide figure), wholesaler Tercile 3 realized spreads are -20.374 bp (SE 3.15) lower than S&P 500 stocks. Verbatim quotes: "To understand the role of inventory costs for wholesalers, we run the following panel regressions:" "What at face value appeared to be excessively large realized spreads of 39.17 bps for Tercile 3 stocks relatively to S&P 500 stocks in column [1] are insufficient to compensate for incremental inventory costs based on columns [3] which shows that realized spreads are 20.37 bps lower."

### Small-Cap Realized Spread Decay at Sub-Second Horizons

- [Tick Size Pilot small-cap liquidity study (Stanford Law / SSRN 3076257)](https://law.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/SSRN-id3076257.pdf), MEASURED on 1,087 binding small-cap stocks from November 7, 2016 to June 30, 2017: even when the SEC artificially quintupled tick sizes to $0.05 (nearly doubling quoted spreads from 30 bp to 67 to 71 bp and effective spreads from 23 bp to 51 to 55 bp), realized spreads collapsed within milliseconds to minutes.
- Measured realized spreads in control stocks decayed from 20.2 bp at 1 microsecond to 12.2 bp at 1 second and 5.1 bp at 5 minutes; in Test Group 1 (nickel tick), realized spread fell from 50.5 bp at 1 microsecond to 33.0 bp at 1 second (a 34.6% drop, with 60% of 1-second price impact occurring in the first millisecond) and 20.0 bp at 5 minutes (another 39.6% drop).
- Verbatim quotes: "We demonstrate that realized spreads decay quickly within the initial microseconds of a trade. The effect reduces the subsidy offered by wider tick sizes, particularly for non-HFT market makers." "To capture the wider spread, liquidity providers need to be faster than human market makers are capable of being and indeed probably even collocated, highlighting that any movement to a nickel quoting environment is likely to favor HFT market making."

### Overnight US Equities (Extended Hours)

- [Nocturnal Trading (AEA 2026)](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2026/program/paper/kRz7yE6Q), MEASURED on Blue Ocean ATS (BOATS, 8:00 p.m. to 4:00 a.m. ET) from January to May 2024: conventional realized spreads turn negative at 60 seconds (-3.11 bp) and 5 minutes (-18.54 bp in Table 3 and Table 6; -18.45 bp in prose) for frequently traded (FT) securities, and fall from +29.68 bp (5 seconds) and +8.91 bp (60 seconds) to -18.66 bp at 5 minutes for less frequently traded (LFT) securities. Verbatim quotes: "The nocturnal period is also the only period with negative realized spreads, and they turn negative at the 60-second horizon (-3.11 bps) before dropping down to -18.45 bps after 5-minutes." "Hence, on average, liquidity providers would need to exit their positions in less than 60 seconds (5 minutes) to make money in the FT (LFT) sample." The Internet Appendix reports effective-spread proxies (not realized spreads) for 132 securities classified as "All Other Security Types (Mainly ETFs)."
- [Lim (April 2026, SSRN 6610883)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=6610883), MEASURED on 30 actively traded symbols across 24 matched sessions from September 2025 through March 2026 comparing Blue Ocean ATS to NASDAQ regular hours: "Effective spreads overnight average 7 cents per share higher, and the 10-second price impact component averages 2.4 cents per share higher" and "exchange-traded funds exhibit larger price-impact premia consistent with the loss of underlying-basket arbitrage when the underlying market is closed." Realized spread is not printed directly in Lim (2026) (UNKNOWN).

### US ETF Realized Spreads, Closed-End Funds, Preferreds, and Baby Bonds

- US ETFs (MEASURED in aggregate; slow small odd-lot retail survival UNKNOWN):
- [The Retail Execution Quality Landscape (AFA working paper, Appendix Table A2)](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=13580), MEASURED across 3,241 US ETFs (January 2019 to March 2022, round lots >= 100 shares, 5-minute full spreads): on exchanges, quoted spread is 24.34 bp, effective spread is 23.23 bp, price impact is 3.70 bp, and 5-minute realized spread is +6.11 bp; at wholesalers, quoted spread is 27.95 bp, effective spread is 17.97 bp, price impact is 3.70 bp, and 5-minute realized spread is +14.28 bp. Verbatim quote: "The table contains execution quality statistics for held liquidity-demanding orders in ETFs. We compute the statistics separately for orders executed by wholesalers (WHOL) and exchanges (EXCH)."
- [Khomyn, Putnins, and Zoican (2024, Review of Financial Studies)](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/37/10/3092/7738093), MEASURED on 105 competing US equity ETFs (January 2016 to December 2020, 1,752 ETF-quarter observations, TAQ 5-minute realized spread): mean 3.19 bp, median 1.23 bp (25th percentile 0.36 bp, 75th percentile 3.32 bp). Verbatim quote: "Realized spread (in bps), computed as the time-weighted average of the signed difference between trade price and the midpoint five minutes after the trade."
- [The Economics of ETF Redemptions (AEA 2023)](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2023/program/paper/QzSBe3FQ), MEASURED on 113 US corporate bond ETFs (January 3, 2012 to December 31, 2021, dollar-volume-weighted 5-minute full realized spread): mean 15.04 bp, median 7.04 bp. Verbatim quote: "The sample period is from January 3, 2012 to December 31, 2021, with 113 unique ETFs."
- [ESRB Working Paper 149](https://www.esrb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/wp/esrb.wp149.en.pdf), MEASURED on US ETFs (December 2017 to December 2023): custom-basket adoption under the SEC ETF Rule significantly tightened realized half-spreads by 84.367 bp in Table 4 (86 bp in prose, 36-month DiD, p < 0.01, against a 6.25% treated mean and 2.08% control mean) and by 115.34 to 206.45 bp in Table 6 matching regressions. Verbatim quote: "In this paper, I demonstrate that the introduction of custom baskets significantly tightens both effective and realized spreads, indicating improved liquidity in the ETF market."
- Limit of all US ETF realized-spread studies: every figure above is an aggregate market-wide or round-lot average dominated by fast professional market makers and HFTs (Khomyn et al. 2024 note that "most of the secondary market trading volume of French ETFs is generated by high-frequency traders who do not participate in the primary creation/redemption market"); none isolates a slow small odd-lot retail order.
- [SEC Staff Background Paper on Thinly Traded Securities (2019)](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/policy/2019/thinly-traded-securities-tm-background-paper.pdf) reports quoted/bid-ask spreads ONLY (MEASURED): corporate stocks with ADV <= 50,000 had "a median bid-ask spread of 234 basis points versus a median of 36 basis points for corporate common stocks overall" and median "~100 trades" per day; thinly traded ETPs (60% of 2,147 US-listed products, ADV <= 50,000) had "an average bid-ask spread of 33 basis points versus 8 basis points for the more actively traded ETPs." The SEC paper prints zero realized-spread figures (UNKNOWN) and does not cover closed-end funds or preferred stocks.
- Closed-end funds, preferred stocks, and $25-par baby bonds: no directly opened modern US empirical study measures post-adverse-selection realized spreads for resting limit orders in these securities (UNKNOWN). A non-US DMM study ([Santiago Stock Exchange 2011 to 2013](https://microstructure.exchange/papers/Paper_DMM.pdf)) measured 66 bp effective half-spread, 16 bp 5-minute price impact, and 50 bp realized spread on 160 Chilean stocks, which does not transfer to US Regulation NMS markets.

### US-Reachable Crypto Venues at Maker Fees

- [Ait-Sahalia et al. (2025, arXiv:2502.18625)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625), MEASURED in two live trading experiments on Binance USDT-margined Bitcoin perpetuals (February 12 to 19, 2024: 232,897 minimum-sized maker orders submitted, 127,051 filled; August 21 to 31, 2024: 172,800 submitted, 95,998 filled):
- Verbatim quotes: "We document a fundamental trade-off: a negative correlation between maker fill likelihood and post-fill returns." "In summary, maker orders experience negative instantaneous price drift and negative expected price drift over longer time scales, amounting to adverse selection." "Each leg of the roundtrip earns a maker rebate of 0.5 bp, thus we add 1 bp to each realized roundtrip return."
- Across all 16 queue-size and queue-position cells, 1-second post-fill markout (before fees) was negative, ranging from -0.058 bp (front of large near-side queue) to -1.157 bp (back of large two-sided queue). Five-second terminal-state expected return was -0.8 bp gross (-0.3 bp net of a 0.5 bp single-leg maker rebate).
- Because the 0.5 bp maker rebate is per leg (1.0 bp per round trip), the unfiltered naive passive maker's -0.4307 bp mean round-trip return (Sharpe -109.0, losing almost 60% in three days and seven hours, average hold 22.56 seconds) corresponds to -1.4307 bp before rebates. Their best logistic-regression maker strategy earned +0.71 bp per round trip after adding the 1.0 bp round-trip rebate (327 round trips/day, p = 0.0160, "likely not scalable"), which is about -0.29 bp per round trip before rebates. Against US retail spot maker fees of 30 bp (Alpaca Crypto) or 80 bp (Kraken Pro) round trip (Coinbase Advanced numeric Intro rate UNKNOWN without sign-in), even that best-case gross edge is deeply negative.
- No directly opened study documents positive net realized spreads for retail-tier maker orders on Coinbase Advanced, Kraken Pro, or Alpaca Crypto (UNKNOWN).

## 3. Mechanics on a $500 US Account (From Official Venue and SEC Documents)

### Alpaca Limit Orders, Fractional Shares, and Extended Hours

- Whole-share limit orders ([Alpaca Placing Orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca), STATED): whole-share limit orders support `gtc`, `day`, `ioc`, `fok`, `opg`, and `cls`.
- Fractional-share limit orders ([Alpaca Fractional Trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/fractional-trading) and [Placing Orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca), STATED): "Alpaca currently supports fractional trading for market, limit, stop & stop limit orders by setting `time_in_force` to `day`, accommodating both fractional quantities and notional values." "Limit orders are supported for both fractional and notional orders. Extended hours are also supported with limit orders (same as whole share orders)." However, the same official Alpaca page carries a conflicting disclosure stating "Fractional share transactions are executed either on a principal or riskless principal basis, and can only be bought or sold with market orders during normal market hours." Because fractional shares execute on a principal or riskless principal basis rather than resting directly on a lit exchange book, any spread-capture test must use whole-share orders only.
- Extended hours ([Alpaca Placing Orders](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/orders-at-alpaca), STATED): Alpaca supports pre-market (4:00 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. ET), after-hours (4:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m. ET), and overnight (8:00 p.m. to 4:00 a.m. ET, Sunday to Friday). "Only limit orders with `time_in_force` set to `day` or `gtc` orders are accepted as extended hours eligible. All other order types and TIF values will be rejected with an error."

### SEC Reg NMS Minimum Price Increments (Rule 612 and 2025/2026 Delays)

- Current enforceable tick size ([SEC Release No. 34-101070](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-101070.pdf) and [SEC Exemptive Order 34-105656, June 11, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2026/34-105656.pdf), STATED): Regulation NMS Rule 612 sets a $0.01 minimum pricing increment for NMS stock quotes and orders priced at or above $1.00 ($0.0001 below $1.00). On September 18, 2024, the SEC amended Rule 612 to add a $0.005 half-penny tick size for stocks with a Time-Weighted Average Quoted Spread of $0.015 or less, originally set for November 2025. That compliance date was extended first on October 31, 2025 to November 2026, and then on June 11, 2026 to November 2027.
- Verbatim quote ([SEC Release No. 34-105656](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/exorders/2026/34-105656.pdf)): "Accordingly, IT IS HEREBY ORDERED, pursuant to section 36(a)(1) of the Exchange Act and Rules 610(f) and 612(d) of Regulation NMS, that the Commission grants exemptive relief, as set forth in this order, from compliance with Rules 600(b)(89)(i)(F), 610(c), and 612, as amended in the Adopting Release until the first business day of November 2027."
- Impact on wide-spread candidates: as of November 26, 2026, $0.01 remains the enforceable tick size for all NMS stocks >= $1.00; even if $0.005 takes effect in November 2027, wide-spread securities (TWAQS > $0.015) remain at $0.01.

### SEC Reg NMS Round-Lot and Odd-Lot Display Rules (Effective November 2025 and May 2026)

- Tiered round-lot definition ([SEC Release No. 34-101070](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-101070.pdf), STATED): "Rule 600(b)(93), as adopted by the MDI Rules, defines a round lot for NMS stocks that have an average closing price on the primary listing exchange during the prior calendar month of: (1) $250.00 or less per share as 100 shares; (2) $250.01 to $1,000.00 per share as 40 shares; (3) $1,000.01 to $10,000.00 per share as 10 shares; and (4) $10,000.01 or more per share as 1 share." "The round lot definition will be implemented on the first business day of November 2025." For a $500 account, every order in any security priced above $5.00 per share is an odd lot (< 100 shares).
- Odd-lot SIP display (BOLO) ([SEC Release No. 34-105259](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/iex/2026/34-105259.pdf), STATED): "Thus, starting on May 1, 2026, the SIPs will begin disseminating the BOLO and the best odd-lot bid and offer priced at or better than the NBBO from each exchange and FINRA." "Best odd-lot order to buy is the highest-priced odd-lot order to buy that is priced higher than the NBB, and Best odd-lot order to sell is the lowest-priced odd-lot order to sell that is priced lower than the NBO." Depth-of-book odd-lot quotes under Rule 600(b)(69)(ii) were deferred until May 2028.
- No NBBO-setting or trade-through protection ([SEC Release No. 34-101070](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2024/34-101070.pdf), STATED): "Unlike orders in the round lot sizes adopted pursuant to the MDI Rules, odd-lots are not 'protected quotations.'" "Pursuant to Regulation NMS, bids and offers can only be in round lot sizes, therefore, the NBBO can only be reflected in round lot sizes." "Orders that remain odd-lots under the new definitions are not bids and offers and therefore do not independently contribute to establishing the NBBO." "Rule 611 currently only applies to round lots." An odd-lot bid from a $500 account can now be seen via BOLO if priced better than NBB (since May 1, 2026), but it still does not set the NBBO and other venues may legally trade through it.

### Pattern Day Trader Replacement (FINRA Rule 4210 Intraday Margin) and T+1 Cash Settlement

- FINRA Rule 4210 amendment ([SEC Release No. 34-105226, April 14, 2026, 91 FR 20731](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-04-17/html/2026-07485.htm) and [FINRA Regulatory Notice 26-10](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10), STATED): replacing the pattern day trader rule is a FINRA rule change (File No. SR-FINRA-2025-017 amending FINRA Rule 4210), approved by the SEC on April 14, 2026, effective June 4, 2026, with an 18-month broker phase-in ending October 20, 2027. It applies to customer margin accounts (excluding good faith and portfolio margin accounts under paragraph (d)(2)). Verbatim quotes: "FINRA has adopted new intraday margin standards to replace in their entirety the outdated day trading margin requirements, including the day trade count requirements for designating a customer as a 'pattern day trader' and the $25,000 pattern day trader minimum equity requirement." "The effective date of the amendments is June 4, 2026, 45 days from publication of this Notice." "Members that need more time to implement the rule change will be permitted to phase in their implementation over a period of 18 months, until October 20, 2027." "The core, operative provisions of the new requirements are set forth in new paragraph (d)(2) of Rule 4210, which establishes the requirement on each member to determine the 'intraday margin deficit' for each margin account of a customer that it maintains, other than a good faith account or portfolio margin account..."
- Broker implementation ([Alpaca Intraday Margin Rule](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/understanding-the-new-intraday-margin-rule), STATED): "No. The 'pattern day trader' designation has been abolished. Brokers are no longer required to monitor or flag accounts that execute four or more day trades within a five-business-day window." "No. The specific $25,000 minimum previously required to maintain 'Pattern Day Trader' status has been removed. However, individual firms may still maintain their own 'house' requirements for risk management." "Even without using leverage, accounts enabled for margin trading are often utilized to avoid the delays associated with T+1 settlement."
- Cash accounts and T+1 settlement (STATED): a $500 cash account was never subject to the pattern day trader rule (which applied to margin accounts under FINRA Rule 4210: "The rule defines the term 'day trading,' subject to specified exceptions, as the purchasing and selling or the selling and purchasing of the same security on the same day in a margin account"). A cash account is instead bound by Regulation T and T+1 settlement: buying with settled cash and selling the same day leaves sale proceeds unsettled until the next business day (T+1), capping full-account round trips to one turnover of $500 of settled cash per trading day (selling shares bought with unsettled proceeds triggers a Good Faith Violation).

### Crypto Lowest-Tier Maker Fees (From Official Venue Docs)

- Alpaca Crypto ([Alpaca Crypto Spot Trading Fees](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/crypto-fees), STATED): Tier 1 ($0 to $100,000 30-day volume) is 0.15% (15 bp) maker and 0.25% (25 bp) taker (30 bp maker-to-maker round trip). Verbatim quote: "A maker is someone who adds liquidity, and the order gets placed on the order book. A Taker on the other hand removes the liquidity by placing a market or marketable limit order which executes against posted orders."

## 4. Data Required for a Fair Test and Conservative Fill Rule

What data exist for a fair quote-and-trade test:

- Historical consolidated quotes (NBBO, plus post-May 1, 2026 BOLO odd-lot quotes) and tick-by-tick consolidated trades with nanosecond or millisecond timestamps and trade/quote condition codes (needed to exclude off-exchange TRF sub-penny prints, out-of-sequence reports, and auctions).
- [Alpaca Market Data API](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api) and [Alpaca Market Data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq) (STATED): historical CTA/UTP SIP data "Since 2016" are queryable on the Free Basic plan provided the query end timestamp is at least 15 minutes old ("For historical queries, the `end` parameter must be at least 15 minutes old to query SIP data without a subscription"); real-time SIP requires Algo Trader Plus at $99/month.
- [Databento Equities](https://databento.com/equities) (STATED): historical usage-based US equities pricing is "from $0.40/GB" across feeds listed on that page including "Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH", "NYSE Integrated", "Blue Ocean ATS MEMOIR Depth", "Databento US Equities Mini", and "tbbo" ("Top of book, sampled in trade space."). Individual per-dataset $/GB rates and consolidated SIP dataset identifiers are not stated on that page (UNKNOWN from that page).

Whether a conservative fill rule (fill only when a later trade prints through the limit by at least one tick, and count missed entries as missed winners) can be applied:

- It can be applied mechanically to historical SIP quotes and trades, but three structural flaws limit what it can prove for a $500 odd-lot account:
- First, odd-lot non-protection: because a $500 order is an odd lot with no Rule 611 trade-through protection, a trade printing one tick through the limit on a different exchange or an off-exchange TRF does not guarantee that a resting odd-lot order on one venue would have filled. Filtering strictly to lit-exchange prints on the order's venue is required, and even then queue position is unknown in L1 SIP data.
- Second, pre-May 2026 odd-lot blindness: historical SIP quotes before May 1, 2026 omit all odd-lot quotes, so any backtest prior to May 2026 cannot see whether competing odd-lot orders sat ahead of our price inside the NBBO.
- Third, conservative adverse-selection bias vs missed-winner accounting: requiring a 1-tick trade-through intentionally forces every simulated fill to occur only when price moves against the resting order (which is appropriately conservative for testing survival against adverse selection), while missed entries that never print through produce zero PnL rather than a gain (useful as an opportunity-cost diagnostic against a passive-midpoint benchmark, not a direct PnL loss).

## 5. Most Likely Way Each Candidate Fools Us

- Closed-end funds: wide quoted spreads (50 to 200+ bp) look like easy passive income on daily bars or midpoint quotes, but a $500 resting odd-lot bid gets ignored when benign retail orders route to wholesalers off-exchange and gets filled disproportionately when NAV drops or an informed seller sweeps the lit book (leaving an unhedged inventory loss that exceeds the spread).
- Preferred stocks and $25-par baby bonds: quoting inside a 50 to 150 bp spread looks profitable on paper, but with 1,000 to 5,000 shares/day and frequent zero-trade days, a filled resting bid cannot reliably exit at the ask; forcing an exit crosses the wide spread, while holding exposes a $500 position to rate, credit, or call-announcement gaps.
- Small and thin US ETFs: positive aggregate 5-minute realized spreads (+6.11 bp on exchanges in AFA Table A2; 3.19 bp mean in Khomyn et al. 2024; 15.04 bp mean in corporate bond ETFs) reflect fast professional market makers and APs quoting continuously off live underlying baskets; a static retail odd-lot order is a free option that gets picked off within milliseconds whenever the underlying basket moves.
- Thin small-cap US stocks: headline wide quoted spreads (234 bp median for ADV <= 50,000) and positive raw wholesaler realized spreads (+33.09 bp on Tercile 3) fool backtesters into thinking lit-exchange resting orders earn the same spread, when in fact exchanges average -0.34 bp overall (+4.65 bp on Tercile 3 before inventory costs), wholesaler Tercile 3 realized spreads fall 20.37 bp below S&P 500 after inventory controls in Table 10, and 35% to 44% of small-cap realized spread decays within one second.
- US-reachable spot coins at maker fees: rebate-subsidized academic results (such as +0.71 bp per round trip after a 1.0 bp round-trip maker rebate, which is -0.29 bp before rebates) and tight 1-tick books hide the fact that naive maker fills suffer negative 1-second markouts across every queue state, while US retail accounts pay 30 bp round trip on Alpaca Crypto and 80 bp round trip on Kraken Pro (Coinbase Advanced numeric Intro tier UNKNOWN without sign-in).
