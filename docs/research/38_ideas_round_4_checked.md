# Round 4 checked: Avoid lists, risk controls, and AI fund records

Checked November 25, 2026. Counts: **27 confirmed, 11 wrong, 8 not found**. Each bullet is one verdict; repeated or closely linked numbers count together. **MEASURED (by its authors)** means a source reports results from data, not that we ran the test. **STATED** means a proposal, description or unaudited statement. **UNKNOWN** means the claimed result was not established. A basis point is 0.01 percentage point. Sharpe compares return with how much returns vary; it is not a return.

## Buy, sell and avoid signals

- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Barber et al. (2001) gross and net.** The Zacks database covers 1985 to 1996, but “we do not include the investment returns from [1985] in our analysis.” In 1986 to 1996, the most and least favored portfolios earned +4.13% and −4.91% annual abnormal gross returns with daily rebalancing. Shorting the latter reverses the sign before costs. After estimated costs, “none of our strategies generates an abnormal net return that is reliably greater than zero.” This is a historical strategy calculation, not a live account ([original paper](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/accounting/trueman_5.pdf), [journal article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/0022-1082.00336)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: UK recommendation revisions.** The main sample is January 1995 to June 2013. The authors say upgrades did not generate significantly positive abnormal returns even before costs, and downgrades' significant gross results generally disappeared after assumed costs. The paper does report exceptions for certain periods in health care and technology; do not write “all downgrades failed” ([Su et al.](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11156-018-0749-y.pdf)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: Kanne and Kreutzmann paper date.** The linked working paper's cover says “October 2008,” although it sits in a 2009 conference folder. Its cost claim is supported: “the abnormal returns do not remain significantly positive after transaction costs”; those costs were estimated, not paid by a test account ([working paper, cover and Table 10](https://cfr-cologne.de/download/kolloquium/2009/kanne-kreutzmann.pdf)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: 2000–2001 reversal.** The 2003 original says “the stocks least favored by analysts earned an average annualized market-adjusted return of 13.44 percent, whereas the stocks most highly recommended underperformed the market by 7.06 percent.” These are returns relative to the market in those two years, not a net reverse-recommendation strategy. The scout's Penn State PDF is a later student review; use the original for these figures ([original Barber et al. article](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/56282f2ae4b06b50e93efd7c/t/5644e5f2e4b0197f17bb13be/1447355916506/FAJ+Reassessing.pdf), [Penn State review](https://honors.libraries.psu.edu/files/final_submissions/3775)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Muravyev, Pearson and Pollet.** The 2025 journal paper reports, across 162 anomalies, 0.14% monthly gross versus −0.01% after its borrow-fee adjustment. Excluding about 14% of stock-months using short interest divided by institutional holdings also left near-zero abnormal returns; the separate filter using actual fee observations removed about 12%. This was a historical long-short analysis, not proof of a long-only veto ([journal paper](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/jofi.13501)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Bedhiaf and Silininkaite long-only exclusion.** The academic year is 2025–2026; the paper describes a changing universe of the 2,000 largest US stocks in 2007–2024. For the one-month holding horizon, “the long-only benchmark earns an annualized net return of 18.85% with a Sharpe ratio of 0.79” and excluding stocks both in the top fifth for short interest and the bottom fifth for past momentum produced 19.54% and 0.83 in the prose. That is +0.69 percentage points a year and +0.04 Sharpe; a numerical table rounds the exclusion return to 19.53%. The paper specifies “25 basis points applied to realized turnover,” not a flat deduction of 25 basis points each month, with no borrow or short-side cost. Excluding just high short interest did worse than the benchmark, and no real money was traded in this test ([thesis, sections 3.2.8 and 4.7](https://www.vernimmen.net/ftp/mif_rp_bedhiaf_and_silininkaite__1_.pdf)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: −215 basis points is not the replication's finding.** That quote describes the 2005 paper's combined-market estimate, July 1988 to December 2002. The August 2026 report's comparable 2003–2024 rerun found −2.48% per month equal-weighted, with −2.10% value-weighted for that narrow group. The original's generally weak value-weighted evidence cannot be applied to every rerun. Both are historical estimated returns, not net account returns ([original Asquith et al. paper](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Short%20Interest,%20Institutional%20Ownership.pdf), [replication](https://dmurav.com/replications/releases/v2026-09-06/reports/paper-e703621346fd.pdf)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Lopez-Lira and Tang gross long and short legs.** October 2021 to May 2024, for overnight news, the long leg averaged 8 basis points a day with Sharpe 0.78 and the short leg 26 with Sharpe 2.01. The ratio is 3.25 in those gross portfolios; short-leg profits are not the same as savings from leaving an existing holding out ([paper and online appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Lopez-Lira and Tang large-stock reversal.** Their negative-news price drift is +0.06% for large stocks versus −0.70% for small stocks. The corresponding short-only Sharpe is −0.61 versus +3.17. These are sample group results, not a result for our 38 names ([paper, Appendix Tables OA9 and OA10](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Lopez-Lira and Tang decay and fees.** The overnight headline Sharpe goes 6.54 in 2021 Q4, 3.68 in 2022, 2.33 in 2023 and 1.22 in January–May 2024. At full rebalancing, equal-weighted overnight Sharpe falls from 2.97 before costs to −0.394 at 20 basis points **round trip**. These are simulated fee assumptions, not broker fills ([paper, Table OA7](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619)).
- **UNKNOWN · NOT FOUND: Our drop and add returns.** The scout's 12%–21% declines for dropped names and about 14% prior increase for additions do not come with a dated output, denominator or comparison in the draft. The papers do not verify our result or prove that “avoid” beats “buy” in a large-stock cash portfolio.

## Models that change risk

- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: VolRouter results and missing comparator.** arXiv 2608.10375 resolves to *Beyond Forecasting: Recasting Volatility Control as a Routing Problem*, posted August 11, 2026. Table 1 has no buy-and-hold or plain trend row, even though buy-and-hold is defined in the policy library. After modeled 5-basis-point S&P 500 costs, router versus plain realized-volatility scaling: annualized return 10.84% vs 11.11%, Sharpe 1.222 vs 0.952, worst fall 12.58% vs 15.10%, average loss in the worst 5% of days 1.32% vs 1.76%. For Bitcoin, with 8 basis points modeled: 30.81% vs 29.56%, Sharpe 1.123 vs 0.747, worst fall 39.39% vs 51.69%. On USDT, both the contextual bandit and the fixed regime selector beat VolRouter on Sharpe. This is a backtest; lower drawdown does not mean higher return or beating holding ([arXiv version dates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10375), [VolRouter, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10375), [scout's ar5iv mirror](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2608.10375)).
- **STATED · WRONG: The exact headline VolRouter window and a specific LLM are not established.** The authors date the S&P 500 “component-ablation” period February 10, 2023 to February 10, 2026, not explicitly Table 1. A separate path check uses 2 basis points, not Table 1's 5. The Bitcoin window and Table 1's router model are not identified. The router “can be instantiated by rule-based, learnable, or LLM-based decision modules,” while fixed policies calculate the weights. Do not credit the whole table to one LLM ([VolRouter, Appendices E and F](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10375)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Inside the Trading Loop same-session numbers.** arXiv 2611.11657v1 is the November 8, 2026 paper described. Table 23 lists 0%, 5%, 10%, 15% and 20% constant cash controls. On the reconstruction from March 12 to August 31, L2 Sizing, 5% cash and 0% cash finished +2.34, +2.57 and +4.94 percentage points **relative to L0**, the quant-only rule, not total returns. All reported paired intervals included zero. Simulated turnover costs were 5 basis points per side; the paper flags same-session timing problems. In a separate next-session check, L2 reached 78.30%, 5% cash 77.14% and 0% cash 81.99%; the 5% comparison changes sign ([Inside the Trading Loop](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11657v1)).
- **STATED · WRONG: Inside the Trading Loop did not test free-form cash weights or funded LLM trades.** The momentum engine “targets ten stocks”; actual holdings can exceed ten. The policy maps a bounded stance to fixed cash levels; “sizing_guidance” is not parsed, and “recommendations concentrate on scale_in,” mapping to 5%. The broker-recorded account was paper money, with deterministic fallbacks throughout; the LLM comparisons were reconstructed. A near-constant allocation here does not show that free-form daily cash setting always fails ([Inside the Trading Loop](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11657v1)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Unusual firm news and risk.** Glasserman and Mamaysky analyze “more than 360,000 articles on 50 large financial companies, published in 1996–2014”; negative, unusual company news forecasts single-firm and aggregate volatility months ahead even with lagged volatility controls ([authors' January 2017 draft](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/19266/glasserman_news_mktstress.pdf)). The October 2019 journal article says, “News measures from articles explicitly about the ‘market,’ which are more easily accessible to investors, do not forecast volatility.” This is not a test of next-open stock or crypto trades or net returns ([journal article](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/does-unusual-news-forecast-market-stress/8B880FAB1CA3036F6403DD353F380CC0)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: FOMC minutes detection.** arXiv 2605.30363 is the Treasury-market regime-shift paper. It uses 120 FOMC minutes from 2010 to 2024 and a 14-variable panel. The text-only score was F1 0.82 with +3.1 days average signed offset; the best data-only score was 0.68. F1 combines the share of true events caught and the share of alarms that were correct. This detects regime changes against a historical reference list, not trading profit or advantage before costs ([regime-shift paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30363)).
- **STATED · WRONG: June 2026 is not the arXiv date for the FOMC regime paper.** The version history gives “[v1] Sun, 17 May 2026” and version 2 on August 2, 2026 ([arXiv submission history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30363)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: FINSABER dates and regime result.** arXiv 2505.07078v6 is the FINSABER version posted June 26, 2026. It tests FinMem and FinAgent on a 2004–2024 period, includes historical delistings, and compares with holding, ARIMA and reinforcement-learning rules including PPO. Authors describe the LLMs as “overly conservative in bull markets” and “overly aggressive in bear markets.” This is a retrospective timing test; the replication models commissions and the separately estimated model charges are not proof of all-in net performance ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v6)).
- **STATED · WRONG: FINSABER is not a cash-weight experiment.** Its timing strategies make daily Buy, Sell or Hold signals applied to stocks individually, not portfolio-wide cash weights. It does not test unrestricted model cash weights against a constant matched-exposure cash mix ([FINSABER, methodology](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v6)).
- **UNKNOWN · NOT FOUND: The field-wide claim that no free-form sizer wins, or that VolRouter is the only positive 2026 evidence.** The cited studies differ in what they let the model decide, their benchmarks and their costs. They establish neither an exhaustive search result nor superiority over passive holding ([VolRouter](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.10375), [Inside the Trading Loop](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11657v1), [FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v6)).

## Four proposed next-open tests

- **STATED · CONFIRMED: Public SEC source and cutoff.** The SEC says its submissions API does not require authentication or API keys. The EveryInfra guide's description of the 10 p.m. same-day Schedule 13D exception matches the rule, which applies to direct transmission commencing by that time. This is distinct from the general 5:30 p.m. filing-date rule ([SEC API](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces), [17 CFR 232.13](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-232/section-232.13), [EveryInfra guide](https://everyinfra.com/en/blog/sec-filing-monitoring-data-product)).
- **STATED · WRONG: SEC timestamp field spelling.** The JSON key is `acceptanceDateTime`, not `acceptanceDatetime`; e.g., `2026-11-05T11:01:52.000Z` for a Form 8-K. Acceptance time has seconds but is not proof that we already had the full text ([SEC submissions example](https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK0000789019.json)).
- **STATED · CONFIRMED: Design A, the 8-K codes.** Item 5.02 covers departures, appointments and other officer/director events, not just sudden resignations. Item 1.05 covers material cyber incidents, Item 8.01 is “Other Events,” and Item 4.02 covers non-reliance on past financial statements or related auditor work. SEC staff recommends using 8.01 for voluntary disclosures of incidents not yet determined material or determined immaterial, not as a substitute when Item 1.05 applies ([official Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/about/forms/form8-k.pdf), [SEC staff statement](https://www.sec.gov/news/statement/gerding-cybersecurity-incidents-05212024)).
- **STATED · WRONG: December 18, 2023 was not the universal effective date.** The SEC says Form 8-K disclosures begin on “the later of 90 days after publication ... or December 18, 2023,” with 180 additional days for smaller reporting companies; the rule itself became effective 30 days after publication. The December date is the main compliance start, not a date that applies equally to every filer ([SEC announcement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-139)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Cyber event-study result.** arXiv 2512.06144 is *Market Reactions to Material Cybersecurity Incident Disclosures*, posted December 5, 2025, with filings through November 12, 2025. It reports 54 filings across 36 companies, average stock return −3.07% and standard deviation 9.36%, over the previous market day through five market days after filing. The study “does not evaluate trading profitability”; the larger-company subgroup averaged +0.43%, not −3.07% ([cyber event study](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06144)).
- **UNKNOWN · NOT FOUND: The cyber event study's December 2023 sample start.** The paper provides its November 2025 cutoff but does not explicitly give December 2023 as the first observation. The main SEC compliance start does not by itself prove the dataset starts that month ([cyber event study](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06144), [SEC announcement](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-139)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: Cyber taxonomy share and claimed trading edge.** arXiv 2607.08346 is the cited taxonomy paper, with 292,984 filings from January 2022 to June 2026. It says “roughly two thirds” of post-rule tagged incidents were disclosed under **other items**, “predominantly” 8.01; it does not say two thirds were specifically under 8.01. Of 309 full-period incident tags, 48 carried 1.05. Finding additional notices through text is not evidence of a profitable screen; the study “makes no return-prediction claims” ([8-K taxonomy paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08346)).
- **UNKNOWN · NOT FOUND: A post-filing net return for sudden 5.02 exits or post-publication decay in the 1.05 result.** The cited taxonomy measures size of reactions, not their direction or net profit, and the cyber paper uses a window that includes prices before filing. Neither tests a next-open 20-day exclusion. This could fool us if the first tradable move is already over or if an “exit” is routine succession ([8-K taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.08346), [cyber event study](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.06144)).
- **STATED · CONFIRMED: Design B, Schedule 13D fields.** Item 2 is “Identity and Background” and Item 4 is “Purpose of Transaction.” A sample filed with the SEC contains both; the headings do not identify a profitable activist trade ([original SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1360555/000095012310050537/o62080sc13d.htm)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Activism event windows.** Brav et al. use 2001–2006 events and report 7.2% abnormal return over days −20 to +20, with “another approximately 2.0 percent” on filing day and the next day. The FDIC paper also says announcement returns “dropped monotonically from 15.9% in 2001 to 3.4% in 2006.” Much of the 7.2% had already arrived around or before filing; it is not a next-open after-cost return ([Columbia paper](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/4132/jiang_activism.pdf), [FDIC paper](https://www.fdic.gov/analysis/cfr/working-papers/2008/2008-06.pdf)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Later-sample and no-takeover checks.** In the 1993–2006 Greenwood–Schor sample, returns were not significantly different from zero for targets that remained independent. Collin-Dufresne and Fos found “no trend in the abnormal return after the filing date” specifically in their 2003–2010 subsample. Neither finding proves zero drift in 2026, and a future takeover cannot be used to choose names at the filing time ([Greenwood and Schor](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/08-004.pdf), [Collin-Dufresne and Fos](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w18452/w18452.pdf)).
- **UNKNOWN · NOT FOUND: Design C's daily news throttle after costs.** The cited forecast is at a months-long horizon and measures volatility, not return from choosing between 50% and 100% invested. The paper does not verify the scout's local feed completeness, dates or next-open fills. The daily version could simply hold less through rebounds, with no benefit over a constant mix holding the same amount on average ([Glasserman and Mamaysky](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/19266/glasserman_news_mktstress.pdf), [Inside the Trading Loop](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11657v1)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Design D, same-day reversals.** FEDS 2026-029 uses 223 statements and 85 press conferences in its **text** sample, 1999 to April 2025, and a broader market-price sample starting in 1995. It reports that the press conference “sometimes reverses” the statement reaction, especially in the 2022–2024 tightening cycle. It does not test whether a reader can earn an after-cost return the next morning ([Federal Reserve working paper](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026029pap.pdf)).
- **STATED · CONFIRMED: FOMC dates and statement time.** There were five scheduled meetings in April–November 2026, not an unresolved four or five: April 28–29, June 16–17, July 28–29, September 15–16 and October 27–28, out of eight regularly scheduled meetings for the year. The October meeting page says the statement was released at 2:00 p.m.; the Fed calendar offers public press-conference materials. This does not establish when a final transcript first became available to a replay ([Fed calendar](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm), [October meeting](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomcpresconf20261028.htm)).
- **UNKNOWN · NOT FOUND: The clean training cutoff and 2–4 p.m. claim.** arXiv 2611.21554 is the November 16, 2026 preprint *Reading or Remembering? Measuring Temporal Contamination in Language Models*. It warns that a model may remember later outcomes, but its document corpus ends in January 2026 and its nowcast test ends in 2024. It does not verify an April–November 2026 cutoff for our selected models, transcript publication times, or that most of a tradable move necessarily finishes by 4 p.m. Five meetings make a weak test, not a mathematical proof that significance is impossible ([temporal contamination preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.21554), [Federal Reserve working paper](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026029pap.pdf)).
- **STATED · NOT FOUND: Design settings and probability guesses.** External papers cannot confirm zero overlap with queue rows 10–13 or Round 2, full local coverage from April 1 for stocks and filings, April 29 for Alpaca news, or from April for crypto. The proposed tests use a 38-name basket, 9:30 a.m. Eastern stock fills or 00:00 UTC crypto marks, and 5 basis points per side. They propose 20 trading days for the filing tests, an April 29–May 28 warm-up and May 29 start with a 20-day risk comparator, and a 5–10-day FOMC step at 50% exposure. Those settings, the seven-month sample and trigger guesses, and the 15%–35% survival odds are not primary-source results.
