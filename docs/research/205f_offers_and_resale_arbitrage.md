## Bottom Line

The honest small-scale picture in this lane is narrow and front-loaded. Most reselling categories are either fee-compressed, inventory-heavy, or legally blocked for automated agents, and most paid-task platforms explicitly ban AI or pay below minimum wage.

The three methods in this lane most likely to produce real, lawful cash for one Idaho resident right now are:

- Bank and brokerage sign-up bonuses paired with disciplined, no-balance credit card rewards: MEASURED live offers from major US banks open in late 2026 pay $425 to $900 per checking account for routing an existing paycheck or direct deposit, with near-zero capital risk if monthly fee waivers are tracked ([Wells Fargo offer terms](https://accountoffers.wellsfargo.com/welcomebonus/), [Chase checking offer terms](https://www.chase.com/personal/offers/checking-tier)). An agent adds clear operational value by tracking offer windows, waiver rules, payout deadlines, and 12-to-24-month cooldowns while the owner opens accounts under their own identity (INFERENCE).
- Owner's own lawful claims, refunds, and unclaimed property audit: MEASURED government data shows Idaho holds more than $291 million in unclaimed funds retrievable for free ([Idaho State Treasurer](https://sto.idaho.gov/About/Current-News/ArticleID/128/Ho-Ho-Ho-Idaho-State-Treasurer-Delivers-Gifts-Early)), class action settlements pay a $69 median to the 9% median share who file ([FTC Class Action Study](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/reports/consumers-class-actions-retrospective-analysis-settlement-campaigns/class_action_fairness_report_0.pdf)), and federal rules force automatic cash airline refunds within 7 business days ([USDOT Automatic Refund Rule](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/what-airline-passengers-need-know-about-dots-automatic-refund-rule)). This is a one-time and episodic cash sweep rather than steady monthly income, but it requires $0 capital and takes days.
- Selective local-to-online physical reselling (used books, niche collectibles, or estate/surplus lots) ONLY if treated as a manual local sourcing business where an agent does research, comp pricing, and listing drafts: eBay charges 13.6% to 15.3% plus $0.40 on the total including shipping and sales tax ([eBay selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)) and Amazon charges 15% plus a $1.80 media closing fee plus plan fees ([Amazon pricing](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing)), so only deeply underpriced local inventory clears fees.

The traps to avoid or mark OUT immediately are:

- Automated event ticket buying and reselling: OUT. Bypassing purchase limits or security controls for venues over 200 seats violates federal law under the BOTS Act and carries multi-million-dollar FTC civil penalties ([15 U.S.C. 45c](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t15/s45c/), [FTC BOTS Act refresher](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/04/bots-act-compliance-time-refresher)).
- Automated buy-for-me shopping bots on eBay: OUT. eBay's official Robots and Agent Policy strictly prohibits automated scraping, buy-for-me agents, and LLM-driven bots that place orders without human review ([eBay robots.txt](https://pages.ebay.com/robots.txt)).
- Running AI agents on paid research panels, user testing, or micro-task sites (Prolific, UserTesting, MTurk): OUT. All three explicitly ban bots and AI-generated answers, enforce live video ID checks or behavioral detection, and ban accounts with balance forfeiture ([Prolific bot detection](https://www.prolific.com/resources/how-prolific-detects-bots-and-ai-in-online-research), [UserTesting Code of Conduct](https://participant-support.usertesting.com/hc/en-us/articles/37395412977427-Contributor-Code-of-Conduct), [MTurk Acceptable Use Policy](https://www.mturk.com/worker/acceptable-use-policy)). Even done manually, US MTurk workers earn a MEASURED median of $3.01 per hour ([PLOS ONE citing Hara et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10550147/)).
- Digital goods resale (used MP3s, ebooks, software licenses): OUT. Federal appellate precedent holds that transferring digital files creates an unauthorized reproduction under 17 U.S.C. 106(1) not shielded by the first-sale doctrine ([US Copyright Office summary of Capitol Records v. ReDigi](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/capitol-records-llc-redigi-inc-no.16-2321-2nd-cir.dec.12.2018.pdf)).
- Amazon retail/online arbitrage at $300 to $500 scale: heavy survivor bias. MEASURED marketplace tracking shows only 7.8% of US Amazon sellers active five years ago remain active today, and 1.6% of sellers generate half of all US third-party sales ([Marketplace Pulse global opportunity](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazons-global-marketplace-opportunity), [Marketplace Pulse top 1.6%](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/top-16-of-sellers-drive-50-of-amazons-3p-gmv)).
- Speculative domain flipping with a few hundred dollars: commissions take 10% to 30% with a $15 minimum on Afternic ([SERPHeroes marketplace comparison](https://serpheroes.io/blog/where-to-sell-domain-names/)), annual renewals drain small budgets, and published venue pages do not disclose sell-through rates (UNKNOWN).

## Method 1: Bank, Broker, and Credit Card Sign-Up Bonuses, Cashback Stacking, and High-Yield Cash

- What it is and who pays: Banks, brokerages, and credit card issuers pay cash or points bonuses to acquire new deposits, direct-deposit relationships, and card spend, while high-yield savings accounts pay market interest on idle cash. Banks pay out of customer acquisition budgets and net interest margin, while Federal Reserve data shows credit card rewards are largely funded by merchant interchange fees and by revolving cardholders who pay interest and fees ([Federal Reserve FEDS 2023-007](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2023007pap.pdf)).
- Evidence that people really earn from it: MEASURED. Live issuer offer pages open to US residents as of November 2026 confirm exact cash payouts: Wells Fargo pays $500 for opening an Everyday Checking account by January 12, 2027 with $1,000 in qualifying electronic deposits within 90 days ([Wells Fargo offer terms](https://accountoffers.wellsfargo.com/welcomebonus/)), and Chase pays $425 for $2,500, $600 for $5,000, or $900 for $10,000 in qualifying direct deposits within 90 days ([Chase checking offer terms](https://www.chase.com/personal/offers/checking-tier)). In credit cards, Federal Reserve account-level FR Y-14M data covering 70% of US balances shows large US banks paid $35 billion in rewards in 2019, averaging $160.08 per reward card per year ([Federal Reserve FEDS 2023-007](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2023007pap.pdf)). Bloggers claiming $5,000 to $15,000 per year in easy churning profit are STATED affiliate claims, not audited medians.
- Base rate: MEASURED for credit cards, UNKNOWN for bank bonus churning. Federal Reserve data shows super-prime cardholders (FICO above 780) net +$16.05 per month per reward card over classic cards and prime cardholders (720 to 780) net +$7.28 per month, whereas near-prime (660 to 720) lose $6.80 per month and sub-prime (below 660) lose $5.37 per month because interest and fees exceed rewards ([Federal Reserve FEDS 2023-007](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2023007pap.pdf)). The CFPB reports consumers forfeit about $500 million in earned rewards annually ([CFPB Circular 2024-07](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/consumer-financial-protection-circular-2024-07-design-marketing-and-administration-of-credit-card-rewards-programs/)).
- Typical dollars per month at small scale: INFERENCE of $100 to $300 per month averaged over a year for one disciplined Idaho resident with a regular paycheck or qualifying electronic income who completes 3 to 5 checking bonuses ($400 to $500 each) plus ordinary spend cashback. With only a few hundred dollars of capital and no employer direct deposit, brokerage deposit bonuses (which require thousands parked) and high-yield cash (about $1 to $2 per month on $500) contribute almost nothing; the real cash comes from routing existing payroll or qualifying income through checking offers ([Wells Fargo offer terms](https://accountoffers.wellsfargo.com/welcomebonus/)).
- Money and time needed before the first dollar: $25 minimum opening deposit for Wells Fargo Everyday Checking ([Wells Fargo offer terms](https://accountoffers.wellsfargo.com/welcomebonus/)); 45 to 120 days from account opening to bonus payout (Wells Fargo pays within 30 calendar days after meeting the 90-day deposit requirement; Chase pays within 15 days after day 90) ([Wells Fargo offer terms](https://accountoffers.wellsfargo.com/welcomebonus/), [Chase checking offer terms](https://www.chase.com/personal/offers/checking-tier)).
- Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus tax handling: Legal nationwide and in Idaho for an individual opening genuine personal accounts under their own legal identity and SSN. Tax handling: bank account sign-up bonuses are taxable interest income reported on IRS Form 1099-INT ([Chase checking offer terms](https://www.chase.com/personal/offers/checking-tier)), whereas standard credit card purchase rebates are generally treated as purchase discounts rather than taxable income unless no spend is required (STATED/general IRS rule; verify on any 1099-MISC).
- Platform rules on automation, bots, AI, or multiple accounts: Issuers strictly enforce one bonus per primary SSN/TIN and cooldown periods: Wells Fargo limits the offer to one bonus per customer/account and excludes anyone who received a Wells Fargo consumer checking bonus in the past 12 months ([Wells Fargo offer terms](https://accountoffers.wellsfargo.com/welcomebonus/)); Chase limits checking bonuses to one every two years and excludes existing customers or accounts closed within 90 days ([Chase checking offer terms](https://www.chase.com/personal/offers/checking-tier)). The CFPB also documents issuers revoking or denying credit card bonuses under "gaming" or "churning" clauses ([CFPB Circular 2024-07](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/circulars/consumer-financial-protection-circular-2024-07-design-marketing-and-administration-of-credit-card-rewards-programs/)). Agents cannot open accounts or pass KYC; only the owner can apply and move funds.
- What an agent adds vs what needs the owner: An agent excels at tracking active US/Idaho-eligible bank offers, checking exact qualifying deposit rules (for example, Wells Fargo accepts ACH, RTP, FedNow, and Visa/Mastercard Original Credit Transactions while excluding Zelle and branch deposits; Chase strictly requires employer payroll, pension, or government benefits and excludes external bank transfers) ([Wells Fargo offer terms](https://accountoffers.wellsfargo.com/welcomebonus/), [Chase checking offer terms](https://www.chase.com/personal/offers/checking-tier)), alerting before monthly fee periods ($15/month at Wells Fargo unless waived by $500 monthly qualifying deposits or $1,500 daily balance), and logging 12-to-24-month cooldown dates. The owner must apply with SSN/ID, change payroll routing, hold accounts open through payout, and report 1099-INT income.
- What kills it: Missing a fine-print rule (sending an excluded transfer type, closing the account before payout, or letting a $15 monthly fee eat the bonus), carrying a credit card balance (interest immediately wipes out rewards per [Federal Reserve FEDS 2023-007](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2023007pap.pdf)), ChexSystems/Early Warning Services denial from too many rapid bank openings, and finite issuer supply per person.
- Cheapest honest test: Over 7 days with $0 spent, have the agent compile a verified checklist of 3 live checking offers eligible in Nampa, Idaho that match the owner's actual deposit stream (payroll or qualifying electronic payments), verify every exclusion and monthly fee waiver directly on each bank's official offer page, and open one single account (such as the $500 Wells Fargo offer with $25 opening deposit) to confirm tracking and payout execution.

## Method 2: Physical Reselling on Marketplaces (Retail/Online Arbitrage, Used Books, Collectibles, Trading Cards)

- What it is and who pays: Buying physical goods below market value (clearance racks, thrift stores, local classifieds, estate sales, or mispriced listings) and reselling them on eBay, Amazon, or niche marketplaces. End buyers pay for convenience, discovery, and immediate shipping of items they cannot find locally.
- Evidence that people really earn from it: MEASURED at aggregate marketplace level, heavily skewed. Marketplace Pulse reports 547,958 active US Amazon third-party sellers averaging $557,088 in gross revenue ([Marketplace Pulse global opportunity](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazons-global-marketplace-opportunity)), but also measures extreme concentration: 7,760 sellers (1.6% of active US sellers) generate 50% of Amazon's $300 billion US third-party GMV, and roughly 20% generate 90% of revenue ([Marketplace Pulse top 1.6%](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/top-16-of-sellers-drive-50-of-amazons-3p-gmv)). Course sellers advertising "$5,000/month easy retail arbitrage" are STATED marketing claims, not independent profit audits.
- Base rate: MEASURED survival is low. Marketplace Pulse reports that among US Amazon sellers active five or more years ago, only 7.8% remain active today (compared with 15.5% in Japan and 12.4% in Canada) ([Marketplace Pulse global opportunity](https://www.marketplacepulse.com/articles/amazons-global-marketplace-opportunity)). Independent median net profit for small-scale eBay or retail-arbitrage resellers is UNKNOWN because marketplaces do not publish seller-level net profit distributions.
- Typical dollars per month at small scale: UNKNOWN from audited records; INFERENCE of $0 to $300 per month net on $300 to $500 of rolling inventory if sourcing locally with high markup, because marketplace fees take a large fixed cut. On eBay (non-Store), sellers pay 13.6% of total sale amount (item plus shipping plus sales tax) plus $0.40 per order over $10, rising to 15.3% for books, movies, and music ([eBay selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822)). On Amazon, sellers pay 15% referral fee in most categories (8% for consumer electronics/computers), plus $0.99 per item on the Individual plan (or $39.99/month Professional), plus a $1.80 closing fee per media/book item before shipping or FBA fees ([Amazon pricing](https://sell.amazon.com/pricing)).
- Money and time needed before the first dollar: $100 to $300 for initial local inventory and shipping supplies; 7 to 30 days from sourcing and listing to buyer payment and payout clearance (INFERENCE).
- Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus tax handling: Legal. In Idaho, anyone making more than two retail sales in any 12-month period generally needs a free Idaho seller's permit, UNLESS they sell exclusively through third-party websites registered with the Idaho State Tax Commission as marketplace facilitators (such as eBay or Amazon), which collect and remit Idaho sales tax on the seller's behalf ([Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/permits/who-needs-a-sellers-permit/)). Tax handling: net reselling profit is taxable self-employment/business income on Schedule C regardless of whether a 1099-K is issued.
- Platform rules on automation, bots, AI, or multiple accounts: CRITICAL RESTRICTION. eBay's official Robots and Agent Policy explicitly states: "Checkouts are strictly for human users. Automated scraping, buy-for-me agents, LLM-driven bots, or any end-to-end flow that attempts to place orders without human review is strictly prohibited" and requires approved enterprise integrations to use the official API under eBay's API License Agreement ([eBay robots.txt](https://pages.ebay.com/robots.txt)). Therefore, automated sniping or buy-for-me online arbitrage bots on eBay are OUT. Manual human buying with permitted API/research tools and human-reviewed listings is allowed.
- What an agent adds vs what needs the owner: An agent can research sold-price comps using permitted public/API data, calculate exact net margin after eBay's 13.6% to 15.3% fee-on-tax-and-shipping or Amazon's 15% plus $1.80 media fee, draft clean titles/descriptions, and track inventory cost basis for taxes. The owner must physically inspect, buy, store, photograph, pack, ship, and handle returns and disputes.
- What kills it: Fee compression and shipping cost spikes on low-price items (a $10 used book on Amazon loses $1.50 referral + $1.80 closing + $0.99 Individual fee = $4.29 before Media Mail shipping), brand gating and authenticity/invoice demands on Amazon (retail receipts often fail brand-gating checks), buyer returns and item-not-as-described claims, slow inventory turnover tying up small capital, and eBay's strict ban on automated buying agents ([eBay robots.txt](https://pages.ebay.com/robots.txt)).
- Cheapest honest test: Spend $0 on new inventory for 7 days. Have the owner pick 10 unused items already at home (books, electronics, collectibles), have the agent calculate exact break-even prices after [eBay selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822) and shipping, list them manually on eBay (within the 250 free monthly listings), and measure actual net profit per hour of owner labor over 14 days.

## Method 3: Liquidation Lots, Government Surplus, and Estate Auctions

- What it is and who pays: Buying returned, overstock, or surplus physical goods in bulk from liquidation platforms, government surplus auctions (municipal, state, federal), or local estate sales, then testing, cleaning, and reselling individual items locally or online. End buyers pay retail-discounted prices for working individual units because they will not buy uninspected bulk lots.
- Evidence that people really earn from it: Some professional resellers operate on this model, but independent audited seller profit data for small buyers is UNKNOWN. YouTube "unboxing a $500 Amazon returns pallet" videos are STATED entertainment and affiliate content, not verified net profit records after dead stock, disposal fees, and labor.
- Base rate: UNKNOWN. No liquidation platform or government auction site publishes buyer net-profit distributions.
- Typical dollars per month at small scale: UNKNOWN. At a $300 to $500 starting budget, most commercial liquidation pallets are out of reach once freight shipping and buyer's premiums are added; only small local pickup lots (local estate auctions, Idaho municipal/state surplus pickup) fit the budget (INFERENCE).
- Money and time needed before the first dollar: $100 to $500 per lot plus local transport/storage space; 14 to 45 days to inspect, repair/clean, photograph, list, and sell enough items to recover cost basis (INFERENCE).
- Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus tax handling: Legal in Idaho. Selling off-platform (local cash/estate/classifieds) more than twice in 12 months triggers the requirement for a free Idaho seller's permit to collect and remit sales tax, whereas selling solely on registered marketplace facilitators does not ([Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/permits/who-needs-a-sellers-permit/)).
- Platform rules on automation, bots, AI, or multiple accounts: Any downstream resale on eBay is bound by eBay's ban on automated scraping and LLM-driven buying/checkout agents ([eBay robots.txt](https://pages.ebay.com/robots.txt)). Government and estate auction portals require real-identity registration and binding bids by the owner.
- What an agent adds vs what needs the owner: An agent can monitor local Nampa/Boise/Treasure Valley estate and municipal surplus auction catalogs, look up model numbers and realistic resale prices net of [eBay selling fees](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822), and set a strict maximum bid cap before the owner bids. The owner must inspect in person, haul, test, store, and ship physical goods.
- What kills it: Unmanifested or cherry-picked return pallets full of broken junk, hidden buyer's premiums and freight costs, storage clutter, high return rates on untested electronics, and tying up the entire $300 to $500 bankroll in one bad lot.
- Cheapest honest test: For 14 days with $0 spent, have the agent track 20 local Boise/Nampa surplus or estate auction lots, record the agent's pre-auction max bid (targeting 3x cost after fees) vs actual hammer prices, and check whether enough underpriced local lots actually clear below max bid to justify driving and hauling.

## Method 4: Event Tickets, Price Mistakes, and Gift Card Resale

- What it is and who pays: Buying underpriced event tickets, retail pricing errors, or discounted gift cards and reselling them at higher market value. Secondary-market fans or bargain hunters pay the spread.
- Evidence that people really earn from it: MEASURED enforcement records prove ticket scalping generates large revenue for illegal bot operators, which is why the FTC sued three ticket brokers in 2021 for using automated ticket-buying software, IP-hiding tools, and hundreds of fictitious Ticketmaster accounts, resulting in $3.7 million in civil penalties ([FTC BOTS Act refresher](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/04/bots-act-compliance-time-refresher)). For lawful small-scale manual ticket flipping, price-mistake flipping, or gift card arbitrage, independent verified net profit records are UNKNOWN; Discord "cook groups" selling $50/month memberships are STATED promotional claims.
- Base rate: UNKNOWN.
- Typical dollars per month at small scale: UNKNOWN (and $0 legally for automated ticket bots, which are OUT).
- Money and time needed before the first dollar: $200 to $1,000+ tied up per event or order; weeks to months if ticket resale platforms hold payout until after the event occurs (INFERENCE).
- Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus tax handling: Automated ticket buying that circumvents security measures, access controls, or posted ticket limits for public events in venues exceeding 200 seats is ILLEGAL under federal law, 15 U.S.C. 45c (the BOTS Act), and reselling tickets known to be acquired that way is also unlawful, enforceable by both the FTC and State Attorneys General ([15 U.S.C. 45c](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t15/s45c/), [FTC BOTS Act refresher](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/04/bots-act-compliance-time-refresher)). Manual resale of lawfully purchased tickets is generally legal subject to venue transfer restrictions.
- Platform rules on automation, bots, AI, or multiple accounts: OUT for automated ticket bots (illegal under [15 U.S.C. 45c](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t15/s45c/)) and OUT for automated buy-for-me bots on eBay ([eBay robots.txt](https://pages.ebay.com/robots.txt)). Retailers routinely cancel obvious pricing-error orders under unilateral pricing-error clauses in their terms of sale, and gift card exchanges impose heavy fraud/chargeback holds and take 8% to 20% spreads that erase thin arbitrage margins (INFERENCE).
- What an agent adds vs what needs the owner: No lawful automated buying edge exists on tickets or eBay. An agent could only monitor public price-drop alerts for human review, where speed competition against thousands of Discord alert members leaves almost no manual margin.
- What kills it: Federal criminal/civil liability under the BOTS Act for ticket automation, instant order cancellations on retail price mistakes, non-refundable losses when event demand drops below face value, and gift card balance drain/fraud risk.
- Cheapest honest test: Do not test automated ticket buying (OUT). For price mistakes or gift cards, no spend test is justified; the legal, cancellation, and fraud risks outweigh any realistic small-scale return.

## Method 5: Domain Names and Expired Domains

- What it is and who pays: Registering newly available or expired domain names (typically $10 to $15/year) and reselling them to startups, local businesses, or brands on aftermarket venues such as Afternic or Sedo. End-user businesses pay for a memorable or brand-matching web address.
- Evidence that people really earn from it: Aftermarket venues exist and process real transactions, as documented in published fee schedules for Afternic and Sedo ([SERPHeroes marketplace comparison](https://serpheroes.io/blog/where-to-sell-domain-names/)). However, published venue pages do not disclose how often listed domains sell or at what median price ([SERPHeroes marketplace comparison](https://serpheroes.io/blog/where-to-sell-domain-names/)). Bloggers posting four- and five-figure flips are STATED cherry-picked wins.
- Base rate: UNKNOWN from venue-published audited records; industry practitioners widely report low single-digit annual sell-through rates (STATED), meaning most hand-registered domains never sell and expire at a 100% loss.
- Typical dollars per month at small scale: UNKNOWN; INFERENCE of negative to near-$0 per month in the first 6 to 12 months on a $300 budget, because 20 to 30 domains at a 1% to 2% annual sell-through rate yield zero expected sales in any given month while renewal fees recur every 12 months.
- Money and time needed before the first dollar: $100 to $300 in registrations/auctions; typically months to years before an end-user buyer appears (INFERENCE).
- Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus tax handling: Legal for generic/descriptive names, but registering a domain confusingly similar to a trademark in bad faith to profit violates the federal Anticybersquatting Consumer Protection Act (ACPA, 15 U.S.C. 1125(d)) and subjects the domain to UDRP seizure and statutory damages.
- Platform rules on automation, bots, AI, or multiple accounts: Aftermarket commission rates are steep for small-ticket names: Afternic's commission schedule dated January 13, 2026 charges 30% with Afternic Boost (or 20% using GoDaddy Aftermarket nameservers; 25% or 15% on Afternic Basic), with a $15 minimum commission on every sale; Sedo charges 10% for parked Buy Now, 15% for Make Offer/auctions/unparked Buy Now, and 20% for SedoMLS partner sales ([SERPHeroes marketplace comparison](https://serpheroes.io/blog/where-to-sell-domain-names/)). Drop-catching auctions are dominated by institutional registrars with massive API pools.
- What an agent adds vs what needs the owner: An agent can filter daily expired-domain lists, check trademark databases to avoid ACPA violations, and draft outbound pitches or landing pages. The owner must fund registrations, manage registrar accounts, and handle escrow/transfers.
- What kills it: Ultra-low sell-through rate (illiquid inventory), annual renewal burn, 15% to 30% marketplace commissions, institutional drop-catchers taking every genuinely valuable expired name, and trademark/UDRP forfeiture risk.
- Cheapest honest test: For 14 days with $0 spent, have an agent shortlist 25 expiring domains that pass trademark screening, track whether any sell at public expired-domain auctions and for how much, and compare hypothetical acquisition plus 20% to 30% Afternic/Sedo commission against realistic end-user comps before spending a dollar.

## Method 6: Digital Goods Resale (Used Music, Ebooks, Software Licenses, Game Keys)

- What it is and who pays: Buying digital files, ebooks, software licenses, or game keys cheaply and reselling them to end users at a markup. Buyers pay a discount off official storefront prices.
- Evidence that people really earn from it: Gray-market key sites exist, but lawful consumer resale of copyrighted digital files was shut down in federal court with a $3.5 million judgment ([US Copyright Office summary of Capitol Records v. ReDigi](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/capitol-records-llc-redigi-inc-no.16-2321-2nd-cir.dec.12.2018.pdf)).
- Base rate: OUT / not applicable for lawful resale.
- Typical dollars per month at small scale: $0 lawfully (OUT).
- Money and time needed before the first dollar: Not applicable (OUT).
- Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus tax handling: OUT / UNLAWFUL for copyrighted digital files. In Capitol Records, LLC v. ReDigi Inc. (2d Cir. Dec. 12, 2018), the US Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit affirmed a $3,500,000 judgment and held that reselling lawfully purchased digital files violates the copyright holder's exclusive reproduction right under 17 U.S.C. 106(1) and is neither protected by the first-sale doctrine (17 U.S.C. 109(a)) nor excused as fair use ([US Copyright Office summary of Capitol Records v. ReDigi](https://www.copyright.gov/fair-use/summaries/capitol-records-llc-redigi-inc-no.16-2321-2nd-cir.dec.12.2018.pdf)). Software and game EULAs also prohibit license resale and region-arbitrage key flipping.
- Platform rules on automation, bots, AI, or multiple accounts: Major storefronts (Apple, Amazon Kindle, Steam, Microsoft, Adobe) prohibit account/license resale and revoke gray-market keys.
- What an agent adds vs what needs the owner: Nothing; the method is legally and contractually OUT.
- What kills it: Federal copyright liability, EULA bans, revoked keys, and chargebacks.
- Cheapest honest test: None. Do not run.

## Method 7: Owner's Own Lawful Claims and Refunds (Unclaimed Property, Class Actions, Airline Refunds, Fee Refunds, Missed Tax Credits)

- What it is and who pays: Collecting money already legally owed to the owner personally: state-held unclaimed property, consumer class action settlements where the owner is a real class member, mandatory cash airline refunds, bank/billing fee waivers, and overlooked tax credits. Governments, defendant settlement funds, airlines, and financial institutions pay because statute, court order, or regulation requires them to return the owner's money.
- Evidence that people really earn from it: MEASURED. The Idaho State Treasurer safeguards more than $291 million in unclaimed funds (forgotten bank accounts, refunds, insurance payouts, rebate checks) claimable for free at YourMoney.Idaho.Gov ([Idaho State Treasurer](https://sto.idaho.gov/About/Current-News/ArticleID/128/Ho-Ho-Ho-Idaho-State-Treasurer-Delivers-Gifts-Early)). An FTC study of 149 consumer class actions measured a 9% median claims rate (4% weighted mean) and a $69 median payout (25th percentile $22, 75th percentile $200), with 94% median check-cashing rate ([FTC Class Action Study](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/reports/consumers-class-actions-retrospective-analysis-settlement-campaigns/class_action_fairness_report_0.pdf)). The USDOT Automatic Refund Rule (April 2024) mandates automatic cash refunds within 7 business days for credit card purchases (20 calendar days for other methods) for canceled flights, domestic delays/changes of 3 or more hours (6 hours international), bags delayed over 12 hours domestic, or unprovided paid ancillary services like Wi-Fi or seat selection ([USDOT Automatic Refund Rule](https://www.transportation.gov/briefing-room/what-airline-passengers-need-know-about-dots-automatic-refund-rule)).
