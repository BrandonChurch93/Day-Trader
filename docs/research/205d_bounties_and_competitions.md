# Paid bounties and competitions for an agent-assisted owner

INFERENCE: The best shot at a first dollar is one human-assigned software job with a fixed price and accepted payment route, not a bot racing to submit reports. Expensify is the clearest lead because it has an explicit AI-assistance policy and a defined payment process; even there, approval and production deployment come before the pay clock ([contributor guide](https://github.com/Expensify/App/blob/main/contributingGuides/CONTRIBUTING.md), [AI etiquette](https://github.com/Expensify/App/blob/main/contributingGuides/AI_ETIQUETTE.md)).

INFERENCE: A Firebase prototype that doubles as a portfolio piece is a reasonable second experiment if already close to done. For a more technical project that can wait, Google's engineering-agent challenge is open into January and explicitly wants an agent, but it is still a three-winner tournament, not customer revenue ([Firebase official rules](https://firebasehybridaihackathon.devpost.com/rules), [Google engineering challenge](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/physical-engineerings-last-exam)). UNKNOWN: No reliable positive monthly income for a new entrant in this lane. The planning assumption for month one is $0. Trap list: speculative bulk bounty reports, stale bounty badges, token stakes, and calling an investor's allocation or an advertised prize pool income.

## Account, legality, and what counts

- MEASURED: Review date November 27, 2026, Boise time. Public reading only; no account, signup, submission, live security testing, or purchase. An awarded credit or organizer's ledger is evidence of the recorded award, not proof the recipient's bank cleared it. A leaderboard without a corresponding award proves rank only. A conditional payment average among winners is not the median of everyone who tried.
- MEASURED: [Idaho Code §18-3801](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title18/T18CH38/SECT18-3801/) excludes bona fide skill contests whose awards go only to entrants or owners of entrants, and bona fide business transactions valid under contract law. [Idaho Code §18-2202](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title18/T18CH22/SECT18-2202/) separately penalizes knowing unauthorized access, including attempts. The [DOJ's CFAA charging policy](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-new-policy-charging-cases-under-computer-fraud-and-abuse-act) directs prosecutors not to charge good-faith research as defined there; it is not immunity from suit or a promise binding a third party or Idaho. INFERENCE: This supports ordinary commissioned work and genuinely free skill contests subject to host terms; do not apply it to token staking, rescue of live assets, or activity outside the sponsor's express written permission. Confirm scope, written safe harbor, third-party exclusions and automation separately. This report recommends no live security testing or automated submissions.
- MEASURED: [IRS Publication 525](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525) covers taxable prizes and U.S. reporting of foreign income. [IRS self-employment guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employed-individuals-tax-center) covers Schedule C, estimated tax and generally applicable self-employment filing at $400 net earnings; [IRS digital-asset guidance](https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets) treats tokens as property, so keep USD value and disposition records. Use the [Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/income-tax/individual-income/online-guide/) for the state return. INFERENCE: Owner supplies identity and tax details, signs all terms, reviews originality and disclosures, and controls payouts. No account sharing, uploaded client secrets, automated posting, fake identities or solicitation spam.

[Upwork's W-9 guidance](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/211063948-How-to-complete-Form-W-9-on-Upwork) requires a verified taxpayer number before US withdrawals. UNKNOWN: current monthly accepted and paid counts, probability of first assignment, median income, net dollars/month and post-fee median.
- MEASURED, terms and timing: The guide says “You are accountable for all AI output you submit,” “ALL NEW PROPOSALS MUST BE DIFFERENT FROM EXISTING PROPOSALS,” and “Do not leave more than one proposal”; the [AI guide](https://github.com/Expensify/App/blob/main/contributingGuides/AI_ETIQUETTE.md) permits drafting and expects understanding, testing, editing and human review. A new contributor handles one job at a time and needs a verified Upwork profile; payment is “no less than 7 days after the pull request is deployed to production,” with regression delays and penalty provisions ([payment and contribution rules](https://github.com/Expensify/App/blob/main/contributingGuides/CONTRIBUTING.md)). INFERENCE: US/Idaho ordinary services under the common legal screen; no stake, owner verifies Upwork identity and U.S. tax setup and checks the actual contract fee. First money could take weeks after assignment or never if not selected; do not pre-build against a badge.
- INFERENCE, advantage and cheapest test: Privately scope one UI or accessibility job in a familiar stack, estimate with one failing test and a short owner-reviewed explanation, check it against public prior proposals without scraping, and require written assignment, price and review path before implementation. In a 7-day no-post research screen, count current open, funded, unassigned jobs that fit a four-hour build, recent payment confirmations and days from merge to due date. No matching job or unclear AI terms means stop. At stake is owner time and fee-adjusted hourly rate, not capital.

UNKNOWN: current active-hunter median, newcomer payout rate/first-dollar time and expected monthly dollars; none of these denominators can be combined.
- MEASURED, AI case distinction: The [XBOW HackerOne business profile](https://hackerone.com/xbow) shows validated/resolved activity and a customer acknowledging a fix. STATED: [XBOW's own June 2025 account](https://xbow.com/blog/top-1-how-xbow-did-it) says it reached US #1 using automated discovery with a team reviewing before submission; it reported about 1,060 submissions, including 208 duplicates, and no paid USD figure. UNKNOWN: XBOW's private cash, cost and per-owner replicate odds. Neither a rank nor a validated VDP finding proves money.
- MEASURED, rule, status and account: HackerOne allows “responsible use of AI tools” but hackbots “must not operate in a fully autonomous manner”; humans must validate and follow program-specific scope, volume and automation limits. One account. At scale, low-quality or unverified submissions can escalate to a 12-month then permanent ban ([code of conduct](https://www.hackerone.com/policies/code-of-conduct)). Its [November volume update](https://www.hackerone.com/blog/ai-driven-report-volume-insights-and-actions) says duplicates, spam and not-applicable reports may be closed by triage and ID verification is required for monetary programs. The [tax and payment rules](https://docs.hackerone.com/en/articles/8395744-tax-forms) require a matching identity and valid tax form; [AI safe harbor](https://docs.hackerone.com/en/articles/13376522-ai-research-safe-harbor-statement) for HackerOne systems does not cover third parties. The Idaho/US express-authorization rule above applies. INFERENCE: Agent can read policy and summarize existing defensive findings for owner review, not perform autonomous hunting or submit. No-spend one-week test: assess scope clarity, public reward receipt and acceptance friction using program documentation only. OUT as agent-operated income.
