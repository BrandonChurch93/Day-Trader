## The lead, as our docs state it

- STATED: At each hourly open, if BTC fell at least 0.015 in log price over the last two closed bars and another coin fell more than twice as far, the rule buys that coin with no BTC hedge, at that open, and sells at the open two hours later ([doc 152](152_plan_70_coin_sweep_search.md)).
- STATED: 691 trades on 435 UTC dates, +56.5 bp plain net a trade, matched t 3.51, best of 188 rules on the same 2020 to 2023 dates ([doc 152](152_plan_70_coin_sweep_search.md)).
- STATED: By half, 2020 to 2021 gave +62.3 bp (t 2.73) and 2022 to 2023 gave +44.4 bp (t 2.08). Trimming the best 1% left +36.4 bp. The 160 bp spot-fee version lost. Carry passed in 20.67% of draws against a 33% bar, so nothing carried ([doc 152](152_plan_70_coin_sweep_search.md)).
- STATED: The prints are Binance global spot USDT hourly bars, not fills on a US contract. Net uses the doc 120 round-trip cost and 1 bp of adverse funding per gross leg per hour ([doc 152](152_plan_70_coin_sweep_search.md), [doc 120](120_round_31_cfm_futures_cost_rescreen.md)).

## a. Published evidence

Studies that find a short reversal after sharp drops:

- STATED: Miralles-Quirós and Miralles-Quirós use Kraken BTC hourly prices from 2016-03-01 to 2021-06-30. After a 1.5% hourly fall the cumulative return two hours later is +0.277% (t 4.683). After a 2.5% fall it is +0.542% at two hours (t 4.58). After a 5% fall it is +1.833% at two hours and +3.132% at 24 hours. No trading costs are applied and no sub-period split is given ([econstor PDF](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/314207/1/1841191159.pdf), [Journal of Applied Economics](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15140326.2022.2151253)).
- STATED: De Nicola uses Bitstamp BTC from March 2015 to June 2018. Return autocorrelation is -0.0557 at one hour and -0.0858 at two hours, and it grows to about -0.33 at two hours after 5-sigma moves. No fees are applied. The paper names liquidation cascades as one cause ([Ledger](https://www.ledgerjournal.org/ojs/ledger/article/download/213/212)).
- STATED: Kitron and Wengrowicz test 183 Binance pairs from January 2025 to February 2026 on 15-minute bars. Reversal grows with move size and with taker flow, holds at one hour (58% of 169 pairs significant) and is gone by four hours. The gross edge peaks near 1.3 bp a trade, below their 5 bp maker and 10 to 20 bp taker costs. All 24 focal coin-years from 2021 to 2026 were significant, with a mild downward drift for BTC ([arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)).
- STATED: The same paper checks artifacts. Skipping one bar kept 71% of the effect, mid-quote bars left it unchanged, and thin-pair staleness showed a volume slope near zero ([arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)).
- STATED: Farag, Luo, Yarovaya and Zieba study over 100 coins from 2017 to 2022 at five minutes and read the reversal as a payment for providing liquidity that rises with crash and tail risk ([Journal of Banking and Finance, Southampton copy](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/501151/2/1-s2.0-S0378426625000317-main.pdf)). UNKNOWN: whether they report a post-2021 split; I did not find one in what I read.
- STATED: Babiak, Bianchi and co-authors find daily reversal from March 2017 to March 2022 in low-volume pairs (1.26% a day equal-weighted), while value-weighted high-volume pairs give -0.19% (p 0.251). They apply 30 and 40 bp costs ([Lancaster copy](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/172093/1/Babiak_Trading_Volume.pdf), [CERGE-EI working paper](http://www.cerge-ei.cz/pdf/wp/Wp730.pdf)).
- STATED: Borgards and Czudaj find overreactions are common in 12 coins from 30-minute to daily bars ([JIFMIM, RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfin/v65y2020ics1042443120300780.html)). Caporale and Plastun find daily overreactions from 2013 to 2017 ([Brunel working paper](https://www.brunel.ac.uk/economics-and-finance/research/pdf/1801-Jan-GMC-Price-Overreactions-In-The-Cryptocurrency-Market.pdf)). A 2022 Economic Modelling paper on BTC from 2013 to 2020 finds intraday momentum and reversal that change around jumps ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4080253), [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ecofin/v62y2022ics1062940822000833.html)).
- STATED: Kozlowski and co-authors find reversal concentrated in small, illiquid coins ([Applied Economics Letters](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/13504851.2020.1784831)).

Negative or weak results:

- STATED: Zaremba and co-authors find the largest coins show daily momentum, not reversal ([IRFA](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521921002349)).
- STATED: A 2026 practitioner test of a close cousin (BTC hourly fall below 3% on more than twice normal volume, long alts for 48 hours, 72 events) reports +2.33% gross, but alpha of +0.98% with p 0.182, a long-alt short-BTC pair at p 0.28, and "54% of return is BTC beta" ([claude-quant notes](https://github.com/Isk4R1oT/claude-quant/blob/main/research/2026-05-27-liquidation-cascade-and-ofi.md), [Medium write-up](https://medium.com/@tigroblanc/chasing-liquidation-cascade-alpha-in-crypto-how-to-get-299-return-with-sharpe-3-58-322ef625a8d1)).
- STATED: A 2025 to 2026 five-coin test finds BTC +0.10%, ETH -0.12% and SOL 0.00% two hours after a dump, and calls it momentum, not mean reversion. It is low quality ([FrankRKNL](https://github.com/FrankRKNL/crypto-research-published/blob/main/EVENT-DRIVEN-INTRADAY.md)).
- STATED: An hourly Hyperliquid test from 2025 to 2026 finds losers bounce about half as far as winners give back, with a hindsight ranking ([Delphic Alpha](https://delphicalpha.substack.com/p/strategy-hourly-crypto-reversal-what)).
- STATED: An hourly stat-arb study traces its edge to stale prices, larger where liquidity is lower ([bryanvine](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper3.html)). An hourly 2023 to 2025 stat-arb test lost money even at zero cost ([FionaNi](https://github.com/FionaNi-DS/crypto-statistical-arbitrage)).
- STATED: A November 2025 account of a liquidation cascade finds no clean reversion ([Coinchange](https://www.coinchange.io/blog/bitcoins-2-billion-reckoning-how-novembers-liquidations-cascade-exposed-cryptos-structural-fragilities)). An on-chain note says alts fall harder because of leverage ([CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/zh/article/altcoin-washout-6-above-50-day-average)).
- STATED: A study of the October 2025 crash finds mark prices undershot and the intra-minute spread reached 6.79% ([SSRN 6579278](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=6579278&mirid=1&type=2)). BitMEX liquidations averaged 3.51% of long open interest a day in an earlier sample ([arXiv 2102.04591](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.04591)).

What this adds up to:

- STATED: Short reversal after large hourly drops is well documented in BTC gross of costs, and it scales with move size ([Miralles-Quirós](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/314207/1/1841191159.pdf), [De Nicola](https://www.ledgerjournal.org/ojs/ledger/article/download/213/212)).
- STATED: Where costs are treated, the edge shrinks to below cost in liquid pairs or lives in thin pairs ([Kitron and Wengrowicz](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1), [Babiak and co-authors](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/172093/1/Babiak_Trading_Volume.pdf)).
- STATED: Evidence after 2021 is mixed. One 2025 to 2026 study still finds it in every focal coin-year ([arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)), while the small practitioner tests find most of it is BTC beta or none at all ([claude-quant](https://github.com/Isk4R1oT/claude-quant/blob/main/research/2026-05-27-liquidation-cascade-and-ofi.md), [FrankRKNL](https://github.com/FrankRKNL/crypto-research-published/blob/main/EVENT-DRIVEN-INTRADAY.md)).
- UNKNOWN: No published study I found tests our exact form, a coin that fell more than twice as far as BTC, bought unhedged.

## b. How this pattern can fool an hourly backtest

Each item names the trap, a check we could run on search dates only (2020 to 2023), and the data it needs. None of these checks has been run.

1. Bid-ask bounce. STATED: Short reversals in trade prints can come mostly from prints moving between bid and ask, not from prices ([Roll 1984](https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/roll1984.pdf), [Kaul and Nimalendran](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/28320/0000076.pdf)). After a one-sided falling hour the close is likely a sell at the bid, so the drop looks deeper. Our entry is the next hourly open, the very next print, which can also be a sell at the bid; the backtest then buys at the bid. The exit open two hours later is a random side. The gain is about half the spread, and spreads widen in crashes ([SSRN 6579278](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=6579278&mirid=1&type=2)). Check: for signal hours only, reprice each entry at the first buyer-initiated trades in the entry minute, and also at 1, 5 and 15 minutes late. Data: Binance spot aggTrades daily files (they carry a buyer-is-maker flag) and 1-minute klines from data.binance.vision, for signal hours only.
2. Stale or thin prints. STATED: Nonsynchronous trading makes thin assets look like they lag and then catch up ([Lo and MacKinlay, contrarian profits](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w2977/w2977.pdf), [Lo and MacKinlay, nonsynchronous trading](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/8919.pdf)), and one hourly crypto stat-arb edge was a stale-price artifact ([bryanvine](https://bryanvine.github.io/alpha-research/paper3.html)). Check: split net by quintile of trade count and quote volume in the signal bar; require at least one trade in the entry minute; measure the gap between the last trade of the signal bar and the bar end. Data: the trade-count and quote-volume fields of the same Binance hourly and 1-minute klines.
3. Outages and halted matching. STATED: On 2021-05-19 Binance halted retail trading and stopped reporting trades from 13:16 to 13:56 UTC, back-filled the data about a week later, kept its liquidation engine running while clients were locked out, and gave some API users priority ([Baumgartner and Guettler, IWH](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/266345/1/1822049504.pdf)). STATED: Binance spot was down on 2023-03-24 from 11:27 to 14:00 UTC ([Binance CEO blog](https://www.binance.com/en/blog/from-our-ceo/6789340645608890113)), and the archive is missing a BTCUSDT hourly bar that day ([issue 501](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data/issues/501), [issue 505](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data/issues/505)). STATED: Monthly and daily archive files disagree on 2020-12-21 and 2021-09-29, the monthly file has synthetic zero-trade bars, some 2022 corrections never reached the monthly files, and the largest close error is about 205 bp ([issue 475](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data/issues/475)); klines go missing in maintenance ([issue 77](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data/issues/77)), and the archive says files "may be updated" ([binance-public-data](https://github.com/binance/binance-public-data)). STATED: Scheduled Binance upgrades fell on [2020-04-25](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/360042668671), [2020-06-28](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/a9d34695cd9345c7a648a882fcd3bcc0), [2020-11-30](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/f5e4c4bca0e440e8b832494faf74016e), [2020-12-21](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/b05b72a7608943b186b981b4cc733e4e), [2021-04-20](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/69e82a64b2c442b18eb1cf11934b27eb), [2021-04-25](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/849160fe70214641baa6385619595aa1), [2021-08-13](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/92a9a5bc0129427f8e9928c9b7b09836) and [2021-09-29](https://www.binance.com/en/support/announcement/detail/e2f674fc961d48af9b28edd82896607c). Binance futures were down on 2021-05-19 from about 12:40 to 13:10 UTC ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/crypto-traders-to-sue-binance-for-millions-after-may-outage/)). Coinbase was down in the same crashes on [2020-05-10](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2020/05/10/coinbase-suffers-brief-outage-as-bitcoin-tumbles-10-in-30-minutes), [2021-05-19](https://postmortem.io/incidents/coinbase--2021-05-19--market-volatility-traffic-spike-outage/) and [2022-05-12](https://www.theverge.com/2022/5/12/23068996/coinbase-outage-withdrawal-cryptocurrency-bitcoin-price-crash). STATED: The search left 122 wrong-close rows missing ([doc 152](152_plan_70_coin_sweep_search.md)). Check: drop every trade whose signal, entry or exit hour falls within 3 hours of a listed halt, upgrade or wrong-close row, and rerun; report the share of total net from the top 5 UTC dates. Data: the halt list above, the wrong-close list from the search loader, and the daily archive files to compare against monthly ones.
4. Prints no order could reach. STATED: Crash prints can sit far from other venues; on 2021-05-19 the Binance price gap to peers averaged $375 against $53 in normal times ([IWH](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/266345/1/1822049504.pdf)). A US trader would hold a Coinbase contract tied to a US index, not a Binance USDT print. Check: for each entry and exit, compare the Binance open with the same-minute Coinbase Exchange USD 1-minute candle, and redo net on Coinbase prices; also measure USDT against USD in those minutes. Data: Coinbase Exchange public candles (api.exchange.coinbase.com, 1-minute, coins listed there in 2020 to 2023) and a USDT-USD series.
5. BTC beta. STATED: In the closest outside test, more than half of the return was BTC beta ([claude-quant](https://github.com/Isk4R1oT/claude-quant/blob/main/research/2026-05-27-liquidation-cascade-and-ofi.md)), and BTC itself rebounds after large hourly drops ([Miralles-Quirós](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/314207/1/1841191159.pdf)). Our rule holds no hedge ([doc 152](152_plan_70_coin_sweep_search.md)). Check: subtract beta times the BTC return over the same two hours, beta from the prior 30 days, and test what is left. Data: the same Binance hourly bars.

## d. My view

1. More likely an artifact plus BTC beta than a clean payment for liquidity, though some real rebound is likely in it.
2. For: large hourly drops do reverse gross of cost in many samples, and both our halves were positive.
3. Against: entry at the next open print gains a crash-wide half spread, the top 1% of trades carry about a third of the net, and the rule is unhedged.
4. Also against: crash hours are when Binance halted, back-filled or misprinted, and the US contracts we would use have per-contract fees of 22 to 41 bp a side on DOT, AVAX and SHIB.
5. The test that would move me most: reprice every search trade at reachable prices (first buyer-initiated aggTrades or same-minute Coinbase prints), minus BTC beta, with halt hours dropped; if it stays above cost, I would take it seriously.
