# After the peak: what followed the entry signal

MEASURED. Ledger 152 is a look, not a test. There is no pass mark. It cannot support a method. It can only shape a later plan, which needs its own ledger row and unseen data. Only the already viewed Plan 13 list and its archive inputs were used; the held-back list was not read.

Each Monday's hot five were ranked on spot exactly on the Plan 17 clock. Live perpetuals used the dated identity, full hourly bars, executable volume and recent funding clocks. We measured every eligible entry signal, even while a live strategy would already have a position. A first signal without an exact next open remains first but has no measured entry. The study ends at 2026-10-26T00:00:00+00:00.

Price move means the perpetual's percentage move from the next hour's executable open to the close after the stated number of elapsed hours; negative is favorable to a short. The fixed look-forward can cross a weekly or year boundary, not an identity fence or the archive cutoff. A missing horizon is dropped from that horizon only.

For the ±5% race we watch the first 72 hourly closes after entry. A gap, dead spell, fence or cutoff before a first hit makes the order unknown, not a tie or a loss. Next-open overshoot is measured from the threshold in basis points of entry; positive means farther below the downside threshold or farther above the upside threshold. A negative overshoot means a rebound inside the threshold. Missing exact next opens have no fill statistic.

The comparison has 200 draws (seed 152). Each draw samples the same number of eligible entry hours without replacement in each observed coin-week, using the same old-bar requirements and entry-open screen but not the dip condition. Signal hours are not excluded. Sampled hours are sorted before applying the corresponding first/later labels. Random rows pool all draws; they are not independent market observations. Numbers below are descriptive, not trades with costs, and are not a result for US execution.
