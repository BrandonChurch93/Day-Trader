# Evidence on LLM Trading Agents: Live Performance, Failure Modes, and Architecture

## Executive Summary

Empirical evidence through November 2026 does not support deploying autonomous large language models (LLMs) as end-to-end day traders. In live, real-capital competitions such as Nof1 Alpha Arena, 31 of 38 AI model accounts lost money across crypto perpetuals and US equities, paying roughly $43,000 in fees on $380,000 of starting capital [MEASURED] ([TradingAgentStack Alpha Arena Audit](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/)). In prospective, look-ahead-free academic benchmarks run in 2025 and 2026, zero of 91 controlled agent runs achieved statistically significant four-factor alpha, with mean annualized alpha of -2.3% and returns driven primarily by market beta [MEASURED] ([Wang 2026, arXiv:2611.19530](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)). Spectacular backtest Sharpe ratios (2.0 to 8.2) reported by early multi-agent papers collapse by 50% to 72% once evaluated after the model's pretraining cutoff or across multi-decade survivorship-free universes [MEASURED] ([Profit Mirage, arXiv:2510.07920](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920); [FINSABER, KDD 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078)). LLMs demonstrate genuine utility in structured text extraction, news triage, earnings-call analysis, strategy code generation, and post-trade review, but fail at numeric time-series forecasting, low-latency execution, deterministic sizing, and adversarial security.

## 1. Live and Forward-Tested Benchmarks and Competitions

Live and contamination-free benchmarks launched in 2025–2026 provide the clearest test of autonomous LLM trading because models cannot memorize future prices. Across almost every real-money or strict post-cutoff test, most models either lost capital or failed to generate statistically significant alpha.

## 3. Look-Ahead Bias and Memorization in LLM Backtests

Backtesting an LLM on any date prior to its pretraining cutoff is methodologically invalid because frontier models encode historical prices, macroeconomic releases, and corporate events directly in their weights.

- Exact historical recall (Lopez-Lira, Tang & Zhu 2025): Testing GPT-4o (October 2023 cutoff) at temperature 0, the model recalled daily S&P 500 closing levels from 1990–2023 with 0.61% mean absolute percent error (MAPE) and 80.58% directional accuracy without context; after its cutoff, MAPE jumped to 16.87% and directional accuracy collapsed to 45.70% [MEASURED] ([The Memorization Problem, arXiv:2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)). For recent pre-cutoff nonfarm payrolls (2014–2023), MAPE was 0.00% vs 97.44% post-cutoff [MEASURED] ([The Memorization Problem, arXiv:2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)).
- Prompting and masking do not fix leakage: Instructing GPT-4o to "use only data before December 2010" in the system prompt still yielded 98.0% threshold accuracy and 0.05% MAE on 2011–2023 GDP growth [MEASURED] ([The Memorization Problem, arXiv:2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)). When given anonymized earnings-call opening statements, GPT-4o still identified Apple, Meta, and Microsoft 100% of the time and nailed Apple's exact quarter-and-year in 93.24% of calls [MEASURED] ([The Memorization Problem, arXiv:2504.14765](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14765v2)).
- Look-ahead bias vs. the distraction effect (Glasserman & Lin 2023/2024): Testing GPT-3.5 on 129,431 scraped headlines and 181,908 Reuters headlines, Glasserman and Lin found two distinct distortions: look-ahead bias (knowing subsequent returns, strongest for large caps) and a "distraction effect" where general background knowledge of a named company interferes with reading the specific headline [MEASURED] ([Glasserman & Lin, arXiv:2309.17322](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17322); [WallStreetDebug](https://wallstreetdebug.com/articles/lookahead-bias-ai-backtests-explained/)). Anonymizing company names reduced market beta and counterintuitively improved headline sentiment accuracy [MEASURED] ([OpenResearch Summary](https://openresearch.sh/abs/2309.17322)).
- The "Profit Mirage" across agent frameworks: Comparing Q2–Q3 2021 (pre-cutoff) against Q3–Q4 2024 (post-cutoff) with GPT-4o, all five tested frameworks suffered 51.48% to 62.23% Sharpe ratio decay and 50.18% to 71.85% total return decay [MEASURED] ([Profit Mirage, arXiv:2510.07920](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920)). Under counterfactual perturbations where inputs were altered, 69% to 82% of agent predictions remained unchanged, proving they were repeating memorized trajectories rather than reacting to inputs [MEASURED] ([Profit Mirage, arXiv:2510.07920](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920)).
- Long-horizon collapse (FINSABER, KDD 2026): Re-testing FinMem and FinAgent over 20 years (2004–2024) and 100+ S&P 500 symbols including delisted stocks and commissions showed Buy & Hold significantly beat both LLM agents in every bias-mitigated setup (paired t-test p <= 0.0117), and neither agent produced significant alpha (p > 0.34) [MEASURED] ([FINSABER Slides](https://waylonli.com/files/finsaber-slides.pdf); [FINSABER, arXiv:2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078)). Both agents were overly conservative in bull markets and overly aggressive in bear markets [MEASURED] ([FINSABER, arXiv:2505.07078](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078)).

What to do instead: Never trust pre-cutoff LLM backtests. Validate any LLM-assisted component using strict post-cutoff forward paper trading on live feeds, point-in-time timestamped news (replayed strictly by publication time, since calendar-date joins alone flipped an earnings strategy from +32.0 bps/trade to -6.7 bps/trade in `rta-bench-1`), realistic commissions/slippage/impact, multiple random seeds, and blind/anonymized replay checks with decision-day cut randomization [MEASURED] ([Wang 2026, arXiv:2611.19530](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)).

## 4. Where LLMs Demonstrably Add Value vs. Where They Are Weak

### Proven Strengths

- News and headline sentiment classification (with strict caveats): In their Journal of Financial Economics study using post-cutoff headlines, Lopez-Lira and Tang showed GPT-4 captures the direction of the immediate (non-tradable) market reaction with ~90% daily portfolio hit rates (93.3% overnight, 88.8% intraday) and statistically predicts 1–2 day subsequent drift, concentrated in small-cap stocks and negative news [MEASURED] ([Lopez-Lira & Tang, JFE 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X26001066); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/chatgpt-forecast-stock-price/)). Crucially, accuracy on any single headline prompt was only ~51%, meaning value comes from diversified statistical aggregation, not single-stock bets, and high turnover can erase the spread once trading costs exceed ~2.7 bps per side [MEASURED] ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2023/04/21/chat-gpt-market-predicting-stock-price-movements/); [Wang 2026, arXiv:2611.19530](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)).
- Earnings call and filing analysis: LLMs outperform lexicon-based methods at extracting segment-level tone, management evasion/dodge scores, and target-specific stance (debt, EPS, sales) from 10-K MD&A sections and earnings transcripts [MEASURED] ([Nature Scientific Reports 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-67608-5); [Santa Clara Practicum, arXiv:2505.16090](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16090)). In ECC Analyzer, directly asking an LLM to predict stock returns performed like random guessing, but feeding LLM-extracted transcript features into downstream models significantly improved volatility and Value-at-Risk (VaR) prediction [MEASURED] ([ECC Analyzer, arXiv:2404.18470](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.18470v1)).
- News triage, event classification, and structured extraction: LLMs reliably filter noisy feeds, classify corporate actions/SEC 8-K events, and convert unstructured text into typed JSON features for deterministic downstream rules [MEASURED] ([FinNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.finnlp-2.23.pdf)).
- Offline strategy research and code generation: Frameworks like Microsoft's R&D-Agent-Quant (on Qlib), FactorEngine, and QuantHarness use LLMs offline to generate hypotheses, write executable Python factor code, and run audited backtests inside deterministic engines — keeping the LLM completely off the live execution path [MEASURED] ([R&D-Agent-Quant, arXiv:2505.15155](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155); [QuantHarness, arXiv:2610.26101](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.26101v1?ref=blog.algorover.ai)).
- Post-trade journaling and audit review: Summarizing realized fills, comparing execution against written rules, and flagging behavioral drift require language synthesis without latency or real-time risk.

### Proven Weaknesses

- Numeric time-series forecasting: A landmark NeurIPS 2024 study ("Are Language Models Actually Useful for Time Series Forecasting?") proved that pretrained LLMs perform no better than simple models trained from scratch, fail to capture sequential dependencies, and do not help in few-shot settings despite massive compute cost [MEASURED] ([Tan et al., NeurIPS 2024](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/6ed5bf446f59e2c6646d23058c86424b-Paper-Conference.pdf)).
- Inference latency: Cloud LLM calls take 500 ms to 5,000 ms (and scale to 6.13 s under concurrency), whereas traditional algorithmic execution operates in 1–100 ms and HFT in microseconds [MEASURED] ([LLM Output Drift, arXiv:2511.07585](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07585); [arXiv:2604.03888](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03888v1)). In `rta-bench-1`, the bulk of earnings/news price discovery occurred within the first hour (+0.40%), leaving no statistically significant edge by the next open [MEASURED] ([Wang 2026, arXiv:2611.19530](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)).
- Nondeterminism and output drift: In a 2025 financial-workflow study, smaller models (7B–8B) reached 100% consistency at T = 0, but a 120B model achieved only 12.5% output consistency even at T = 0 [MEASURED] ([LLM Output Drift, arXiv:2511.07585](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07585)). In `rta-bench-1`, identical models on identical periods differed by 5.2 percentage points in total return purely across random seeds, and by 5.4 percentage points on reruns with the same seed [MEASURED] ([Wang 2026, arXiv:2611.19530](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)). AlphaForgeBench similarly documented irrational action flipping across adjacent steps even under deterministic decoding [MEASURED] ([AlphaForgeBench, ACM 2026](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3770855.3817500)).
- Hallucinated numbers and sycophancy: LLMs fabricate technical indicator readings, miscalculate position sizes, and mirror conviction implied by user prompts [MEASURED] ([ReignEdge Handbook](https://www.reignedge.com/library/llm-trading-handbook); [Beneat.ai](https://beneat.ai/blog/why-your-ai-trading-bot-is-probably-hallucinating)).
- Overtrading and fee burn: In Alpha Arena Season 1, Gemini 2.5 Pro executed 1,286 fills ($1,347 in fees on $10,000 capital) and lost 57.7%; in Season 1.5, Qwen3 Max fired 1,418 trades under a single prompt vs 158 for Grok 4.20 [MEASURED] ([TradingAgentStack Alpha Arena Audit](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/); [Briefs.co](https://www.briefs.co/news/public-ai-trading-contests-largely-lose-money-and-raise-new/)).

## 6. Best-Practice Architecture Consensus and Tiered Model Routing

### The Deterministic-Core / LLM-Periphery Consensus

Practitioner and academic literature in 2026 converges on a strict separation of responsibilities: the LLM acts as an analyst, event classifier, hypothesis proposer, or risk reviewer, while a deterministic code engine owns market data arithmetic, order generation, position sizing, risk limits, and broker execution [MEASURED] ([ReignEdge Handbook](https://www.reignedge.com/library/llm-trading-handbook); [The Alpha Illusion, arXiv:2605.16895](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895)).

Key architectural rules:
- Zero unbounded order authority: The LLM outputs structured JSON (e.g., thesis, catalyst classification, regime flag, or proposed order ticket) validated against strict schemas; it never holds raw withdrawal keys or unrestricted order-submission tools.
- Hard-coded deterministic risk gates: Position caps, leverage limits, per-order notional limits, daily loss limits, max drawdown kill switches, symbol allowlists, and rate limiters must be enforced in deterministic code outside the LLM because a probabilistic model cannot enforce a hard limit [MEASURED] ([ReignEdge Handbook](https://www.reignedge.com/library/llm-trading-handbook)).
- Human-in-the-loop (HITL) staging: Start in paper mode, progress to draft-then-approve live orders (HITL), and only automate narrowly scoped deterministic rules after statistically meaningful forward paper performance.
- Separate broker state from agent memory: Always pull balances, open orders, and positions directly from the broker API rather than trusting an agent's conversation history or vector memory [MEASURED] ([arXiv:2512.02261](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.02261)).

### Evidence on Tiered Model Cascades and Routing

The user's proposed tiered design (cheap small models monitoring continuously, escalating only important events to an expensive frontier model) is well-supported by cost-efficiency literature, with one critical multi-step caveat:

- FrugalGPT (Chen et al. 2023/2024): Sequential LLM cascades matched GPT-4 accuracy with up to 98.3% cost reduction on financial HEADLINES, 73.3% on OVERRULING, and 59.2% on COQA, or improved accuracy by up to 4% at the same cost [MEASURED] ([FrugalGPT, arXiv:2305.05176](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.05176)).
- RouteLLM (Ong et al., ICLR 2025): Pre-generation routing between GPT-4 and Mixtral-8x7B retained 95% of GPT-4 performance on MT-Bench while sending only 13.4% of queries to GPT-4 (3.66x cost reduction; >85% savings on MT-Bench, 45% on MMLU, 35% on GSM8K), with router overhead under 0.4% [MEASURED] ([RouteLLM, arXiv:2406.18665](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18665); [arXiv:2611.15911](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15911)).
- Multi-step workflow caveat (AgentRouter, ICML 2026): Single-turn routers like FrugalGPT (44% savings) and RouteLLM (31% savings) degrade in multi-step agentic workflows because a subtly wrong cheap-model summary at Step 1 corrupts the frontier model's context at Step 2 [MEASURED] ([AgentRouter, ICML 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.22951)). Trajectory-aware routing achieved 72% cost reduction with <3% quality loss [MEASURED] ([AgentRouter, ICML 2026](https://icml.cc/media/icml-2026/Slides/74502.pdf)).
- Calibration matters (UCCI 2026): Calibrated uncertainty thresholds cut production structured-extraction inference costs by 31% while beating uncalibrated FrugalGPT thresholds by 8% at equal F1 [MEASURED] ([UCCI, arXiv:2605.18796](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18796)).

For trading, this means the cheap screener should pass raw primary evidence (exact headline, timestamp, ticker, price/volume delta) alongside its triage tag to the frontier model — never just its own paraphrase — so screening errors do not poison escalation.

## Implications for Our Build

Based on the empirical evidence above, do not build an autonomous LLM that watches price bars and submits live day trades on its own. Instead, use a hybrid architecture that puts Perplexity's tiered models where LLMs have proven edge and keeps all execution, math, and risk enforcement in deterministic code:

1. Deterministic code owns the entire money path: Write a deterministic execution and risk engine (in Python, connected to Alpaca paper/live API for US equities and Coinbase Advanced / CCXT for crypto) that computes all technical indicators, enforces hard position caps, max daily loss limits, max order notional, symbol allowlists, PDT/margin checks, and a one-click kill switch. Never expose `update_account_config`, withdrawal scopes, or raw wallet private keys to an LLM.
2. Cheap small-model tier = event screener and structured extractor: Run periodic lightweight Perplexity/small-model jobs to scan breaking news, SEC 8-K filings, earnings transcripts, and crypto protocol announcements, outputting strict JSON (event type, polarity, novelty, source provenance, and raw headline/timestamps). Pass the raw evidence — not just a summary — up the cascade to prevent context corruption.
3. Expensive large-model tier = infrequent thesis reviewer and regime auditor: Escalate only high-signal catalysts (e.g., major earnings surprises, regulatory events, macro shocks) to a frontier reasoning model to produce a structured trade thesis, bull/bear risk checklist, and invalidation criteria — or to veto a deterministic rule when a qualitative catalyst (such as a buyout or hack) invalidates technical assumptions.
4. Never use LLMs for intraday price forecasting or per-bar signals: For any intraday or high-turnover crypto/equity strategy, use deterministic rules or traditional ML (e.g., trend-following, XGBoost/LightGBM) trained offline. Use the LLM offline (like R&D-Agent-Quant / QuantHarness) to help write, test, and audit strategy code, and for end-of-day post-trade journaling.
5. Enforce a strict forward-testing gate: Ignore any pre-cutoff LLM backtest. Run the full pipeline in paper trading (e.g., Alpaca Paper + CCXT sandbox) for at least 60–90 trading days with realistic fees and slippage logged against SPY/BTC buy-and-hold and simple trend-following baselines, requiring human approval per order (draft-then-submit) during initial live deployment with a small, ring-fenced capital allocation.
