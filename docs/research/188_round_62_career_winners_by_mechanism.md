# Idea round 62: career winners sorted by the mechanism that paid them

Date: 2026-11-26 (Idaho evening; second-checked same day). Scope: people and firms with a long, outside-checked record of trading gains without inside information, sorted by what paid them, and what (if anything) a $500 to $2,000 US account run by a slow AI agent can take from each. Adds to `docs/research/17_verified_traders_and_their_methods.md`, `docs/141_idea_round_40.md`, `docs/research/169_what_successful_small_account_traders_look_for.md` and `docs/research/21_systematic_funds_and_published_rules.md`. No price file was opened and no repo script was run.

## Plain answer

1. The best-checked long careers were paid by five things: a paid role in the middle of trades (Virtu, Jane Street, Jump), hedged mispricing in derivatives (Thorp), breadth of many weak short-horizon predictors with careful cost control (Renaissance, Thorp's later stat arb), patience with sellers who must sell or holders who must wait (Buffett workouts, Greenblatt spin-offs), and a model blended with the market price inside a pool too small for big money (Benter, Woods).
2. The first three need speed, shorting, leverage or special access that this account does not have; even the professionals lost about half of gross to costs (Thorp, STATED), so their small versions are what this project already ran and mostly failed (reversal, funding carry, market making).
3. Patience with forced sellers is the one mechanism that fits a small, slow, reading-heavy agent, and it is where this project's two counted reachable equity-side passes came from (Replay 25 TLT month-end, Replay 48 muni CEF January); nearly every workout class has already been tested or closed on count.
4. Second check overruled both proposed next steps to DROP: operating-company plans of liquidation are a multi-year illiquid wind-down outside our hold horizon, lack post-announcement net-of-cost evidence, and cannot plausibly clear 30 listed events a year or be counted from EDGAR form types alone; a Benter-style taker blend on Kalshi BTC contracts contradicts `docs/research/53_prediction_markets_checked.md` (where takers lose and 0 of 527 ForecastBench entries beat shown market prices), while the maker side is already tracked in `docs/QUEUE.md` rows 22, 44 and 53.
5. Honest result of this round: nothing new to price-test or count.

### Ed Thorp and Princeton Newport Partners (warrants, convertibles, then statistical arbitrage)

- What paid him: STATED, [Thorp, Quantitative Finance Review 2003](https://catalogimages.wiley.com/images/db/pdf/0470023511.excerpt.pdf): a pricing model for warrants and convertibles years before others had one (he writes he guessed the Black-Scholes formula in late 1967 and used it from late 1967 on), hedged with short stock; Princeton Newport Partners started in 1969 and made about $250 million for its partners, with half or more from convertibles. Who lost: holders who priced warrants and convertibles by rule of thumb. Mechanism: modelling more than others, plus shorting and leverage.
- Later stat arb: STATED, same source: a short-term price-reversal study in December 1979 or January 1980 gave about 20% annualized returns before commissions and market impact (with about 20% annualized standard deviation) for long the most-down and short the most-up stocks; his August 1992 through October 2002 fund ran typically about 200 long and 200 short positions, 10-day average turnover, about 10,000 separate bets a year, gross about 50% a year cut to about 26% net before performance fee by commissions and market impact, and about 20% net to investors (about $350 million total profit). He writes that competition tends to drive down returns.
- Record check: Princeton Newport's monthly record (about 15.1% annualized net of fees over 19 years, 3 losing months in 230) is STATED only in a secondary retelling of Jack Schwager's book ([Collateral](https://www.collateral.com/blog/edward-thorp-hedge-fund-edge-decay)), so it is a claim with no audit cited. The firm was forced to liquidate due to the financial burdens of a RICO investigation; six people were convicted in 1989, their racketeering convictions were overturned in 1991, and Thorp and other principals were eventually cleared of wrongdoing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_Newport_Partners)). Verified: no auditor or filing was opened.
- Does the source still exist: STATED by Thorp that competition drives returns down; UNKNOWN how much is left in listed warrants today.
- Small AI version: long-only short-term reversal in stocks or coins. Covered: Round 17 skip (short-term reversal), Round 30 skip (1 to 3 day crypto reversal), crypto dip cards E09/E11, Plan 75 as the trend side. What is different now: nothing; INFERENCE from Thorp's 50% to 26% gross-to-net drop is that a small account paying retail spread on every trade starts further behind.
- Can a $500 to $2,000 account place it: long side yes; the hedge needs borrow and margin. Dollars a year: UNKNOWN.
- Strongest reason false or gone: Thorp himself; commissions and market impact ate about half the gross even for him.
- Verdict: DROP (covered).

### Renaissance Technologies (what the public record shows)

- What paid them: STATED, [Senate PSI basket options report (2014)](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/imo/media/doc/REPORT-Abuse%20of%20Structured%20Financial%20Products%20(Basket%20Options)%20(7-22-14,%20updated%209-30-14).pdf): Renaissance's own estimate of 100,000 to 150,000 trades a day with each of two banks (Deutsche Bank and Barclays), an SEC examination finding of about 129 million trade orders from April 1, 2008 to March 31, 2009 across nearly 10,000 financial instruments in 18 countries, positions held up to three months 86.97% of the time (rounded to 87% in report text, 2007 to Q1 2009), and basket-option leverage up to 20 to 1 at Barclays and 18 to 1 at Deutsche Bank (average 11.7 to 1 and 12.9 to 1 in 2005 to 2006; also covered in `docs/research/21_systematic_funds_and_published_rules.md`).
- Return figures: STATED in [Cornell Capital's summary](https://www.cornell-capital.com/uploads/papers/medallion-fund.pdf), reproducing Appendix 1 of Gregory Zuckerman's book: Medallion averaged 66.07% annual gross return (after trading costs) and 39.20% net from 1988 through 2018, growing from $20 million to $10 billion. The paper does not cite an audit or independent verification, so the return table is a claim.
- Mechanism: breadth (millions of tiny bets, each right a little more than half the time), cost modelling, and leverage. Who lost: counterparties on the other side of each tiny trade, mostly slower traders and liquidity takers.
- Does it still exist: UNKNOWN from public records; the fund is closed to outsiders and capacity-capped, which INFERENCE suggests means the edge does not scale.
- Small AI version: there is none worth testing. Breadth needs thousands of trades a year; at retail spreads each trade must clear 20 to 120 bp (Measurement 132 and `docs/research/186_round_61_thin_cheap_venue.md`). Covered: Round 17 skip, doc 186, Plans 69, 70 and 71 sweeps.
- Can the account place it: no shorting at scale, no 10x to 20x basket-option leverage, no sub-second speed.
- Strongest reason false or out of reach: the edge per trade is too small to survive retail spreads and fees.
- Verdict: DROP (covered).

### Buffett Partnership "workouts" and Joel Greenblatt's Gotham years

- What paid Buffett: STATED, [1963 partnership letter](https://buffett-letters.com/en/02-letters/partnership/1963-Partnership-Letter-(3)): workouts are securities with a timetable arising from publicly announced corporate activities such as sell-outs, mergers, reorganizations and spin-offs (liquidation appears in the Texas National Petroleum example rather than the general definition list), with risk tied to whether the corporate event occurs rather than general market direction; Peat, Marwick, Mitchell & Co. audited the 1963 partnership financial statements (verified at the fund level). STATED, [1965 partnership letter](https://buffett-letters.com/en/02-letters/partnership/1965-Partnership-Letter-(3)): about $6 million average in workouts earned about $1,410,000, about 23.5% on an all-equity basis, with borrowed money used and over half of workout earnings from one situation; he wrote he thought it unlikely that a really interesting rate of return could be earned consistently on large sums in this business under present conditions. STATED, [1968 partnership letter](https://buffett-letters.com/en/02-letters/partnership/1968-Partnership-Letter-(3)): $18,980,602 average in workouts earned $7,317,128; large arbitrage houses may do 50 or more workouts per year.
- What paid Greenblatt: STATED, [The Hedge Fund Journal](https://thehedgefundjournal.com/detachment-rewarded-at-gotham-asset-management/): compounded at 50% a year gross from 1985 to 1994 (34.4% net), noting that around 30 companies in Gotham's universe might be in the process of a spin-out at any one time and that resulting stubs (often 10% of parent market cap) can be too small and illiquid for a substantial investor. No auditor or audit is named, so this is a claim. STATED, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Greenblatt): Gotham returned about $500 million of outside partner capital in January 1995 (and reports 1985 to 1994 returns as 50% before incentive allocation / 30% net, showing secondary accounts do not agree on the net figure).
- Who loses and why: holders who must or will not wait: index funds dumping spin-offs, funds that cannot hold a small stub, tax sellers, holders who do not read the documents.
- Does it still exist: partly. MEASURED in this project: muni CEF January rebound (`docs/research/103_replay_48_muni_cef_january.md`) and TLT month-end (`docs/research/69_replay_25_treasury_month_end.md`) passed; odd-lot cash tenders failed 4 of 6 marks (`docs/research/97_replay_21_odd_lot_tenders.md`, 14 trades vs 30 required); CEF liquidations closed on count (Plan 50 / Queue 88, 22 strict events vs 30); post-spin-off rebound closed on synthetic power before prices (Plan 55 / Queue 58); SPACs under trust and small merger arb skipped (Rounds 25, 33 and 39); ETF cash liquidations blocked on free primary payout feed (Queue 95, `docs/research/114_row95_etf_liquidation_payout_feasibility.md`).
- Small AI version: a clerk that reads every filing with a timetable and acts only when the price gap clears the cost. That matches what Buffett and Greenblatt describe, but almost every reachable US sub-class has already been tested, closed on count, or blocked on payout data.
- Verdict: DROP (covered across Replays 21, 25, 48, Plans 50 and 55, Rounds 25, 33 and 39, and Queue 95; operating-company liquidations audited separately below and dropped).

### Operating companies with a plan of liquidation and an estimated distribution (NEW; overruled to DROP)

- What it is: an operating company (not a fund or SPAC) adopts a plan of complete liquidation and dissolution, files a proxy with a board-estimated range of liquidating distributions per share, and sells assets and pays out over months to years. Example STATED, [Stratus Properties DEFM14A (2026)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/885508/000119312526174846/d57844ddefm14a.htm): board-approved Plan of Complete Liquidation and Dissolution with estimated liquidating distributions of about $29.73 to $37.69 per share, aiming to finish asset sales within 24 months after stockholder approval (low end assumes 36 months), and warning that reduced liquidity may cause the stock to trade at a significant discount to ultimate value. Who loses: holders who want cash now, index and mandate funds that exit a wind-down, and buyers who cannot hold a delisted shell or non-transferable liquidating trust claim.
- Why an AI can and a person or fund won't: reading the liquidation analysis, reserve, timing and tax notes in each proxy is tedious; positions are small, illiquid, and end in a non-trading claim. Strongest counter: specialist activists and event-driven funds already bid up cash-rich wind-downs (UNKNOWN how much discount remains after announcement).
- Evidence: STATED, [Kim and Schatzberg, Journal of Financial Economics 1987](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/26484/0000020.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y): 73 NYSE/AMEX firms that announced liquidation from July 1963 through December 1981 and completed liquidating dividend payouts before December 31, 1982 (conditioned on completed liquidations); three-day excess return (day -2 to 0) was +13.53% (z = 50.07) at the press announcement and +2.84% (z = 7.95) at shareholder confirmation; Table 5 reports a +30.09% holding-period prediction error from press date to confirmation date on N = 50 (z = 13.15). That study is 40 years old, before costs, conditioned on completion, and does NOT report a separate return for a buyer who enters after the press announcement (post-announcement return after costs is UNKNOWN). Verified track record: none found for this class.
- Can the account place it: yes during the listed period at Alpaca or any $0-commission broker (minimum one share, no borrow), but holding through a 24 to 36 month wind-down violates this project's minutes-to-weeks horizon, locks up scarce capital on a $500 to $2,000 account, and exposes the account to delisting, OTC illiquidity, non-transferable liquidating trust units, and UNKNOWN mandatory-liquidation handling fees at Alpaca. Dollars a year: UNKNOWN (and likely tiny on $500 to $2,000).
- Data and why EDGAR form types alone cannot run a clean count: MEASURED in round 62 that the Stratus DEFM14A and EDGAR quarterly `form.idx` returned HTTP 200 and three XBRL frame guesses returned 404. However, EDGAR form types alone (`DEFM14A`, `DEF 14A`) do NOT separate operating-company liquidations from mergers, SPACs (Round 25 already counted 477 SPAC `DEFM14A` issuer-years alone), or routine proxies, and many operating-company liquidation plans are filed on `DEF 14A` rather than `DEFM14A`. Because SEC full-text search (EFTS) is blocked from this machine, filtering thousands of proxy filings per year from `form.idx` is not feasible under the 40-request host cap.
- Independent trades a year: UNKNOWN, and 30 listed operating companies a year is INFERENCE-implausible given Kim and Schatzberg's ~4 completed NYSE/AMEX liquidations per year (73 over 18.5 years) and this project's Plan 50 CEF liquidation closure (22 strict events in 10 years).
- Strongest reason false or gone: the bulk of the documented gain occurs at the announcement (STATED), post-announcement return after costs is UNKNOWN, the 24 to 36 month payout tail sits outside our minutes-to-weeks hold horizon, and event count is almost certainly far below 30 per year.
- Verdict: DROP (second check overrules the scout's COUNT FIRST).

### Bill Benter and Alan Woods (Hong Kong pari-mutuel; Kalshi taker blend overruled to DROP)

- What paid them: STATED, [Benter 1994](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/decision/1994-benter.pdf): a fundamental horse-racing model combined in a second-stage logit with the public odds; public odds alone scored pseudo-R² 0.1218, his fundamental model alone scored 0.1245 (already stronger than public odds), and the combination scored 0.1396 (a 0.0178 lift over public odds). About 470 races a year, average track take about 19%, conservative fractional Kelly; 4 of 5 seasons profitable, one losing about 20% of starting capital. Pool size was "Perhaps the most serious and inescapable limitation on profitability": realistic maximum expected profit about 0.25% to 0.5% of total per-race turnover for an infinite bankroll (0.1% to 0.2% for a startup). He warns that published competent computer ratings would likely make the market efficient to such predictions. Self-report: a claim. STATED, [Bloomberg (2018)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code): Benter called using public odds as a starting point his "single most important innovation"; the model later tracked more than 120 variables per horse.
- Woods: STATED, [Bloomberg (2018)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code): Benter and Woods began together in Hong Kong in 1985 and lost $120,000 of a $150,000 stake in the first season before splitting; Woods made $10 million in 1994 to 1995; a book (In the Shadow of the Noonday Gun) quoted Woods's will listing assets of A$939,172,372.51. STATED, [Queensland Bridge Association obituary](https://qldbridge.com.au/players/alan-woods): Woods's syndicate was estimated at about 2% of Hong Kong Jockey Club turnover in 2006 to 2007 (the obituary cites a $670 million fortune and does not mention the will). Not audited trading records: claims.
- Rebates: STATED for modern US tracks, [Thoroughbred Daily News](https://www.thoroughbreddailynews.com/computer-assisted-wagering-anatomy-of-a-deal/): computer teams receive rebates roughly equal to takeout minus host fee, up to 13% on a 20%-takeout / 7%-host-fee deal; [CHRB Q&A](https://www.chrb.ca.gov/misc_docs/Computer_Assisted_Wagering.pdf) says Santa Anita targets around 20% of handle from computer-assisted wagering teams. That is a paid role by volume, not reachable by a small bettor. UNKNOWN whether Benter or Woods received rebates in the 1990s; Benter's 1994 paper does not mention rebates.
- Who loses: recreational bettors paying the full take.
- Does it still exist: STATED by [TDN on HKJC](https://www.thoroughbreddailynews.com/computer-assisted-wagering-different-worlds-different-fixes/): the Hong Kong Jockey Club charges computer teams a 19% host fee on the 25%-take Triple Trio, with its executive director stating any rebate is "likely zero or maybe 2 or 3% at the most", and professional turnover dropped about 8% in 2023/24.
- Small AI version and why Kalshi BTC taker fails: In this project's asset list there is no pari-mutuel pool market. The scout proposed a Benter-style taker blend on Kalshi daily BTC threshold contracts. Second check overrules this to DROP for three reasons already established in `docs/research/53_prediction_markets_checked.md` and `docs/QUEUE.md`: (1) Benter's fundamental model already beat public horse odds on its own (0.1245 vs 0.1218) against recreational horse bettors, whereas Kalshi BTC threshold contracts are continuous limit order books arbitraged against live BTC spot, futures and options markets; we have no outside BTC probability model shown to beat liquid options pricing; (2) `docs/research/53_prediction_markets_checked.md` documents that Kalshi takers pay `0.07 * p * (1 - p)` plus spread and lost heavily across published studies (-31.46% in GWU 2021 to April 2025, -16.0% to -30.8% across six AI models in Prediction Arena, and 0 of 527 ForecastBench entries beating the shown market price); (3) the reachable patient maker side on Kalshi BTC 5 p.m. thresholds is ALREADY tracked in `docs/QUEUE.md` rows 22, 44 and 53 (and `docs/research/74_round_10_forced_flow_ideas.md`), still blocked on an owner Kalshi account and Kalshi's data terms (`docs/research/187_data_sources_for_an_ai_desk.md`).
- Verdict: DROP as a taker blend (second check overrules the scout's WATCH; maker side remains covered in Queue rows 22, 44 and 53).

### Market makers with public filings: Virtu, Jane Street, Jump

- Virtu: STATED, [Virtu S-1 (2014)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1592386/000104746914002070/a2218589zs-1.htm): one losing trading day in 1,238 NYSE trading days (excluding holidays and half days) from January 1, 2009 through December 31, 2013 on Adjusted Net Trading Income (a non-GAAP metric that deducts brokerage, exchange and clearance fees but excludes operating costs such as payroll and technology, and includes pre-acquisition Madison Tyler Holdings before July 8, 2011); 2013 Adjusted Net Trading Income averaged $1.645 million per day (~$1.6 million across 252 days); made markets in over 10,000 securities and financial instruments on over 210 venues in 30 countries. Deloitte & Touche LLP audited the 2011 to 2013 GAAP consolidated financial statements (verified at the GAAP firm level; the daily non-GAAP chart is management-reported). Mechanism: a paid role plus speed plus breadth.
- Jane Street: STATED, [SEBI interim order, 3 July 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2025/1751584518593.pdf): from January 1, 2023 to March 31, 2025, total net profit across all NSE segments was INR 36,502.12 crore, consisting of index options +INR 43,289.33 crore, stock options +INR 899.99 crore, and losses in stock futures (-INR 7,208.23 crore), index futures (-INR 190.81 crore) and cash (-INR 288.17 crore); SEBI alleged prima facie intraday index manipulation and extended marking the close and ordered INR 4,843.57 crore (INR 48,43,57,70,168) impounded into escrow. Interim ex-parte allegations, contested, not a final finding. What it shows about mechanism: profits sat in options against retail option buyers at institutional scale and access.
- Jump (Tai Mo Shan): STATED, [SEC order 33-11349 (2024)](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2024/33-11349.pdf): directs Tai Mo Shan Limited (a wholly owned subsidiary of Jump Crypto Holdings LLC) to pay $123,095,287 total ($73,452,756 disgorgement, $12,916,153 prejudgment interest, $36,726,378 civil penalty) tied to a May 2021 agreement to buy over $20 million of UST in exchange for accelerated vesting of LUNA tokens. Special access, the opposite of a reachable edge.
- Small AI version: resting limit orders at a 0% maker fee. Covered: `docs/research/186_round_61_thin_cheap_venue.md` (DROP standalone market making), E11 resting-bid line, Measurement 132. Nothing new.
- Verdict: DROP (covered).
