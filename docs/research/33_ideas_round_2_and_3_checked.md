# Checked trading ideas: rounds 2 and 3

Status: CURRENT. Source checks and published designs, not a test by this project. Last checked: 2026-11-25.

STATED means a source reports it and the check found support for the wording. UNKNOWN means the evidence does not establish it. Nothing here is MEASURED by us. One basis point (bp; bps in the plural) is 0.01 percentage points.

## What public tests of AI traders in 2025 and 2026 showed

This set does not establish that AI traders consistently beat a matching passive investment after costs. Some models did better in particular comparisons, but the tests range from hypothetical returns on live signals to funded contests, and those are not interchangeable ([MarketSenseAI](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17327v1), [Alpha Arena reconstruction](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/), [AI-Trader](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10971v1), [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/)).

| Test | What the check confirmed |
|---|---|
| MarketSenseAI | STATED: Across 19 monthly observations on 467 S&P 500 names, strong buys averaged +2.18% per month against +1.15% for the equal-weight stock universe, or +46.8% against +21.6% compounded ([April 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17327v1)). The developers report making recommendations live, then calculate hypothetical forward returns ([April 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17327v1)). The headline figures are gross and do not show funded trades or an audited track record ([April 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17327v1)). |
| Alpha Arena S1 and S1.5 | STATED: A third-party reconstruction from public records found 31 of 38 funded model accounts lost money and paid a combined $43,000 in fees on $380,000 of stakes. A separate secondary scoreboard reports different numbers ([AI Crypto Exchange](https://ai-crypto.exchange/ai-crypto-trading-agents/alpha-arena-results/)). The reconstruction's figures are account counts across two seasons with different conditions, not 38 independent tests or proof that the winners would repeat ([TradingAgentStack reconstruction](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/)). |
| LiveTradeBench | STATED: All 21 equity portfolios gained, from +1.78% to +6.25%, over 50 trading days in 2025 ([LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628)). Four portfolios gained on Polymarket ([LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628)). Live feeds drove simulated portfolios; the paper does not report a matching S&P 500 return or model low-level execution ([LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628)). |
| AI-Trader | STATED: Seven of 18 model-market runs gained and four beat their own benchmarks: three beat QQQ, none beat SSE 50, and one beat the CD5 crypto index ([AI-Trader](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10971v1)). The equity runs were October 1 to November 7, 2025; the crypto runs were November 1 to 14 ([AI-Trader](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10971v1)). The authors do not document funded execution or a full cost schedule ([AI-Trader](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10971v1)). |
| Unified crypto benchmark | STATED: In the reported August 24 to September 21, 2026 real-capital results table, only Qwen gained (+3.72%), and none of the four listed methods beat holding Bitcoin (+11.63%) ([unified crypto benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510)). Qwen was the only large language model in that table ([unified crypto benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510)). The authors list taker fees but do not give the amount funded; the methods section and results table differ on the number of methods ([unified crypto benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510)). |
| TradeRank | STATED: On the operator's November 24, 2026 all-time paper leaderboard, one of 22 displayed model families was above SPY and 18 were above BTC ([TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/)). The board carries each family's starting money across seasons, and families did not all run in the same seasons ([TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/)). This is a snapshot, not a funded result or an explanation of why they beat BTC ([TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/)). |
| rta-bench-1 | STATED: No alpha was statistically significant across 91 completed replays ([rta-bench-1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)). In a separate news-rule test, the reported average moved from +32.0 bps per trade with calendar-date close-to-close matching to minus 6.7 bps when both publication and entry timing were corrected ([rta-bench-1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)). Neither is a live record, and the difference cannot be assigned to just one of the two timing changes ([rta-bench-1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)). |
| TradingAgents and QuantAgent | STATED: In an independent five-stock historical reproduction with a $100,000 starting portfolio, TradingAgents finished at $106,400 gross and $102,300 net; QuantAgent at $81,400 gross and $77,900 net; holding at $104,800 ([Alpha Illusion reproduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895v1)). Model costs, commission, spread and impact were modeled, not paid in live trades ([Alpha Illusion reproduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895v1)). Both agents gained on MSTR while holding lost and trailed on the other four stocks; the paper does not establish which decision caused the MSTR result ([Alpha Illusion reproduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895v1)). |

## Ideas 1 to 12

The signals below are things to test, not trading instructions. In the last column, a risk is a reason not to treat the linked observation as an executable return.

| Idea | What the evidence is | Before or after costs | Money traded or paper signal | How it could fool us |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Compare fixed and trailing exits | STATED: Stop rules depend on the price process; a futures study found favorable cases under trend and used ideal stop fills ([Acar and Toffel](https://www.actuaries.org.uk/system/files/documents/pdf/stop-loss-and-investment-returns.pdf)). A later stock study found tight exits often lagged; a 2026 report tried 8,960 exit configurations on a historical trade log ([Lo and Remorov](https://alo.mit.edu/topic/risk-management-and-systemic-risk/), [exit-optimization preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27150)). | Futures results before slippage and costs; the stock study modeled half the quoted spread ([Acar and Toffel](https://www.actuaries.org.uk/system/files/documents/pdf/stop-loss-and-investment-returns.pdf), [Lo and Remorov text](https://www.scribd.com/document/820461845/Stop-loss-strategies-with-serial-correlation-regime-2017-Journal-of-Financ)). | Historical tests; no funded result for the selected exits ([exit-optimization preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27150)). | A winner picked after thousands of tries may just fit that log; the best table row is not necessarily the authors' suggested recipe ([exit-optimization preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.27150)). |
| 2. Rank stocks monthly with several AI readers | STATED: The developers' first-Friday portfolio showed a gross lead in their 19-observation experiment; a separate earlier study tested a different sample and modeled 10 bps per trade ([April 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17327v1), [MarketSenseAI 2.0](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.00415)). | The headline 19-observation result is before costs; do not borrow the older study's cost result ([April 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17327v1)). | Live recommendations, hypothetical stock returns ([April 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17327v1)). | A small basket or a fill after the signal could behave differently from the study's average 35.1 strong-buy names ([April 2026 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.17327v1)). |
| 3. Reduce exposure when volatility rises | STATED: Scaling by inverse prior-month variance gave a 4.86% annualized market alpha in the original historical test. An expanding-window study found better Sharpe ratios in 45 of 103 strategies, not across the board ([Moreira and Muir](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/Volatility%20Managed%20Portfolios.pdf), [Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). | The 4.86% headline is before costs; the draft's modeled 10 bps case was 3.98% ([Moreira and Muir](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/Volatility%20Managed%20Portfolios.pdf)). | Historical simulations, not funded trading ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). | The proposed cash cap and inverse-volatility sizing are not the original inverse-variance rule; the 103-strategy main comparison allowed leverage up to five times ([Moreira and Muir](https://www.stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/Volatility%20Managed%20Portfolios.pdf), [Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). |
| 4. Use bad news as a reason to sit out | UNKNOWN: No confirmed test isolates the proposed headline veto. A reported crypto hack and price decline overlapped, but the source does not identify cause; the five-stock agent study does not isolate a cash gate ([21Shares](https://www.21shares.com/en-us/insights/bitcoin-outlook-november-2026), [Alpha Illusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895v1)). | No net result for the veto ([Alpha Illusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895v1)). | Actual market event and a separate backtest, not trades from the veto ([21Shares](https://www.21shares.com/en-us/insights/bitcoin-outlook-november-2026), [Alpha Illusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895v1)). | The price could already have moved or recovered before a usable headline; the linked commentary does not identify the time effect of the replacement announcement ([21Shares](https://www.21shares.com/en-us/insights/bitcoin-outlook-november-2026)). |
| 5. Read what the 8-K filing actually says | STATED: Bankruptcy filings were followed by negative benchmark-adjusted returns over 30 to 90 calendar days. A conference study found the largest pre-filing run-ups also had the largest next-half-hour moves in regular hours, across several SEC forms ([Lerman and Livnat](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jlivnat/f8k%20current.pdf), [AEA slides](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/powerpoint/str4n3nr)). | No after-cost result for a minute-entry trade or veto ([AEA slides](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/powerpoint/str4n3nr), [Lerman and Livnat](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jlivnat/f8k%20current.pdf)). | Historical filing and price observations, not strategy fills ([Lerman and Livnat](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jlivnat/f8k%20current.pdf)). | Different filing items and event windows carry different results; a bankruptcy event average does not tell us what to do on a contract filing ([Lerman and Livnat](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~jlivnat/f8k%20current.pdf)). |
| 6. Read earnings-call text after the release | STATED: After-hours quotes adjusted quickly; little midpoint adjustment remained after about an hour ([Grégoire and Martineau](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1475-679X.12394)). Call text predicted more drift than the earnings number in a historical study, and another preprint found call sentiment strongest at the next open ([Meursault et al.](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/working-papers/2021/wp21-07.pdf), [Fast Numbers, Slow Language](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29734)). | Crossing the after-hours spread erased average gains under the first study's exit assumption; the next-open preprint modeled a 1 bp one-way cost ([Grégoire and Martineau](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1475-679X.12394), [next-open preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29734)). | Historical quotes and modeled trades, not funded fills ([next-open preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29734)). | A transcript is not a press release ([Meursault et al.](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/working-papers/2021/wp21-07.pdf)). The stronger historical signal says nothing about when our copy of the text became available ([Meursault et al.](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/working-papers/2021/wp21-07.pdf)). |
| 7. Wait after forced crypto sales | STATED: In one October 2025 Hyperliquid episode, 87.8% of forced selling after onset fell in the first half hour and 96.5% in the first hour. In an unrelated Upbit interruption, the estimated price effect became statistically unclear before service returned ([liquidation preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03616v1), [Lee and Wie](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10395473/pdf/main.pdf)). | No after-cost rebound result ([liquidation preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03616v1)). | Observed market activity, not funded rebound trades ([liquidation preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03616v1)). | Forced selling slowing is not evidence that prices will rise or that a venue has recovered ([liquidation preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.03616v1), [Lee and Wie](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10395473/pdf/main.pdf)). |
| 8. Watch a stablecoin's return to $1 | STATED: In March 2023, USDC recovered sharply after depositor protection was announced and fully recovered once Circle resumed redemptions. A separate daily study found severe USDT discounts coincided with drops in ten other coins and often a partial next-day bounce ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/in-the-shadow-of-bank-run-lessons-from-the-silicon-valley-bank-failure-and-its-impact-on-stablecoins-20251217.html), [Accounting & Finance](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/acfi.70201)). | No after-cost return for a reversal trade ([Accounting & Finance](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/acfi.70201)). | Historical prices, not trades from this rule ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/in-the-shadow-of-bank-run-lessons-from-the-silicon-valley-bank-failure-and-its-impact-on-stablecoins-20251217.html)). | A headline does not prove reserve access or a fill, and a partial bounce can leave most of a loss intact ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/in-the-shadow-of-bank-run-lessons-from-the-silicon-valley-bank-failure-and-its-impact-on-stablecoins-20251217.html), [Accounting & Finance](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/acfi.70201)). |
| 9. Read the court ruling | STATED: In a 509-case Supreme Court sample, winning firms averaged +0.55% and losing firms minus 0.37% relative to expected returns on decision day. A separate intraday study found reactions could build into the next session and documented one initial move that reversed ([Suslava](https://www.gc.cuny.edu/sites/default/files/2021-07/Kate-Suslava-Supreme-Court-Market-Reaction_1.pdf), [Katz et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1508.05751)). | No documented trading-cost deduction ([Suslava](https://www.gc.cuny.edu/sites/default/files/2021-07/Kate-Suslava-Supreme-Court-Market-Reaction_1.pdf)). | Historical prices, not strategy trades ([Katz et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1508.05751)). | An early jump can eat the tradable move; these courts and cases do not cover every legal decision ([Katz et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1508.05751)). |
| 10. Compare insider buys with and without wire coverage | STATED: The no-Dow-Jones comparison was roughly flat for two minutes. A commercial tracker reports +0.23% relative to SPY over 90 days across 67 purchase clusters at activist targets ([Rogers et al.](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Shared%20Documents/conferences/2013-imo/RSZ%20draft%2020130530_hbs.pdf), [13dwatch](https://13dwatch.com/blog/form-4-cluster-buys-vs-single-buys/)). | No documented cost deduction in the tracker; benchmark-adjusted is not net ([13dwatch](https://13dwatch.com/blog/form-4-cluster-buys-vs-single-buys/)). | Historical events and quotes, not booked fills ([Rogers et al.](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Shared%20Documents/conferences/2013-imo/RSZ%20draft%2020130530_hbs.pdf)). | A midpoint need not be fillable, and no matching item in our news log would not establish no wire coverage ([Rogers et al.](https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Shared%20Documents/conferences/2013-imo/RSZ%20draft%2020130530_hbs.pdf)). |
| 11. Separate FDA approvals from refusals | STATED: A 2010 to 2024 thesis found weak evidence of a consistent return direction but persistent volatility, especially after Complete Response Letters. A vendor's decision-day averages were 0.0% for 39 approvals and minus 13.7% for 15 letters ([Princeton thesis](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/bd23f089-38e8-4418-98ad-0d470d265e28), [BioRadar](https://bioradar.io/how-biotech-stocks-move-around-fda-decisions/)). | No after-cost exit test ([Princeton thesis](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/bd23f089-38e8-4418-98ad-0d470d265e28)). | Historical observations, not exit fills ([BioRadar](https://bioradar.io/how-biotech-stocks-move-around-fda-decisions/)). | The first accessible price may already reflect the decision, and a rounded decision-day average is not proof of zero later drift ([Princeton thesis](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/bd23f089-38e8-4418-98ad-0d470d265e28), [AEA study](https://benny.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/paper/AfbKdS7N)). |
| 12. Sit out large unlocks; compare listings and index additions | STATED: A hand-collected study of major Binance-listed unlocks found 46 of 52 down within 72 hours, by 16.97% on average. A vendor found a median loss of 2.3% relative to BTC across 310 unlock weeks, compared with 2.5% in ordinary weeks. Listing gains tended to fade and a separate study found weaker index-addition effects in its later sample ([Kim](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6632838), [TrueHold](https://www.truehold.xyz/unlocks/price-impact), [The Tie](https://www.thetie.io/insights/what-does-an-exchange-listing-actually-deliver-in-2026), [Greenwood and Sammon](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30748/w30748.pdf)). | No documented net result for an unlock veto or first-minute entry ([TrueHold](https://www.truehold.xyz/unlocks/price-impact), [The Tie](https://www.thetie.io/insights/what-does-an-exchange-listing-actually-deliver-in-2026)). | Historical prices, not funded strategy trades ([Kim](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6632838), [The Tie](https://www.thetie.io/insights/what-does-an-exchange-listing-actually-deliver-in-2026)). | Ordinary weeks were also weak in the vendor comparison; daily listing prices cannot show what was tradable at minute one ([TrueHold](https://www.truehold.xyz/unlocks/price-impact), [The Tie](https://www.thetie.io/insights/what-does-an-exchange-listing-actually-deliver-in-2026)). |

## Published designs behind queue rows 10 to 13

These are historical observations and portfolio calculations, not proof of funded execution. The survival guesses are the scout's opinions for a long-only account at 5 bps per side, not probabilities measured from data. Where a study buys one group and shorts another, its reported spread is not the return on the buy side alone.

### Row 10: Which company filings changed least?

- Original design, STATED: Lazy Prices compares the text of US quarterly and annual filings from 1995 to 2014. The portfolio bought companies that changed their wording least and shorted those that changed it most, earning what the NBER Digest calls a “value-weighted abnormal return of between 34 and 58 basis points per month” over the following year. The risk-factor section result was 188 bps per month. Cost treatment in the public summary is UNKNOWN ([NBER paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25084), [NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/nov18/are-investors-inattentive-sec-mandated-corporate-reports)).
- After publication, STATED: An open check on the current S&P 100 reported minus 0.9% a year in factor-adjusted return, with a t-statistic of minus 0.5, from March 2009 through September 2026. A separate public replication log reports a t-statistic of 0.26 for 2007 to 2021. Neither found a statistically clear effect in its sample ([S&P 100 replication](https://github.com/iqueipopg/lazy-prices), [separate replication log](https://github.com/bhoult/correlation/blob/main/experiments/prior-work.md)).
- Free inputs needed: Original 10-K and 10-Q filing text with point-in-time publication records, plus daily stock prices and delisting coverage if the test goes beyond a fixed universe. SEC submissions and financial data are available without an API key; check that filing text and usable timestamps are captured for the test ([SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)).
- Scout's opinion: About 15% to 25% for a standalone long-only buy signal and about 40% for an exclusion filter. Those are two different tests; excluding heavy rewrites does not reproduce the original short leg.

### Row 11: Does customer news reach the supplier's price later?

- Original design, STATED: The paper used principal customer links from Compustat and sorted suppliers each month by the previous month's customer stock return. The authors say buying the top fifth and shorting the bottom fifth “yields abnormal returns of 1.55% per month”, before documented trading-cost deductions. The customer data covered 1980 to 2004. This was a monthly stock-return sort, not a strategy keyed to customer earnings headlines ([Cohen and Frazzini](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~afrazzin/pdf/Economic%20Links%20and%20Predictable%20Returns%20-%20Cohen%20and%20Frazzini.pdf)).
- After publication, STATED: The same design in January 2005 to December 2024 had a 0.092% monthly long-short excess return and a t-statistic of 0.34. A separate 2005 to 2018 check had a 0.6% monthly value-weighted spread that lost statistical significance. A critique reports the delay in information spread was a month or less after removing a scaling factor ([replication report](https://dmurav.com/replications/releases/v2026-09-06/reports/paper-8a5bb9c63c95.pdf), [independent check](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.11394v1), [JFQA appendix](https://static.cambridge.org/content/id/urn:cambridge.org:id:article:S0022109020000885/resource/name/S0022109020000885sup001.pdf)).
- Free inputs needed: Build dated customer-supplier links from original filings rather than assume the paid database can be replicated. Also collect public customer earnings releases, supplier report dates, and prices for both sides. EDGAR has no-key submission and filing data; the completeness of a free supplier map is UNKNOWN ([SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)).
- Scout's opinion: About 15% to 20% for a monthly sort in liquid large caps, versus about 25% for an event-driven test on smaller suppliers. The latter changes the signal, timing, and universe; it is not a repeat of the published sort.

### Row 12: Score earnings-call answers apart from prepared remarks

- Original designs, STATED: Price and coauthors found call tone dominated earnings surprises over 60 trading days and that “the question and answer portion of the call has incremental explanatory power”, mainly at non-dividend payers ([Price et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i4p992-1011.html)). A separate call-text study on 2010 to 2019 results reported 2.87% cumulative abnormal return for the text-based long-short spread over 63 trading days; the authors reported 1.31% for the top fifth of the text ranking. These are historical returns without a documented cost deduction, and neither result validates a 15-day hold ([PEAD.txt working paper](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/-/media/frbp/assets/working-papers/2021/wp21-07.pdf)).
- After publication, STATED: A FinBERT preprint reports 2.03% monthly long-short alpha. Its daily event-return definition starts at the prior close, before the call, and it does not model strategy costs or capacity. Do not treat the headline as an executable gain after a transcript becomes available ([FinBERT preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13260v1)). Another paper associates conflicting tones in releases and calls with negative subsequent abnormal returns, not proof that a veto works after costs ([AFA paper](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=198884)).
- Free inputs needed: Dated releases, separate remarks and Q&A transcript text, a record of when that version was first available, and session prices. SEC APIs do not supply call transcripts; Equibles advertises a free tier with 100 requests a day, but its example gives a date rather than a precise publication time ([Botminds on SEC coverage](https://www.botminds.ai/solutions/earnings-call-analysis/earnings-call-transcripts-api-what-you-bring), [Equibles free tier](https://equibles.com/earnings-call-transcript-api), [Equibles API example](https://equibles.com/docs/api/endpoints/earnings.md)). ROIC.ai reports a median raw-transcript delay of 1 hour 58 minutes, so the test should require evidence of availability, even at the next open ([ROIC.ai timing](https://www.roic.ai/api/docs/earnings-calls)).
- Scout's opinion: About 35% to 45% for a 30- to 63-session hold with next-session entry after availability is verified. The scout did not give those odds to a 15-session hold.

### Row 13: New contracts large against a company's sales

- Original designs, STATED: An IMF paper used a procurement dataset from 2000 Q1 through 2012 Q4 and reported that “the average monthly strategy return is .5 percent” before documented costs for buying prior-month winners and shorting firms without a win ([Hebous and Zimmermann](https://www.imf.org/external/pubs/ft/wp/2016/wp1660.pdf)). Another paper reported about 12 bps of abnormal return one day after competitive announcements and no similar evidence for noncompetitive awards ([Budrys](https://zymantasbudrys.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/ZymantasBudrys_JMP_LastConsumer.pdf)). An 8-K agreement study found 35.4 bps by buying when a deal was struck and selling just before disclosure, not by buying after filing ([Cohen, Jackson, and Mitts](https://hls.harvard.edu/bibliography/the-8-k-trading-gap/)).
- After publication, STATED: A 2025 paper reports that portfolios based on large government contracts outperformed the market, but the available abstract leaves the exact return and costs UNKNOWN ([Pyun](https://econpapers.repec.org/article/eeeecolet/v_3a252_3ay_3a2025_3ai_3ac_3as0165176525001727.htm)). A newer study found no significant overall relationship between procurement exposure and returns in its 118-firm panel. That is not a like-for-like test of a trade after a particular public announcement ([Stockholm School of Economics thesis](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=6756)).
- Free inputs needed: Public award records, original Item 1.01 filings with availability times, trailing sales as known then, and stock prices. USAspending's API does not require authorization, but its guide says contract data are generally available within five days of award or modification, and the Congressional Research Service warns of gaps and inaccuracies. Use evidence of public availability rather than treating the award date or a fixed delay as a guaranteed publication time ([USAspending endpoints](https://api.usaspending.gov/docs/endpoints), [USAspending data guide](https://www.usaspending.gov/data/about-the-data-download.pdf), [CRS audit summary](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R44027), [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces)).
- Scout's opinion: About 20% to 30%, favoring a test on smaller contractors with a publicly timed filing for a deal large against sales. An estimated chance of surviving is not a test of the proposed size filter.

## Could not confirm or found wrong

- AI benchmarks: The competing Alpha Arena summaries do not give interchangeable scores; the original Qwen loss range was wrong. LiveTradeBench did not establish how many beat the S&P 500. TradeRank does not establish a common start or explain its BTC comparison. The MSTR result was not merely a smaller loss; its cause is not established. The timing test does not show that trading frequency explained the losses across benchmarks, and its exposure share is not variance explained ([Alpha Arena reconstruction](https://tradingagentstack.com/alpha-arena-results/), [separate contest summary](https://ai-crypto.exchange/ai-crypto-trading-agents/alpha-arena-results/), [LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628), [TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/), [Alpha Illusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16895v1), [rta-bench-1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.19530)).
- Exits, sizing, and headlines: The exit-sweep date and abbreviated recipe were wrong, the best table result does not belong to that recipe, and the sources do not show that wide or trailing stops consistently add value after costs. The volatility studies do not validate the proposed cash-capped sizing rule.
