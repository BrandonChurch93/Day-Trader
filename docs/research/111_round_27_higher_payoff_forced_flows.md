No market prices or return calculations were run.

## Dropped Candidates (Added to Round 27 Skip List)

Closed-end fund transferable and non-transferable rights offerings (DROPPED: main forced-flow move is short-only; published long-only post-expiration rebound is weak/permanent):
- MEASURED: SEC EDGAR EFTS (2016 to 2026) returns 343 filings across 109 unique CIKs matching `"transferable rights" "closed-end" "subscription price"` (Forms N-2, N-2/A, 497, 8-K) and 97 filings matching `"non-transferable rights" "closed-end" "subscription price"`.
- STATED: The documented price move is a decline during the offering period (requiring shorting, unreachable on $500 cash). Per [CFA Institute Research Foundation summary of Khorana, Wahal and Zenner (2002)](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/-/media/documents/book/rf-publication/2002/rf-v2002-n4-3920-pdf.pdf): "Khorana, Wahal, and Zenner (2002) studied 120 rights offerings by U.S. closed-end funds over the 1988-98 period and found that, on average, rights offerings are announced when funds trade at a premium and that this premium turns into a discount over the course of the offering."
- STATED: On the long-only post-expiration side, [Brigham (LSU Dissertation)](https://repository.lsu.edu/gradschool_disstheses/7143/) states: "Although the significant negative returns found during the offering periods are consistent with temporary price pressure, the very weak price rebound observed after the completion of the offers is more supportive of a permanent price pressure effect on the shares of closed-end funds that issue additional shares in equity offerings."

Small-cap and micro-cap index pro-forma reconstitutions: S&P SmallCap 600, CRSP US Small Cap, MSCI Small Cap, and Nasdaq-100 annual (DROPPED / BLOCKED: no free lawful public pro-forma archive; Nasdaq-100 annual also < 30 dates):
- STATED: Published evidence shows large reconstitution price pressure. Per [Dimensional Fund Advisors (July 2024)](https://www.dimensional.com/chmedia/383623/source/measuring-the-costs-of-index-reconstitution-a-10-year-perspective.pdf), S&P 600 additions and deletions (n = 327, 2014 to 2023) averaged 8.7% cumulative excess return from t-20 to t and -0.5% from t to t+20, while CRSP US Small Cap (n = 643) averaged 2.8% from t-20 to t and -3.3% from t to t+20. The report states: "We find that, on average, additions outperform their respective index, while deletions underperform in the days leading up to the reconstitution."
- STATED: None of the four families offers a free lawful public historical pro-forma list. For CRSP, the [CRSP Market Indexes Methodology Guide (March 2025)](https://www.crsp.org/wp-content/uploads/guides/CRSP_Market_Indexes_Methodology_Guide-March_2025.pdf) states: "Pro forma files are made available to index subscribers via FTP at 7:00 p.m. ET (6:00 p.m. CT) on the Sunday following the ranking day (the first Sunday after the first Friday of March, June, September, and December)." For S&P SmallCap 600 and Nasdaq-100, Row 76 and Row 87 already documented that S&P Dow Jones Indices Legal Disclaimers and Nasdaq Legal Section 11 prohibit storing or reproducing their announcement archives without written permission (HTTP 403, and SEC 8-K searches returned 0 hits for S&P 600 and Nasdaq-100 deletions). Nasdaq-100 annual reconstitution also has only 10 annual dates in 2016 to 2025 (below 30). MSCI Small Cap pro-forma lists are similarly paid licensed feeds (and MSCI additions were dropped in Round 9, `docs/research/67_round_9_outside_evidence.md`).

Leveraged and inverse ETF reverse splits (DROPPED: no long-only NAV dislocation; reverse-split abnormal returns are negative / short-only):
- MEASURED: SEC EDGAR EFTS (2016 to 2026) returns 297 Form 497/497K/8-K filings matching `"reverse share split" "ETF"`, including 56 ProShares and 56 Direxion reverse-split filings.
- STATED: Open-end leveraged and inverse ETFs track daily index multiples via Authorized Participant creation and redemption arbitrage, so a reverse share split does not create a closed-end discount to NAV. Empirical reverse-split evidence points to negative post-split abnormal returns (short-only, unreachable on $500 cash), with no published long-only rebound above 0.25% per trade.

Crypto index and ETF rebalance dates reachable from a US spot account: GDLC and BITW (DROPPED: < 30 quarterly/constituent-change dates and no published basis):
- MEASURED: Grayscale CoinDesk Crypto 5 ETF (`GDLC`, CIK 0001729997) filed 66 Form 8-Ks from July 2021 to October 2026, with quarterly rebalances occurring 4 times per year (~20 quarterly dates since 2021, below 30). Per [GDLC Form 8-K (April 30, 2026)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1729997/000119312526209619/gdlc-20260430.htm): "The Index is rebalanced quarterly, during a period beginning 30 days before the last business day of each January, April, July and October."
- MEASURED: Bitwise 10 Crypto Index ETF (`BITW`, CIK 0001723788) filed only 14 Form 8-Ks total (September 2021 to December 2025). Its public monthly rebalance notices ([Bitwise August 2026](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/indexes/rebalance-results/bitwise-crypto-asset-indexes/august-2026) and [Bitwise November 2026](https://bitwiseinvestments.com/indexes/rebalance-notifications/bitwise-crypto-asset-indexes)) show BTC and ETH make up over 90% of index weight (`BTC` 76.21%, `ETH` 14.42% in August 2026) and frequent `"No changes"` months. Actual US-spot constituent additions and deletions are far below 30, and no published basis shows a > 0.25% forced-flow return on US-spot coins around these rebalances.
