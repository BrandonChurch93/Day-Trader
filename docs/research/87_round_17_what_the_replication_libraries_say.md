# Round 17: What the Published Replication Libraries Say (Post-2015, After Costs, Long-Only)

## Lead Agent Rulings

- No new monthly cross-sectional equity factor sort is queued for a $500 whole-share, <= 1-month long-only account.
- Future scouting rounds must skip the dead families listed below, where the published replication libraries agree that post-publication or after-cost net returns are zero or negative in liquid US stocks.
- The few surviving post-2015 net factors (Profitability/RMW, Multi-Factor combos, Quality composites, and marginally Momentum) are 6-to-12-month diversified tilts requiring 100 to 200+ stocks (reachable on $500 whole shares only via factor ETFs such as `QUAL` or `MTUM`, not <= 1-month single-stock sorts).
- The narrow <= 1-month long-only US stock exceptions with free data are discrete SEC-filed corporate events already covered in the queue (Plans 36, 38, and 43; Queue 64 for PEAD).

## Dead Families (All Replication Libraries Agree: Skip in Future Rounds)

- Trading frictions, liquidity, and market microstructure anomalies: MEASURED: "With microcaps mitigated via NYSE breakpoints and value-weighted returns, 65% of the 452 anomalies in our extensive data library, including 96% of the trading frictions category, cannot clear the single test hurdle of the absolute |t|-value of 1.96." ([Hou, Xue, and Zhang, 2020](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/33/5/2019/5236964)). Price and trading-volume signals retain only 29.7% of backtest return post-publication since 2000 ([BlackRidge / OSAP, 2026](https://blckridge.com/research/signal-commoditization-20260929/)).
- Size (`SMB` / small-cap premium): MEASURED: Fails the 50% Bayesian replication threshold in [Jensen, Kelly, and Pedersen (2023)](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/95651880/theis_ingerslev_jensen_et_al_is_there_a_replication_crisis_in_finance_publishersversion.pdf) ("We estimate a replication rate greater than 50% in 11 of the 13 themes (based on the Bayesian model including MT adjustment), the exceptions being 'low leverage' and 'size' factor themes"), earned -0.9%/yr from 2007 to 2026 ([BlackRidge, 2026](https://blckridge.com/research/signal-commoditization-20260929/)), and earned -2.12%/yr net of TAQ costs from 2004 to 2024 ([Naranjo, 2026](https://www.lorenzonaranjo.com/class-materials/from-papers-to-portfolios/notebooks/trading-costs.html)).
- Short-term reversal (`ret_1_0`) and industry-relative reversal: MEASURED: Monthly turnover of 70.8% to 90%+ generates costs exceeding gross returns ([Novy-Marx and Velikov, 2016](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf): "The cost of trading the high-turnover strategies (those with monthly turnover in excess of 90% per month), at least when designed with complete disregard for trading costs, always exceeds 1% per month"; [Naranjo, 2026](https://www.lorenzonaranjo.com/class-materials/from-papers-to-portfolios/notebooks/trading-costs.html): -9.35%/yr net 1972 to 2025 and -1.16%/yr net TAQ 2004 to 2024).
- Low-leverage theme: MEASURED: One of only two themes failing the 50% Bayesian replication threshold in [Jensen, Kelly, and Pedersen (2023)](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/95651880/theis_ingerslev_jensen_et_al_is_there_a_replication_crisis_in_finance_publishersversion.pdf).
- Calendar and seasonal anomalies (January effect, same-calendar-month seasonality): MEASURED: Seasonality fails the 75% replication bar in [Jensen, Kelly, and Pedersen (2023)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28432/w28432.pdf) ("with the exceptions being the seasonality, leverage, and size themes") and fails standardized replication, multiple testing, and after-cost hurdles ([QuantDecoded, 2026](https://quantdecoded.com/en/replication-crisis-asset-pricing-which-anomalies-survive)).
- Idiosyncratic volatility (`IVOL`) and Skewness/MAX for long-only accounts: MEASURED: Driven by overpricing in the short leg under short-sale constraints ([Stambaugh, Yu, and Yuan, 2015](https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/fin/event/CUHK-RAPS-Conference2023/paper/098.pdf)), fails after costs in [QuantDecoded (2026)](https://quantdecoded.com/en/replication-crisis-asset-pricing-which-anomalies-survive), and cannot be captured in a long-only account.
- Analyst-forecast revisions and standalone accounting accruals: MEASURED: Analyst signals suffer the steepest post-publication decay (-61% across 18 signals in [OSAP 2026.10](https://github.com/mishu1177karan/factor-decay/blob/main/README.md)) and fail after high-turnover costs ([QuantDecoded, 2026](https://quantdecoded.com/en/replication-crisis-asset-pricing-which-anomalies-survive)). Accounting-based anomaly alphas declined 47 to 62 bps/month after EDGAR adoption, concentrated in the short leg ([Muravyev, AFA](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=178164): "accounting-based anomaly alphas declined by 47 to 62 basis points per month (or 5.6% to 7.4% per year) because of the EDGAR introduction... profit attenuation is concentrated among the short legs").
- Intangibles (patents-to-R&D, organizational capital): MEASURED: Only 25.2% replicate in NYSE-breakpoint value-weighted tests ([Hou, Xue, and Zhang, 2020](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2019/5236964?guestAccessKey=7fd97e02-18ad-4e38-aec9-44cac1b9f75a)), and `PatentsRD` shows an out-of-sample Sharpe of -1.02 in [OSAP / ConvexPi (2026)](https://www.convexpi.ai/anomalies).

## Headline Synthesis Across the Six Libraries

Scope: All figures below are taken verbatim from the cited papers and published summary tables. No return files were downloaded and no returns were computed. Claims are labeled MEASURED (reported empirical figure in a cited study), STATED (methodological note or structural fact), or UNKNOWN (not broken out in public tables).

- McLean and Pontiff (2016, Journal of Finance, 97 predictors): MEASURED: "Portfolio returns are 26% lower out-of-sample and 58% lower post-publication." ([McLean and Pontiff, 2016](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.12365)).
- Chen and Zimmermann Open Source Asset Pricing (OSAP, 2022 CFR; 2025 and 2026 releases of 212 predictors through Dec 2025): MEASURED: Average monthly long-short return falls from 0.686% in-sample to 0.308% post-publication (-55.1%) ([OSAP 2026.10 replication](https://github.com/mishu1177karan/factor-decay/blob/main/README.md)). Value-weighted decay is sharper ([BlackRidge / OSAP Oct 2025](https://blckridge.com/research/signal-commoditization-20260929/)): "A full 34.0% of predictors earn zero or less after publication when value-weighted, and the 2015 to 2024 average fell to 0.08%." A 2026 audit ([arXiv:2607.06502](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06502)) confirms: "Post-2005, in the top 90% of stocks by market capitalization, the median published anomaly earned about 7 bps per month (1% per year) before transaction costs."
- Chen and Velikov (2023, JFQA, 204 OSAP anomalies): MEASURED: After effective bid-ask spreads, post-publication decay, and post-2005 liquidity ([Chen and Velikov, 2023](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/)): "Net of these effects, the average anomaly's expected return is a measly 4 bps per month. The strongest anomalies net, at best, 10 bps after controlling for data mining. Several methods for combining anomalies net around 20 bps."
- Hou, Xue, and Zhang (2020, RFS, 452 anomalies, NYSE breakpoints and value-weighted returns): MEASURED: Category replication rates at `|t| >= 1.96` are Investment 73.7%, Momentum 63.2%, Profitability 44.3%, Value-versus-growth 42.0%, Intangibles 25.2%, and Trading frictions 3.8% ([Hou, Xue, and Zhang, 2020](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/5/2019/5236964?guestAccessKey=7fd97e02-18ad-4e38-aec9-44cac1b9f75a)).
- Jensen, Kelly, and Pedersen (2023, Journal of Finance, 153 factors in 13 themes; updated April 2026 through Dec 2025): MEASURED: Gross capped value-weighted CAPM alpha still shows 47% post-sample decay ([Jensen, Kelly, and Pedersen, 2023](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/95651880/theis_ingerslev_jensen_et_al_is_there_a_replication_crisis_in_finance_publishersversion.pdf)): "the average in-sample alpha is 0.49% per month and the average out-of-sample alpha is 0.26% when looking postoriginal sample, implying a decline of 47%."
- Novy-Marx and Velikov (2016, RFS, 23 anomalies): MEASURED: "Most anomalies with turnover less than 50% per month generate significant net spreads when designed to mitigate transaction costs; few with higher turnover do." ([Novy-Marx and Velikov, 2016](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf)).
- Long-only vs short-leg asymmetry: MEASURED: Across 162 anomalies, [Anomalies and Their Short-Sale Costs (Journal of Finance, 2025)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13501?af=R) reports: "Using a comprehensive sample of 162 anomalies, the average long-short portfolio return is a significant 0.14% per month before short-sale costs, and the returns are due to the short leg. However, the average is -0.01% once returns are adjusted for borrow fees." For the core low-turnover factors (Value, Momentum, Profitability/Quality, Low Risk), [Blitz, Baltussen, and van Vliet (2020, FAJ)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0015198X.2020.1779560) find: "(1) most added value comes from the long legs, (2) the long legs offer more diversification than the short legs, and (3) the performance of the short legs is generally subsumed by that of the long legs."

## Evidence Notes on the Top Ranked Families

- Profitability (Rank 1): MEASURED: [BlackRidge / Kenneth French data](https://blckridge.com/research/signal-commoditization-20260929/) reports RMW earned +2.8% per year from 2007 to 2026 while Value (-1.9%), Size (-0.9%), Investment (-0.2%), and Momentum (0.0%) stalled or turned negative. MEASURED: [Naranjo (2026, From Papers to Portfolios)](https://www.lorenzonaranjo.com/class-materials/from-papers-to-portfolios/notebooks/trading-costs.html) reports 2004 to 2024 profitability (`ope_be`) gross return of 3.58%/yr, TAQ trading cost of 0.18%/yr (7.13% monthly turnover), and net return of +3.39%/yr. Reachable on $500 only via a factor ETF (`QUAL`, 127 holdings per [The Mulden, 2026](https://themulden.com/posts/voo-vs-mtum-vs-qual-which-smart-beta/)).
- Multi-Factor Combinations (Rank 2): MEASURED: [Novy-Marx and Velikov (2016)](https://mysimon.rochester.edu/novy-marx/research/ToAatTC.pdf) state: "The best performing strategy, after accounting for transaction costs, is the one sorted on the basis of the combined value, momentum and gross profitability signals." Requires 150 to 250 stocks ([StockValueCheck, 2026](https://stockvaluecheck.com/blog/multi-factor-etf-investing-blending-value-momentum)).
- Momentum (Rank 6): MEASURED: [arXiv:2512.11913 (Dec 2025)](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2512.11913) finds: "Crowding accelerated post-2015. Training on 1995-2015 and predicting 2016-2024, the model over-estimates remaining alpha (0.30 predicted vs. 0.15 actual)." MEASURED: [Naranjo (2026)](https://www.lorenzonaranjo.com/class-materials/from-papers-to-portfolios/notebooks/trading-costs.html) shows 2004 to 2024 momentum (`ret_12_1`) earned +1.85%/yr gross and +1.26%/yr net of TAQ costs (23.26% monthly turnover).
- Account-size mismatch: STATED: [DataToBrief (2026)](https://www.datatobrief.com/blog/factor-investing-value-momentum-quality-size-explained) notes: "A well-constructed factor portfolio should hold 100-200 names with strong factor characteristics, not the market portfolio with mild tilts." A $500 whole-share account cannot diversify a cross-sectional monthly sort across 100+ stocks, leaving factor ETFs for slow tilts or discrete SEC-filed corporate events for <= 1-month single-stock trades.
