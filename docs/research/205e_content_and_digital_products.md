# 205e. Content, digital products and small online businesses run mostly by agents

Status: deep dive lane E, written 2026-11-27 by a helper. Not a test. Nothing was posted, listed, submitted or signed up for. Every link below was opened on 2026-11-27.

Labels: MEASURED means read in a primary record (a filing, a platform's own report or rule page) or worked out by plain arithmetic from one. STATED means someone's claim, including surveys of self-reported income, vendors' datasets and press reports. INFERENCE is this page's reasoning. UNKNOWN means no source found.

## Bottom line

- Most likely to make real money for this owner: small paid software tools (micro software, browser extensions, small API products) that he builds with agents for a narrow paying audience; then engine plugins and 3D or game asset packs on Fab and the Unity Asset Store, where his 3D skill counts and AI help is allowed when disclosed; then design templates. All three are small at first: typical results are tens to low hundreds of dollars a month, and most launches earn close to nothing (MEASURED and STATED below).
- None of this is "reliably quick". The best documented base rate in the lane is RevenueCat's: 17% of new subscription apps reach $1,000 a month within two years, and the middle half earn $16 to $429 a month one year after launch ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-26-insights), STATED vendor dataset). Weeks to a first dollar is possible; weeks to a reliable income is not supported by any source found.
- Traps: mass AI-written niche sites and programmatic pages (Google's scaled content abuse policy), faceless mass-produced video channels (YouTube's inauthentic content policy), AI book volume on KDP, AI stock images (banned at Shutterstock, Getty, Pond5 and Envato), AI music uploads to streaming (Spotify removed more than 75 million spam tracks), print on demand (about $83 a year per selling artist at Redbubble and TeePublic), and course selling as a business model (the sellers of "how to make money with AI" courses are the ones being paid).
- The pattern across platforms: AI help is allowed almost everywhere if disclosed, but volume is what platforms now police. An agent's main strength, making many near-identical items, is exactly what the rules target. The agent advantage is real only where each item is distinct and useful, and the owner's own skill sets the quality.

## Common facts for every entry

- Tax, US: platforms and payment processors file Form 1099-K only above $20,000 and 200 transactions a year after the One Big Beautiful Bill restored the old threshold ([IRS 1099-K FAQ](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/form-1099-k-faqs-general-information), MEASURED). The income is taxable either way, and self-employment tax applies to a business (INFERENCE, standard rule).
- Tax, Idaho: "Retailers with a physical presence in Idaho must collect Idaho sales tax on their sales in Idaho" ([Idaho State Tax Commission online sellers guide](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/online-sellers/online-guide/), MEASURED). Digital music, books and videos sold with a permanent right to use are treated as taxable tangible property, while subscriptions are not ([Idaho rule memo, docket 35-0102-1503](https://legislature.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/sessioninfo/2015/interim/adminrules/3501021503G24.pdf), MEASURED). The guide does not say whether a marketplace collects the tax on an Idaho seller's behalf; that is UNKNOWN from the page and worth one call to the Commission before selling direct.
- Legal: nothing in this lane needs a state license that was found (UNKNOWN for any city business license in Nampa). It is legal in Idaho and the US as ordinary self-employment, subject to copyright, trademark and consumer law (INFERENCE).
- Consumer law that matters here: the FTC's final rule bans fake or false consumer reviews and testimonials, explicitly including AI-generated ones, with civil penalties ([FTC, August 14, 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials), MEASURED). Affiliates must disclose their relationship to the retailer "clearly" ([FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ](https://consumer.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking), MEASURED). Seeding a product with agent-written reviews or using multiple accounts is OUT.
- What agents cannot do in any entry: open seller, payment or tax accounts, pass identity checks, sign marketplace agreements or receive money. The owner does all of these.

## Candidate methods

### 1. Niche sites with affiliate links and display ads

- What it is: a site of articles that rank in Google and earn commissions on products readers buy (for example Amazon Associates) or ad revenue per visit. The money comes from retailers paying for referred sales and from advertisers paying for attention.
- Evidence: the clearest verified record is the sale market. Empire Flippers says it sold about 70 content sites in 2023, about 50 in 2024 and about 30 by late May 2025, with multiples falling from about 30 to about 24 times monthly profit; it blames Google updates and AI answers ([Empire Flippers, July 2025](https://empireflippers.com/you-can-sell-your-business-for-life-changing-money-in-2025-heres-the-secret/), STATED by the broker from its own sales). Pew's browsing panel found Google users clicked a regular result on 8% of visits with an AI summary against 15% without one, and clicked a source inside the summary on 1% ([Pew Research, July 22, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), MEASURED by Pew, 900 adults, 68,879 searches).
- Base rate: UNKNOWN for new sites. One traffic-estimate study found that of 1,172 sites that lost half their Google traffic in the September 2023 helpful content update window, 18.9% had recovered by late 2026 ([PageSpeed Matters, November 2026](https://www.pagespeedmatters.com/resources/data-studies/helpful-content-update-recovery), STATED; traffic is modelled and the provider is not named).
- Typical dollars a month at small scale: UNKNOWN. Ad networks gate on traffic: Raptive from 25,000 monthly pageviews and Mediavine Journey from 1,000 sessions ([Affground comparison](https://affground.com/blog/mediavine-vs-raptive), STATED, not the networks' own pages).
- Money and time before the first dollar: a domain and hosting, under $50 (INFERENCE). Amazon gives a new Associate 180 days to refer three qualifying sales, and the application "can be withdrawn" otherwise ([Amazon Associates help](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/G7MJTPEP9NC3YKMG), MEASURED). New sites usually take months to rank (INFERENCE).
- Platform rules: Google says "Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users" is scaled content abuse, and "Sites that violate our policies may rank lower in results or not appear in results at all" ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), MEASURED). Google's AI guidance says AI "can be particularly useful when researching a topic, and to add structure to original content" ([Google on generative AI content, updated 2026-10-01](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content), MEASURED). So an AI-assisted site with real first-hand value is allowed; a mass-written one is not.
- Enforcement: Google announced the scaled content, expired domain and site reputation policies on March 5, 2024 ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies), MEASURED). One tracker found 837 of 49,345 monitored sites fully deindexed early in that update ([Search Engine Journal, March 12, 2024](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-march-2024-core-update-impact-hundreds-of-websites-deindexed/510981/), STATED). Google tightened site reputation abuse on November 19, 2024 ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse), MEASURED) and ran a spam update from August 26 to September 22, 2025 ([Google Search Status](https://status.search.google.com/incidents/a7Aainy6E9rZsmfq82xt), MEASURED).
- What an agent adds: research, drafting, internal links, updating old pages. What still needs the owner: first-hand testing, photos and judgment, which is what Google rewards (INFERENCE).
- What kills it: AI answers taking the click, core updates, the 180-day affiliate clock, and many competitors with older domains.
- Cheapest honest test: not worth a two-week test; a new site produces no measurable search traffic in two weeks (INFERENCE).

### 2. Programmatic pages (thousands of template pages from a data set)

- What it is: one template filled from a database to make many pages, such as "X vs Y" or "tool for city Z". Money comes from ads or affiliate clicks, as in entry 1.
- Evidence: none independent found (UNKNOWN). Sellers of programmatic SEO tools are STATED claims.
- Base rate: UNKNOWN.
- Typical dollars a month: UNKNOWN.
- Money and time: low money; months to traffic (INFERENCE).
- Platform rules: the scaled content abuse policy names this directly, including "Scraping feeds, search results, or other content to generate many pages" and "Creating many pages where the content makes little or no sense to a reader but contains search keywords" ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), MEASURED). Pages built on a genuinely useful data set the owner controls are a different case, but most programmatic plays are what the policy describes.
- What an agent adds: exactly the volume Google targets.
- What kills it: the policy itself.
- Verdict: OUT in its usual form. Cheapest test: none.

### 3. Video channels (including faceless AI channels)

- What it is: videos earning a share of YouTube ad and Premium revenue, plus sponsors. Advertisers and Premium subscribers pay; YouTube shares 55% of long-form and 45% of Shorts revenue ([YouTube blog, August 10, 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/), MEASURED).
- Evidence: YouTube says more than 3 million creators are in the Partner Program but gives no payout distribution (same source, MEASURED). No public count of all channels exists, so the share that monetizes is UNKNOWN.
- Base rate: UNKNOWN. Entry needs 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours in 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers and 10 million Shorts views in 90 days ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851), MEASURED). From February 1, 2027, new applicants need 8,000 watch hours in 365 days or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days, and Shorts revenue sharing needs 10 million views in 90 days ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/), MEASURED). That doubles the bar for a new channel.
- Typical dollars a month: UNKNOWN.
- Money and time: little money; months to qualify (INFERENCE from the thresholds).
- Platform rules: content must "Not be mass-produced, generic, repetitive, or manipulative"; the "repetitious content" policy was renamed "inauthentic content" on July 15, 2025 ([YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392/), MEASURED). YouTube also said "channels that use AI in their content remain eligible to monetize", and gave as inauthentic examples "A channel that uploads narrated stories with only superficial differences between them" and "A channel that uploads slideshows that all have the same narration" ([YouTube, July 10, 2025](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/356734251/response-to-creator-questions-about-ypp-policies-july-2025?hl=en), MEASURED). Realistic AI content must be disclosed ([YouTube disclosure help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en), MEASURED).
- Enforcement: YouTube suspended ads on Screen Culture and KH Studio, AI fake-trailer channels with over 2 million subscribers, earlier in 2025 and terminated both by December 18, 2025 for spam and misleading metadata ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2025/12/youtube-terminates-screen-culture-kh-studio-fake-ai-trailer-1236652506/), STATED).
- What an agent adds: research, scripts, edits, captions, thumbnails, upload metadata. What needs the owner: his film skill, an on-screen or recognisable voice, and a reason to watch. A faceless template channel is OUT.
- What kills it: the doubled February 2027 entry bar, the inauthentic content policy, and saturation.
- Cheapest honest test: not quick. A channel built on the owner's real film and 3D craft is a long-term brand, not a weeks-scale earner (INFERENCE).

### 4. Newsletters

- What it is: a paid email subscription or a free list sold to sponsors. Readers or sponsors pay.
- Evidence: Substack said it reached 5 million paid subscriptions on March 11, 2025 ([Substack Post](https://post.substack.com/p/5-million), STATED by the platform). A third-party list puts the top earners near $1 million a year or more ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/guide/substack-users), STATED, revenue estimated from public subscriber badges).
- Base rate: UNKNOWN from a primary source. No median from Substack or beehiiv was found.
- Typical dollars a month: UNKNOWN; near $0 for a new list in the first months (INFERENCE).
- Money and time: $0; many months to build a list (INFERENCE).
- Platform rules: no Substack rule specific to AI was found in this pass (UNKNOWN). Medium's AI page was blocked to this machine and was not read.
- What an agent adds: research and drafts. What needs the owner: a voice and expertise people pay for. An anonymous AI digest has many free substitutes (INFERENCE).
- What kills it: slow growth; readers do not pay for summaries they can get free.
- Cheapest honest test: none in two weeks that predicts paid income.

### 5. Self-published books (KDP)

- What it is: ebooks and print-on-demand books on Amazon, paid by sales and Kindle Unlimited page reads. Readers and Amazon's Kindle Unlimited fund pay.
- Evidence: the Authors Guild's 2023 survey of 5,699 published authors found median book income in 2022 of $2,000 for all authors and $12,800 for full-time self-published authors ([Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/app/uploads/2026/11/Income-Survey-2023-Report.pdf), STATED survey, self-reported). Written Word Media's 2025 survey of 1,346 mostly prolific indie authors found 44% earn $100 a month or less, about 80% of authors with 1 to 3 books are under $100 a month, and the median at 5 to 9 books is about $50 a month ([Written Word Media, December 2, 2025](https://www.writtenwordmedia.com/2025-indie-author-survey-results-insights-into-self-publishing-for-authors/), STATED, self-reported and skewed toward serious authors).
- Base rate: from those surveys, most new authors earn under $100 a month (STATED).
- Typical dollars a month: under $100 for a small catalog (STATED, above).
- Money and time: $0 to publish; covers and editing cost money if done well (INFERENCE).
- Platform rules: KDP requires authors to "inform us of AI-generated content (text, images, or translations)", where AI-generated means content "created by an AI-based tool ... even if you applied substantial edits afterwards"; AI-assisted editing need not be disclosed ([KDP content guidelines](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390?embed=true), MEASURED).
- Enforcement: on September 18, 2023 KDP lowered new title limits to three a day "in order to help protect against abuse" ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/content-and-e-books/article/93207-kdp-will-limit-daily-number-of-new-titles.html), STATED). Amazon removed AI books sold under author Jane Friedman's name in August 2023 ([CNN, August 10, 2023](https://www.cnn.com/2023/08/10/tech/ai-generated-books-amazon), STATED).
- What an agent adds: drafting, formatting, metadata. A disclosed AI-generated book competes against a flood of the same (INFERENCE).
- What kills it: the volume cap, disclosure labels that buyers can see, and survey medians near zero for small catalogs.
- Verdict: weak; mass AI books are a trap. Cheapest test: none useful in two weeks.

### 6. Print on demand (Redbubble, TeePublic, Merch by Amazon, Etsy with a print partner)

- What it is: upload designs; the platform prints shirts, mugs and posters on order and pays the designer a margin. Buyers of the products pay.
- Evidence: Articore (Redbubble and TeePublic) reports artists earned $59 million in the year to June 30, 2024 across about 0.7 million selling artists, meaning artists who sold at least one item ([Articore FY24 annual report](https://www.articore.com/FormBuilder/_Resource/_module/VFV_6YfHZ0qKveaD4XGZZA/files/presentations/FY24_Annual_Report.pdf), MEASURED). That is about $83 a year, under $7 a month, per artist who sold anything, and the median is lower (MEASURED arithmetic; median INFERENCE). Artists' revenue fell from $87.6 million in FY23 to $69.9 million in FY24 (same report, MEASURED).
- Base rate: UNKNOWN for all uploaders; the average above counts only those who sold.
- Typical dollars a month: single digits (MEASURED average above).
- Money and time: $0; Merch by Amazon starts new accounts at 10 published designs and raises tiers by sales ([Merch by Amazon FAQ](https://merch.amazon.com/resource/201846470), MEASURED).
- Platform rules: Merch by Amazon has no AI-specific rule; normal IP and quality rules apply ([Merchbanao summary of Amazon's pages](https://merchbanao.com/blog/amazon-merch-ai-policy/), STATED). Etsy allows AI designs with disclosure (entry 10).
- What an agent adds: many design variants and tags, which the tier caps and duplicate detection limit; Articore says it uses AI to "detect duplicate content" (report above, MEASURED).
- What kills it: about $7 a month per selling artist on average.
- Verdict: trap at this scale. Cheapest test: none worth the time.

### 7. Stock images made with AI

- What it is: license images to buyers through stock sites, paid per download. Designers and marketers pay.
- Platform rules: Shutterstock "will not allow AI-generated content to be submitted by contributors" ([Shutterstock](https://submit.shutterstock.com/help/en/articles/10594622-content-policy-updates-ai-generated-content), MEASURED). Getty "does not accept AI-generated visuals in our Creative and Editorial libraries" ([Getty Images](https://www.gettyimages.co.uk/company/ai-free-imagery-policy), MEASURED). Pond5 "does not allow the submission of AI-generated content" ([Pond5](https://www.pond5.com/help/en/articles/10086182-does-pond5-allow-ai-generated-content-for-licensing), MEASURED). Envato: authors "may not publish or submit AI-generated content, either as a standalone item or as the primary component" ([Envato author help](https://help.author.envato.com/hc/en-us/articles/13313674070681-AI-generated-content-policy-for-Market-and-Elements), MEASURED). Adobe Stock accepts it if labelled, lists "Submit multiple versions from the same prompt or similar iterations of a prompt" as a don't, and warns "Violations of this policy may result in the removal of content or the termination of the account" ([Adobe Stock generative AI guidelines](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/contributor/submit-your-content/submit-generative-ai-content/generative-ai-content-guidelines.html), MEASURED).
- Evidence and base rate: Shutterstock's 10-K does not state total contributor royalties or contributor counts; it says the top five contributors were under 4% of downloads in 2025 ([Shutterstock 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1549346/000154934626000008/sstk-20251231.htm), MEASURED). Per-contributor income is UNKNOWN.
- Typical dollars a month: UNKNOWN; AI images compete with every other prompter on the one big site that takes them (INFERENCE).
- What an agent adds: prompting and keywording at volume, which Adobe's near-duplicate rule limits.
- Verdict: trap. Only Adobe is open, and the rules forbid the volume play.

### 8. Stock footage the owner shoots, with agents doing the metadata

- What it is: the owner's own real video clips, licensed through stock sites; agents handle the tedious work of trimming, titling, keywording and submission prep. Editors and agencies pay per license.
- Evidence: Envato says it has paid authors over $1.3 billion in total and that 16 new authors passed $1 million in lifetime earnings in 2023 ([Envato 2023 Wrapped](https://author.envato.com/hub/2023-wrapped/), STATED by the platform, lifetime totals only). No median for footage contributors was found (UNKNOWN).
- Base rate: UNKNOWN.
- Typical dollars a month: UNKNOWN; small for a new portfolio (INFERENCE).
- Money and time: $0 if the owner has footage; review queues of unknown length (UNKNOWN).
- Platform rules: human-made footage is accepted on all the sites in entry 7; AI-generated footage is barred on Shutterstock, Pond5, Getty and Envato (sources in entry 7, MEASURED).
- What an agent adds: large; keywording and metadata are the tedious part. What needs the owner: shooting, releases for people and property, and the accounts.
- What kills it: low per-clip earnings and a slow ramp (INFERENCE).
- Cheapest honest test: if the owner has 50 or more existing clips, agents prepare metadata for an Adobe Stock or Pond5 batch in a day; record approvals and any sales over the following weeks. Expect first sales in weeks to months (INFERENCE).

### 9. AI music on streaming services

- What it is: upload AI-made tracks through a distributor and earn per stream. Streaming subscribers and advertisers pay through a shared pool.
- Enforcement: Spotify says it removed more than 75 million spammy tracks for serious or repeated violations and took more than 50 million slop and spam tracks out of recommendations this year ([Spotify newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/tag/update/), STATED by the platform). Deezer says about 90,000 fully AI-generated tracks a day passed 50% of all new music deliveries at peak in June 2026 ([Deezer, July 21, 2026](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/21/), STATED by the platform).
- Base rate and dollars: UNKNOWN, and with more than half of new uploads AI-made on one service, near zero for a new uploader (INFERENCE).
- Verdict: OUT as a volume play; it is the exact pattern being removed. Not tested further.

### 10. Templates, plugins, game and design assets on marketplaces

- What it is: sell digital assets that save other makers time: Unreal and Unity plugins, 3D kits, design templates, presets. Developers, studios and designers pay.
- Evidence, 3D and engines: Epic says Fab creators earned more than $24 million in 2025, across more than 20,000 publishers and over 420,000 live listings ([Fab 2025 Year in Review, January 22, 2026](https://www.unrealengine.com/news/fab-2025-year-in-review), MEASURED). That averages about $1,200 a year, or $100 a month, per publisher; the median is lower (MEASURED arithmetic; median INFERENCE). A seller blog puts the split at 88/12 in the seller's favour ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/fab-marketplace-12-month-retrospective-seller-2026), STATED).
- Evidence, Etsy digital and design: Etsy's 2025 10-K reports $10.46 billion of marketplace gross sales and 5.6 million active sellers ([Etsy 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1370637/000137063726000019/etsy-20251231.htm), MEASURED). That is about $1,870 a year, or $156 a month, of gross sales per active seller before fees, for physical and digital goods together; the median is UNKNOWN and lower (MEASURED arithmetic).
- Base rate: UNKNOWN on every marketplace; none publishes a median.
- Typical dollars a month: likely $0 to $100 for a new seller with a few items (INFERENCE from the averages).
- Money and time: $0 to list on Fab or Unity (UNKNOWN for current seller onboarding steps); Etsy charges $0.20 a listing and 6.5% of the price ([Etsy fees](https://www.etsy.com/legal/fees/), MEASURED). Review queues are UNKNOWN.
- Platform rules: Fab requires a "Created with AI" label, and "Content that's not identified properly with the 'Created with AI' setting violates Fab terms" (quoted from Epic in [Asset Forge Index](https://assetforgeindex.com/policies/fab), STATED); Epic says it "strengthened how we handle AI disclosures and spam prevention" in 2025 ([Fab year in review](https://www.unrealengine.com/news/fab-2025-year-in-review), MEASURED). Unity allows AI content if disclosed and rejects work that does "not provide a significant value to end users" ([Unity support](https://support.unity.com/hc/en-us/articles/16456407029524-Can-I-publish-and-sell-content-generated-with-AI-on-the-Asset-Store), MEASURED). TurboSquid "will not accept content ... that is generated by AI" ([TurboSquid](https://www.turbosquid.com/help/en/articles/12633683-turbosquid-s-policy-on-publishing-ai-generated-content), MEASURED). Etsy allows "Seller-prompted AI creations" with disclosure but bans "AI prompt bundles", "Bot-generated content" and "AI-generated functional instructions, such as craft patterns, recipes, and instructions" ([Etsy creativity standards](https://www.etsy.com/legal/creativity), [Etsy help](https://help.etsy.com/hc/en-us/articles/360024112614-What-Can-I-Sell-on-Etsy), MEASURED). Envato bars AI content as the main component (entry 7).
- Where the owner's skills change the odds: 3D modelling and Unreal or Unity work are scarce skills; a working engine plugin or a clean, well-documented kit is not something a prompt alone produces (INFERENCE).
- What an agent adds: code for plugins, documentation, demo scenes, LOD and format variants, thumbnails, listing copy, support replies. What needs the owner: the 3D work itself, quality judgment, the seller account.
- What kills it: thin averages ($100 a month per Fab publisher), sale-driven revenue (Epic says event sales drove up to half of a month's revenue), and a tripled listing count.
- Cheapest honest test: one Unreal editor tool or one 3D kit built in a week with agents writing code and docs, published on Fab with the AI label set correctly where it applies. Measure approval time, views and sales over two weeks after approval. Cost $0.

### 11. Small paid apps and tools (micro software, browser extensions, API products)

- What it is: a narrow paid tool, such as a browser extension, a small web app or an API, that saves a specific group time. Users or businesses pay a subscription or one-off fee.
- Evidence: RevenueCat's data from over 115,000 subscription apps says 17% of new apps reach $1,000 a month within two years and 4.6% reach $10,000, and one year after launch the middle half earn $16 to $429 a month ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-26-insights), STATED vendor dataset; it covers apps already using a subscription tool, so the true base rate is lower). TrustMRR, which lists startups with payment-provider-connected revenue, puts 68.5% of listed startups in its $0 to $1,000 band and the median all-time revenue of Stripe-connected startups at $240 ([TrustMRR stats](https://trustmrr.com/stats), STATED; it does not say what the band measures or how verification works, and founders choose to list). One dataset of 112 revenue-verified extensions says 57.1% earn $0 a month and 6.3% clear $1,000 ([BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/how-to-monetize-a-chrome-extension), STATED; the page names no payment providers and notes self-selection). Acquire.com reports SaaS businesses sold at a median 3.9 times profit in 2024 and 2025 ([Acquire.com, January 2026](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/), STATED by the marketplace).
- Base rate: about a sixth of new subscription apps reach $1,000 a month in two years; the median new product earns little or nothing (STATED, above).
- Typical dollars a month: $0 to a few hundred for most (STATED, above).
- Money and time: a domain and hosting under $50 (INFERENCE). Apple's developer fee and app review apply on iOS. The Chrome Web Store has had no payments since February 1, 2021, so extensions need an outside processor ([Chrome payments deprecation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/cws-payments-deprecation.md.txt), MEASURED). First dollar can come in days if a narrow paying audience is found (INFERENCE).
- Platform rules: Apple's guideline 4.3 says "Don't create multiple Bundle IDs of the same app" and "Don't submit apps that are indistinguishable from what's already widely available" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), MEASURED). Chrome bars "Multiple extensions with highly similar functionality, content, and user experience" ([Chrome Web Store spam FAQ](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/spam-faq/), MEASURED). Google Play bars apps "that serve only to spam users or Google Play" ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/topic/9876964), MEASURED). None of these bans AI-written code. Testing many distinct small products is allowed; cloning one product many times is not.
- Enforcement: in 2024 Apple reviewed 7,771,599 submissions, rejected 1,931,400 (378,300 under Design, which covers copycats, minimum functionality and spam), removed 82,509 apps (294 for spam) and terminated 146,747 developer accounts, nearly all for fraud ([Apple 2024 App Store Transparency Report](https://www.apple.com/legal/more-resources/docs/2024-App-Store-Transparency-Report.pdf), MEASURED).
- Where the owner's skills change the odds: he is a software engineer and designer, so agent-built code gets real review, design and debugging; that is the gap between a demo and a product people pay for (INFERENCE).
- What an agent adds: large. It can build, test and document several small distinct tools in the time a person builds one, answer support email, and watch errors. What needs the owner: choosing the problem, the payment account, any developer accounts, and talking to users.
- What kills it: finding buyers. Distribution, not code, is the usual failure, and spam-posting to find them is OUT (INFERENCE).
- Cheapest honest test: in two weeks, build two or three narrow tools for problems the owner has hit in his own software, design, 3D or film work, each with a free tier and one paid tier on a hosted checkout he opens. Post once, honestly, where those users already talk, as himself. Pass mark fixed in advance: at least one paying stranger within 14 days of launch. Cost: a domain or two.

### 12. Online courses

- What it is: recorded lessons sold on a marketplace like Udemy or on one's own site. Learners or their employers pay.
- Evidence: Udemy's 2025 10-K says instructors earned $168 million across more than 91,000 instructors, 11 earned over $1 million each, and "a significant portion" of revenue comes from "a limited number of our instructors" ([Udemy 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1607939/000160793926000034/udmy-20251231.htm), MEASURED). That averages about $1,850 a year, or $154 a month, per instructor, with the median well below (MEASURED arithmetic; median INFERENCE). Kajabi's figure of $5 billion lifetime sales by 60,000 creators is a 2023 press release with no median ([PR Newswire, March 13, 2023](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/kajabi-creators-reach-landmark-milestone-of-5-billion-in-total-earnings-301770735.html), STATED). Course sellers' income screenshots are STATED claims and are not counted.
- Base rate: UNKNOWN.
- Typical dollars a month: likely under $100 for a new instructor (INFERENCE from the Udemy average and concentration).
- Money and time: weeks to make a good course (INFERENCE).
- Platform rules: the Udemy 10-K states no AI policy for instructors and says Udemy offers AI tools to help instructors make courses (same source, MEASURED).
- What an agent adds: outlines, scripts, slides, captions. What needs the owner: real expertise and on-camera teaching; his film and 3D skills fit a niche technical course (INFERENCE).
- What kills it: concentration at the top, and buyers' distrust of AI-made courses (INFERENCE).
- Cheapest honest test: none quick; a course takes weeks before the first sale.
