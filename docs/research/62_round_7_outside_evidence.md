# Round 7: What outside evidence says about crypto trading

Status: CURRENT. Outside reading, not a test. Sources checked 2026-11-25. Where the scout and checker differ, the checker's reading of the cited source takes precedence. Ranks 5 to 7 are scout leads only and were not checked.

STATED means the linked paper or source reports it. UNKNOWN means the cited evidence does not establish it. Nothing here was measured by us. One basis point (bp, plural bps) is 0.01 percentage points.

## AI crypto traders with real money

- STATED: In Barton's DX Terminal Pro experiment with funded vaults trading Base memecoins, only 16.2% finished profitable ([Barton](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)).
- STATED: Barton's DXAP Hyperliquid fleet included real-money and paper fills; the paper reports a realized loss of $217,000 from June 8 to July 26, 2026 when restated at 5.5 bps per side, and a loss of $148,000 at zero fees ([Barton](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)). Zero fees is a recalculation, not another funded run, and the paper trades do not include slippage or funding ([Barton](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)).
- STATED: The same paper reports a median account return of +0.23% on positions held at least 24 hours and -0.15% on positions held under an hour ([Barton](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)). UNKNOWN: Whether requiring a longer hold would improve returns; the comparison groups trades by how long they actually lasted ([Barton](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.05663)).
- STATED: In a separate real-money OKX benchmark, the median return of four deployed methods was -0.56% versus +11.63% for holding bitcoin, and none beat bitcoin ([OKX benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510)). Qwen, the only large language model in that live group, gained 3.72% but still trailed bitcoin ([OKX benchmark](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510)).

## AI proposing factors, not placing orders

- STATED: Hu's preprint used GPT-5.4 to propose signals, then combined them into a rule rather than letting the model place orders ([Hu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2604.26747)). The backtest reports a 44.55% annualized long-short return and a Sharpe ratio of 1.55 after a 5 bps fee per side ([Hu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2604.26747)).
- STATED: The checker found that the paper calls the held-out period 2024 to 2026, but its data section ends in December 2025 ([Hu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2604.26747)). All five groups lost money on their own; the reported spread came from shorting small coins that fell more, not from a profitable buy side ([Hu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2604.26747)). The paper says weighting by market size performed poorly and gives no numerical results for it ([Hu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2604.26747)).
- STATED: The reported Sharpe ratios at 10, 20 and 30 bps in the fee sensitivity table fall to 0.57, 0.52 and 0.46 ([Hu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2604.26747)). UNKNOWN: How many coins were included, whether delisted coins were retained, and how much the newer model might already have known about the old sample ([Hu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2604.26747)). The paper does not discuss that cutoff risk or report a funded test ([Hu](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2604.26747)).

## Spot-perpetual basis and funding

Basis is the price gap between a derivative and the coin it tracks. Funding is a periodic transfer between holders of long and short perpetual positions. A narrow price gap and a small funding transfer are distinct observations.

- STATED: He and coauthors opened hedged positions when the price gap exceeded modeled round-trip costs and closed when it went back to zero ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v5)). Their historical Sharpe ratios ranged from 1.80 to 4.84 under their high-cost maker assumptions, with 6.75 bps on spot and 1.44 bps on futures per side ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v5)). The checker corrected the scout's common start date: the five surviving coins entered the sample on different dates in 2020, and all ended in March 2024 ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v5)). These were neither US retail taker fees nor a live or reported held-back result ([He et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888v5)).
- STATED: Fracassi reports a mean absolute bitcoin basis of 3.2 bps for the US Coinbase Derivatives perpetual over the launch comparison, versus 27.0 bps for the dated CME future ([Fracassi's CFTC comment](https://cesare-fracassi.github.io/files/pdf/Research/Fracassi_2026_CFTC_247_Perpetuals.pdf)). Separately, on offshore Coinbase International, bitcoin funding fell from 12.91% in 2024 to 5.99% in 2025 and 1.11% in 2026 through July ([Fracassi's CFTC comment](https://cesare-fracassi.github.io/files/pdf/Research/Fracassi_2026_CFTC_247_Perpetuals.pdf)). The checker distinguishes the US basis figure from the offshore funding history; neither is an after-cost strategy return ([Fracassi's CFTC comment](https://cesare-fracassi.github.io/files/pdf/Research/Fracassi_2026_CFTC_247_Perpetuals.pdf)).
- STATED: Zhivkov and coauthors use 20 bps as a plausible institutional round-trip cost floor for perpetual arbitrage, including fees, slippage and uncertainty about funding timing ([Zhivkov et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/14/5/103)). That is the study's assumption, not our cost estimate for a US account ([Zhivkov et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/14/5/103)). The scout also applied Kraken's non-US app fee to US fractional perpetuals; the US page quotes a per-contract rate, and the charge for a fractional order is UNKNOWN ([Kraken US futures fees](https://support.kraken.com/articles/us-futures-fees), [Kraken's separate app terms](https://support.kraken.com/articles/kraken-perps)).

## Monday Asia open and the trading clock

- STATED: The Concretum summary of the SFI research reports a gross Sharpe ratio of about 1.6 for its bitcoin intraday trend benchmark across 2018 to 2025, not for the Monday window by itself ([Concretum Group / SFI working paper](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/)). In the sample after mid-2020, benchmark performance improved around Sunday at 7 p.m. New York time and stayed elevated into Monday ([Concretum Group / SFI working paper](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/)). It reports no cost-adjusted figures and does not establish the same pattern on ether ([Concretum Group / SFI working paper](https://concretumgroup.com/seasonality-in-bitcoin-intraday-trend-trading/)).
- STATED: The checker found the scout cited the wrong follow-up for the US stock-session versus overnight claim ([Papers With Backtest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/blog/bitcoin-never-sleeps-exploiting-seasonality)). The linked account attributes that comparison to Padysak and Vojtko, and says Beluska and Vojtko extended trend-following and mean-reversion tests, not the session split ([Papers With Backtest](https://paperswithbacktest.com/blog/bitcoin-never-sleeps-exploiting-seasonality)).
- STATED: A separate funding study found a small time-of-day spread relative to trading costs and no strong weekend effect ([Zhivkov et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/14/5/103)). After CME moved to continuous trading in May 2026, a separate study still found a Sunday volume spike but no statistically significant net profit for its contrarian rule ([Runa Lee](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=7546659)). Neither paper directly replicates the Monday trend test ([Zhivkov et al.](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7072/14/5/103), [Runa Lee](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm?abstractid=7546659)).

## Scheduled token unlocks

- STATED: Tokenomist reports that negative funding was more common around large unlocks than on ordinary days for the same coins, but found no clear change for smaller unlocks ([Tokenomist research](https://tokenomist.ai/research/what-the-market-does-around-a-token-unlock)). It warns that unlocking does not mean selling, and that a crowded short can be squeezed or pay funding before prices fall ([Tokenomist research](https://tokenomist.ai/research/what-the-market-does-around-a-token-unlock)).
- STATED: A hand-collected study of major Binance-listed unlocks found that most coins fell within three days ([Kim](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6632838)). Other reports describe pressure before some unlocks and rallies when crowded shorts were squeezed ([Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/from-locked-to-liquidity-what-16000-token-unlocks-teach-us/), [6th Man Ventures](https://6thman.ventures/writing/token-unlocks)). UNKNOWN: A net trading edge; these reports do not establish a costed, held-back or live strategy that includes delisted coins ([Kim](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6632838), [Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/from-locked-to-liquidity-what-16000-token-unlocks-teach-us/), [6th Man Ventures](https://6thman.ventures/writing/token-unlocks), [Tokenomist research](https://tokenomist.ai/research/what-the-market-does-around-a-token-unlock)).
- STATED: The only dated historical source found in this check is paid: Tokenomist's point-in-time API shows the schedule as it was known on a past date ([Tokenomist API](https://docs.tokenomist.ai/api-documents/introduction)). DefiLlama has free upcoming schedules, not an archive of what those schedules showed before revisions ([DefiLlama Unlocks](https://defillama.com/unlocks)). UNKNOWN: Any free equivalent that would support an honest retrospective test ([Tokenomist API](https://docs.tokenomist.ai/api-documents/introduction), [DefiLlama Unlocks](https://defillama.com/unlocks)).
