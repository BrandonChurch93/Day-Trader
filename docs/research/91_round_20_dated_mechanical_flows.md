## Detailed Audit of Each Dated Mechanical Flow

### 1. Treasury Coupon and Principal Payment Dates Reinvested into Bond Funds (`TLT`, `IEF`)

- Mechanical flow and pre-announced size: STATED: US Treasury 3-year, 10-year, and 30-year notes and bonds pay semi-annual coupons and mature on the 15th of the month (with the largest quarterly refundings on February 15, May 15, August 15, and November 15), whereas 2-year, 5-year, and 7-year notes pay and mature at month-end ([TreasuryDirect Table of Treasury Securities](https://www.treasurydirect.gov/auctions/when-auctions-happen/table-of-treasury-securities/): "3-Year | Every Month | 15th of month", "10-Year | ... | 15th of month", "30-Year | ... | 15th of month", "2-Year, 5-Year, 7-Year | Every Month | End of month"). Maturing amounts are published weeks in advance, e.g. $96.3 billion maturing on August 15, 2026 ([US Treasury Quarterly Refunding Statement sb0590](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0590/): "The U.S. Department of the Treasury is offering $125 billion of Treasury securities to refund approximately $96.3 billion of privately-held Treasury notes and bonds maturing on August 15, 2026.").
- Relationship to Replay 25 and Plan 33: The month-end coupon and principal payment leg IS already inside Replay 25's month-end Treasury window (`docs/research/69_replay_25_treasury_month_end.md`). The auction-day supply/reversal leg is already frozen as Plan 33 (Queue row 52, `research_rig/PREREG_33_treasury_auctions.md`).
- Published prior and post-2016 evidence on mid-month (15th) dates: STATED: Hartley and Schwarz (2019, 1990 to 2018) examined within-month daily excess returns across US Treasury maturities and found no excess return outside the last few days of the month ([Hartley & Schwarz, Wharton Rodney White WP 17-19](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/17-19.Schwarz.pdf): "Average returns are positive and highly significant in the last few days of the month, and are not significantly different from zero at other times."). Structurally, on the 15th of refunding months, Treasury settles $125B+ of new issuance against ~$96B maturing, so net private-market supply is positive rather than negative.
- $500 whole-share reachability and costs: `TLT` ($82.39 on Nov 25, 2026; 6 shares = $494.34) is reachable at ~2 bps round trip, but there is no empirical edge on the 15th.
- Verdict: DROP (month-end leg is Replay 25; auction leg is Plan 33; mid-month 15th has zero documented excess return).

### 3. Municipal Fund Reinvestment in June, July, and December (`VTEB`, `MUB`)

- Mechanical flow and pre-announced size: STATED: Distinct from monthly index rebalancing (Queue row 69), municipal bonds concentrate semi-annual coupons, maturities, and call redemptions on June 1, July 1, and December 1. June and July account for up to 60% of annual municipal redemptions ([VanEck](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/blogs/municipal-bonds/finding-yield-opportunities-in-renewed-volatility/): "June and July represent the heaviest period of maturing bonds and coupon payments and these two months normally represent up to 60% of annual redemptions."), roughly doubling monthly cash returning to investors from $30B to $35B to $55B to $60B ([AAM](https://www.aamlive.com/blog/202406/aam-viewpoints-with-increased-summer-demand-where-should-investors-look-for-yield): "For most of the year, the monthly total debt service has been $30-$35 billion, but beginning in June, that number almost doubled to $57 billion returning to investors."). Net negative supply (30-day scheduled redemptions minus visible new issuance, published in advance by Bloomberg and Bond Buyer) frequently hits -$20B to -$54B ([Western Asset](https://www.westernasset.com/us/en/research/blog/weekly-municipal-monitor-riding-the-redemption-wave-2023-08-22.cfm): "In addition to lower than average new-issue supply, market redemptions and maturities were an above-average $112 billion, contributing to negative net supply of $54 billion."; [Western Asset, Jul 2026](https://www.westernasset.com/us/en/research/blog/weekly-municipal-monitor-summer-technicals-2026-07-21.cfm): "From 2015 through 2023, the municipal market recorded negative net supply, on average, from June through September."). December also carries elevated reinvestment cash (~12% to 14% above October; [Seeking Alpha, Nov 2026](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4971947-thoughts-from-the-municipal-bond-desk)).
- Post-2016 empirical evidence and limitations: STATED: Over 2016 to 2026, `MUB` average monthly price returns were +0.17% in June (8 of 11 positive), +0.27% in July (6 of 11 positive), and +0.19% in December (5 of 10 positive) ([Yahoo Finance MUB Seasonality, Nov 2026](https://www.finance.yahoo.com/quote/MUB/seasonality/)), while 10-year total returns (including ~0.27%/month income) averaged +0.34% in June (8/10), +0.69% in July (8/10), and +0.59% in December (7/10) ([SeasonalityX, Nov 2026](https://seasonalityx.com/stocks/mub-seasonality)). However, (a) no peer-reviewed study isolates post-2016 `MUB`/`VTEB` summer reinvestment alpha net of Treasury duration (`IEF`), (b) heavy municipal issuance in 2025 and 2026 overwhelmed reinvestment cash, driving `MUB` price returns of -0.58% in July 2025 and -1.84% in July 2026 ([Parametric, 2025](https://www.parametricportfolio.com/-/media/files/insights-and-research/municipal-bond-market-insight/27547-parametric-municipal-market-insight---glass-half-full.pdf): "Although we did experience the year's highest amount of reinvestment cash, there was no supply dwindling to speak of for most of the summer."), and (c) there are only 2 to 3 windows per year (20 to 30 observations in 2016 to 2026), far below our statistical sample-size bar.
- $500 whole-share reachability and costs: STATED: Reachable cleanly via `VTEB` at $48.92 (10 shares = $489.20, ~98% deployed) or `MUB` at $104.19 (4 shares = $416.76) as of Nov 25, 2026 ([BriMindInvest](https://brimindinvest.com/compare/mub-vs-vteb): "MUB ... $104.19", "VTEB ... $48.92"), at ~2 to 4 bps round trip.
- Verdict: WATCH (too few observations for a standalone test and unverified net of Treasury duration; fold into Queue row 69 if munis are ever checked for a residual beyond Treasury exposure).

### 4. Japan Fiscal Year-End Repatriation in March (`FXY`, `EWJ`)

- Mechanical flow: Japanese corporations, insurers, and banks close their fiscal year on March 31, creating a popular market narrative of yen-strengthening repatriation flows in late February and March ([PipTheory, Nov 2026](https://www.piptheory.com/research/forex-seasonality): "Japan's 31 March fiscal year-end historically creates a yen-strengthening bias in late February-March.").
- Published prior and post-2016 evidence: STATED: Direct empirical testing of Japanese cross-border flows and yen carry in March refutes the narrative ([Rosewood Trading](https://www.rosewoodtrading.com/uploads/5/8/0/2/58027535/atrad43.pdf): "What happens, though, when we combine the purchase and sale data into a net flows number? Here statistically significant seasonality disappears." and "First, the seasonality is statistically insignificant and therefore no better than random. Second, it is the dollar that is (insignificantly) stronger in March; the yen's strength, such as it is, comes in September-November."). Structurally, Japanese manufacturers moved production overseas and reinvested primary income abroad, eroding March corporate repatriation ([Reuters, 2014](https://www.reuters.com/article/economy/yen-repatriation-fiesta-becomes-legacy-as-more-japan-exporters-move-abroad-idUSBREA2Q0FK/): "Repatriation still occurs during this season, but it no longer resembles the vibrant market activity we observed in the past"; [JRI Research Journal, Sept 2026](https://www.jri.co.jp/en/MediaLibrary/file/english/periodical/jrirj/2026/26/ishikawa.pdf): "the weak domestic repatriation of earnings from overseas securities and direct investment, reflecting reinvestment abroad"). Moreover, March 31 occurs only once per year (10 events in 2016 to 2026).
- $500 whole-share reachability and costs: `FXY` and `EWJ` fit $500, with ~4 to 10 bps round-trip spreads, but there is no statistically significant edge.
- Verdict: DROP (statistically indistinguishable from random; structural offshoring weakened corporate flows; only 10 events in 10 years).

### 5. Target-Date Fund Quarter-End Rebalancing After Large Equity/Bond Divergences (`SPLG`, `TLT`)

- Relationship to Plan 41: STATED: This IS the same rebalancing mechanism already frozen in **Plan 41** (`research_rig/PREREG_41_pension_month_end.md`, Queue row 54: choose the laggard between `SPY` and `TLT` by the fifth-last close of each month, buy fourth-last close and sell last close, judged against always-TLT across 130 months).
- Published prior and post-2016 evidence: STATED: Parker, Schoar, and Sun (JF 2023, 2008 to 2018, [NBER w28028](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28028/w28028.pdf)) showed TDFs rebalance ~80% of predicted equity drift and 60% to 70% of bond drift over one to two quarters, while noting aggregate market effects from TDFs alone are likely modest ([SSRN 5122748](https://www.edhec.edu/sites/default/files/2026-03/Scientific%20paper.%20ssrn-5122748.pdf): "Parker, Schoar, and Sun (2023) find that, while rebalancing by target date funds (TDFs) influences the cross-sectional pattern of returns across stocks, the aggregate effects are likely to be negligible given the current size of TDFs."). Harvey, Mazzoleni, and Melone (March 2025/2026, [NBER w33554 / SSRN 5122748](https://www.edhec.edu/sites/default/files/2026-03/Scientific%20paper.%20ssrn-5122748.pdf)) modeled market-wide 60/40 Threshold and Calendar rebalancing and found: "a one-standard-deviation increase in the Threshold (Calendar) signal leads to a decrease in equity returns of approximately 16 basis points (bps) (17 bps) and an increase in bond returns of about 4 bps (2 bps) over the next trading day" and "both signals' predictive power and economic significance increase toward the quarter-end." Conversely, an independent 2003 to 2020 `SPY`/`AGG` quarter-end test found no exploitable anticipatory effect ([Humble Student of the Markets](https://humblestudentofthemarkets.com/2021/03/28/will-portfolio-rebalancing-sink-equities/): "The surprising result from this study was there was little or no anticipatory rebalancing effect... The study also shows that there was no significant rebalancing effect on bond market returns, either before or after quarter-end.").
- Why not queue separately: Restricting Plan 41 to quarter-ends cuts the 2016 to 2026 sample from 130 months to ~40 quarter-ends (and only ~10 to 15 large-divergence quarter-ends), which is too small to judge independently. Also note on $500 whole shares, `SPY` ($714.54) is unreachable and `SPLG` ($80.46, 6 shares) is required for the equity leg alongside `TLT` ($82.39, 6 shares).
- Verdict: DO NOT QUEUE SEPARATELY (same mechanism as Plan 41; wait for Plan 41's frozen monthly run, and check quarter-end amplification only as a secondary diagnostic if Plan 41 passes).

### 6. VIX Futures Monthly Settlement (`SVIX`, `SVXY`, `VXX`, `VIXY`, `UVXY`)

- Mechanical flow: Monthly VIX futures and options cash-settle on the Wednesday that is 30 days prior to the next month's third Friday SPX expiration against the Special Opening Quotation (SOQ / `VRO`) computed from opening SPX option prices ([DailyTickers, Oct 2026](https://articles.dailytickers.com/series/mastering-the-vix/part4-trading/): "VIX futures are cash-settled against the VIX Special Opening Quotation (SOQ), which is calculated from the opening prices of SPX options on settlement day."). (Distinct from Round 14 Candidate 8, which dropped daily VIX ETP roll rebalancing.)
- Published prior and post-2016 evidence: STATED: First, US-listed VIX ETPs (`VXX`, `VIXY`, `UVXY`, `SVXY`, `SVIX`) maintain a constant 30-day weighted maturity by rolling daily from M1 to M2, so they hold 0% weight in the expiring contract on settlement morning and do NOT experience a forced settlement-day rebalance. Second, empirical SOQ analysis shows the opening distortion is an unexecutable auction print that dissipates within minutes ([Tilburg University Thesis](http://arno.uvt.nl/show.cgi?fid=192324): "For standard settlement days, series that open above VRO ('Standard Positive') show mildly negative returns in the first few intervals, while those that open below VRO ('Standard Negative') show mildly positive returns; in both cases, the average 3-minute changes are small and quickly fluctuate around zero."). Third, Hu and Jacobs (JFQA 2026, 2004 to 2022, [JFQA PDF](https://jfqa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Expected__Realized_Returns_on_Volatility.pdf): "the average returns to holding VIX futures are negative regardless of the horizon") confirmed that VIX futures returns are driven by persistent contango/volatility risk premia rather than a settlement-day forced flow.
- $500 whole-share reachability and risk: `SVIX` ($17.22 on Nov 24, 2026; [Perplexity Finance](https://www.perplexity.ai/finance/SVIX/historical-data?period=2026-01-01~2026-12-31)) fits $500, but fell -32.73% in November 2026 alone and carries catastrophic tail-blowup risk during volatility spikes (Feb 2018, Mar 2020, Aug 2024).
- Verdict: DROP (VIX ETPs have zero expiring-contract exposure on SOQ morning; SOQ distortion is unexecutable and vanishes in minutes; extreme tail risk).

### 7. Currency-Hedged ETF Month-End FX Forward Rolls (`HEFA`, `DXJ`, `HEDJ`, `HEWJ`)

- Mechanical flow: Currency-hedged international equity ETFs (`HEFA` $45.39, `HEWJ` $59.51, `DXJ`, `HEDJ`) track indexes that roll 1-month FX forward contracts on the last trading day of each month using weights fixed two weekdays prior ([MSCI FX Hedge Methodology](https://www.msci.com/eqb/methodology/meth_docs/MSCI_FX_Hedge_Global_Currency_Methodology_Aug2021.pdf): "The MSCI FX Hedge Indexes are rebalanced monthly on the last trading day of the month, when the index will take into account the effect of rolling into new 1-month Forward contracts based on the newly determined weights of currency to be sold for the next month's index calculation.").
- Relationship to Round 14 and post-2016 evidence: STATED: The directional spot-FX hedge-rebalancing flow into the month-end London 4 p.m. fix (Melvin & Prins 2015) was ALREADY DROPPED in Round 14 (`docs/research/83_round_14_treasury_month_end_relatives.md`) after a 2005 to 2026 replication failed (EUR/USD -1,911 pips, other pairs flat). The separate forward-roll pricing distortion (Covered Interest Parity / cross-currency basis widening across quarter-ends due to bank balance-sheet reporting) is real but tiny: Du, Tepper, and Verdelhan (JF 2018, [AEA WP](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2018/preliminary/paper/8sr7nGYG): "the month-end deviation relative to the rest of the quarter is on average 3 to 5 basis point higher post-crisis than the level pre-crisis and increases by another 8 basis point in the post-2015 sample") and Accominotti, Cen, and Chambers (July 2025, 1921 to 2025, [NBER Conf Paper](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f226460.pdf): "On average, CIP deviations increase by 2 basis points (bps) at quarter-end compared to non-quarter-end periods.").
- $500 whole-share reachability: `HEFA` ($45.39 on Nov 24, 2026, 11 shares = $499.29; [iShares](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/259622/ishares-currency-hedged-msci-eafe-etf)) and `HEWJ` ($59.51; [Perplexity Finance](https://www.perplexity.ai/finance/HEWJ)) fit $500, but holding a currency-hedged equity ETF exposes $500 to 15% to 20% annual equity volatility and cannot isolate a 2 to 8 bps annualized FX forward basis.
- Verdict: DROP (directional FX leg already failed in Round 14; CIP basis is 2 to 8 bps annualized and unreachable via long-only equity ETFs).

### 8. Large ETF Capital-Gain Distribution Dates (December)

- Mechanical flow: Registered investment companies must distribute at least 98.2% of net realized capital gains by December to avoid excise tax ([UMB](https://www.umb.com/insights/fund-services-insight-mutual-fund-year-end-distributions): "Managers must distribute at least 98.2% of net short-term and net long-term capital gains during this period."). Mutual funds post estimates in November and pay in December, when NAV drops by the payout and enrolled shareholders automatically reinvest.
- Published prior and post-2016 evidence: STATED: Almost no liquid US ETFs pay meaningful capital-gain distributions because they purge low-basis shares tax-free via in-kind creation/redemption baskets ([Moussawi, Shen, & Velthuis, 2023, 1993 to 2017](https://haslam.utk.edu/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Moussawi-ETF_Tax.pdf): "In 2017, for example, around 65% of U.S. equity mutual funds distributed capital gains, while only 6.1% of ETFs have non-zero capital gains distributions."). On ETF ex-dividend dates generally, prices adjust cleanly without abnormal returns ([Wiley JFR 2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1475-6803.2011.01308.x): "We document that for exchange-traded funds (ETFs), the price falls on average by the dividend amounts on the ex-dividend day... transaction costs and risk are indeed negligible for ETFs"). For open-end mutual funds that do pay large December capital gains (e.g., 20% to 25% of NAV in Dec 2026; [Morningstar, Nov 2026](https://www.morningstar.com/funds/is-your-fund-about-hand-you-big-tax-bill)), reinvestments settle internally at end-of-day NAV with the fund sponsor rather than creating open-market secondary-market ETF buying pressure. (Discounted closed-end fund DRIP pay dates, where open-market buying does occur, are already tracked in Queue row 70.)
- Verdict: DROP (94% of ETFs pay zero capital gains; ETF ex-days show no abnormal return; mutual fund DRIPs settle at NAV).

### 9. Bonus A: US Treasury Liquidity Support Buyback Operations (`TLT`, `IEF`)

- Mechanical flow and pre-announced size: STATED: The US Treasury relaunched regular debt buybacks in May 2024 on a pre-announced quarterly schedule (1:40 to 2:00 PM ET operation window) and doubled long-end liquidity-support ceilings in September 2026 to up to $6B per operation in 10Y to 20Y and 20Y to 30Y nominal coupons ([US Treasury Press Release sb0607](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0607): "The U.S. Department of the Treasury is increasing, by at least double, the size of liquidity support buyback operations for longer-dated nominal coupon securities"; [Tentative Buyback Schedule, Nov 4, 2026](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/221/Tentative-Buyback-Schedule.pdf)). Through November 19, 2026, Treasury accepted ~$528B across 175 operations ([500metrics](https://500metrics.com/treasury/buybacks/results)).
- Published evidence (2025 to 2026): STATED: IMF Working Paper 2025/088 ([IMF WP 2025/088](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2025/088/article-A001-en.xml): "Bid and ask prices rise by approximately 10 and 4 cents following listing and purchase, respectively.") found ~4 to 10 bps price lifts concentrated in the specific off-the-run CUSIPs listed for repurchase. At the aggregate Treasury market level, CME Group (Oct 2026, [CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/insights/economic-research/2026/can-treasury-buyback-reverse-bearish-sentiment-in-bonds.html): "would represent only an additional 0.4% of total outstanding marketable U.S. debt and roughly 2% of the amount of longer-term debt issued into the market") showed total buybacks remain small relative to supply. Crucially, the program has only ~2.5 years of history (May 2024 to Nov 2026), and a $500 ETF account (`TLT` $82.39) cannot buy individual off-the-run Treasury CUSIPs.
- Verdict: WATCH (genuine pre-announced government buyer, but only 2.5 years of history and primary ~4 to 10 bps lift is in off-the-run individual bonds rather than `TLT`).

### 10. Bonus B: IRS September 15 Defined-Benefit Pension Funding Deadline (`TLT`, `LQD`)

- Mechanical flow: STATED: IRC Section 430(j) sets a hard deadline of 8.5 months after calendar plan year-end (September 15) for sponsors to deposit minimum required DB pension contributions, subject to a 10% excise tax ([Fidus Actuarial, Nov 2026](https://fidusactuarial.com/insights/minimum-required-contribution-september-15-deadline/): "Section 430(j)(1) sets the due date for any payment of any minimum required contribution at 8 1/2 months after the close of the plan year. For a calendar-year plan that is September 15.").
- Evidence and limits: STATED: Goldman Sachs and Citi documented elevated corporate pension buying of long-duration bonds and STRIPS ahead of September 15, 2018 ([CNBC, Oct 2018](https://www.cnbc.com/2018/10/08/us-markets-bond-yields-are-moving-higher-but-may-fall.html): "companies increasing their defined benefit contributions through purchases of long-term bonds before the September 15 corporate tax deadline, which allowed them to benefit from higher tax deductions based on 2017 tax rates."), but that surge was a one-off arbitrage ahead of the 2018 TCJA corporate tax-rate cut (35% to 21%). UNKNOWN: No peer-reviewed study documents a recurring annual September 15 excess return in `TLT` or `LQD`, and the calendar yields only 1 event/year (10 observations in 2016 to 2026).
- Verdict: DROP (1 event/year; 2018 surge was a one-off tax-rate arbitrage).
