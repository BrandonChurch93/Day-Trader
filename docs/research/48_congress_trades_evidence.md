# 48. Following members of Congress: the data, the real-money funds and the published evidence

Status: gathered 2026-11-25 by a helper; the fund figures were measured separately by the builder from our own price feed and agree. No return has been worked out on the collected trades; that is replay 12 (`research_rig/PREREG_12_congress_followers_DRAFT.md`). The trade list and the scripts are on the research machine, not in the repo.

Why this was done: the owner, 2026-11-25 (`docs/DECISIONS.md`): "I hear you really can't go wrong by following them".

## In plain words

- The record exists and is good enough to test: 85,291 disclosed stock and option trades from 2019 to 2026, each with the day of the trade and the day it was filed.
- A follower is always late. The middle filing comes 28 days after the trade. The law then allows up to 30 more days before the filing has to be public. The day a filing could first be read online is not recorded anywhere we found, so the test uses three entry days: the day after filing, five trading days later, and 30 days later.
- Real money has tried it since February 2023. The fund that copies Democratic members made +85.29% to 2026-11-24, the Republican one +61.04%, and SPY +81.50%.
- Every published test that buys after the filing, on thousands of trades, found no gain over the market for the average disclosed purchase. The famous Pelosi figures (65.5% in 2023, 70.9% in 2024) are a tracker's estimate of the household's own account from the day of each trade, not what a follower got.
- Still open, and what replay 12 tests: whether a small group (large purchases, or members whose earlier trades did best) does better than the average.
- STATED, for the owner if this ever goes near real money: federal law restricts using these disclosures for a commercial purpose ([5 U.S.C. 13107(c)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title5/html/USCODE-2024-title5-partIV-chap131-subchapI-sec13107.htm)). Whether trading one's own account counts is UNKNOWN to us and is a question for a lawyer, not for this repo.

## Data and point-in-time rules

- **MEASURED** (this project): trades.csv contains 85,291 rows: 84,129 stocks and 1,162 stock options, with transaction dates January 2, 2019 through November 19, 2026 and filing dates January 2, 2019 through November 24, 2026, from the pinned [point-in-time archive](https://huggingface.co/datasets/austin-starks/congressional-stock-trades/tree/fb9d784a63173c320179792eeb99227613bef114).
- **MEASURED** (this project): The median filing lag is 28 calendar days, the 90th percentile is 48 days, and 9,753 rows (11.44%) are more than 45 days late by that simple date comparison; the 10th and 99th percentiles are 12 and 562 days, and the range is 0 to 1,932 days ([point-in-time archive](https://huggingface.co/datasets/austin-starks/congressional-stock-trades/tree/fb9d784a63173c320179792eeb99227613bef114)). **STATED**: The requirement is usually 30 days from awareness and no later than 45 days from the transaction; the delay statistic is not a finding of legal violations, and the long tail can include amendments, late/backfilled reports, or dates qualified by the parser ([House Ethics calculator](https://ethics.house.gov/periodic-transaction-report-calculator/), [archive methodology](https://huggingface.co/datasets/austin-starks/congressional-stock-trades/blob/main/METHODOLOGY.md)).
- **STATED**: The source uses the official House filing-index date and Senate eFD filed date, with an as-of timestamp at the end of the Eastern date. It does not independently observe when reports appeared online; House Ethics says member PTRs must be posted within 30 days of filing, and federal law generally requires public access within 30 days of receipt ([point-in-time archive](https://huggingface.co/datasets/austin-starks/congressional-stock-trades), [House Ethics instructions](https://ethics.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/7-8-2026-2025-Published-Instruction-Guide.pdf), [5 U.S.C. § 13107](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title5/html/USCODE-2024-title5-partIV-chap131-subchapI-sec13107.htm)). **UNKNOWN**: First historical web-publication time is not verified; next-session entry is the optimistic filing-date proxy, not proof of a tradable signal, and the conservative fallback is the first trading session strictly after filing plus 30 calendar days, assuming compliance with posting rules ([House Ethics instructions](https://ethics.house.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/7-8-2026-2025-Published-Instruction-Guide.pdf)). **MEASURED** (this project): The file retains version 1 only and does not move 114 later event versions back in time; a simulation using subsequent amendments should import the version history separately and apply changes only when those versions became available ([point-in-time archive](https://huggingface.co/datasets/austin-starks/congressional-stock-trades)).

## Academic and other published evidence

- **MEASURED** (Ziobrowski et al., Senate, 2004): 1993–1998 transactions, synthetic purchases/outperformance measured from original trade dates, not disclosure; the reported headline was near 1% per month, or about 12% a year, with an imputed portfolio and no cost deduction verified. A later reanalysis says the largest Senate result depended on specification and does not show advantage for the average senator ([original Senate paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/abnormal-returns-from-the-common-stock-investments-of-the-us-senate/A39406479940758D59E09FDCB8EE9BEC), [Eggers and Hainmueller](https://j-hai.github.io/assets/pdf/capitol.pdf)).
- **MEASURED** (Ziobrowski et al., House, 2011): 1985–2001 transactions; synthetic trade-date purchase portfolio beat the market about 0.5% monthly, or 6% annually; cost deduction not verified. A later reanalysis points out that stocks lawmakers sold did about as well as those bought, so the long-only headline did not establish informed timing ([original House paper](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/buspol/v13y2011i01p1-22_00.html), [Eggers and Hainmueller](https://j-hai.github.io/assets/pdf/capitol.pdf)).
- **MEASURED** (Eggers and Hainmueller, 2013): 2004–2008; synthetic transaction-day buy and sell portfolios held for 255 trading days, with no stable positive hedged result. Reconstructed holdings underperformed a passive index by 2–3% annually before expenses (−2.8% annualized estimated alpha, confidence interval −4.9% to −0.5%); those are reconstructions, not a post-filing entry strategy ([Capitol Losses](https://j-hai.github.io/assets/pdf/capitol.pdf)).
- **MEASURED** (Belmont, Sacerdote, Sehgal and Van Hoek, 2022): January 2012–December 2020; post-transaction benchmark comparisons found no superior performance, including among senators specifically accused of informed trading. At six months, House buys underperformed by 26 basis points and stocks sold underperformed by 11 basis points; this is not a cost-adjusted follower return and a trading-cost deduction was not verified ([published study abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/pubeco/v207y2022ics0047272722000044.html), [authors’ earlier working paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26975/w26975.pdf)).
- **MEASURED** (Wei and Zhou, 2025 working paper): 1995–2021 trade-level sample of 20 leadership entrants with matched controls; 250 trading days after transaction, the estimated effect of taking office was +47.4 percentage points for buy timing and +41.7 for sell timing versus matched ordinary members, not a 47-point tradable market premium. In a separate post-STOCK Act disclosure-date test, the equal-weight leader-buy alpha was +0.042% per trading day after ascension, versus −0.019% for regular buys; it enters on the disclosure day, not demonstrably after it, and no implementation costs are deducted in the reported regressions ([Captain Gains, Tables 2 and 11](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34524/w34524.pdf)).
- **MEASURED** (Molk and Partnoy, 2025): PTR filings July 2012–May 15, 2023, 587 bearish trades including 116 identified short sales; reported benchmark-adjusted gains for bearish trades peaked around 1–2% approximately 10–15 trading days after transaction. The short-sale targets fell about 1.34–1.35% in one set of seven-day windows centered on disclosure, with weak significance; the window includes pre-publication days, and the results do not deduct borrow or execution costs or price the options themselves ([Negative Trading in Congress](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/ilj/article/download/58197/58929/144444)).
- **MEASURED** (Legislative Trades, 2025 working paper): 4,254 reports covering 2014–2022 House disclosures; purchases are associated with 17–29 basis points of higher stock return on the disclosure day, not a costed next-session strategy. The event-day approach cannot promise that a retail entrant booked that move, and no net trading-cost number was verified ([Legislative Trades](https://economics.indiana.edu/news-events/conferences-and-workshops/legislative_trades_v3_20250921.pdf)).
- **MEASURED** (Houghton, Wharton thesis, 2026): for disclosures in 2022–2024, mean equal-weight abnormal returns were 0.00% for buys and 0.03% for sells around publication in a two-trading-day window; some larger-trade results were positive. The window crosses publication and is not an executable after-filing portfolio; trading costs were not reported ([Wharton thesis](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0ff4c92b-d958-49a9-bb3b-16577e3b5ce5/content)).

## Tests a follower could have implemented

- **MEASURED** (preregistered public working paper): 3,942 House purchases by 124 members in 2020–2025; a hypothetical 62-trading-day holding rule entering on the first session after filing returned −1.63% annual Carhart alpha (95% interval −6.5% to +3.2%, p = 0.51), or −1.66% at next-session open. The record does not specify a deduction for trading costs ([Zenodo working paper](https://zenodo.org/records/24185824)).
- **MEASURED** (Equibles, vendor research): 17,859 events from filings January 2020 through August 2025, entering on the first close strictly after filing. Median one-year market-relative price return was −5.36 percentage points for disclosed buys and −6.39 for stocks lawmakers sold; the difference was not significant, and dividends, costs and some split/delisting cases are absent ([Equibles](https://equibles.com/research/what-happens-after-congress-buys-or-sells-a-stock-evidence-from-17-859-disclosures)).
- **MEASURED** (Yield Theory, vendor research): 4,967 purchases transacted June 2024 through September 2026, buying at the first close after filing and using dividend-adjusted prices. Median returns relative to SPY were −0.6, −1.9, −3.5 and −8.1 points at 30, 90, 180 and 365 calendar days; at 180 days 42% beat SPY and the average excess return was −0.5 points before costs ([Yield Theory](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/does-copying-congress-stock-trades-work)).

## Pelosi: family estimates versus copying after disclosure

- **MEASURED** (tracker estimate): The widely repeated 65.5% return for 2023 and 70.9% return for 2024 (against a 24.9% SPY comparison in 2024) came from Unusual Whales’ reconstructed performance, not an audit or a return followers locked in ([Unusual Whales 2023 report](https://unusualwhales.com/politics/article/congress-trading-report-2023), [ThinkAdvisor’s 2023 coverage](https://www.thinkadvisor.com/2024/01/03/a-very-bullish-2023-for-many-in-congress-report), [Xinhua’s 2024 coverage](https://www.bignewsnetwork.com/news/274924996/dozens-of-us-lawmakers-beat-wall-street-gains-in-2024-report)).
