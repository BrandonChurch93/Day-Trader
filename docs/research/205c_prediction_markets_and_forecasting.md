# 205c. Prediction markets and paid forecasting

Status: Public rules, filings and published results checked November 27, 2026, Mountain Time. This adds access, incentives, wallet-level evidence and non-trading income routes to [the earlier prediction-market reading](53_prediction_markets_checked.md). No market feeds, bets, accounts, submissions or payouts were touched.

INFERENCE: The best shot at a first dollar is to sell one narrow, source-checked research brief, with the owner doing the selling and reviewing. The best fit for a mostly autonomous agent is a free, explicitly bot-eligible forecasting contest, but two weeks buys a score, not a paycheck. Reviewing contract wording for bounties is a plausible small side bet on time, not money. Retail trading and market-maker rebates are not reliable quick money. No route here has a defensible estimate of recurring income for a new Idaho entrant.

## Idaho: access is not the same as legal certainty

- MEASURED: Idaho's constitution prohibits gambling except for specified exceptions, and Idaho Code § 18-3801 includes staking value on events, including sports, in its definition, with exceptions for bona fide skill contests and business transactions, among others ([Idaho Constitution art. III, § 20](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idconst/ArtIII/Sect20/), [Idaho Code § 18-3801](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title18/t18ch38/sect18-3801/)).
- STATED: The CFTC says federally regulated prediction markets can operate in all 50 states; Idaho's attorney general joined a 39-state coalition arguing that federal law does not displace state authority over sports betting and specifically identified Kalshi and Crypto.com ([CFTC consumer guidance](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnandProtect/PredictionMarkets), [Idaho Attorney General](https://www.ag.idaho.gov/newsroom/ag-labrador-defends-idahos-authority-to-regulate-sports-betting/)). MEASURED: The Supreme Court docket in the separate New Jersey Kalshi dispute showed a pending petition with a response due December 7; that is not a final national ruling or an Idaho injunction ([Supreme Court docket 26-299](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/26-299.html)).
- INFERENCE: Treat Idaho sports contracts as contested, not as cleared by a signup screen. I found no Idaho-specific injunction or state licensing decision establishing that the owner can lawfully trade them. Non-sports products on actual regulated venues and free skill contests have a stronger case, but check exact products and enrollment terms before any funding ([Congressional Research Service](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13187), [Idaho statute](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title18/t18ch38/sect18-3801/)).
- MEASURED: U.S. prizes generally go on the recipient's tax return; a service fee is not a prize, and a trading rebate, incentive coupon or event-contract gain is not necessarily taxed the same way as either. Keep the payment and cost records and confirm treatment of trading products before filing ([IRS Publication 525](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p525)).

## Trading venues and what they actually pay for

### Kalshi: retail liquidity bounty, not a reason to bet

- What / payer: Trade against other participants on a regulated exchange; Kalshi separately pays for useful resting orders. This pays for quotes that others can actually execute, not for being right about events ([CFTC consumer guidance](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnandProtect/PredictionMarkets), [Kalshi liquidity incentives](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)).
- Access / fees: MEASURED: The venue has a U.S. individual enrollment path, and most regular U.S. members can enter its retail program; no Idaho exclusion was identified in the public terms checked. The schedule gives the general taker formula as multiplier × 0.07 × contracts × p × (1 − p), subject to rounding and series-specific terms; the stated maker formula where applicable uses 0.0175 instead, and there is no settlement fee ([Kalshi individual enrollment](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823778-signing-up-as-an-individual), [Kalshi liquidity incentives](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program), [fee schedule](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf)). Sports: contested in Idaho, as above.
- Automation: MEASURED: “Use of Kalshi APIs is expressly limited to facilitating a members own trading on the Exchange; all other usages are disallowed and may result in account suspension.” The data clauses are not a free standing licence for a private research-dataset project or a commercial dashboard ([Kalshi Developer Agreement](https://assets.kalshi.com/Kalshi-Developer-Agreement.pdf)).
- Retail evidence: MEASURED: The open Liquidity Incentive Program lists $1 to $1,000 per market per day, $1 minimum per market program, generally daily credits, and an advertised January 1, 2027 end. Orders can qualify without filling; snapshots can be excluded for insufficient two-sided depth, and advertised pools are cut by the share of excluded snapshots. This is not the Volume Incentive Program, which the termination filing ends by October 13, 2026 ([Kalshi retail program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program), [CFTC liquidity filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules07152610358.pdf), [CFTC volume termination filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09282630083.pdf)).
- Designated maker evidence: MEASURED: A separate Liquidity Provider Program requires an executed Market Maker Agreement and series selection; the approved provider must meet the quoting requirements and can bid the minimum reward it would accept. The filed cap is $50,000 per incentivized series per week, not an entitlement or a paid total ([Kalshi designated program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/15410219-liquidity-provider-program), [CFTC filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules0518263592.pdf)).
- Base rate / monthly / first dollar: UNKNOWN realized program-wide payouts, share of users paid, median, and small-account monthly net. Even next-day credit, if earned, is not durable profit. INFERENCE: A funded live test would tie up capital and take on filled-order risk; use $0 now ([Kalshi retail program](https://help.kalshi.com/en/articles/13823851-liquidity-incentive-program)).
- Agent / killer / cheapest test: An agent could monitor stale quotes and keep a ledger; the owner must be the member and control permissions, money and risk. Adverse fills, crowded qualifying depth, reward minimums and short program dates kill thin margins. In a week, build a made-up order ledger and stress the published reward formula down to zero plus adverse fills; pass only if the hypothetical net survives, without touching an API or placing an order.

### Polymarket US: direct API, but not the offshore venue

- What / payer: QCX LLC is the U.S. exchange and QC Clearing LLC its clearinghouse. Other traders pay winning positions through settlement; the U.S. venue pays separate rebates and liquidity credits, not the offshore wallet rewards seen in research ([U.S. legal agreements](https://docs.polymarket.us/partners/onboarding/legal-agreements), [CRS comparison](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF13187)).
- Access / fees: STATED: The operator's app listing describes U.S. availability and names Nevada as unavailable; Idaho is not listed as a blanket exclusion, but product-specific access is subject to eligibility and the participant agreement's jurisdiction clause ([operator's app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/polymarket/id6648798962), [participant agreement](https://polymarketexchange.com/files/legal/latest/participant-agreement)). MEASURED: App/web standard taker coefficient ranges from 0.11 to 0.0695 by volume; straight API trades use 0.0695 × contracts × p × (1 − p) before a temporary 70% taker rebate through December 31, 2026. This reduces the coefficient to 0.02085 for qualifying API taker fills before any rounding; the rebate is a reduced expense, not revenue. Standard maker rebates start at $10 million of qualifying maker volume over 30 completed days, so the quoted “up to ~15%” is not a small-account rate ([U.S. fee schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees.md), [U.S. API quickstart](https://docs.polymarket.us/getting-started/quickstart)). Sports: contested in Idaho.
- Automation: MEASURED: “Complete identity verification” before you “can trade or access the API”; the rulebook identifies API order entry as part of its trading system. No wash trades, spoofing or coordinated reward farming; the owner, not the bot, verifies and accepts terms ([API quickstart](https://docs.polymarket.us/getting-started/quickstart), [U.S. rulebook filing](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules03202641475.pdf), [integrity rules](https://polymarket.us/trust)).
- Open vs designated: MEASURED: Open liquidity credits score genuine resting size and closeness; eligible accounts are auto-enrolled. The November amendment permits market-specific caps and minimum volume, requires qualifying two-sided depth/spread for snapshots, and pays only amounts at least $1, calculated within five business days and credited within two more. The programs page also lists open taker volume rewards. A separate signed and approved Liquidity Provider Agreement sets required spread, size, uptime, share or volume and the payment terms; headline maker rebates are separate from both ([amended open program](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules11032642173.pdf), [designated program](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules11032642168.pdf), [U.S. programs overview](https://docs.polymarket.us/incentives/overview)).
- Base rate / monthly / first dollar: UNKNOWN paid totals, fraction paid, median and beginner net. Credits could arrive in days if earned after account approval, but floor, caps and trading losses can make real net negative. No money or account needed for our research-only test. INFERENCE: Recalculate the unit economics with the rebate set to zero, and skip if it does not work; do not treat the CFTC filing's worked examples as realized awards ([amended open program](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules11032642173.pdf), [fee schedule](https://docs.polymarket.us/fees.md)).
- Agent / killer / cheapest test: Same agent advantage and real-world constraints as Kalshi. Use one week and fabricated order scenarios, no real quotes. Kill it if adding spread, slippage and full post-promotion fees wipes out an otherwise charitable result.

### ForecastEx through Interactive Brokers: known fees, pending retail rewards

- What / payer: A customer trades via a member futures broker; ForecastEx pays interest-like collateral coupons to members, which may pass them through. Other participants fund settlement; a distinct exchange liquidity program is scheduled to launch December 7, not live on the date of this report ([ForecastEx FAQ](https://forecastex.com/faq), [ForecastEx Notice 2026-282](https://data.forecastex.com/notices_to_members/NTM_2026-282.pdf)).
- Access / fees: MEASURED: ForecastEx is a registered DCM and clearing organization; eligible U.S. clients can use its member and affiliate IBKR LLC. The direct broker lists no commission and a 1¢ per contract exchange fee; ForecastEx says it charges each side on execution. No specific Idaho product screen was verified ([IBKR fee schedule and eligibility](https://portal.interactivebrokers.com/en/pricing/commissions-events.php), [ForecastEx FAQ](https://forecastex.com/faq)).
- Automation: MEASURED: “Submission of orders for Event Contracts via the Web API functions like orders for any other instrument.” The broker also documents ScaleTrader for ForecastEx contracts. These are broker-supported routes, not permission to use undocumented endpoints or transfer account control ([IBKR Web API](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/ibkr-api-page/event-contracts/), [IBKR ScaleTrader](https://portal.interactivebrokers.com/en/trading/scaletrader-forecast-contracts.php)).
- Maker or liquidity evidence: MEASURED: Rule 416 provides for designated members under a maker agreement; the December program explicitly does not need that agreement. It allocates announced period pools pro rata for genuine, competitively priced resting orders; a broker customer only qualifies if their FCM has arranged to pass incentives through to all its customers. I found no public confirmation that a small IBKR client will receive the new award. Coupon and quoting reward are not the same. ForecastEx says collateral investment earnings are passed to member firms monthly, but that is not a monthly income figure for the customer ([ForecastEx rulebook](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules09032623618.pdf), [December notice](https://data.forecastex.com/notices_to_members/NTM_2026-282.pdf), [ForecastEx FAQ](https://forecastex.com/faq)).
- Base rate / monthly / first dollar: UNKNOWN realized maker payouts, entrant base rate and typical monthly profit. The future open program cannot have paid as of this check; any coupon is incidental and tied to the value of a funded position. INFERENCE: At a hypothetical annual rate of 3%, even a steady $200 eligible value would earn just $0.50 a month before risk, fees and taxes.
- Agent / killer / cheapest test: Agent can check confirmation and reconciliation; owner gates the broker and any live account action. No-cost watch item after December 7: verify broker pass-through and cost schedule in writing; without pass-through do not build for the reward.

## Who makes money, and what the studies cannot say

- Offshore wallet base rate: MEASURED: Akey and coauthors' working paper reconstructs over 2.4 million users and reports roughly 69% lost money; the median user lost $2 over the sample, not per month. The top 1% **of profitable users**, not the top 1% of all users, capture 76.5% of winning profit. The top 5% of earners made 47.3% of volume as makers, versus 17.1% for the bottom 95%; a one-standard-deviation higher maker share was associated with 9.0 points higher probability of positive profit. Some extreme winners relied on liquidity provision, others on directional bets, and month-to-month persistence was modest. Wallets need not be people, and this is offshore, not Polymarket US ([Akey et al., working paper](https://www.carf.e.u-tokyo.ac.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/260714_polymarket.pdf)).
- Bots vs retail: MEASURED: Della Vedova classifies wallets by trading behavior in 222 million resolved offshore trades; the “bot” group earned $133 million at 49.9% pick accuracy and “retail” lost $79 million despite 51.3% accuracy. This is an inferred automation label, not verified AI identity, and a group net is not the bot share of gross profits or the return on a new bot; the Akey paper describes the classifier as ad hoc. UNKNOWN: reliable bot share of profits on Kalshi, ForecastEx or Polymarket US, or a new solo trader's median after fees ([Della Vedova working paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/6191618.pdf?abstractid=6191618&mirid=1), [Akey et al.](https://www.carf.e.u-tokyo.ac.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/260714_polymarket.pdf)).
- Favorite versus longshot: MEASURED: Whelan's through-April-2025 Kalshi sample found buyers of sub-10¢ contracts lost over 60% and favorites above 50¢ had a small positive average; the maker/taker gap was observed before maker fees were introduced, in a sample filtered for at least $1,000 of market volume. This is contract-sample evidence, not account-level evidence or proof a present-day order would fill at the observed price ([GWU working paper](https://www2.gwu.edu/~forcpgm/2026-001.pdf)). INFERENCE: Better execution can matter more than being right; that does not turn a small API account into a profitable dealer.

## Forecasting cash competitions

### Metaculus FutureEval and MiniBench: the cleanest agent test

- What / payer: Metaculus awards a fixed pool for forecasting accuracy relative to other eligible bots, not for staking against users. Main FutureEval is $50,000 per four-month season; MiniBench is $1,000 per two-week round, with about 60 questions, but payment is bundled with the season, not every two weeks. The platform says MiniBench questions and resolutions are now automated, an extra scoring-noise risk ([Fall announcement](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/45615/announcement-of-futureeval-fall-2026/), [bot resources and payment](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/38928/bot-tournament-resources-page/)).
- Evidence / actual split: MEASURED from the June-quarter 2025 published prize table: 96 ranked rows, 19 with an award (19.8% of displayed rows), $30,000 total awarded, a $0 median across rows and a $7,550.76 top award. Ranks 1–10 captured $24,881.73 (82.9% of the pool), and several high ranks had no award. Displayed rows can include ineligible benchmarks, so the table is not a clean denominator for prize-eligible humans or newcomers. Spring 2026 listed 111 outside bots plus 65 in-house bots, with $50,000 for outside competitors. I did not verify a full spring or MiniBench realized payout table ([2025 payout table](https://www.metaculus.com/aib/2025/q2/), [spring 2026 results](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/45373/spring-2026-futureeval-analysis/)).
- Expected money: INFERENCE: As a concrete “competent” proxy, draw evenly from displayed ranks 21–40 in 2025, about the upper middle of that field. The public table gives 7 paid of 20, $1,258.47 in combined prizes, $62.92 expected gross per contest and a $0 median, with observed awards from $0 to $255.42. That is about $16 a month if spread over a modern four-month season, before compute; not a forecast for 2026 or an estimate conditional on verified prize eligibility. A $20 compute budget now is an experiment, not a business case. Most planning cases should use $0 until the bot shows sustained results ([2025 prize table](https://www.metaculus.com/aib/2025/q2/), [current season format](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/38928/bot-tournament-resources-page/)).
- AI against people: MEASURED: In spring 2026 the 10 professional forecasters beat the top-10 bot aggregate by 1.25 points per question on 99 shared questions, one-sided p = 0.247, and nine of the ten individual pros beat every individual bot. This is evidence of a small team gap in that test, not of human parity in every setting or of a tradeable edge ([Metaculus spring analysis](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/45373/spring-2026-futureeval-analysis/)).
- Eligibility / terms / payment: MEASURED: Free skills competition and a route for a U.S. adult, subject to rules. “Bots may not have a human in the loop when forecasting”; submit as a labeled bot, include a reasoning comment, do not blindly copy the community, and enter one prize-eligible bot. Disclosed secondary bots are eligible for practice, not additional payouts. A closed-source commercial bot cannot win under current guidance. Winners verify identity, may have to show code and demonstrate on fresh questions, complete a survey, and get bank transfer through Ramp after final resolutions and checks; general terms allow W-9 requests ([bot rules](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/28751/q4-ai-forecasting-benchmark-rules/), [current resources](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/38928/bot-tournament-resources-page/), [general tournament rules](https://www.metaculus.com/tournament-rules/), [Idaho skill-contest exception](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title18/t18ch38/sect18-3801/)).
- Money / time / killer / cheapest test: INFERENCE: Two weeks for a local scorecard; first payout months rather than weeks, if any. Cap at $0 to $20 and 8 owner hours. Time-lock 30 non-market questions at their original deadlines, use only sources available then, and compare a simple fixed base-rate baseline with a cited agent under the same coverage and a proper scoring rule. Record cost, cadence failures and bad citations. Then if the owner later wants to enter, check performance on a new live cohort in the permitted bot test area, not by submitting in a human prize lane. Kill on poor coverage, unsupported citations or no out-of-sample edge against the baseline. The owner handles accounts, keys and any prize paperwork; the agent supplies continuous research and forecasting.

In the RAND contest, the accuracy pool was $7,000 and top accuracy awards were $927/$785/$774/$752/$615. Six registered-student commentary winners earned $3,800 combined, $200 to $1,600 each. The ACX 2026 sponsors published ten question-writing awards totaling $3,000, individually $150 to $700, from a process that drew more than 600 question submissions; number of distinct authors UNKNOWN ([Summer Cup awards](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/45734/announcing-the-metaculus-cup-summer-2026-winners/), [RAND awards](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43488/rand-x-metaculus-policy-challenge-winners/), [ACX sponsor report](https://www.astralcodexten.com/p/acxmetaculus-prediction-contest-2026), [ACX entry announcement](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/41230/the-acx-2026-prediction-contest-is-live-10000-prize-pool/)).

Actual eligible-human share and typical monthly net UNKNOWN ([Summer Cup awards](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/45734/announcing-the-metaculus-cup-summer-2026-winners/), [scoring and minimum](https://www.metaculus.com/help/scores-faq/#tournament-scores)).
- Access / AI / payment: MEASURED: “No purchase, fee, or payment is required to participate” in general Metaculus rules, with contest-specific eligibility and Idaho's skill exception; verification, W-9 and payment details can be required, and the exact payment form is at the organizer's discretion. Cup: “Bots aren't eligible for prizes or medals.” ACX welcomed bot accounts to participate; that alone is not proof that an automated entrant can take a cash prize or that AI-written questions qualify. Do not enter agent-generated work under a human identity or enter a student-only lane unless eligible ([general rules](https://www.metaculus.com/tournament-rules/), [Summer Cup announcement](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/45734/announcing-the-metaculus-cup-summer-2026-winners/), [ACX announcement](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/41230/the-acx-2026-prediction-contest-is-live-10000-prize-pool/), [Idaho statute](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title18/t18ch38/sect18-3801/)).

Agent can find sources or cases, subject to the contest's stated permissions; owner supplies and signs the actual work. ## Sell research without staking money

### Source-checked technical brief for a client

- What / payer: An engineering or motion-production team pays for a recommendation it can audit, not for a raw model answer. Example: compare three rendering options against a fixed workload, include what the benchmark does not cover, and supply a reusable checklist. Fiverr is one rail; direct invoicing is another.
