### Pattern Day Trader (PDT) vs Cash Account T+1 Settlement Rules

- STATED (Margin account, legacy FINRA Rule 4210): Under FINRA Rule 4210, a customer who executes 4 or more day trades in 5 business days (exceeding 6% of total trades) is a Pattern Day Trader and must maintain $25,000 minimum equity ([FINRA Notice 24-13](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-13): "Broadly, a 'pattern day trader' is defined to mean any customer who executes four or more day trades within five business days" and "the minimum equity required for the accounts of customers deemed to be pattern day traders shall be $25,000"). That caps a sub-$25,000 margin account at 3 day trades per rolling 5 business days. Note that on June 4, 2026, FINRA replaced the $25,000 PDT minimum with an intraday margin framework under Rule 4210, with broker migration phased in through October 20, 2027 ([AdvoraHQ, Oct 28, 2026](https://advorahq.com/good-faith-violation/): "Intraday margin framework under Rule 4210; PDT designation and $25,000 minimum removed (firms may phase in through October 20, 2027)"). Until a specific broker migrates, the 3-in-5 margin cap still applies.
- STATED (Cash account, SEC Rule 15c6-1 T+1 settlement): Cash accounts are exempt from PDT and the 2026 intraday margin rule, and since May 28, 2024 US equities and ETFs settle T+1 ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/investors/insights/frequent-intraday-trading): "The current settlement time for most equity trades is T+1, or the next business day. Therefore, if you want to engage in intraday trading in a cash account, be sure that you fully pay for those securities at the time of purchase with settled funds."; [FINRA Notice 24-13](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-13): "There are no restrictions on paying for a security and selling it the same day in a cash account, and the proceeds would be available to pay for purchases the next day.").
- Practical rule fit for a $500 cash account:
- Daily intraday-only rules (buy and sell once on the same day, e.g., 15:30 to 16:00 or 09:35 to 10:00): FIT A CASH ACCOUNT EVERY TRADING DAY at 100% size (buy with settled cash on day T, sell same day T; proceeds settle morning of T+1 for the next day's trade; never reuse unsettled proceeds same day to avoid a Good Faith Violation). They DO NOT fit a legacy sub-$25,000 margin account (exceeds 3 day trades per 5 business days).
- Multi-day or overnight event rules (Fed days 8/yr, first day of month 12/yr, rebalance Fridays 4 to 6/yr): FIT BOTH a cash account and a sub-$25,000 margin account (zero day trades if held overnight; low frequency).

## Mechanism-by-Mechanism Audit

### 1. Last-Half-Hour Momentum (Gao, Han, Li, and Zhou 2018; Baltussen, Da, Lammers, and Martens 2021)

- Who is forced to trade and when: Baltussen, Da, Lammers, and Martens (2021) identify short-gamma hedging by option market makers and daily leveraged ETFs concentrated in the last 30 minutes before the 16:00 ET close ([Baltussen et al., JFE 2021](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/): "Hedging short gamma exposure requires trading in the direction of price movements, thereby creating price momentum." and "We provide novel evidence that links market intraday momentum to the gamma hedging demand from market participants such as market makers of options and leveraged ETFs.").
- Published number and sample dates: STATED: Gao, Han, Li, and Zhou (JFE 2018) studied SPY from February 1, 1993 to December 31, 2013 and reported an R-squared of 1.6% and a long/short strategy returning 6.85% per year with a 1.22 Sharpe ratio ([Gao et al. working paper, SSRN 2440866](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/SSRN-id2440866.pdf): "Using the first half-hour returns to forecast the last half-hour returns yields an average returns of 6.85% per annum and a standard deviation of 5.62% per annum. So the portfolio yields a Sharpe ratio of 1.22 with large positive skewness and kurtosis."). Baltussen, Da, Lammers, and Martens (JFE 2021) studied over 60 futures contracts from 1974 to 2020 and reported annualized Sharpe ratios of 0.87 to 1.73 across asset classes, with equity-index futures at 6.86% per year and Sharpe 1.73 ([Baltussen et al., JFE 2021 PDF](https://pure.eur.nl/ws/portalfiles/portal/58145484/1_s2.0_S0304405X21001598_main.pdf): "A simple market intraday momentum trading strategy produces consistent returns over time, translating into high and attractive (annualized) Sharpe ratios between 0.87 and 1.73 at the asset class level.").
- Best evidence after publication (2016 on): MEASURED in our own repo (`docs/research/27_replay_07_intraday_spy_qqq.md`): Replay 07 already tested long-only `N3` (Gao et al.) and `N8` (Baltussen et al.) on `SPY` and `QQQ` 1-minute bars through November 2026. Both failed all net marks: at 2 bps per side, `N3` returned -6.5%/yr on `SPY` (break-even vs zero: 0.02 bps) and -6.3%/yr on `QQQ` (never positive even at 0 bps); `N8` returned -5.5%/yr on `SPY` (break-even vs zero: 0.73 bps) and -4.9%/yr on `QQQ` (break-even vs zero: 0.50 bps). STATED independently: A November 2026 replication of Gao et al. (2018) on `SPY`, `QQQ`, `IWM`, and `DIA` at 2 bps round trip over 2014 to 2026 confirmed negative net returns across all four funds ([DeadSignalsLab, Nov 2026](https://deadsignalslab.substack.com/p/replication-intraday-momentum-eight): "In the common panel from 2014 to 2026 the average is −2.30 basis points per day, with a day-clustered t of −5.17, which annualized is around −5.8%, and the proportion of winning days stays between 43% and 49%, below the coin flip in all four cases.").
- Turnover, $500 cost, and PDT/cash fit: Up to 252 round trips/year (long/short) or ~125 to 140/year (long-only), costing ~2.5% to 5.0%/year at 2 bps round trip. Fits a T+1 cash account (1 round trip/day on settled cash); fails legacy sub-$25,000 margin PDT. Requires `SPLG` or `TLT` for whole shares on $500.
- Verdict: DROP (already tested and failed in Replay 07 `N3`/`N8`, and independently confirmed negative post-2014).

### 2. Leveraged-Fund (LETF) End-of-Day Rebalancing on Large-Move Days

- Who is forced to trade and when: Daily leveraged (+2x, +3x) and inverse (-1x, -2x, -3x) ETF issuers (ProShares `SSO`/`SDS`/`UPRO`/`SPXU`/`TQQQ`/`SQQQ`, Direxion `SPXL`/`SPXS`/`TNA`/`TZA`) and their swap counterparties must rebalance daily exposure `L(L-1) * AUM * r` in the same direction as the day's return near the 16:00 ET close ([SUERF](https://www.suerf.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/l_8dd48d6a2e2cad213179a3992c0be53c_26265_suerf.pdf): "Daily rebalancing demand is L(L −1)AUMt−1rt" and "Always rebalance in the same direction as the benchmark return").
- Published number and sample dates: STATED: Cheng and Madhavan (2009) modeled theoretical rebalancing up to 16.8% of MOC volume on a 1% index move ([Shum et al., 2016](https://www.academia.edu/121362341/Intraday_Share_Price_Volatility_and_Leveraged_ETF_Rebalancing): "Cheng and Madhavan (2009) estimate that if the market moves by 1% during the day, leveraged ETF rebalancing could account for up to 16.8% of the market-on-close (MOC) volume."). Shum, Hejazi, Haryanto, and Rodier (RoF 2016, 2006 to 2011) found end-of-day volatility rose significantly on extreme (>=3%) days, though they also found an insignificant volatility impact on `SPY` itself ([Shum et al., 2016](https://www.academia.edu/121362341/Intraday_Share_Price_Volatility_and_Leveraged_ETF_Rebalancing): "These reasons support our result that the potential rebalancing amounts of leveraged ETFs have an insignificant impact on the volatility of SPY at the market's close when the relative volume is high."). Barbon, Beckmeyer, Buraschi, and Moerke (2012 to 2019) reported large last-30-minute return pressure per standard deviation of LETF flow ([Barbon et al., SSRN 3925725](https://abarbon.com/assets/Liquidity_Provision_to_Rebalancing_Flows_from_Leveraged_ETFs_and_Equity_Options.pdf): "a standard-deviation increase in leveraged ETF rebalancing flows increases end-of-day returns by 430% of the average return in the last half hour.").
- Best evidence after publication (2016 on): STATED: Ivanov and Lenkey (JFM 2018) proved that daily investor creations/redemptions (capital flows) heavily offset mechanical rebalancing ([SSRN 4710812 / QFE 2024](https://www.scribd.com/document/924053621/ssrn-4710812): "Specifically, on the quintile of days with the largest magnitude of index returns, the authors calculate that capital flows reduce LETF rebalancing demand by 85% for ETFs with a leverage ratio of +3 and by 66% for ETFs with a leverage ratio of +2."). Barbon et al. (SSRN 3925725) explicitly found the LETF channel faded over time ([Barbon et al., SSRN 3925725](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3925725): "While gamma effects are persistent throughout our sample, LETFs effects have decreased over time."). A 2024 peer-reviewed survey concluded the effect is economically insignificant ([Quantitative Finance and Economics, 2024](https://www.aimspress.com/article/doi/10.3934/QFE.2024031?viewType): "However, most of the available papers suffer from potentially serious methodological errors, and the economic associations appear to be insignificant."). Note also that `docs/QUEUE.md` already dropped the closing-auction fade variant (`docs/research/67_round_9_outside_evidence.md`), and long-only late-day continuation already failed in Replay 07 `N8`.
- Turnover, $500 cost, and PDT/cash fit: ~20 to 60 trades/year if gated on large (>=1.5% to 2%) moves, costing ~0.4% to 1.2%/year at 2 bps round trip. Fits a T+1 cash account and usually stays under the 3-in-5 margin PDT cap.
- Verdict: DROP (capital flows offset 66% to 85% of theoretical demand, price impact decayed to economic insignificance in broad index ETFs, and long-only late-day trend already failed in Replay 07).

### 3. Option Dealer Gamma Effects and the 0DTE Era Evidence

- Who is forced to trade and when: Options market makers (OMMs) dynamically delta-hedge net gamma exposure intraday in `SPX`/`ES` futures, `SPY`, and `QQQ`, selling rallies when net long gamma (stabilizing) and buying rallies / selling selloffs when net short gamma (amplifying).
- Published number and sample dates: STATED: Barbon and Buraschi (2021) documented that negative aggregate dealer gamma combined with illiquidity predicts intraday momentum, while positive gamma predicts reversal ([Barbon & Buraschi, 2021](https://alexandria.unisg.ch/entities/publication/b0c4de3d-74dd-4e62-b465-2d0337fe2904): "We find supporting evidence that intra-day momentum (reversal) is explained by the interaction of negative (positive) aggregate gamma imbalance and market illiquidity.").
- Best evidence after publication (2016 on, 0DTE era 2022 to 2026): STATED: After Cboe expanded SPX/SPY daily 0DTE expiries in 2022, 0DTE options grew past 50% of SPX volume (63% record in Feb 2026). Contrary to destabilization fears, Dim, Eraker, and Vilkov (June 2025) found 0DTE market makers hold positive gamma on average ([Dim, Eraker, & Vilkov, SSRN 4692190, June 2025](https://www.scribd.com/document/927867468/ssrn-4692190): "We find that Market Makers' inventory, as measured by the net gamma of their positions, is on average positive and negatively related to future intraday volatility."). Cboe's own May 2025 empirical study found customer flows are balanced and net dealer hedging is tiny ([Cboe Insights, May 2, 2025](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact/): "Instead, we find that customer activity continues to be extremely balanced in 0DTE options, and as a result, net market maker gamma hedging remains de minimis, representing at best, just 0.2% of the SPX daily liquidity."). A 2026 Princeton thesis directly testing the canonical gamma hedging feedback channel found no predictive gain ([Shi, Princeton University, April 2026](https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/61115cd1-cb09-4a8b-9230-ff488fc2a386/full): "Our empirical results show that the canonical hedging feedback channel does not add meaningful predictive power for intraday variance forecasts on either a Quasi-Likelihood (QLIKE) loss or Pearson and Spearman correlation basis"). Furthermore, a 1,090-session study (Jan 2022 to May 2026) showed SPY-only and SPX-only 0DTE gamma walls sit $2 to $3 apart ([Cralle, 2026](https://github.com/DarkHorseTrading/dealer-gamma-precision): "The SPY-only and SPX-only walls sit at genuinely different strikes - a median $2-3 apart at 0DTE - and it is not a strike-grid rounding artifact."), and computing dealer gamma requires paid intraday options-chain and customer-side flow data that we do not hold in our SIP minute equity bars.
- Turnover, $500 cost, and PDT/cash fit: Daily intraday trading fits a T+1 cash account (1 RT/day) but fails legacy margin PDT; data cost (paid historical options chains) alone breaks $500 economics.
- Verdict: DROP (no intraday options-positioning data in our SIP feed; 0DTE net dealer gamma is mostly positive and ~0.2% of SPX liquidity; monthly op-ex is already tracked in Queue row 68).

### 4. Market-on-Close (MOC) Imbalance Publication at 15:50 ET

- Who is forced to trade and when: Passive index funds, mutual funds, and ETFs submit MOC and LOC orders before the 15:50 ET cutoff to match official closing NAV; NYSE and Nasdaq disseminate net imbalance feeds starting at 15:50 ET ([NYSE](https://www.nyse.com/research/insights/the-busiest-room-on-wall-street): "The NYSE begins publishing closing imbalance information for the Closing Auction at 3:50:00 p.m., updated every second, and Closing D Orders are included in that information in their discretionary price range.").
- Published number and sample dates: STATED: Bogousslavsky and Muravyev (JFM 2023) documented closing auction distortions in liquid ETFs that revert overnight ([Bogousslavsky & Muravyev, 2021/2023](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7): "We also study price deviations for large ETFs (SPY, QQQ, and S&P sectors) and find that they behave similar to large stocks with average deviation of 3.63 bps, and 99th percentile of 16.32 bps"). On individual stocks, a long/short cross-sectional MOC imbalance reversal strategy showed 13.2 bps/day gross ([SSRN 3440239](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID3440239_code2427035.pdf?abstractid=3440239&mirid=1): "a long/short trading strategy exploiting this reversal results in a significant risk-adjusted return of 13.2 basis points per day").
- Best evidence after publication (2016 on): STATED: Nasdaq's own empirical study of Q2 2019 Nasdaq-100 imbalances proved that high-speed liquidity providers eliminate almost the entire move in a fraction of a second ([Nasdaq, Sept 2019](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/how-much-does-the-moc-imbalance-matter-2019-09-27): "In fact we see that about 80% of the ultimate price move into the close was priced in within 300ms" and "More importantly, the liquidity premium, or profit earned by those facilitating the close imbalance news, is just 1.7bps on average, or less than half the spread."). NYSE similarly documented narrowing close slippage since 2023 ([NYSE Data Insights, Oct 2023](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/nyse-closing-auction-dynamics-2023): "The slippage, or deviation, between the official closing price and NYSE VWAP during those time groups between 3:57PM and 4:00PM has been steadily narrowing since the start of 2023."). Crucially, real-time and historical NYSE/Nasdaq imbalance feeds are paid proprietary data products not present in our minute SIP equity bars.
- Turnover, $500 cost, and PDT/cash fit: Up to 252 RT/yr (~5.0%/yr at 2 bps RT), which exceeds the ~1.7 bps gross liquidity premium. Fits a T+1 cash account if intraday-only (15:50 to 16:00), or every other day at 100% size if held overnight (16:00 to next open).
- Verdict: DROP (80% priced in within 300 milliseconds; ~1.7 bps gross edge below costs; requires paid proprietary imbalance feeds not in our SIP data).

### 5. Overnight Gap and First-30-Minute Continuation or Fade

- Who is forced to trade and when: Overnight retail order accumulation and opening-auction imbalances are absorbed by daytime market makers and arbitrageurs between 09:30 and 10:00 ET ([Iwanaga & Sakemoto, Nov 2025](https://studylib.net/doc/28584823/5807282): "These findings suggest that intraday reversal is associated with the presence of opposing investor clienteles - overnight buying by retail investors and daytime selling by arbitrageurs.").
- Published number and sample dates: STATED: Iwanaga and Sakemoto (Nov 2025) studied `SPY` from January 2, 1996 to December 31, 2024 (7,188 sessions) and found overnight returns negatively predict first-half-hour returns, concentrated in crisis periods ([Iwanaga & Sakemoto, Nov 2025](https://studylib.net/doc/28584823/5807282): "Intraday reversal strengthens during periods of high volatility, including the global financial crisis and the early phase of the COVID19 pandemic. In contrast, it weakened after the 2010s and during the later phase of the pandemic.").
- Best evidence after publication (2016 on): STATED: Multiple 2025 to 2026 out-of-sample tests show the executable post-open fade or continuation is dead. Akula (2026) tested fading `QQQ` overnight gaps and found it is an unexecutable opening-print artifact ([Akula, 2026, Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23383273/files/1791504701857_5%20-%20Does%20the%20Basket%20Lead%20the%20Fund%20-%20Akula%202026.pdf?download=1): "On broad gaps, fading from the open to 10:30 earns 0.85 bp before costs (95% interval −2.04 to 4.07; t = 0.54). Starting the reaction window at 09:35 instead of the open turns the fade P&L to −2.31 bp, and to the close it is −0.13 bp. The 'fade' lives in the first five minutes and is largely a reversal of the opening print."). Mesfin (May 2026) tested gap-fill fade and Kalman gap continuation out of sample on MNQ (2023 to 2025) and every variant failed ([Mesfin, arXiv:2605.04004, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.04004): "Gap Fill Fade | 09:30 | 766 | −1.92 | −0.44 ... FAIL", "Gap Fill Fade | 09:45 | 721 | −1.05 | −0.26 ... FAIL"). A second 2026 study on 947 MNQ days (2021 to 2025) found both opening reversal (t = +0.86) and opening continuation (t = -0.44) failed and flipped sign in 2024 ([arXiv:2605.11423, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11423): "Strategy 1: Reversal entry (fade opening direction, exit at close) | 289 | +1.37 | +0.86 | 51.9% ... FAIL", "Strategy 2: Continuation entry (follow opening direction, exit at close) | 1,175 | −3.22 | −0.44 | 48.8% ... FAIL"). Kagels Trading (SPY 2001 to Oct 2026) found gap-ups >=0.25% filled 50.5% of the time ([QuantifiedStrategies, Nov 2026](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/gap-trading-strategies): "Of 1,967 qualifying higher opens, 50.5% filled that day."). Note also that our own Replay 07 already failed opening range (`N1`) and night-only (`N5`), and Queue row 67 already holds the overnight-vs-intraday research slot.
- Turnover, $500 cost, and PDT/cash fit: High turnover (~100 to 252 RT/yr), and our own Queue row 9f measured that first-half-hour half-spreads are 2.4 bps (~4.8 bps round trip), more than double midday costs. Fits Cash T+1 (1 RT/day); fails legacy margin PDT.
- Verdict: DROP (executable post-09:35 fade is negative; 2021 to 2025 OOS tests fail with |t| < 0.9; high opening spreads).

### 6. VWAP and Closing-Auction Index Flows on Rebalance Days

- Who is forced to trade and when: Passive index funds tracking S&P 500 / Nasdaq-100 (quarterly on the 3rd Friday of March, June, September, December), FTSE Russell (annual June reconstitution, expanding to semi-annual June and December starting 2026; [LSEG](https://www.lseg.com/en/ftse-russell/russell-reconstitution): "Beginning in 2026, the Russell Indexes are reconstituted semi-annually, in June and December."), and MSCI rebalance at the 16:00 ET closing auction to minimize tracking error.
- Published number and sample dates: STATED: Bogousslavsky and Muravyev (2021/2023) measured huge closing-auction volume spikes on Russell rebalance days ([Bogousslavsky & Muravyev](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7): "Auction and pre-close (3:55-4:00) turnovers are 230% and 78% higher, whereas intraday (3:30-3:55) turnover is only 7.8% higher on index rebalancing days."). Dimensional (2026) documented that single-stock additions and deletions move ~4% over the 20 days pre-reconstitution and reverse 5.7% over the 20 days after ([Dimensional, 2026](https://www.dimensional.com/us-en/insights/index-reconstitution-costs-continue-to-add-up-for-index-strategies): "The average excess return to additions and deletions is around 4% over the 20 days leading up to reconstitution, with a reversal of 5.7% over the 20 days after reconstitution.").
- Best evidence after publication (2016 on) for liquid ETFs (`SPY`, `QQQ`, `IWM`): STATED: Because index funds simultaneously buy additions and sell deletions inside the same index, net directional pressure on the broad index ETF (`SPY`, `QQQ`, `IWM`) largely cancels out. Dimensional's 2019 to 2023 measurement found tiny index-level closing distortion ([LegalClarity citing Dimensional](https://legalclarity.org/how-index-fund-rebalancing-and-reconstitution-work/): "Research from Dimensional Fund Advisors found that a stock receiving a 2% increase in index share saw its closing auction price rise by about 0.15 basis points relative to non-rebalanced stocks, followed by a 0.55 basis point reversal by the next morning. Over the five years from 2019 to 2023, the cumulative drag on the S&P 500 from these price reversals was about 0.33 basis points."). NYSE (2023) also documented that institutional D-Orders shifted earlier before 15:30 on rebalance days and closing-auction slippage narrowed since 2023 ([NYSE Data Insights, 2023](https://www.nyse.com/data-insights/nyse-closing-auction-dynamics-2023): "pre-3:30PM D-Orders account for double their normal share on the largest such events (e.g., Russell Reconstitution, quarterly rebalances)"). Single-stock index addition effects are already tracked separately in Queue row 42 (Russell 2000, blocked on license) and Queue row 76 (S&P SmallCap 600, WATCH).
- Turnover, $500 cost, and PDT/cash fit: Only 4 to 6 events/year (~0.08% to 0.12%/yr cost at 2 bps RT). Fits both Cash T+1 and margin PDT.
- Verdict: DROP for broad ETFs (`SPY`, `QQQ`, `IWM`: opposing single-stock flows cancel at the ETF level, leaving <1 bp noise).

### 7. Fed Announcement Days Pre-Drift (Lucca and Moench 2015) and Post-2015 Evidence

- Who is forced to trade and when: Not a mechanical forced-flow mandate, but pre-announcement uncertainty resolution and positioning in the 24 hours ahead of the 8 scheduled annual FOMC statements at 14:00 ET (14:00 ET day T-1 to 14:00 ET day T).
- Published number and sample dates: STATED: Lucca and Moench (JF 2015) documented +49 bps average excess return on SPX over September 1994 to March 2011 ([Federal Reserve FEDS 2026-023](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026023pap.pdf): "They document that, in a sample from September 1994 to March 2011, the average excess return on the S&P 500 in the 24 hours from 2:00 PM to 2:00 PM leading up to the FOMC announcement at 2:15 PM was 49 bps. With eight scheduled FOMC meetings per year, the 24-hour window return, dubbed the 'pre-FOMC announcement drift,' is about 4% per year and accounts for about 80% of annual realized excess stock returns over their sample."), and +57 bps on `SPY` from January 1996 to March 2011 with t > 5 ([Lucca & Moench, Boston Fed PDF](https://www.bostonfed.org/-/media/Documents/conference/PDF/Lucca_preFOMCDrift.pdf): "Over the shorter samples the pre-FOMC drift is 57 basis points for the SPY and 56 basis points for the E-mini with t-statistics above 5.").
- Best evidence after publication (2016 on): STATED: Peer-reviewed and 2026 empirical updates show the drift disappeared after 2015. Kurov, Halova Wolfe, and Gilbert (FRL 2021) extended the sample through December 2019 ([Kurov et al., PMC7525326](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7525326/): "We find that after first appearing before FOMC announcements accompanied by the Fed Chair press conferences, the pre-FOMC drift essentially disappeared after 2015 in both announcements accompanied by press conferences and announcements not accompanied by press conferences." and "In contrast, the mean pre-FOMC return in the second half of the sample (January 2016 to December 2019) is 0.092."). A November 2026 32-year `SPY` backtest confirmed zero edge through 2026 ([RipBacktest, Nov 2026](https://ripbacktest.com/posts/pre-fomc-drift-spy/): "In our test, yes, for now: +0.46% a trade from 2006 to 2015, -0.01% from 2016 to 2026."), and a separate November 2026 study measured the FOMC morning move since 2016 at +0.04% ([TradeWize, Nov 2026](https://tradewize.io/blog/do-stocks-go-up-on-fed-days): "Since 2016 the typical morning before the statement has moved +0.04%.").
- Turnover, $500 cost, and PDT/cash fit: Only 8 overnight round trips/year (~0.16%/yr at 2 bps RT). Fits both Cash T+1 and margin PDT cleanly.
- Verdict: DROP (disappeared after 2015 across both peer-reviewed and 2026 replications; only ~80 events in 2016 to 2026).

### 8. First Trading Day of the Month (and Turn-of-the-Month)

- Who is forced to trade and when: Monthly 401(k) payroll deductions, pension allocations, and automated mutual-fund/ETF inflows deploying cash around the last trading day of the month and the first trading day (Day +1) of the new month.
- Published number and sample dates: STATED: Lakonishok and Smidt (1988) and McConnell and Xu (2008) documented strong turn-of-the-month returns (1926 to 2005, t = 8.2; [bhoult replication](https://github.com/bhoult/correlation/blob/main/experiments/-9.6-turn-of-month.md): "Our code reproduces their 1926-2005 result exactly (t 8.2)"). Up to 2008, Day +1 alone averaged +36 bps on `SPY` and the 4 turn-of-the-month days generated 81% of `SPY`'s total return ([Gupta, Nov 2026](https://github.com/guptagaurav-info/turn-of-month): "1996-2008: the four turn-of-the-month days (19% of trading days) earned 81% of the S&P 500's total return (SPY)" and "S&P 500 average on day +1: 36 bp up to 2008, 15 bp since.").
- Best evidence after publication (2016 on): STATED: Four independent 2026 replications confirm severe post-2008 and post-2016 decay to statistical insignificance:
- Gupta (Nov 2026, 2009 to Oct 2026): "2009-2026: those days earned 24% of SPY's return, 21% of DIA's, 20% of MDY's and 12% of IWM's, close to their 19% share of days" and "Average return on the first trading day of the month, before and after 2009: p = 0.33" ([Gupta, Nov 2026](https://guptagaurav.info/articles/is-the-turn-of-the-month-effect-still-there)).
- FinLab (Nov 2026, Jan 2016 to Oct 2026): "The first trading day of the month was among the best days for QQQ (0.19% on average) and SPY (0.15%). Across the four-day window, QQQ averaged 0.108% a day against 0.076% on the other days (t = 0.47), and SPY 0.076% against 0.059%." ([FinLab, Nov 2026](https://finlab.finance/en/blog/santa-claus-rally-calendar-effects-backtest)).
- FinancePaperMill (Oct 2026, Jan 2016 to Sep 2026): "For SPY from January 2016 through September 2026, those four sessions earned 7.04 bps per day before costs, versus 5.95 elsewhere." ([FinancePaperMill, Oct 2026](https://financepapermill.com/research/turn-of-month-etf-calendar-effect)).
- bhoult replication (2006 to Jun 2025): SPY Day +1 averaged +0.128% (t = +0.98) vs +0.041% on other days, and the 4-day TOM window over 2006 to 2019 had t = -0.07 ([bhoult, Oct 2026](https://github.com/bhoult/correlation/blob/main/experiments/-9.6-turn-of-month.md): "over 2006-2019 those days earned no more than the rest (t -0.07)").
- Turnover, $500 cost, and PDT/cash fit: Only 12 overnight round trips/year (~0.24%/yr cost at 2 bps RT). Fits both Cash T+1 and margin PDT cleanly. Even at ~+15 bps gross on Day +1 (~1.8%/yr gross across 12 days), t is below 1.0 and cannot clear our statistical pass bars or rival buy-and-hold. (Round 17, `docs/research/87_round_17_what_the_replication_libraries_say.md`, also placed calendar/seasonal effects on the skip list.)
- Verdict: DROP (faded from +36 bps to ~+15 bps with t < 1.0 post-2016; 4-day TOM lead is +1.1 bps/day, t = 0.47; only 12 events/year).
