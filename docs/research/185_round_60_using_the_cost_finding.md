## Part A: What the public data can tell us

- STATED: The [Binance.US API docs](https://docs.binance.us/) list public REST klines, aggregate trades and older trades, all capped at 1,000 rows per call, with weights of 1, 4 and 25 respectively. Kline intervals are 1m, 3m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h, 4h, 6h, 8h, 12h, 1d, 3d, 1w and 1M. Klines accept time bounds. Aggregate trades accept time or ID bounds and include the buyer-maker flag; older trades can be paged by trade ID. Book ticker and depth show current books, and no historical best bid and ask or depth endpoint is documented there.
- STATED: The [market history page](https://www.binance.us/institutions/market-history) and [archive release](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9843310-introducing-historical-market-data-from-binance-us-download-for-free) advertise daily and monthly ZIPs for candles, trades and aggregate trades across trading pairs, with archive history back to September 2019. That is archive-wide, not a guarantee for a given pair or month. The [Finder](https://www.binance.us/finder) displayed its spot folder, and the [raw archive](https://data.binance.us/) displayed a truncated public object listing. The folder listing did not establish pair-by-pair ZIP coverage; no ZIP was downloaded. No historical book snapshots are advertised. The scout's full-span bar totals and two ZIP byte sizes are not independently verified here.
- MEASURED: Four bounded REST requests, retaining only monthly open dates, checked [BTC/USD](https://api.binance.us/api/v3/klines?symbol=BTCUSD&interval=1M&startTime=1688169600000&endTime=1743465599999&limit=21), [ETH/USD](https://api.binance.us/api/v3/klines?symbol=ETHUSD&interval=1M&startTime=1688169600000&endTime=1743465599999&limit=21), [SOL/USD](https://api.binance.us/api/v3/klines?symbol=SOLUSD&interval=1M&startTime=1688169600000&endTime=1743465599999&limit=21) and [BTC/USDT](https://api.binance.us/api/v3/klines?symbol=BTCUSDT&interval=1M&startTime=1688169600000&endTime=1743465599999&limit=21), from July 2023 through March 2025. Each USD pair returned July 2023, February 2025 and March 2025 only. BTC/USDT returned 21 consecutive monthly timestamps. This verifies monthly continuity for that control only, not continuous trading hour by hour or for every USDT pair.
- STATED: The [Binance.US removal notice](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9843567-binance-us-will-remove-select-usd-advanced-trading-pairs) names all three USD pairs and says trading ceased July 13, 2023 at 8 p.m. PDT as part of a shift to crypto-only; the help page header is dated March 6, 2025. The same notice says USDT-quoted crypto trading continued and USDT/USD remained available; the [crypto-only guide](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9843321-a-complete-guide-to-using-binance-us-in-a-crypto-only-state) also describes USDT-quoted trading and an earlier June 26 removal of most USD pairs. Binance.US's [February 19, 2025 announcement](https://blog.binance.us/usd-services-return/) describes the July crypto-only transition following the SEC's June 2023 civil claims and the need for a banking partner to restore ACH. It names all three USD pairs among the ten returning at the start of the phased rollout. These are the venue's statements, not a finding about the merits of the claims or a precise activation date for every user.
- INFERENCE: No bar estimator can recover a contemporaneous spread for a discontinued USD market out of USDT prices. For dates with trading, OHLC and signed trades can support a spread proxy, not reconstruct what a $250 order would have paid against historical depth. The [fee schedule](https://www.binance.us/fees) lists 2 bp taker on the general spot schedule and 1 bp on BNB/USD; that is a stated present rate, not a schedule for earlier years.

## Part A: What the spread papers tested

- STATED: [Corwin and Schultz (2012)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2012.01729.x) build a low-frequency stock spread estimator from 1-day and 2-day highs and lows. Their [appendix](https://sites.nd.edu/scorwin/files/2019/11/Internet-Appendix_FINAL.pdf) also tests consecutive 15-minute bars rolled up to daily estimates on NYSE, AMEX and NASDAQ stocks against trade-weighted daily TAQ effective spreads. With negative estimates excluded, mean absolute error is 26 bp in 1993 and 13 bp in 2006. [Abdi and Ranaldo (2017), Tables 5 and 6](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/30/12/4437/4047344), add the close. They use daily CRSP prices on the same three exchanges to estimate monthly spreads. Against their October 2003 to December 2015 Daily TAQ benchmark, the average cross-sectional correlation is 0.738 for their estimator versus 0.642 for the high-low estimator; average within-stock time-series correlation is 0.518 versus 0.510. The [earlier conference draft](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2017/preliminary/paper/GbeDTRrB) ends in 2014. These are stock results, not crypto accuracy.
- STATED: [Brauneis et al. (2021), Tables 7 and 12](https://d-nb.info/1258712520/34), use trades and 50 levels per side for BTC/USD and ETH/USD on Bitfinex, Bitstamp and Coinbase Pro from December 16, 2017 to December 16, 2019. They compare estimates from minute bars against hourly benchmarks, hour bars against daily benchmarks, and daily bars against 15-day benchmarks. Effective-spread mean absolute errors in bp, in Corwin-Schultz then Abdi-Ranaldo order: BTC 1.61/1.49, 18.72/20.41, 134.9/132.3; ETH 4.60/3.47, 21.59/24.79, 171.4/170.6. The reported 0.54 to 0.75 range is for daily BTC time-series correlations with quoted spread, effective spread and sized round-trip cost, not an ETH result or level accuracy. Corwin-Schultz did poorly in ETH cross-venue ranking. Thin Bitstamp ETH had only 4,410 hourly observations meeting the 80% input coverage rule, versus more than 11,920 for BTC.
- STATED: Brauneis et al. say the two estimators captured the effective-spread level well at the highest frequency they tested and poorly at lower frequencies. They recommend them for tracking variation and favor Kyle-Obizhaeva and Amihud for levels overall ([Brauneis et al.](https://d-nb.info/1258712520/34)). INFERENCE: This is not a universal “level only on 1-minute bars” rule and does not validate EDGE or thin Binance.US altcoins.
- STATED: The [2022 EDGE working paper](https://sites.insead.edu/facultyresearch/research/file.cfm?fid=68847) models infrequent trading. In one simulation, Abdi-Ranaldo remains biased below about 30 expected trades per simulated day, not a universal trades-per-bar cutoff, and Corwin-Schultz improves more slowly. EDGE uses all four OHLC fields and reduces bias from discrete trading under the paper's assumptions. The draft's stock-month non-positive estimate shares were 24.78% EDGE, 31.74% Abdi-Ranaldo and 29.26% Corwin-Schultz; the [2024 paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X24001399) reports 25.63%, 31.87% and 29.18% in its updated stock sample.
- STATED: The [published EDGE paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X24001399) compares monthly estimates from daily U.S. stock prices with matched trade-and-quote effective spreads; it reports Pearson correlation 0.7886 for EDGE in the main stock sample. On Binance global it uses minute, hourly and daily OHLC on 2,163 pairs, July 2017 to December 2021, with 53,865 pair-month estimates per frequency and estimator. EDGE mean estimates were 0.68% to 0.70% across frequencies. The authors had no trade-and-quote benchmark for crypto, so agreement gives no tested level accuracy. UNKNOWN: whether half of an OHLC effective spread plus a fee matches either side of a $250 book walk on Binance.US, on the same clock or across regimes.

- INFERENCE: Prefer genuinely forward outcomes. Old outcomes, old flags and known dates under a new feed or lower cost are not independent evidence. No historical opening is proposed here.
