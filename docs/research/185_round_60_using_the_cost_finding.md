## Part A: Estimating Past Binance.US USD Spreads From Free Data

### 1. What Binance.US Publicly Serves

STATED from the [Binance.US API Documentation](https://docs.binance.us/) (opened normally):

- Candlesticks (GET /api/v3/klines): "Use this endpoint to get Kline/candlestick bars for a token symbol. Klines are uniquely identified by their open time. Please note the maximum limit is 1,000 bars." Weight is 1, authentication is NONE ("If no security type is stated, assume the security type is NONE."), and supported intervals are 1m, 3m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 2h, 4h, 6h, 8h, 12h, 1d, 3d, 1w, and 1M.
- Aggregate trades (GET /api/v3/aggTrades): "Use this endpoint to get compressed, aggregate trades. Trades that fill at the same time, from the same order, with the same price, will have the quantity aggregated. Please note the maximum limit is 1,000 trades." Weight is 4, authentication is NONE, and each row carries the buyer-maker flag `m` (indicating whether the buyer was the market maker).
- Older raw trades (GET /api/v3/historicalTrades): "Use this endpoint to get older trades. Please note the maximum limit is 1,000 trades." Weight is 25 and "Security Type: NONE (no API key required)".
- Live top-of-book only, no historical quotes: the API documents current top-of-book (GET /api/v3/ticker/bookTicker: "Use this endpoint to get the best available order book price.") and current depth, but documents no historical order-book or historical BBO quote endpoint.
- Free bulk archive: Binance.US hosts its own public archive separate from Binance global's `data.binance.vision`. The [Binance.US Historical Market Data page](https://www.binance.us/institutions/market-history) states: "We've made Candlestick, Trade, and AggTrade data for all pairs available for download." The [Binance.US Help Center announcement](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9843310-introducing-historical-market-data-from-binance-us-download-for-free) states: "At launch, our datasets include historical candlesticks, trades, and aggregated trade data for all trading pairs." and "Our historical data library supports market data dating back to September 2019." Daily and monthly ZIP files live under `data.binance.us` (browsable at [Binance.US Finder](https://www.binance.us/finder)) and contain no historical order-book or BBO quote snapshots.

MEASURED from lightweight metadata-only checks run on 2026-11-26 (five requests total; no bulk data downloaded):

- Monthly klines on `api.binance.us` for `BTCUSD` and `ETHUSD` run from 2019-09 through 2026-11 (69 bars each), and `SOLUSD` runs from 2020-09 through 2026-11 (57 bars).
- All three USD pairs are missing 18 straight calendar months from 2023-08 through 2025-01 inclusive, matching the Binance.US USD pause before USD services resumed in February 2025 (`docs/research/180_round_57_cost_as_the_binding_limit.md`). Continuous post-restoration USD history is therefore only 22 months (2025-02 through 2026-11).

Supporting quotes and exact findings:

- STATED, [Brauneis, Mestel, Riordan, and Theissen (2021, Journal of Banking and Finance)](https://d-nb.info/1258712520/34) tested Corwin and Schultz (2012) and Abdi and Ranaldo (2017) against two years of 50-level order books and trades for BTCUSD and ETHUSD on Bitfinex, Bitstamp, and Coinbase Pro (2017-12-16 to 2019-12-16): "The Corwin and Schultz (2012) estimator exhibits the highest time series correlation and the Abdi and Ranaldo (2017) estimator the second-highest correlation with the quoted spread, the effective spread and the cost of a roundtrip trade, with correlations ranging from 0.54 to 0.75."
- STATED, same paper on level failure at lower frequencies: "The Corwin and Schultz (2012) and Abdi and Ranaldo (2017) proxies capture the level of the percentage effective spread very well at the highest data frequency but very poorly at lower frequencies." and "The Roll (1984) estimator and the two measures based on high and low prices, the Abdi and Ranaldo (2017) and Corwin and Schultz (2012) estimators, are unable to capture the levels of the benchmark measures." In Table 7 (BTCUSD) and Table 12 (ETHUSD), effective-spread MAE jumped from 1.61 bp (CS) and 1.49 bp (AR) at 1m-to-hourly up to 18.72 bp (CS) and 20.41 bp (AR) at 1h-to-daily, and 134.9 bp (CS) and 132.3 bp (AR) at daily-to-15-day.
- STATED, same paper on thin pairs and cross-venue ranking: "Evidently, ETHSUD is a rather infrequently traded pair on Bitstamp (roughly 5 million transactions over our investigation period, compared to roughly 24 million on Bitfinex and Coinbase Pro) which is why we only have 4410 observations of hourly data on Bitstamp that match the 80% data availability criterion (compared to more than 11,920 one hour intervals that match this criterion on Bitstamp for the pair BTCUSD." and "The Corwin and Schultz (2012) estimator that performed well when applied to BTCUSD, does poorly when applied to ETHUSD."
- STATED, [Ardia, Guidotti, and Kroencke (2022 working paper, INSEAD)](https://sites.insead.edu/facultyresearch/research/file.cfm?fid=68847) on thin trading and zero-trade bars: "The estimators in Roll (1984), Corwin and Schultz (2012), and Abdi and Ranaldo (2017) lead to biased results when trading is infrequent, our generalized estimators remain unbiased." and "CS is significantly biased and converges slowly to the true spread as the expected number of trades per day increases." and "AR converges faster, but it is still biased when the expected number of daily trades is below 30." and "EDGE produces unbiased estimates regardless of the numbers of trades per day, suggesting it works well in practice even in the case of illiquid assets or in high frequency when only a few trades are observed per minute."
- STATED, [Ardia, Guidotti, and Kroencke (2024, Journal of Financial Economics)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X24001399) on crypto pairs: "Our sample consists of 2163 crypto pairs and 53,865 pair-month spread estimates for each frequency and estimator in the sample period from July 2017 to December 2021." and "Our efficient estimator produces consistent estimates regardless of whether daily or intraday prices are used, while other estimators produce a tenfold difference between daily and intraday estimates." Crucially, they state: "As trade and quote data are unavailable to us, we cannot compute bid–ask spreads obtained by matching trades with quotes."
- INFERENCE: For Binance.US USD altcoins, zero-volume 1-minute bars and thin books are the core problem. EDGE is theoretically the only one of the three built for discrete zero-trade bars, and 1-minute bars are required if we want a level estimate rather than a rough direction signal. Even then, EDGE estimates average-trade effective spread, not the order-book walk of a $250 market order and not the taker fee (2 bp on Binance.US, 1 bp on BNBUSD). Whether EDGE matches $250 Binance.US USD market-order costs on thin altcoins is UNKNOWN until tested directly against Measurement 132.

### 3. Validating an Estimator Against Measurement 132 Without Tuning It

INFERENCE: To test whether an OHLC estimator can safely stand in for past Binance.US spreads without overfitting it to Measurement 132, freeze every choice before looking at the overlapping klines:

- Lock one primary specification before pulling data: uncalibrated EDGE on Binance.US 1-minute USD klines (`data.binance.us` monthly or daily ZIPs) over the exact 30-day Measurement 132 calendar window for all 20 Plan 70 coins, divided by 2 to convert full spread to one-side half-spread, plus the published taker fee (2 bp, or 1 bp for BNBUSD). Run uncalibrated CS and AR on the same 1-minute bars as secondary comparators only.
- Allow zero free parameters: no regression slope, no additive offset, no frequency search, no outlier trimming, and no dropping thin coins after seeing errors.
- Compare against Measurement 132's live per-coin median $250 one-side cost across three pre-fixed slices matching Measurement 132 (`docs/research/182_measurement_132_venue_cost.md`): all hours, 00:00 UTC midnight wakes, and BTC-drop hours.
- Pre-fixed validation gate to permit historical use: (1) cross-coin Spearman rank correlation at least 0.70 across the 20 coins, (2) median absolute error at most 5 bp across the 15+ qualifying coins, and (3) worst-tenth coins (thin books) not underestimated by more than 10 bp.
- Decision rule: if uncalibrated EDGE clears all three marks, it may be used as a documented historical spread proxy for post-2025-02 Binance.US USD bars (still UNKNOWN during the 2023-08 to 2025-01 USD gap). If it misses any mark, do not fit a multiplier to Measurement 132; treat past Binance.US spreads as UNKNOWN and use Measurement 132's live floor for future plans only.

## Part C: Four Ranked Clean Next Tests and Hard Prohibitions

All four tests below are clean first looks. Each has a pre-fixed two-branch rule tied to Measurement 132's 30-day readout (`docs/research/182_measurement_132_venue_cost.md`: line met = 15 or more of 20 coins with paired buy and sell $250 costs on at least 25 of 30 midnight 00:00 UTC wakes and each side's own median at or under 25 bp).

### Rank 1: Uncalibrated EDGE Spread-Estimator Check Against Measurement 132 (Data Infrastructure Gate)

- What it tests: Whether free Binance.US 1-minute USD klines (`data.binance.us`) fed into uncalibrated EDGE (plus CS and AR comparators, zero tuned parameters, plus published 2 bp/1 bp taker fee) match Measurement 132's live per-coin $250 one-side costs over the exact same 30 days.
- Branch 1 (Measurement 132 line met): Pass if cross-coin Spearman rho >= 0.70, median absolute error <= 5 bp on qualifying coins, and worst-tenth underestimation <= 10 bp. Only then may EDGE on post-2025-02 Binance.US 1m bars be used as a historical spread proxy.
- Branch 2 (Measurement 132 line not met): Same diagnostic still runs once to document whether bar estimators understate thin-book $250 impact, but the main cost line stays at 75 bp/side regardless.
- Main reason it would fail: On thin Binance.US USD altcoins, small $5 to $20 trades set the 1-minute OHLC bars while a $250 market order walks deeper book levels, causing EDGE to underestimate true $250 slippage on the worst tenth of coins.

### Rank 2: E10 Forward Log for Plan 75 on Live Binance.US vs Coinbase Order Books (Already Queued Reward)

- What it tests: Plan 75's frozen 3-leg Donchian ratchet rule (`docs/research/181_plan_75_heldback_result.md`) scored strictly forward from 2026-11-26 on live 00:00 UTC quotes, with two paper books (Coinbase 60 bp fee + live spread versus Binance.US 2 bp fee + live spread). Because Plan 75 spent 14.5% of starting capital on costs at 75 bp/side over 34 months, live execution cost is the single largest lever on a rule that already passed held-back.
- Branch 1 (Measurement 132 line met): Binance.US becomes the primary forward paper book for E10; track realized 00:00 UTC fill cost against the 25 bp/side ceiling and judge E10 on its pre-registered forward marks (no early stop, no parameter edits).
- Branch 2 (Measurement 132 line not met): Keep 75 bp/side (Coinbase book) as the primary E10 benchmark and log Binance.US as a shadow comparison only.
- Main reason it would fail: Plan 75 earned +18.8% in 2024 and lost -4.6% in 2025 and -3.8% in 2026 to October (`docs/research/181_plan_75_heldback_result.md`). Even with near-zero fees, a prolonged sideways or choppy crypto regime will grind down a breakout-ratchet rule through whipsaws.

### Rank 3: Live Crash-Hour Execution Check on E09 (`70-102`) Before Spending the Coin Vault

- What it tests: Plan 73 (`docs/research/157_plan_73_first_minute_diagnosis.md`) proved that half of `71-013`'s edge sits inside the first 60 seconds after the hour (+75.6 bp at open vs +39.4 bp at 1 minute late) and that a 2024 to 2026 vault test has very low statistical power (~13% on 315 days). Before spending the one-shot 2024 to 2026 coin vault on a spot-priced version of `70-102` or `71-013`, measure live Binance.US USD book cost specifically during BTC-drop hours (already a pre-fixed slice of Measurement 132) and log E09 forward at realistic 60-second-delayed executable quotes.
- Branch 1 (Measurement 132 line met): Proceed to consider a single pre-registered spot-cost vault run ONLY if Measurement 132's BTC-drop-hour median $250 cost is at or under 25 bp/side AND E09 live 1-minute-delayed fills do not show worse than 50% decay from the hourly open. Otherwise keep the vault unspent and continue E09 forward only.
- Branch 2 (Measurement 132 line not met): Do not spend the 2024 to 2026 vault on `70-102` or `71-013` spot; at 75 bp/side (150 bp RT) the reachable 1-minute-late mean (+39.4 bp) is deeply negative. Keep E09 forward only as a lead.
- Main reason it would fail: Crash-hour altcoin books on Binance.US widen sharply right when BTC drops 1.5% in 1 to 2 hours, so BTC-drop-hour $250 spreads can easily jump above the ~39 bp reachable 1-minute-late gross edge even when calm-hour medians are 7 to 8 bp.

### Rank 4: One New Pre-Registered Medium-Frequency Coin Plan (1 to 7 Day Hold) Priced at the Post-M132 Floor

- What it tests: A brand-new coin rule family never previously tested on 2024 to 2026 (for example, a slow cross-coin or volatility-scaled breakout/carry rule with 50 to 150 trades/yr, where 15 to 25 bp/side versus 75 bp/side saves 15 to 35 pp/yr in friction without depending on first-minute crash fills), pre-registered after Measurement 132 reads out and scored once on the unspent 2024 to 2026 vault or strictly forward.
- Branch 1 (Measurement 132 line met): Pre-register the new plan with the Measurement 132 per-coin measured cost floor (at or under 25 bp/side for qualifying coins, plus the measured worst-tenth penalty for thin names) fixed before touching any bar.
- Branch 2 (Measurement 132 line not met): Do not launch a medium-turnover spot coin plan; keep 75 bp/side as the main cost line and restrict any new spot coin idea to low-turnover holds (under ~30 round trips/yr) where 75 bp/side is survivable.
