# Can we cut weekly model noise? Screen for queue 122 to 124

INFERENCE. Try row 122 first as a small noise and turnover rehearsal, not as a return test. Row 124 is worth a separate test if the aim is cheaper operation. Do not start row 123 on its own: reuse suitable repeats if they exist, but first verify that two model families can run the same task with the intended decoding and thinking settings. None of the three is well powered for a 20 bp weekly net improvement on seven or fourteen weeks. No prices, live listeners, replay packets or real-data model sessions were used for this screen.

## What the direct calls actually did

- MEASURED. The sandbox's installed Python LLM SDK exposes `SamplingParams` with `temperature`, `top_p`, `top_k` and a token limit; it has no `seed` field. The transport code serializes the supplied temperature and top-p, including zero. The bundled bare prompt CLI does not expose those sampling switches. Sending a setting and the backend honoring it are different things.
- MEASURED. The probe sent the same invented six-number game to each model in a fresh conversation, with no tools and automatic prompt caching disabled. Default means temperature and top-p omitted, not a known backend value. The two supported repeat probes alternated the order of default and zero. We counted the selected numbers and exact text variants separately; the counts agree here. Top-p was checked once at 0.5 with temperature omitted. Responses counted only when the reported model ID matched the requested one.

## Smallest tests worth considering

- STATED. A session here is one fresh portfolio book across a full week, not one wake or one model call. Choose unused price-only weeks entirely after both compared model versions' verified knowledge lines, outside registered and judged windows. Seal dates, ties, costs, packets, prompts, missing-data rules and comparisons before release. Start from cash each week and pair both arms on the same market clock. Account for every scheduled week; do not replace failures or treat stocks, wakes or copies as independent weeks. Hold the data and deterministic order rules fixed unless part of the named intervention.
- **122, grid and deadband.** STATED. Begin with one supported model, seven weeks and two independent books per arm each week, 28 sessions. Compare free-form target weights against the same model returning a five-level tilt grid with the proposed two-percentage-point weight-change deadband. Use the same requested temperature zero in both arms, clock, packet and execution costs; this tests the grid and deadband together, not temperature. Seal the mapping to weights, long-only cash and cap rules, how exits interact with the deadband, tie and rounding behavior, and a fail-closed schema. Measure repeat-gap SD (`P1 - P2`) and interval by arm, executed turnover and weekly paired net return. INFERENCE. A halving is the useful noise target; lower turnover or smaller label differences alone do not establish it. The upper interval bound matters, and a noisy mean near zero does not establish no harm. Worth a seven-week screen, with fourteen weeks or a separate sealed confirmation if a precise bound is needed. Do not promote an economic edge on this pilot.
- **123, two families versus two copies.** STATED. Use two fresh copies each of A and B on the same fixed policy. At seven weeks this is 28 sessions; at fourteen it is 56. Compare two equally capitalized sleeves, prespecifying A1+B1 against A1+A2 and reporting the B baseline as a sensitivity. Keep the shared sleeve and costs in both; do not call averaged full-budget returns a single portfolio with whole-share fills. Measure within-family and cross-family correlation in weekly returns relative to the same fixed hold benchmark, the SD ratio of the two averages, net differences, and uncertainty across weeks. The usual effective count formula is only for equal marginal variances; also report unequal variances and the actual paired variance. INFERENCE. An actionable result is a lower SD for the mixture at equal call count, with uncertainty and no material net loss, not merely a lower correlation. Use fourteen weeks over seven if attempting a correlation estimate, and show the direction as exploratory until it replicates. If row 122 ran one matching policy for A, it saves only the A copies: adding B still costs 14 or 28 sessions at seven or fourteen weeks. Do not buy a standalone sample or expand to more families on this evidence.
- **124, one wake.** STATED. Begin with one model, two copies per side and seven weeks, 28 sessions. Compare one decision at the first eligible weekly wake with the multi-wake schedule. Give both arms the same deterministic trailing indicators and single-name caps so the smallest honest comparison isolates cadence; disable reflection and debate on both. A comparison to the older card without those controls is a bundle test and cannot attribute a win to cadence. Seal warm-up, stale/missing features, the holiday rule, caps and cost path; no indicator may include the bar in progress. Measure completed model calls, repeat-gap SD and interval, turnover and paired weekly net. INFERENCE. The queued noise target is a quarter lower; no material loss needs a separately sealed margin and a sufficiently tight one-sided interval. The short test is justified as an operational rehearsal, not as a test for a modest return gain.
- MEASURED from the registered clock and the design count. TM09 has three slots per full trading day, up to 15 wakes in a normal week, not five per week; early closes and holidays can remove slots. Under the queue's one-versus-five assumption, the single-wake arm uses 80% fewer decision calls, 84 calls across both arms in seven weeks. Against the full registered clock it is one versus 15, 224 calls across both arms in seven full weeks, before shared scouts, lookups or retries. The reduction applies to the single-wake arm, not the combined trial or a promise about credits.

## Synthetic count

- INFERENCE. Doc 163's **91.4 bp** is a weekly repeat-gap SD proxy derived from six whole-run TM04 gaps assuming independent weekly changes; the underlying weekly stock repeats no longer exist. It is not a single-copy SD and not a measurement on these models. Settings live in `screen_122_124`. The simulation draws 120,000 independent normal weekly worlds per case. One whole week is one paired observation. Marks are a paired t of at least 2.43 and at least 60% strictly positive weeks, the registered 12-of-20 rule scaled to 5 of 7 or 9 of 14. These are two-mark chances conditional on full completion, not probabilities of a full TM09 label, economic noninferiority, or a liquidity pass.
- INFERENCE. For rows 122 and 124, equal shared market noise cancels. Two repeats averaged per arm, with independent remaining repeat errors and treatment repeat-gap SD ratio r, give contrast SD of 91.4 times the square root of (1 + r squared) divided by 2 inside the root. An unchanged arm therefore gives 91.4 bp, even though it has two repeats. For 123, the common A1 sleeve cancels. With equal within- and across-family correlations, the contrast SD is half the repeat gap. The 0.85 and 0.38 sensitivity uses doc 167's non-trading analogy purely as a numerical scenario, with equal marginal SD and all correlations applied to benchmark-relative weekly innovations. It gives the mixture 0.864 times the comparison average's SD and effective counts of 1.08 versus 1.45 out of two, yet a noisier paired comparison because the replaced sleeves move less together. It is not a forecast of trading correlations.

| Scenario | Weekly contrast SD | 7 weeks: +20 / +50 / +100 bp | 14 weeks: +20 / +50 / +100 bp |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 122 or 124, repeat gap unchanged | 91.4 bp | 7.3% / 23.4% / 68.3% | 7.7% / 38.0% / 93.8% |
| 122, treatment repeat gap halves | 72.3 bp | 9.2% / 34.3% / 86.2% | 11.1% / 57.4% / 99.3% |
| 123, same within- and cross-family correlation | 45.7 bp | 16.6% / 68.3% / 99.7% | 25.0% / 93.8% / 100.0% |
| 123, illustrative correlation gap | 92.9 bp | 7.2% / 22.8% / 66.9% | 7.5% / 36.8% / 93.1% |
| 124, treatment repeat gap falls a quarter | 80.8 bp | 8.2% / 28.6% / 78.3% | 9.2% / 47.4% / 97.8% |

- INFERENCE. At zero true edge the simulated pass rates are 2.5% and 1.5% for seven and fourteen weeks. Even at twenty weeks, the +20 bp cases above range from 8.8% to 33.8%. If the new design targets noise instead, a single gap SD has an idealized 90% interval of 0.69 to 1.92 times the estimated SD at seven weeks, or 0.76 to 1.48 times at fourteen. Under independent, normal gap series, a one-sided 90% variance-ratio bound detects any reduction in about 62% or 87% of pilots if the true gap halves, but only 26% or 39% if it drops a quarter. These are not chances of proving the full targeted reduction. Common shocks, dependent weeks and missing tickets can make these counts optimistic; the script does not model them.
