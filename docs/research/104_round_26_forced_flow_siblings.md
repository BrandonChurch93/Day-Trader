## Dropped Candidates (Added to Round 26 Skip List)

Exchange-listed oil and gas royalty trusts (DROPPED: live count < 30, no published basis):
- MEASURED: Official SEC [SEC company_tickers_exchange.json](https://www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers_exchange.json) lists only 8 live US exchange-listed common royalty trusts (`CRT`, `MTR`, `NRT`, `PBT`, `PRT`, `PVL`, `SBR`, `SJT`; 7 under Nasdaq Trader symbol-directory name matching). This is far below the 30-instrument floor and has no published academic tax-loss rebound basis.

Exchange-listed Master Limited Partnerships / MLPs (DROPPED: no published basis, K-1 and ordinary-income recapture tax friction):
- MEASURED: Official SEC [SEC company_tickers_exchange.json](https://www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers_exchange.json) lists 42 exchange-listed LP tickers, of which 9 are commodity pools (`USO`, `UNG`, `BNO`, `UGA`, `UNL`, `USL`) or preferred issues (`BEPJ`, `BIPJ`, `BPYPP`), leaving 33 operating LPs/MLPs.
- STATED: No peer-reviewed paper documents a December-to-January MLP tax-loss rebound. Furthermore, per [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2008/aug/apracticalguidetothetaxissuesofinvestinginmasterlimitedpartnerships/): "One of the potentially most unusual tax compliance issues is dealing with the passive activity rules for PTPs under Sec. 469(k), in which the passive activity loss limitation rules are applied to MLPs on an entity-by-entity basis." Per [Baird MLP Tax FAQ](https://content.rwbaird.com/RWB/Content/PDF/Help/Taxation-Master-Limited-Partnerships-FAQs.pdf): "Because the return of capital distributions are due to depreciation and other similar deductions claimed by the MLP, those deductions must be recaptured upon the sale - meaning that portion of the gain is taxed as ordinary income, not as a capital gain." Selling a losing MLP can still generate ordinary-income recapture, and buying 5 MLPs for a one-month trade in a $500 US retail account creates 5 separate Schedule K-1 filings.

Reverse side of tax-loss rebound (late-January CEF fade short, or early-January tax-gain selling of winners) (DROPPED: requires shorting / margin unreachable on $500):
- STATED: Shorting thin CEFs after the January rebound requires a margin account (minimum $2,000 equity under FINRA Rule 4210, unreachable on $500), faces thin stock borrow, and has no published academic evidence of a systematic February reversal.
- STATED: For early-January tax-gain selling of prior-year winners, [D'Mello, Ferris and Hwang (2003, Journal of Financial Markets)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S138641810200023X) state: "there is selling pressure in the new year for stocks that have increased in value from investors motivated to reduce their current year's tax liability by delaying realizing capital gains." That selling pressure is a short-side signal on winners in early January (unreachable on a $500 US long-only account) with no published long-only post-dip rebound rule.

ETF and mutual fund December capital-gain distribution dates (DROPPED: already on Round 20 skip list for ETFs; mutual funds not reachable as discounted listed instruments):
- STATED: ETF capital-gain distribution dates were already dropped in Round 20 (`docs/research/91_round_20_dated_mechanical_flows.md`). Confirmed by [Moussawi, Shen and Velthuis (2025, Review of Financial Studies)](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/38/10/2988/8191041): "As a result, ETF outflows mostly occur through in-kind redemption transactions with APs, thereby allowing ETFs to benefit from the Section 852(b)(6) exemption to avoid the distribution of capital gains." Also confirmed by [Johnson and Poterba (2008, NBER)](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w13884/w13884.pdf): "Most ETFs avoid taxation of realized capital gains by using redemption-in-kind distributions as described in Poterba and Shoven (2002)."
- STATED: For open-end mutual funds, [Moussawi, Shen and Velthuis (2025, Review of Financial Studies)](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/38/10/2988/8191041) note: "Therefore, it is likely advantageous for tax-sensitive investors to exit the fund upon learning about these realized capital gains and before the distribution ex-date, typically in mid-December according to our data." Open-end mutual funds transact once daily at NAV (no exchange discount dislocation to buy), cannot be shorted on $500, and frequently carry $1,000+ minimums or redemption fees. The actionable equity-market spillover from mutual fund tax year-ends is instead captured cleanly in Row 93 (October 31 statutory tax year-end).

New empirical evidence in [Albiez (2026, SSRN)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7567460) across 913 scheduled cliff unlocks confirms: "Markets absorb unlocks far better: net of token-specific drift, prices show no detectable three-day response (market-model abnormal return-0.14%, 95% CI-0.81% to +0.53%)." Even where unlocks lean negative, a $500 US spot account cannot short small altcoins.
