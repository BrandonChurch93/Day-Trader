## Candidate 1: Binance.US Lagging Bitstamp at the Hour Scale (COUNT FIRST, Forward Only)

- Plain-words rule: Every UTC hour (10 seconds after the hour, respecting the shared Binance.US rate-limit gate), compare Binance.US's $250 USD ask walk against Bitstamp's synchronized USD midpoint across all USD pairs listed on both venues (excluding any pair with an announced or observed deposit, withdrawal, or USD-rail halt). When Binance.US's $250 ask sits at least 40 bp below Bitstamp's USD midpoint and remains at least 40 bp below on a second check 60 seconds later (ruling out a 1-second tick mismatch), buy $250 on Binance.US and sell back on Binance.US when the Binance.US $250 bid rises within 10 bp of Bitstamp's USD midpoint or after a 6-hour time stop. No cross-venue transfer and no short leg.
- Who loses the money and why they keep doing it: Impatient retail sellers on Binance.US who sell via market orders or Convert into a thin USD book, pushing the local USD quote below the global USD price. Cross-US arbitrageurs ignore $250 to $1,000 gaps because the other US leg costs 40 to 80 bp/side at entry tiers (Kraken/Coinbase) and USD rebalancing hits Binance.US's unwaivable 7-day ACH hold or $25 wire fee (`docs/research/186_round_61_thin_cheap_venue.md`).
- Why a human cannot do it but an hourly agent can: Checking synchronized $250 book depth across 10 to 20 overlapping USD pairs on two venues every hour 24/7, verifying rail-health flags, and exiting on a fixed convergence rule requires around-the-clock monitoring that a human cannot sustain overnight and on weekends.
- Count stage that reads no price outcome: Run a 30-day forward-only logger (720 hourly wakes) recording ONLY the entry-moment spread between Binance.US $250 ask and Bitstamp USD midpoint, plus whether the gap persists 60 seconds later and whether both venues' pair statuses are normal. Compute **zero** forward returns or exit prices. Pass line to build a profit card: at least 15 distinct UTC days with at least one clean >= 40 bp persistent gap on a healthy non-stablecoin pair (at least 30 total coin-hour events). Below 15 distinct days in 30 days, close on count.
- Pass mark and cost fixed before any later profit run: Judged cost is actual Binance.US $250 book walk on entry and exit plus STATED 2 bp/side taker fee (1 bp on BNBUSD). Pass requires at least 60 judged trades on at least 30 distinct UTC days over up to 180 days, mean net return strictly above zero, day-clustered t at least 2.0, mean net return above zero without the best 2 trades, and positive excess return over the same-time Bitstamp midpoint move (proving basis recovery rather than a lucky broad-market bounce).
- What in the ledger it would repeat: **None.** Measurement 133 (ledger 232, queue 137) reads Binance.US alone and cannot measure cross-venue gaps. E12 (ledger 230, queue 135) is a single-venue Bitcoin-drop dip-buy. Plan 76 (ledger 229, queue 134) is a Bitstamp-only 24-hour washout. This operationalizes the existing Round 61 Question 5 and Round 68 Decision 3 WATCH using Bitstamp in place of the barred Coinbase comparator.
- Data terms status: `api.binance.us` public `NONE` endpoints (allowed on API docs; full Terms of Use **UNKNOWN**, same condition as ledgers 230 to 232) and `www.bitstamp.net` public API (allows statistics/calculations; non-commercial and US terms **UNKNOWN**, `doc 194`). Keep raw snapshots off the public repo.
- Verdict: **COUNT FIRST (forward only).** Sole survivor, kept only because the 30-day count stage costs almost nothing and cleanly tests whether hour-scale local discounts actually exist on thin overlapping USD pairs.

## Candidate 2: Deep Resting Bids Far Below Touch on Thin Pairs (KILL / SKIP)

- Plain-words rule: On thin Binance.US USD or USDT altcoin pairs, post a `LIMIT_MAKER` (STATED 0.00% fee) $250 buy order resting 3% to 5% below the current best bid, refreshed every hour, aiming to fill only on a thin-book retail market-sell wick, then exit with a limit sell near the pre-wick midpoint or a taker exit after 2 to 6 hours.
- Comparison with Measurement 121 on closed-end funds (`docs/research/170_m121_result.md`, ledgers 223 and 224): In Measurement 121, pretend $500 bids resting at the **best bid** for 30 minutes on 23 CEFs filled 7.56% of the time (227 of 3,003), and 5 minutes after fill the best bid sat **-26.6 bp below the fill price** (95% interval -30.3 to -23.0 bp). Deep bids 3% to 5% below touch on Binance.US differ in two ways: they do not try to earn a 12.6 bp half-spread against ordinary order flow (targeting 100 to 300+ bp wick rebounds instead), and Binance.US charges 0% maker fee. However, they suffer a **worse** form of the exact same adverse-selection trap for an hourly-waking agent: in crypto, most 3% to 5% hourly drops are correlated market-wide crashes (such as the Oct 10 crash simulation in Bieganowski and Ślepaczuk 2026 cited in `doc 196`), not isolated local fat-finger wicks. Because an hourly agent cannot cancel its bid during the 59 minutes between wakes, any sudden BTC drop leaves our stale bid sitting on the book to be hit by fast arbitrageurs right as prices fall further.
- Who loses the money and why they keep doing it: Ideally, a retail seller who dumps a large market order into a thin altcoin book. In practice, with a 60-minute wake interval, **we** are the stale liquidity provider who loses to faster traders whenever the whole crypto market drops mid-hour.
- Why a human cannot do it but an hourly agent can: An agent can maintain resting bids across 20 to 50 thin pairs 24/7, but an **hourly** wake is structurally too slow to cancel stale bids during mid-hour market-wide moves.
- Count stage that reads no price outcome: Would require 30+ days of forward order-book and `aggTrades` logging to count how many 3% to 5% wicks are Binance.US-only vs correlated BTC drops (not worth running while E12 already logs resting bids).
- Pass mark and cost if ever tested: 0% STATED maker entry plus 2 bp taker exit plus exit spread walk; strict trade-through fill only; positive 5-minute and 2-hour bid markout with 95% day-clustered CI strictly above zero (matching Measurement 121's bar).
- What in the ledger it would repeat: Overlaps Measurement 121 (ledgers 223 and 224, closed on adverse selection), E12's already-running 300-second strict-trade-through resting-bid line on Binance.US USD pairs (ledger 230, queue 135), Round 61 Question 3, and Round 68 Decision 2.
- Data terms status: `api.binance.us` (`NONE` endpoints; full Terms **UNKNOWN**).
- Verdict: **KILL / SKIP.** Unattended hourly deep bids are stale-quote prey during market-wide crashes; wait for E12's already-running resting-bid readout before any new maker study.

## Candidate 4: Same-Venue USD-Stablecoin and Coin-Coin Pair Gaps (KILL / SKIP)

When buying a coin with USD, selling it for USDT, and selling USDT for USD clears the 6 bp 3-leg taker fee (or 2 to 4 bp with maker legs), execute the triangle on Binance.US with zero transfers.
- Who loses the money and why they keep doing it: Retail traders who hit a thin USD book while ignoring the deeper USDT book on the same exchange.
- Why a human cannot do it but an hourly agent can: A human cannot compute 56 triangles every hour, **but neither can an hourly agent win this race**. Because there is zero transfer delay, zero withdrawal hold, and only 6 bp total taker fee (0% maker), any colocated API bot (`docs.binance.us` allows 6,000 weight/min and 50 orders/10s) sweeps a genuine crossed triangle (`ask < bid - 6 bp`) in milliseconds. Any apparent gap left open at an hourly wake is either inside the bid-ask spread (not executable) or a stablecoin-peg stress episode on USDT/USD or USDC/USD (which the Round 69 checker already killed: capped 25 to 50 bp upside, open-ended downside, clustered in a few stress weeks).
- Count stage that reads no price outcome: Not warranted for a standalone hourly strategy. (For execution only, comparing direct USD walk vs USD-to-USDT-to-coin walk on thin pairs is a pure cost comparison that requires no return calculation.)

- What in the ledger it would repeat: Stablecoin-leg depeg trades repeat the Round 21 skip list (`docs/QUEUE.md` line 17) and the Round 69 checker ruling (`docs/research/197_idea_round_69_dated_crypto_events.md`).
