# 209f. On-chain jobs built for bots

INFERENCE: Machines are the intended workers in several protocols. That does not make them good businesses for a new machine. Brevis is the strongest evidence of a later-joining prover ID receiving fees; its displayed fees are too concentrated and too small to establish newcomer profit. Tellor is a plausible no-money accounting screen, not a launch candidate. UNKNOWN: No method reviewed demonstrates repeatable net bankable cash for a new solo operator using a few hundred dollars. This extends 205g by examining unattended operating steps, rather than reopening its freelance or staking-only ideas.

## Gates for the whole lane

- MEASURED: The [IRS digital asset guide](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/digital-assets) treats digital assets as property and calls for USD records for receipts and dispositions. MEASURED: Idaho [requires licensing for money transmission unless exempt](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title26/T26CH29/SECT26-2903/) and [FinCEN guidance](https://www.fincen.gov/sites/default/files/2019-05/FinCEN%20Guidance%20CVC%20FINAL%20508.pdf) distinguishes own use from accepting and transmitting value as a business. INFERENCE: Own-work fees and stake incentives do not by themselves establish Idaho venue access or eligibility. In particular, bridge repayment and settlement of other people's trades need a facts-specific review before any live activity. No customer custody or funds routed in this research.
- INFERENCE: Never infer one person per wallet, that an empty wallet always operated without stake, or that total network fees equal net cash. Include quiet and unsuccessful operators and dates, as well as fees, capital held, downtime, payouts that cannot be withdrawn, exit costs, and machine time. UNKNOWN remains UNKNOWN if logs cannot show failed attempts or work assignments. No margin, APIs requiring keys, account creation, contacts, purchases, submissions or live calls were needed to write this page. Rules and public pages checked November 27, 2026.
- INFERENCE: Every replay is a future proposal only. Freeze a recorded history interval, documented rules in force, a virtual USD capital ceiling of $500 and fixed costs before opening outcomes; use only authorized public archives or licensed downloads and keep any restricted source data off the repo. Replay only information visible by each deadline, not a hindsight selection of successful jobs. Unrecorded private opportunities are not credited and a replay cannot invent a fill or acceptance. For attack-adjacent roles the only exercise is descriptive historical accounting; no transaction construction, targeting, signing or bot development. No stake is committed even for a replay.
- INFERENCE: Common bank step for any live route, if later approved: OWNER ONCE opens an Idaho-eligible exchange and links a bank, and completes KYC and tax setup. No confirmed cash-out for a given token means no bankable income. AGENT ALONE prepares the monthly receipts, USD basis, fees, losses and cutoff report. OWNER EVERY TIME authorizes conversion, transfer and bank withdrawal when taking cash; owner files taxes periodically. This batching does not block unattended protocol work but does block literal end-to-end autonomy under the keys and money rule. Nobody is assumed to perform customer service, refunds or dispute resolution for free.

MEASURED: Summing the ten Brevis GPU-labeled rows gives 2,239.33 BREV, 97.3% of displayed network fees; 16 of the 17 displayed rows had positive fees, not 16 of 17 new people trying ([dashboard](https://provernet.brevis.network/)). 5. INFERENCE: $0 accounting replay, at most one existing-machine day. Collect recorded job posting, stake tier, submission deadline, fulfilled fee and nonfulfillment, if publicly archived under permitted terms; freeze first 21 days and hold back the next seven. Benchmark only known local toy/authorized proof inputs; charge full occupancy, power, fees and downtime. If no timestamped cohort and job requirements, stop with no performance claim.
