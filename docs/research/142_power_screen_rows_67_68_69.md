## Row 67: Low-frequency overnight filter

- **STATED, closest match:** [EdgeLab's QQQ study](https://edgelabtrading.com/blog/overnight-trading-strategy-nasdaq/) uses March 1999 to June 9, 2026 and reports +4 bp per trade net (5 bp per side), 55.7% winners and a 0.16 Sharpe for buying the third consecutive down close and selling the next open. The same entry rule with a next-close exit had 584 trades over that period, roughly 21 to 22 a year, and +35 bp net per trade, but that includes the next session and is not an overnight estimate ([EdgeLab's QQQ study](https://edgelabtrading.com/blog/overnight-trading-strategy-nasdaq/)). The close-to-open variants from two to five down closes ranged from −1 to +25 bp per trade, but the publisher did not map those endpoints to exact counts or cutoffs, or publish a separate modern mean or dispersion for the three-down overnight rule ([EdgeLab's QQQ study](https://edgelabtrading.com/blog/overnight-trading-strategy-nasdaq/)).
- **STATED, order timing:** The rule uses the final close to decide whether to buy at that close. A spot check starting in August 2023 had only 24 of 46 signals in place at 15:30 ET ([EdgeLab's timing check](https://edgelabtrading.com/blog/overnight-trading-strategy-nasdaq/)). The measured study rule does not establish a pre-order signal for the closing print. The count of actionable nights and the exact matched-night edge after costs are UNKNOWN. The 2,740 ceiling counts adjacent sessions only; 240 is a generous assumption, not a count of historical signals. A full-year model would have 242 triggers at 22 a year, not 242 known dates.
- **STATED, other evidence:** [Georgeded's CRSP replication](https://github.com/georgeded/overnight-intraday-anomaly) reports 2021 to 2025 overnight-minus-intraday gaps of −1.0 bp/day for SPY and −0.1 bp/day for QQQ. Its prior-close VIX filter ranked at the 58th and 52nd percentiles of random sets of nights, respectively, before costs in the reworked 2019 to 2025 test, and is not a few-dozen-a-year rule ([Georgeded's CRSP replication](https://github.com/georgeded/overnight-intraday-anomaly)). [Bartolini's SPY study](https://alphaarchitect.com/trading-costs-wipe-out-the-overnight-return-anomaly/) reports −32% cumulative after spread and commission on 6,800 nights, January 1993 to January 2020; its high-earnings grouping covered eight named months and made +12% cumulatively after those costs, with no post-2016 isolated estimate or separate count. [Quantified Strategies' SPY filters](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/4-overnight-trading-strategies/) show 35 to 168 trades in October 2005 to 2012, but with incomplete triggers, no matching noise estimate and no later period.
- **MEASURED on synthetic data:** There is no matching published paired SD. For a favorable sensitivity, the 47 bp SD is from 59 prior-close-to-open ES futures gaps before FOMC announcements in September 2016 to December 2023; the reported mean was +19 bp with 7 bp SE, and fees were not specified ([Pre-FOMC Uncertainty Accumulation, Table III](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=260400)). That is a noise proxy only, not a QQQ measurement, not a $500 cash trade, and not a proposal to reopen the FOMC rule. Give the entire +4 bp QQQ net return to the edge over cash, with no deduction for a matched ordinary night: 21.72% at 47 bp noise and 9.94% at 94 bp. At 47 bp the first 33% grid point is +5.51 bp net. Close before prices; revisit only with a timestamped pre-close signal, enough eligible nights and an independent paired after-cost measurement.

## Row 68: Monthly options expiration week

- **STATED, direct post-2016 SPY:** [Stock Alarm's comparison](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/options-expiration-week-stock-behavior) uses dividend-adjusted SPY from January 2016 through October 2026, prior Friday close to expiration close. The 130 expiration weeks averaged +1 bp (52% up) against +37 bp for 437 regular weeks (60% up); non-quarterly expirations averaged +18 bp across 87, quarterly ones −34 bp across 43. It states no execution costs, formal SD or standalone daily-expiry-era mean ([Stock Alarm's comparison](https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/options-expiration-week-stock-behavior)). [FinLab's separate five-session comparison](https://finlab.finance/en/blog/quad-witching-backtest), January 2016 to November 5, 2026, reports +26 bp SPY for 87 non-quarterly events and −35 bp for 43 quarterly events versus +30 bp for any five-session hold, before execution costs on those event averages. Dropping quarterly expirations does not produce a positive premium over the published regular-week alternatives.
- **STATED, options regime:** Cboe announced Tuesday and Thursday SPXW listings for April and May 2022, and the corresponding SPY/QQQ weekly listings for November 14 and 16, 2022 ([Cboe SPXW notice](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/how-to-make-the-most-of-added-spxw-options-expirations/); [Cboe SPY/QQQ notice](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/product_update/2022/Cboe-Options-to-List-SPY-and-QQQ-Tuesday-and-Thursday-Expiring-Weekly-Options.pdf)). The 48 completed months from December 2022 have their own calendar count, but a separate matched SPY estimate and paired SD for those 48 are UNKNOWN. [ChartingLens](https://chartinglens.com/blog/options-expiration-week-stock-returns) reports S&P 500 index price returns of −27 bp vs +38 bp for other weeks in the 2020s (starts before the five-day era), roughly 200 bp weekly SD, and no execution-cost treatment. Its full sample reports +22 bp vs +23 bp; the published 443 OpEx observations are one higher than a completed-week count through its stated November 16, 2026 cutoff would allow. Use its noise as approximate only, not its count; the repo's dates-only Plan 66 calendar gives 131 completed expirations through November 20. December is future at cutoff. Plan 66 was a different settlement-day clock and is not reopened here.
- **MEASURED on synthetic data:** The older [Quantified Strategies SPY test](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/options-expiration-week/) reports +20 bp from the opening print of expiration week to expiration close; its equity chart states 3 bp in commissions and slippage per trade, but whether the quoted average incorporates it is unclear. The same article reports +30 bp for the alternate S&P 500 prior-week-close entry, with no distinct cost note or dated modern split ([Quantified Strategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/options-expiration-week/)). Give the +30 bp entirely to the old rule, ignoring normal-week drift and subtracting only 4 bp: 28.88% (32.26% at 2 bp). Treat the +20 bp as already net and give it all to SPY: 20.09%. At 4 bp cost, the modern direct SPY difference passes in 4 of 50,000 simulations (0.008%), and the 2020s index difference passes in none. The first grid point crossing 33% is +30 bp net on the full calendar; the pure five-day-options subset is also below the fixed 100-date mark.

## Row 69: Muni month-end and reinvestment

- **STATED, month-end:** S&P identifies the VTEB benchmark as monthly; its municipal methodology describes the standard change as effective after the last business-day close ([Vanguard VTEB](https://investor.vanguard.com/investment-products/etfs/profile/vteb); [S&P national index](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/indices/fixed-income/sp-national-amt-free-municipal-bond-index/); [S&P municipal methodology](https://www.msrb.org/sites/default/files/Standard-and-Poors-Index-Methodology.pdf)). This establishes a possible prior-close to month-end-close clock, not a premium. The cutoff excludes November and December 2026 month-ends; the November 27 early close does not make it month-end ([NYSE market calendar](https://www.nyse.com/markets/hours-calendars)). MUB and VTEB share the calendar, so two tickers do not double the sample.
- **STATED, seasonal:** [AllianceBernstein](https://www.alliancebernstein.com/content/dam/global/insights/insights-external-pdf/2024/muniland/04-22-24-the-week-in-muniland.pdf) reports raw Bloomberg Municipal Bond Index mean returns since 1991 of +31 bp in June and +80 bp in July, not duration-adjusted ETF premiums or net trading returns. [Western Asset](https://www.westernasset.com/us/en/research/blog/weekly-municipal-monitor-summer-technicals-2026-07-21.cfm) reports summer net supply averaged negative in 2015 to 2023 but turned positive in 2024 and 2025; its single 2026 Memorial-Day-to-July-17 comparison is +119 bp for the muni index and +69 bp for a broad Treasury index, with no duration match specified. [BCA Research's July 2018 note](https://www.bcaresearch.com/topic?name=MBS&page=14) and [December 2020 note](https://www.bcaresearch.com/topic?name=MBS&page=10) report +66 bp and +56 bp, respectively, versus duration-equivalent Treasury indices. Each is one month of index evidence, before any specified ETF execution costs, not a repeated seasonal mean.
- **UNKNOWN, needed to justify a replay:** Neither MUB nor VTEB has a located 2016-on published seasonal or month-end return series that is both based on executable ETF prints and paired against an ex-ante duration-matched Treasury alternative. The same-window paired SD is also UNKNOWN. Doc 80's finding on four taxable bond ETFs is a warning, not evidence about these muni funds. Replay 48's muni CEF January losers and Replay 53's taxable CEF January losers are different strategies and are not reopened here.
- **MEASURED on synthetic data:** The zero-edge month-end case passes 0.22%, with the 33% grid point at +5.30 bp net. At full-month frequency, repeat the selected +66 bp July and +56 bp December index observations in every such month and give June zero, with 4 bp friction on the difference. Even this favorable synthetic profile passes 25.04%; the first 33% grid point for a constant net edge is +43.55 bp. Neither is an estimate of the ETF residual. Both use the published 5.68% three-year MUB standard deviation as of October 31, 2026, scaled by square-root time to one session or one month ([iShares MUB statistics](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/239766/ishares-national-amtfree-muni-bond-etf)). This is ETF volatility, not measured residual dispersion. Revisit only with a published matched ETF residual and noise, not another raw index gain or a supply forecast.
