## Deep Dive 1: Paid Public-Data and Federal Grant/Tender Alert Subscriptions

Scope: Automated daily or hourly monitoring of official public government feeds (such as SAM.gov public contract opportunities APIs, Grants.gov, SEC EDGAR, and Treasury Fiscal Data) filtered by industry code, keyword, and deadline, delivered by email or webhook to small B2B subscribers billed via Stripe.

1. Process from nothing to money in a bank account:
- Step 1: Pick one narrow B2B niche (for example, small-business set-aside federal IT/engineering solicitations or state/federal grant notices) and register a domain, landing page, and transactional email sender.
- Step 2: Register a public API key on the official government data portal (such as SAM.gov public APIs via Open GSA and SAM Data Services, rotating keys every 90 days as required) ([SAM.gov Terms of Use](https://sam.gov/about/terms-of-use)).
- Step 3: Set up a Stripe account, tax/identity verification, bank payout link, and self-serve subscription checkout page ([Stripe Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)).
- Step 4: Build an automated pipeline that polls public API endpoints on a schedule, deduplicates new notices, summarizes scope/eligibility/deadlines with an LLM, and sends tailored email/RSS/webhook alerts.
- Step 5: Publish a public archive of daily sample alerts (and optional free weekly tier) so search engines and niche buyers can verify signal quality before paying.
- Step 6: Handle self-serve upgrades, cancellations, failed-card retries, and automated refund rules inside Stripe Customer Portal.
- Step 7: Handle edge-case customer support messages, tax reporting (IRS Schedule C / 1099-K), and 90-day API key rotation.

2. Who can do each step:
- Step 1 (domain, hosting, email DNS): OWNER ONCE + SCAFFOLDING.
- Step 2 (SAM.gov account and API key, 90-day rotation): OWNER ONCE for initial Login.gov account, then OWNER ONCE every 90 days for key rotation (SAM.gov requires updating the API key every 90 days and forbids sharing individual API keys) ([SAM.gov Terms of Use](https://sam.gov/about/terms-of-use)).
- Step 3 (Stripe account, KYC, bank, tax form): OWNER ONCE.
- Step 4 (polling public API, filtering, LLM digest generation, alert delivery): SCAFFOLDING + AGENT ALONE.
- Step 5 (public archive and free sample feed): SCAFFOLDING + AGENT ALONE.
- Step 6 (billing, cancellations, receipts, payouts): SCAFFOLDING (Stripe handles automatically).
- Step 7 (rare billing disputes and annual tax filing): OWNER EVERY TIME for rare escalated disputes and annual tax filing, not per alert or per subscriber.
- Blocking step for 100% zero-touch automation: SAM.gov's 90-day API key rotation and initial customer acquisition (distribution is the real bottleneck, not fulfillment).

3. Platform and payer rules on AI or automated work:
- SAM.gov Terms of Use (MEASURED): Explicitly provides public APIs and extracts via Open GSA and SAM Data Services and states "If you want to share data publicly, only share data from public versions of APIs." ([SAM.gov Terms of Use](https://sam.gov/about/terms-of-use)). At the same time, SAM.gov prohibits automated website extraction tools outside the official APIs, prohibits using FOUO or sensitive APIs to build a public view, and prohibits commercial or resale use of Dun and Bradstreet (D&B) data (except limited D&B Open Data with attribution and no bulk sharing) ([SAM.gov Terms of Use](https://sam.gov/about/terms-of-use)). INFERENCE: A compliant alert product must read ONLY public non-FOUO federal contract/grant APIs and strip any D&B-sourced fields.
- Stripe Prohibited and Restricted Businesses (MEASURED): Does not list paid data subscriptions, alert digests, or general SaaS tools as prohibited or restricted ([Stripe Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)).

4. Evidence that it pays:
- STATED: Subscription SaaS and software tools command the highest acquisition multiples among small online businesses, with Acquire.com reporting a 3.9x median annual profit multiple across confirmed SaaS sales in both 2024 and 2025, Flippa H1 2026 averaging 2.47x (4.06x top quartile), and Exit Spring 2026 SaaS listings asking a median 3.25x profit at 3.5 median owner hours per week ([Surges Sold-Deal Guide](https://www.surges.co/guides/startups-actually-sell/), [Exit Spring 2026 Report](https://exitspring.com/blog/online-business-market-report)).
- UNKNOWN: No public government or marketplace base rate exists for the share of new solo public-data alert subscriptions that reach profitability or their median monthly revenue.

5. What it needs before the first dollar:
- Money: Domain and small server/email hosting (low fixed cost, exact amount UNKNOWN until provider chosen); zero inventory, zero ad spend required to test organically, zero staked capital.
- Time and hard-to-get assets: Building the ingestion and filtering scaffolding once, plus distribution to reach niche buyers (government contractors or compliance teams) without cold-email spam.

6. Agent advantage versus crowding:
- INFERENCE: Existing federal procurement alert incumbents (GovWin, GovTribe, HigherGov, BidNet) already automate raw keyword matching, so raw listing dumps are crowded. An AI agent's plausible edge is reading attached 50-page PDF solicitation documents and summarizing exact eligibility, set-aside rules, and hidden technical requirements into a 10-line go/no-go brief faster and cheaper than a human bid manager.

7. Honest test before any money goes in:
- INFERENCE: Run a 30-day private dry run against live public SAM.gov/Grants.gov/SEC feeds, log precision and recall against real solicitation PDFs (checking for hallucinated deadlines or missed eligibility clauses), and publish a free public sample digest on a static page with a waitlist signup before activating paid Stripe checkout. If zero real users subscribe to the free digest, stop before paying for infrastructure or marketing.

8. Verdict: WORTH A TEST. Clear public API rules, zero inventory or staking, full post-setup automation, and a zero-dollar dry run that tests both accuracy and real demand first.

## Deep Dive 2: Rental of Owned Compute Hardware (GPU Hosting on Vast.ai)

Scope: Connecting an owned GPU server to a decentralized peer-to-peer GPU rental marketplace (Vast.ai) where the platform handles customer discovery, billing, and payouts automatically.

1. Process from nothing to money in a bank account:
- Step 1: Acquire or repurpose GPU server hardware, configure Linux/Docker and reliable power/internet, and register as a host on Vast.ai.
- Step 2: Complete Vast.ai host verification and connect a verified B2B-capable payout account on Wise, PayPal, or Stripe ([Vast.ai Host Payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment)).
- Step 3: Set on-demand or interruptible hourly pricing for GPU, CPU, storage, and bandwidth in the Vast.ai host console.
- Step 4: Vast.ai automatically matches renters, runs Docker containers on the host hardware, monitors uptime, and bills renters.
- Step 5: Every Friday, Vast.ai generates an invoice once the balance reaches at least $20 USD, and schedules payout the following Friday via Wise, PayPal, or Stripe (direct bank/ACH/wire transfers are not available) ([Vast.ai Host Payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment)).
- Step 6: Monitor hardware health, electricity bills, cooling, and occasional reboots or component failures; report net income on taxes.

2. Who can do each step:
- Step 1 (hardware purchase, physical assembly, network setup): OWNER ONCE (physical).
- Step 2 (host verification, Wise/PayPal/Stripe B2B account, tax verification): OWNER ONCE ([Vast.ai Host Payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment)).
- Step 3 (pricing optimization): SCAFFOLDING + AGENT ALONE (via pricing updates, within Vast.ai Terms).
- Step 4 (renter matching, container orchestration, billing, customer support): SCAFFOLDING (platform-automated).
- Step 5 (weekly Friday invoicing and payouts above $20): SCAFFOLDING (platform-automated) ([Vast.ai Host Payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment)).
- Step 6 (physical repairs, power outages, hardware replacement): OWNER EVERY TIME when hardware fails.
- Blocking step for full automation: Upfront physical hardware capital and physical maintenance when hardware or power fails.

3. Platform and payer rules on AI or automated work:
- Vast.ai Host Payouts (MEASURED): Explicitly built for independent hosts earning automated payouts via Wise, PayPal, or Stripe once verified for B2B payments, with a $20 USD minimum payout threshold and weekly Friday invoicing ([Vast.ai Host Payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment)).
- Vast.ai Terms of Service (STATED from search snippet; full terms page not separately opened): Prohibits automated extraction of marketplace data to build competing price-comparison or market-data products, while permitting hosts to operate independent hosting services.

4. Evidence that it pays:
- MEASURED: Vast.ai's official payout documentation confirms weekly Friday payouts above $20 USD through Wise, PayPal, and Stripe, but explicitly disclaims guaranteed returns: host earnings depend on hardware specifications, pricing, uptime and reliability, geographic location, and current customer demand, and "Market conditions change over time, so earnings cannot be guaranteed." ([Vast.ai Host Payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment)).
- UNKNOWN: Vast.ai gates its Market Stats page behind a host login with a registered machine ([Vast.ai Host Payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment)), so verified median net profit after electricity and hardware depreciation is UNKNOWN from public pages.

5. What it needs before the first dollar:
- Money: Physical GPU hardware (zero new spend ONLY if testing on an already-owned idle GPU; otherwise thousands of dollars of hardware capex), plus ongoing residential/commercial electricity and high-upload internet.
- Time and hard-to-get assets: Initial machine verification and building a high uptime/reliability score, plus up to two weeks for the first payout ([Vast.ai Host Payouts](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment)).

6. Agent advantage versus crowding:
- INFERENCE: Heavily crowded by commercial colocation datacenters and hobbyist rigs competing on commodity $/GPU-hour. An AI agent cannot improve physical GPU compute speed or lower Idaho residential electricity rates; it can only automate dynamic pricing adjustments.

7. Honest test before any money goes in:
- INFERENCE: If the owner already owns an idle CUDA-capable GPU, a zero-capex test is possible by measuring actual metered kWh cost against simulated/live listed Vast.ai rates for that exact GPU model before buying any hardware. Without existing spare hardware, there is no zero-capital test.

8. Verdict: WATCH. Platform automation and payouts are genuine, but buying new hardware fails the pretend-money-first rule and competes against low-cost commercial datacenters; only worth revisiting if already-owned idle GPU hardware exists.

## Deep Dive 3: Amazon FBA Reimbursement and Parcel Refund Recovery as a Service

Scope: Auditing Amazon FBA sellers' inventory, return, and removal ledgers to identify lost or damaged units that Amazon did not automatically reimburse, filing claims within Amazon's windows, and charging a contingency fee or monthly SaaS subscription.

1. Process from nothing to money in a bank account:
- Step 1: Acquire Amazon FBA seller clients who authorize read-only SP-API inventory/reimbursement reports (and grant Seller Central case-filing access or file cases themselves).
- Step 2: Pull FBA inventory ledger, shipment, removal, and reimbursement reports on a rolling schedule to catch discrepancies before the 60-day deadline.
- Step 3: Cross-reference lost/damaged units, unreturned customer refunds, and removal discrepancies against automatic reimbursements already issued by Amazon.
- Step 4: Collect supplier invoices or sourcing-cost documentation from the seller to substantiate unit valuation ([Amazon FBA Reimbursement Policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G200213130)).
- Step 5: File eligible reimbursement cases inside Seller Central (or Seller Assistant) within the 60-day dispute/claim window ([Amazon FBA Reimbursement Policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G200213130)).
- Step 6: Track Amazon's approvals and denials, reply to Seller Support documentation requests, and invoice the seller via Stripe (monthly fee or percentage of confirmed reimbursements).
- Step 7: Handle seller churn, billing disputes, and tax reporting.

2. Who can do each step:
- Step 1 (client sales, trust, SP-API authorization): OWNER EVERY TIME for enterprise/agency sales, or OWNER ONCE + SCAFFOLDING for self-serve SaaS onboarding.
- Step 2 and Step 3 (ledger ingestion and discrepancy detection): SCAFFOLDING + AGENT ALONE.
- Step 4 (sourcing-cost invoices from the seller): CLIENT / OWNER EVERY TIME when Amazon requests cost validation ([Amazon FBA Reimbursement Policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G200213130)).
- Step 5 (filing claims in Seller Central): CLIENT or OWNER EVERY TIME if automated bulk case filing is restricted.
- Step 6 (Stripe billing and payout): OWNER ONCE + SCAFFOLDING.
- Step 7 (tax reporting and escalated disputes): OWNER ONCE / rare OWNER EVERY TIME.
- Blocking step for full automation: Client acquisition plus Seller Central manual claim filing and supplier invoice validation.

3. Platform and payer rules on AI or automated work:
- Amazon FBA Inventory Reimbursement Policy (MEASURED): For pre-order shipment-to-Amazon, removal, and fulfillment-center-operations claims, Amazon reimburses the item's "sourcing cost" (excluding shipping, handling, customs duties, or other costs), caps single-unit reimbursements at $5,000, allows valuation disputes via Seller Central or Seller Assistant within 60 days after reimbursement, and may request documentation to validate seller-submitted sourcing cost ([Amazon FBA Reimbursement Policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G200213130)). The official policy page does not state permission for third-party automated claim-filing bots (UNKNOWN) ([Amazon FBA Reimbursement Policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G200213130)).
- Stripe Prohibited and Restricted Businesses (MEASURED): Billing sellers for software or auditing services is not prohibited, whereas receiving settlement proceeds on behalf of third-party sellers (payment aggregation) is restricted ([Stripe Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)).

4. Evidence that it pays:
- MEASURED / STATED: Amazon FBA is the largest online business model listed for sale on Empire Flippers in 2026 (89 of 210 listings, median $107,371 yearly profit) ([Exit Spring 2026 Report](https://exitspring.com/blog/online-business-market-report)), confirming a large active seller base. However, Amazon's switch to sourcing-cost valuation (instead of retail price) and automatic reimbursement for standard warehouse losses materially reduced the unclaimed pool ([Amazon FBA Reimbursement Policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/gp/help/external/G200213130)).
- UNKNOWN: No public base rate exists for net revenue earned by new solo FBA reimbursement tools in 2026.

5. What it needs before the first dollar:
- Money: Low software/hosting cost; zero inventory.
- Time and hard-to-get assets: Amazon SP-API developer registration and seller trust to grant account report access.

6. Agent advantage versus crowding:
- INFERENCE: Crowded by established FBA audit firms and software tools (Sellerboard, GETIDA, Carbon6) that already reconcile FBA ledgers. Amazon's own automatic reimbursement rollout and sourcing-cost reduction have compressed margins across the category.

7. Honest test before any money goes in:
- INFERENCE: A software-only audit script can be built and tested on synthetic FBA ledger CSVs locally, or offered free to one volunteer seller in read-only audit mode (producing a report the seller files themselves) to measure whether unclaimed discrepancies after Amazon's auto-reimbursements are large enough to justify paying.

8. Verdict: WATCH. Real B2B pain point, but squeezed by Amazon's 2025 to 2026 sourcing-cost and auto-reimbursement changes, silent on third-party automated filing, and dependent on seller onboarding.

## Deep Dive 4: Local Lead Generation (Rank-and-Rent and Pay-Per-Lead Sites)

Scope: Building local city-plus-service websites (for example, Boise/Nampa roofers, plumbers, or HVAC) to rank in organic search, capture phone calls or web forms, and sell those leads to local contractors.

1. Process from nothing to money in a bank account:
- Step 1: Research local service keywords, register domains, and build city-specific websites with phone tracking numbers and quote forms.
- Step 2: Publish local service pages and build backlinks/citations to rank in Google Search.
- Step 3: Route incoming calls or form leads to a local contractor.
- Step 4: Cold-call or email local contractors to negotiate a per-lead or monthly rental fee.
- Step 5: Invoice contractors via Stripe, handle disputed or junk leads, and replace contractors who churn.
- Step 6: Report income and taxes.

2. Who can do each step:
- Step 1 and Step 2 (site generation and publishing): SCAFFOLDING + AGENT ALONE (but directly collides with Google Spam Policies if scaled across cities).
- Step 3 (call/form forwarding): SCAFFOLDING.
- Step 4 (selling local contractors and verifying lead quality): OWNER EVERY TIME.
- Step 5 (dispute resolution on invalid leads and contractor replacement): OWNER EVERY TIME.
- Step 6 (Stripe setup and tax filing): OWNER ONCE.
- Blocking step for full automation: Google Search's anti-spam rules against scaled/doorway sites plus human B2B sales and lead-dispute handling.

3. Platform and payer rules on AI or automated work:
- Google Search Spam Policies (MEASURED - BLOCKING): Explicitly bans both Scaled Content Abuse and Doorway Abuse. Verbatim: "Scaled content abuse is when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users. This abusive practice is typically focused on creating large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value to users, no matter how it's created." Examples include "Using generative AI tools or other similar tools to generate many pages without adding value for users" and "Creating multiple sites with the intent of hiding the scaled nature of the content" ([Google Search Spam Policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). Doorway Abuse verbatim: "Doorway abuse is when sites or pages are created to rank for specific, similar search queries. They lead users to intermediate pages that aren't as useful as the final destination." 4. Evidence that it pays:

- Money: Domains, hosting, call-tracking numbers, and months of SEO wait time.
- Time and hard-to-get assets: Organic search rankings against entrenched directories (Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack, Google Local Services Ads) and signed contractor buyers.

## Deep Dive 5: Unclaimed Property Finding as a Paid Service (Idaho Statutory Check)

Scope: Searching public state unclaimed-property databases to match forgotten funds to living owners or heirs, signing a contingency fee agreement, and filing the claim on their behalf.

1. Process from nothing to money in a bank account:
- Step 1: Search state unclaimed-property databases for high-value unclaimed records that are legally eligible for finder agreements.
- Step 2: Locate current contact information for the apparent owner or heirs (skip tracing).
- Step 3: Contact the owner by mail or phone, explain the claim, and obtain a signed locator agreement plus formal power-of-attorney or agent authorization.
- Step 4: Gather identity proof, heirship documents, or address history from the owner and submit evidence of authority to the state administrator ([Idaho Code 14-5-1302](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title14/t14ch5/sect14-5-1302/)).
- Step 5: Wait for state verification and payout, collect the contingency fee, and report taxes.

2. Who can do each step:
- Step 1 (searching public lists): SCAFFOLDING + AGENT ALONE (subject to each state portal's access terms).
- Step 2 (skip tracing and identity matching): SCAFFOLDING + AGENT ALONE (requires paid lawful people-search data).
- Step 3 (outreach, overcoming scam skepticism, notarized authorization): OWNER EVERY TIME.
- Step 4 (assembling sensitive identity/probate proof and submitting statutory authority): OWNER EVERY TIME.
- Step 5 (fee collection and tax reporting): OWNER ONCE / OWNER EVERY TIME.
- Blocking step for full automation: Statutory waiting periods, formal agent authorization requirements, and human trust/document collection.

3. Platform and payer rules on AI or automated work:
- Idaho Code 14-5-1301 (MEASURED - BLOCKING): Verbatim: "(1) Subject to subsection (2) of this section, an agreement to locate property held by the administrator is void if it is entered into during the period beginning on the date the property was presumed abandoned by a holder to the administrator and ending twenty-four (24) months after the payment or delivery. (2) This section does not apply to an apparent owner's agreement with an attorney to pursue a claim for recovery of specifically identified property held by the administrator or to contest the administrator's denial of a claim for recovery of the property." ([Idaho Code 14-5-1301](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title14/t14ch5/sect14-5-1301/)).
- Idaho Code 14-5-1302 (MEASURED): An apparent owner may authorize an attorney or designate an agent under Title 15, Chapter 12 (Uniform Power of Attorney Act), and the administrator releases property information only "Upon submission of evidence of authority satisfactory to the administrator" ([Idaho Code 14-5-1302](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title14/t14ch5/sect14-5-1302/)).

4. Evidence that it pays:
- UNKNOWN: While commercial heir-finding firms operate legally across the U.S. (often with attorneys or state-specific licenses), no public base rate or median net income exists for an automated solo finder.

5. What it needs before the first dollar:
- Money: Postage/mailers, skip-tracing data subscriptions, and state-by-state legal compliance review (multi-state fee caps and private-investigator/attorney rules vary by state).
- Time and hard-to-get assets: High consumer skepticism (because state portals are free to search directly) and slow state claim processing.

6. Agent advantage versus crowding:
- INFERENCE: Automated matching on stale 24+ month lists is already run by incumbent asset-recovery firms; the hard part is persuading skeptical strangers to sign a power of attorney and hand over sensitive ID documents, which an AI agent cannot do autonomously.

7. Honest test before any money goes in:
- INFERENCE: No autonomous zero-contact test exists because the core conversion step requires contacting real people and obtaining signed legal authority.

8. Verdict: OUT. Idaho Code 14-5-1301 voids non-attorney agreements for 24 months, Idaho Code 14-5-1302 requires formal power-of-attorney authorization, and every conversion requires human trust and document handling.
