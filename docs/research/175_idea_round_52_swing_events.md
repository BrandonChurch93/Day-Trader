### Rank 1: Earnings gap-up with high volume (Episodic Pivot or Power Earnings Gap, 20 to 60 session hold)

Why this is distinct from Plan 74 Card A: Plan 74 Card A (`Ledger 226`, `Queue 126`) tests a leading stock near its 52-week high with a tape switch and tight stop; adding is shown only. The proposed combination here is a quarterly earnings gap up in a stock flat or neglected over about six months, with abnormal volume and a 20- to 60-session holding window. The sources below do not test that same combination. A gain below 30% is a testable ceiling, not by itself a definition of neglect or the opposite of trading near a high.

Exact rule as stated:
- STATED, daily Episodic Pivot proxy ([TickerGuard scanner](https://tickerguard.com/stocks/scanner/qullamaggie-ep-original)): open at least 10% above prior close, day volume at least 3 times the 20-day average, less than 30% gain over 126 sessions and no 10%+ gap in the prior 63. The stated fill is the gap day's open; exclude the trade if that day's low is more than 1.5 average daily ranges from entry. Neither full-day volume nor the full day's low is known at the open. A causal daily version needs the next eligible print after the completed day; an intraday version needs newly specified filters known by the order, including a low so far and a completed early-volume window. Neither earns the published open-fill return.
- STATED, broad Power Earnings Gap version ([StreetAlpha](https://streetalpha.app/backtests/earnings-gap-up)): "Power earnings gap: on the first session after the report, the stock opens 8% or more above the prior close, closes at or above the open, on at least 3 times its 50 day average volume."
- STATED, intraday checklist version ([What Works in Trading](https://whatworksintrading.substack.com/p/i-coded-the-six-rules-of-the-episodic)): enter on an opening-range break, with stop at the day low and skip if more than one average daily range away. Sell one-third to one-half after three to five days and trail the rest with a 10- or 20-day average. Skip biotech. TickerGuard's companion also scales out on day 3 and trails, not a fixed 20- or 60-session hold.

Stated numbers, sample, years, costs, and post-publication behavior:
- STATED, TickerGuard commercial scanner and Thomas Mücke's AI-assisted companion article, not a journal paper: 1976 to 2026, 2,843 all-catalyst EP trades, +3.89% mean, −0.31% median, 48.2% wins. The article describes 9,262 US-listed series including delistings, excludes OTC, and charges 20 bp per leg in the base test; the scanner describes its raw $1 minimum, $3 million median dollar volume and 4% average daily range filters, with no global cap. The scanner alone gives +4.8% and 55% wins on earnings, versus +3.7% and 43% without, but does not give subgroup count, fixed 20-/60-session return, matched benchmark, earnings-only modern split, or explicit confirmation that those subgroup figures use the article's costs. The companion's 2020s +4.09% covers both setup families, not earnings-only pivots. The shared open-fill bias makes even an after-fee gross-to-net label unusable for this plan ([scanner](https://tickerguard.com/stocks/scanner/qullamaggie-ep-original), [companion study](https://tickerguard.com/articles/qullamaggie-backtest-study), [doc 51](51_stock_swing_evidence_checked.md)).
- STATED, Whitman Trading, an AI-assisted site article with no peer review stated: 52 largely large-cap US stocks all still listed at publication, January 2000 or listing through September 25, 2026. At least 10% gap and 3 times the prior 50-session average volume, no required earnings event or neglect filter, measured from the signal-day close. It found 239 gaps in 40 names on 230 dates. 141 of 239 (59.0%) beat SPY at 20 sessions versus 51.5% of ordinary stock-days (reported p = 0.023); 147 of 239 (61.5%) at 60 versus 52.6% (p = 0.006). Median lead at 60 sessions was +3.07 points against +0.74 for an ordinary day. At 60 sessions in 2013 to 2026 it was 88 of 148, 59.5% (p = 0.10). Those figures are beat rates and a median, not mean paired net returns; costs are UNKNOWN. Issuer clustering and overlapping holds weaken the reported p-values. Measuring from the completed close does not establish a fill there ([Whitman Trading](https://whitmantrading.com/terms/episodic-pivot/)).
- STATED, StreetAlpha, an unsigned vendor backtest with no peer review stated: mid-2018 to May 2025, US names above $10 and at least $20 million average daily turnover, including some later discontinued. Its power gaps need earnings, an 8%+ opening gap, close at/above the open, and at least 3 times 50-day volume, but no neglect filter. It buys at that close and reports a 10 bp round-trip cost, comparison with the average eligible stock over the same days and random same-day picks. Among 815 outside-S&P-500 trades, the average-stock comparison was −0.26% at 20 and −0.08% at 60 sessions; 53.1% of 20-session trades made money and the raw median was +1.1%. The site calls the latest 18 months a separate holdout and gives 306 trades, +1.85% versus average stock, 52.6% profitable, and says it cannot distinguish that result from luck. UNKNOWN: exact holdout dates, whether the 306 overlap the 815, whether that paragraph is specifically the non-S&P arm, precise holdout horizon, and whether both benchmark and trade paid the cost. Do not add the counts, treat +1.85% as clean validation, or call −0.26% an exact refutation of neglected stocks. The close participates in selecting and filling; a next-print fill was not demonstrated ([StreetAlpha](https://streetalpha.app/backtests/earnings-gap-up)).
- STATED, Ney Torres's October 2026 Substack vendor/author post, free excerpt only: 2015 to June 2026, selected liquid fast US stocks including names that later disappeared, without a complete universe specification. The 291 entries and 126.82R earned by the best 5% are for the BASE entry and exit rules (5 and 6), not the full six-rule subset. Rule 2 in the full checklist uses within 30% of six months earlier, not simply below a +30% rise. Costs included but amounts UNKNOWN; full-checklist count, mean paired return and executable opening-range and day-low details are UNKNOWN in the excerpt. A November correction says the gap to SPY grew after a benchmark calendar error ([What Works in Trading](https://whatworksintrading.substack.com/p/i-coded-the-six-rules-of-the-episodic)).

Data needed, event-count estimate, and Doc 117 power statement:
- Data: dated original earnings releases (SEC EDGAR Item 2.02 acceptance is useful, but 10-Q filing dates alone are not announcement timestamps), point-in-time issuer/stock roster including delistings, and Alpaca SIP daily bars from 2016; minutes if using an early entry. Dead-name coverage and $500 fill feasibility need checking, not assumed.
- Event count: STATED 815 broad gaps over about seven years in StreetAlpha, about 116 per year; 291 base intraday entries over about 11.5 years in Torres, about 25 per year. UNKNOWN: strict six-rule and neglected earnings gap counts, issuer and announcement-date overlap, and actual effective independent counts.
- Doc 117 scenarios: MEASURED arithmetic, inputs STATED assumptions. `400 * (0.02 / 0.15)^2 = 7.11`, and `150 * (0.035 / 0.20)^2 = 4.59375`. This needs at least +1.50% or +3.27% paired net respectively just to meet the heuristic 4 at those counts and noise assumptions. A +2.0% edge in the strict scenario scores only 1.50. Even allowing StreetAlpha's +1.85% as an illustrative true effect gives 6.08 in the broad, not the strict, scenario. The 400/150 effective events and 15%/20% paired noise were not estimated from the exact strategy; raw trade counts cannot substitute. At −0.26% the expected direction fails; the one-sided chance of a false positive is small, not literally zero (about 0.95% in the simple broad normal approximation with a cutoff of 2). The screen uses net mean against a same-clock alternative, not gross return, hit rate, median, or payoff on a selected tail ([doc 117](117_power_screen_before_data.md)).

Honest reasons it may be nothing:
- StreetAlpha's closest modern after-cost estimate is adverse over the main 815-trade span, in liquid non-index stocks, not necessarily neglected small caps.
- TickerGuard's plus signs do not fix the time-of-fill bias. Its negative median applies to all EPs, not necessarily earnings only. Neither it nor Whitman supplies an eligible recent causal matched mean.
- The base-rule tail in Torres shows that large winners matter in one sample, not how much the full checklist earns or how long its losing spells last. A single small book may miss those rare winners.

### Rank 4: Weekly cross-sectional momentum and reversal in liquid coins (Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu and post-publication follow-ups)

Repo overlap: `Queue 110` (`Ledger 216`, `docs/STATUS.md`) already closed weekly crypto dip versus breakout on power citing Grobys et al. (2025) at -0.19% (t = -0.13), and Round 46 (`docs/QUEUE.md` skip list) skipped US spot crypto weekly factor spreads. Because Round 52 specifically asked what happened after publication of Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu (2022), the 2026 replications are summarized here.

Exact rule as stated:
- STATED ([Karaptic Research / Arefev 2026](https://karaptic.com/blog/cross-sectional-momentum-net-of-costs/)): rank by prior returns, long winners and short losers in futures. The exact portfolio cut was not specified on the pages checked. The two prespecified rules are 1-week formation and 1-week hold, or 2-week formation and 2-week hold. The 2-week rules run on alternating Monday phases; neither study arm tests long-only US spot.

Stated numbers, sample, years, costs, and post-publication behavior:
- STATED post-publication reassessment ([Han Oct 2026, SSRN 7624971](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7624971)): "Liu, Tsyvinski, and Wu (2022) document size, price, momentum, volume, and volatility premia in cryptocurrency returns and explain them with a three-factor model. We replicate their study on independently collected data, extend the sample to May 2026, and mark portfolios to market daily. The volume and volatility premia flip sign in replication and vanish out of sample, the size and price premia lose significance out of sample, and no strategy performs consistently over 2014--2026."
- STATED, Arefev's self-published Karaptic article and [SSRN preprint](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7404139): 832 Binance USDⓈ-M perpetual futures symbols from January 2020 to July 2026. The 338 one-week windows do not overlap; independence is not established. The spread was +0.573% gross (52.7% wins); at 0.05% taker fee and 0.05% slippage per side on each of two legs, 0.40 percentage points round trip, it was +0.173% net (50.0% wins), not distinguishable from zero. The 2-week rule earned +0.429% vs −0.076% net per week on alternate Monday phases, not in early and late regimes ([Karaptic](https://karaptic.com/blog/cross-sectional-momentum-net-of-costs/)).

Data needed, event-count estimate, and Doc 117 power statement:
- Data: already held (Binance archive daily bars and Coinbase USD spot access ledger from Plan 75). However, US spot is long-only and costs about 75 bp per side (150 bp round trip).
- Event count: 338 weekly turns from 2020 to mid-2026 (52 per year).
- Doc 117 illustration: 338 effective weeks, if independent, and 10% paired noise need about +1.09% weekly net for score 4. With full weekly turnover and 150 bp assumed round-trip US spot costs, that would need about +2.59% gross from the long leg. Its gross and same-clock paired net returns are UNKNOWN; subtracting spot costs from a two-leg futures spread is not a test.

Honest reasons it may be nothing:
- The modern evidence does not support a dependable weekly cross-sectional return after modeled futures costs. It does not prove the US long-only spot rule is dead, or disprove time-series momentum. The October 2026 Han SSRN study is a working paper, not evidence of net US spot costs. A separate journal paper by Han, Kang and Ryu likewise reports little cross-sectional evidence but strong time-series evidence, with frequent liquidations and a distinction between average returns and profits ([Review of Asset Pricing Studies](https://academic.oup.com/raps/advance-article/doi/10.1093/rapstu/raag011/8914819)). The original Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu paper is peer-reviewed ([Journal of Finance](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13119)); the Grobys et al. journal result cited in the repo is a raw estimate, not US spot net ([Review of Quantitative Finance and Accounting](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)).

- STATED, peer-reviewed [Martineau, Critical Finance Review](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf): for 2016 to 2019, analyst surprises no longer predict a positive significant day +2 to +60 drift in microcaps, though day +2 to +5 survives. A surprise relative to the prior year's same-quarter earnings still predicts small positive microcap +2 to +60 drift, mostly concentrated early. Not net returns or a volume-filtered gap test.
- STATED, [Nyllinge and Oldenburg, Stockholm School of Economics master's thesis](http://arc.hhs.se/download.aspx?MediumId=6317): 220,228 announcements from 6,958 US firms, 2005 to 2024. Table 3 gives 6.381 points of +2 to +60 small-cap fitted drift across standardized surprise ranks for the 2005 to 2020 baseline, with an insignificant incremental change for 2021 to 2024. It does not establish a post-2015 long-only net return. Lack of a significant change is not a finding of no drift.
- STATED, [Noronha, 2026 SSRN working paper](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7683647): US micro- and small-cap long-only study, 2015 to 2024, using a price-reaction surprise and default QuantConnect fees. Reports positive modeled portfolio returns and alpha; the abstract does not establish trade count, exact horizon, executable fill, spread/slippage, or 2% net per event. Directionally encouraging, not a validation of the exact filter.
- STATED, peer-reviewed [Lan et al., International Review of Financial Analysis](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v95y2024ipbs1057521924003922.html): an overnight price-jump surprise measure predicts drift in China, with 6.78% quarterly market-excess return reported for the strategy. Not US evidence, and not an established net return for this rule. Peer-reviewed [Ng, Rusticus and Verdi, Journal of Accounting Research](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1475-679X.2008.00290.x) shows that trading costs can substantially reduce PEAD profits.

Verdict: WATCH only. Do not register a count stage now. The nearest after-cost modern vendor test misses the bar, and the academic evidence shows that survival depends on the surprise measure, size and horizon. The exact 20- to 60-session neglect and high-volume rule still lacks an executable fill clock, a credible post-2015 same-clock paired net mean near +2% or better, and an effective event count. A dated-release metadata census cannot tell whether those releases generated the required price and volume gaps. Revisit only with credible support for the same rule and a fixed clock, benchmark and costs; no new queue row or price pull from this review.
