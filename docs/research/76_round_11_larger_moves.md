## Bucket (a): US-Listed Small-Stock Events with a Mechanical Cause and Known Clock

### Candidate 1: Corporate Spin-Off Forced Institutional Selling (Post-Distribution Subsidiary Long)

- Source opened: [McConnell, Sibley, and Xu (2015, Journal of Portfolio Management)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf) and [McConnell, Ozbilgin, and Wahal (2001, Journal of Business)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/spinoffs-ex-ante-2001-vol74.pdf).
- Published evidence: STATED in [McConnell et al. (2015)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf): "We study the stock market performance of parent shares and spun-off subsidiaries that are members of a comprehensive set of 146 spin-offs that occurred from 2001 to 2012." STATED in [McConnell et al. (2015)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf): "Specifically, over the first 22 months following the spin-offs, an equally weighted portfolio of subsidiaries' shares earned an average raw buy-and-hold return of 1.26% per month, for a cumulative raw return of 31.6%." STATED in [McConnell et al. (2015)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf): "When measured against a size and market-to-book benchmark, the spun-off subsidiaries outperformed the benchmark by 0.72% per month, for a cumulative excess return of 17.1%." STATED caveat on parents in [McConnell et al. (2015)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/The%20Stock%20Price...2013%20jpm.2015.42.1.143.pdf): "When we remove the outlier, the parent shares' average cumulative return is just equal to that of the benchmarks." STATED caveat on ex-ante benchmark sensitivity in [McConnell, Ozbilgin, and Wahal (2001)](https://business.purdue.edu/faculty/mcconnell/publications/spinoffs-ex-ante-2001-vol74.pdf): "When compared with the matched firm benchmark used by Cusatis et al. and the Fama and French (1993) 3-factor model, the strategy does not beat the benchmark. When compared with size- and book-to-market-matched portfolios, the strategy typically beats the benchmark."
- What forces the flow: STATED: Index funds and mandate-constrained parent shareholders receive pro-rata shares of a smaller spun-off subsidiary on distribution day and are forced to sell if the spin-off does not meet parent index or market-cap rules, depressing initial trading prices before institutional re-accumulation.
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: Long-only US common stock on a $500 account using zero-commission fractional shares; typical small/mid-cap spin-off spread is 10 to 30 bps round-trip, which is small relative to a multi-week or multi-month +5% to +17% target move. No short borrow required.
- Trades a year: STATED: Roughly 10 to 25 US corporate spin-offs per year (146 in 12 years = ~12/year in McConnell et al. 2015).
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Alpaca SIP daily and minute stock bars from 2016 onward; we do NOT yet hold a parsed spin-off distribution calendar.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: Yes. SEC EDGAR Form 10 and 8-K distribution dates are public, free, and lawful.
- Verdict: KEEP WITH CHANGES. Restrict strictly to spun-off subsidiaries (drop parents), test both distribution-day entry and post-forced-selling dip entry (days +5 to +15), and verify post-2016 out-of-sample performance on Alpaca SIP before live capital.

### Candidate 2: Seasoned Equity Offerings (SEOs) Priced Overnight vs Marketed Non-Overnight SEOs

- Source opened: [Gustafson (2018, Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/price-pressure-and-overnight-seasoned-equity-offerings/DE87F9B6B394706A5371AABDB87B2227) and [Corwin (2003, Journal of Finance)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.1111/1540-6261.00604).
- Published evidence: STATED in [Gustafson (2018)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/price-pressure-and-overnight-seasoned-equity-offerings/DE87F9B6B394706A5371AABDB87B2227): "Between 2009 and 2014, 75% of seasoned equity offerings (SEOs) were announced and issued overnight, compared to 27% between 2000 and 2008." STATED in [Gustafson (2018)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/price-pressure-and-overnight-seasoned-equity-offerings/DE87F9B6B394706A5371AABDB87B2227): "Consistent with these favorable returns being due to the avoidance of pre-issue selling pressure, non-overnight issuers experience a 2.5% pre-issue stock-price decline that reverses within 7 days." STATED in [Gustafson (2018)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/price-pressure-and-overnight-seasoned-equity-offerings/DE87F9B6B394706A5371AABDB87B2227): "This post-issue reversal is increasing in SEO offer size and bigger following large pre-issue price declines. In contrast, returns following overnight offerings are less positive and unrelated to SEO offer size or pre-issue returns."
- What forces the flow: STATED: In marketed (non-overnight) SEOs, pre-pricing hedging and shorting create temporary selling pressure between filing/marketing and pricing; once the offering prices and new shares settle, short-covering and demand normalization drive a 7-day post-issue rebound. Overnight SEOs bypass the marketing window, removing that pre-issue pressure.
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: Buying marketed non-overnight SEOs on the pricing open (long-only, $500 account, zero commission) targets a ~2.5% 7-day gross rebound (larger for big offer sizes and steep pre-issue drops), minus ~20 to 50 bps small-cap spread = ~1.8% to 2.2% net per trade. Pre-pricing shorting is NOT feasible on $500 due to SEC Rule 105 and HTB borrow.
- Trades a year: STATED: Overnight deals now dominate (~75%), leaving roughly 25 to 60 marketed non-overnight US SEOs per year.
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Alpaca SIP daily and minute bars from 2016; no parsed SEC 424B5 SEO filing table yet.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: Yes. SEC EDGAR Form 424B5 prospectus supplements (filing date vs pricing date distinguishes marketed from overnight) are free and lawful.
- Verdict: KEEP WITH CHANGES. Drop overnight SEOs (the evidence rejects a size-driven bounce there) and test only marketed non-overnight SEOs with large pre-issue declines as a 7-day long-only reversal.

### Candidate 3: SPAC Redemption Deadlines (Pre-Redemption Trust Arbitrage vs Post-Merger Dilution Short)

- Source opened: [Klausner and Ohlrogge (2023, Yale Journal on Regulation Bulletin)](https://www.yalejreg.com/wp-content/uploads/Michael-Klausner-Michael-Ohlrogge-Was-the-SPAC-Crash-Predictable-40-Yale.-J.-on-Regul.-Bulletin-101-2023.pdf) and [AQR Arbitrage (October 2024)](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/Are-SPACs-Still-Alive.pdf).
- Published evidence: STATED on post-merger losses in [Klausner and Ohlrogge (2023)](https://www.yalejreg.com/wp-content/uploads/Michael-Klausner-Michael-Ohlrogge-Was-the-SPAC-Crash-Predictable-40-Yale.-J.-on-Regul.-Bulletin-101-2023.pdf): "Average post-merger returns are negative 62%; returns in excess of the Nasdaq are negative 44%; and returns in excess of the Russell 2000 are negative 51%." STATED on dilution mechanism in [Klausner and Ohlrogge (2023)](https://www.yalejreg.com/wp-content/uploads/Michael-Klausner-Michael-Ohlrogge-Was-the-SPAC-Crash-Predictable-40-Yale.-J.-on-Regul.-Bulletin-101-2023.pdf): "Public shareholders have a right to redeem their shares rather than invest in a proposed merger. To the extent they do, net cash per share drops." STATED on pre-redemption trust arbitrage in [AQR (2024)](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/Are-SPACs-Still-Alive.pdf): "The option to redeem for the trust value causes SPAC investments to have very low fundamental risk." STATED on post-2022 contraction in [AQR (2024)](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Insights/White-Papers/Are-SPACs-Still-Alive.pdf): "In contrast, only $8 billion was raised by 66 vehicles over the following seven quarters between the SEC's proposal and the end of 2023."
- What forces the flow: STATED: Before the redemption deadline, SPACs trade near or slightly below trust value (backed by T-bills); after the redemption deadline removes the $10 floor, remaining holders absorb sponsor promote, warrant overhang, and deferred fees.
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: Two fatal $500 retail blockers. First, pre-redemption trust arbitrage earns T-bills plus a modest liquidity spread (not fast money above cash), and retail brokers commonly charge $38 to $100 voluntary corporate-action redemption fees that wipe out a $500 position. Second, shorting high-redemption de-SPACs after the redemption deadline faces extreme HTB borrow fees, buy-ins, and violent low-float squeezes that a $500 retail account cannot survive.
- Trades a year: STATED: Roughly 30 to 60 US SPAC mergers/extensions per year post-2023 (far below the 2020 to 2021 boom).
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Alpaca SIP stock prices from 2016; no SPAC trust-value or redemption-rate dataset.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: SEC DEFM14A / 8-K filings are free and lawful, though parsing exact trust cash per share and redemption rates requires substantial filing extraction.
- Verdict: DROP. Pre-redemption arbitrage is too slow and killed by retail redemption fees on $500; post-merger shorting is unexecutable on a $500 US retail account due to HTB borrow and low-float squeeze risk.

### Candidate 4: At-the-Market (ATM) Equity Shelf Takedowns

- Source opened: [Billett, Floros, and Garfinkel (2019, Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis)](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/jgarfinkel/pubs/ATM.pdf).
- Published evidence: STATED in [Billett, Floros, and Garfinkel (2019)](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/jgarfinkel/pubs/ATM.pdf): "We find that 95.4% of ATM programs are announced on (or after) the closing date of the commencement of the program. The dates of the securities' sale are not announced." STATED in [Billett, Floros, and Garfinkel (2019)](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/jgarfinkel/pubs/ATM.pdf): "For the full sample of firms (in Panel A), average actual issuance is slightly less than half of the announced plan size (43% in the mean), while the median firm issues just over one quarter (27%)." STATED in [Billett, Floros, and Garfinkel (2019)](https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/jgarfinkel/pubs/ATM.pdf): "The data are consistent with firms following a mechanical rule of issuing when the stock price is rising and not doing so in the face of falling prices."
- What forces the flow: STATED: Issuers dribble new shares into secondary-market rallies opportunistically over up to 3 years, with zero pre-announced sale schedule.
- Size after US retail costs: UNKNOWN / NOT APPLICABLE because there is no observable pre-sale clock to trade around.
- Trades a year: STATED: Hundreds of ATM shelf registrations per year, but actual takedown days are undisclosed until quarterly 10-Q/10-K filings.
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Alpaca SIP stock bars from 2016; no ATM filing table.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: SEC 424B5 filings are free and lawful, but they do not contain future sale dates.
- Verdict: DROP. Fails the known-clock requirement completely: 95.4% of ATM programs are announced on or after commencement and actual share-sale dates are never announced in advance.

### Candidate 5: Corporate Rights Offerings (Non-CEF Small-Cap Stocks)

- Source opened: [Holderness and Pontiff (2016, Journal of Financial Economics)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v120y2016i2p252-268.html).
- Published evidence: STATED in [Holderness and Pontiff (2016)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v120y2016i2p252-268.html): "Shareholder participation in valuable domestic rights offerings averages only 64%, which is considerably lower than previously asserted. This causes wealth transfers from nonparticipating to participating shareholders that average 7% of the offering. Wealth transfers are larger in nontransferable and bigger offerings. The stock market reacts more negatively to larger wealth transfers."
- What forces the flow: STATED: Roughly 36% of existing holders fail to exercise valuable discounted subscription rights before the expiration clock, transferring value to holders who exercise or oversubscribe.
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: Although the gross wealth transfer averages 7% of the offering, capturing it requires exercising rights or oversubscription privileges via broker voluntary-reorganization instructions, where many US retail brokers charge $30 to $50 per corporate-action election (6% to 10% of a $500 account), wiping out the gain. Note: CEF post-rights-offering secondary-market snapbacks (no election fee required) are already queued separately as Row 55.
- Trades a year: STATED: Rare in US operating companies (typically 5 to 15 per year, heavily skewed toward distressed microcaps).
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Alpaca SIP stock prices from 2016; no corporate rights-offering terms dataset.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: SEC S-1 / 424B3 prospectuses are free and lawful, but no clean free structured database exists.
- Verdict: DROP. Too few US corporate events per year and voluntary corporate-action exercise fees destroy a $500 ticket (CEF rights snapbacks remain queued in Row 55).

### Candidate 6: ETF Closures and Liquidations (Post-Announcement Discount to NAV)

- Source opened: [Are Investors Better Off Doing Nothing During Exchange-Traded Fund Closures? (ACFR / SSRN working paper)](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/809644/Exchange-Traded-Fund-Closures-FULL-ssrn-maf2.pdf) and [Sherrill and Stark (2018, Journal of Empirical Finance)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v48y2018icp357-373.html).
- Published evidence: STATED in [ACFR / SSRN ETF Closures](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/809644/Exchange-Traded-Fund-Closures-FULL-ssrn-maf2.pdf): "Our dataset consists of a sample of 633 ETFs that closed from January 2012 to December 2019." STATED in [ACFR / SSRN ETF Closures](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/809644/Exchange-Traded-Fund-Closures-FULL-ssrn-maf2.pdf): "Examining the premium/discount even nearer to the closure announcement, e.g., ten days before the announcement, we also remark that ETF shares trade at a premium for several days, whereas following the announcement, closing ETF shares trade at a high discount." STATED in [ACFR / SSRN ETF Closures](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0004/809644/Exchange-Traded-Fund-Closures-FULL-ssrn-maf2.pdf): "Regardless of the timing of buying the closing ETF shares, average daily returns are usually negative whenever investors sell them after the closure announcement, namely, either one day or a week, but as they wait for longer, the worse the performance. Interestingly, investors typically earn positive average returns while keeping calm and waiting for the liquidation date without selling their shares after the closure announcement." Table 5 reports time-series average Premium/Discount moving from -0.016 before to -0.031 after announcement (a -0.015 widening; unit not explicitly stated in Table 5 excerpt, UNKNOWN whether percentage points or decimal).
- What forces the flow: STATED: Retail and institutional holders sell closing ETFs in the secondary market after closure notices, pushing shares below NAV, while remaining holders on liquidation day receive cash at NAV without paying the exit spread.
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: Dying ETFs (typically under $25M AUM) have wide secondary-market bid-ask spreads (often 30 to 150 bps) on entry, lock up capital for 2 to 6 weeks between last trading day and cash payout, and remain exposed to underlying market beta until the fund liquidates its holdings. On $500, any residual discount after entry spread is too small and capital-inefficient.
- Trades a year: STATED: Roughly 100 to 150 US ETF liquidations per year.
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Alpaca SIP daily/minute bars for active and delisted ETFs from 2016; no ETF NAV or liquidation-payout feed.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: Closure press releases and SEC 497 supplements are public, but historical daily ETF NAVs and final cash liquidation payouts are not available in a clean free feed.
- Verdict: DROP. Wide entry spreads on illiquid closing ETFs, unhedged market exposure during wind-down, and missing free historical NAV/liquidation payout data make it unsuitable for fast $500 compounding.

### Candidate 7: SPAC and Small-Cap Warrant Exercise / Redemption Call Windows

- Source opened: [Gahng, Ritter, and Zhang (2023, Review of Financial Studies / AEA 2022 draft)](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2022/preliminary/paper/Far3hShT).
- Published evidence: STATED in [Gahng, Ritter, and Zhang (2022/2023)](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2022/preliminary/paper/Far3hShT): "Another notable feature of the SPAC warrants is that merged companies usually have call rights for the warrants when the stock price hits a certain level, usually $18." STATED in [Gahng, Ritter, and Zhang (2022/2023)](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2022/preliminary/paper/Far3hShT): "The equally weighted average one-year warrant return is a remarkable 68.0%." STATED in [Gahng, Ritter, and Zhang (2022/2023)](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2022/preliminary/paper/Far3hShT): "Table 9 shows that both for the SPAC period and the deSPAC period returns, we find a clear monotonic relation in which SPACs with higher potential dilution substantially underperform."
- What forces the flow: STATED: When a post-merger stock trades above $18 (or triggers a 30-day redemption notice), issuers call warrants for cash or cashless exercise, forcing warrant holders to exercise or sell before expiration and creating dilution supply in the common stock.
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: Shorting the common stock into a warrant-redemption dilution window requires borrowing a high-volatility de-SPAC on $500 (high HTB borrow and squeeze risk). Buying warrants outright is a directional high-beta call-option bet, not a forced-flow clock, and the 68.0% average was dominated by the 2020 to 2021 SPAC bubble.
- Trades a year: STATED: Fewer than 10 to 15 US warrant redemption calls per year after the 2022 SPAC crash (most post-2021 de-SPACs never reached the $18 call threshold).
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Alpaca SIP common-stock bars; no warrant price or redemption-notice dataset.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: SEC 8-K warrant redemption notices are free, but historical warrant OHLCV data is not held and is sparse in free feeds.
- Verdict: DROP. Too rare post-2021, HTB-blocked on the short side for $500, and lacking free historical warrant price data.

### Candidate 8: Leveraged ETF Reverse Splits

- Source opened: [Avellaneda and Zhang (2009, NYU Courant / SIAM 2010)](https://math.nyu.edu/inmemoriam/avellaneda/LeveragedETF20090515.pdf).
- Published evidence: STATED in [Avellaneda and Zhang (2009)](https://math.nyu.edu/inmemoriam/avellaneda/LeveragedETF20090515.pdf): "We derive an exact formula for the return of an LETF as a function of its expense ratio, the applicable rate of interest, and the return and realized variance of an unleveraged ETF tracking the same index (the 'underlying ETF', for short). In particular, we show that the holder of an LETF has a negative exposure to the realized variance of the underlying ETF." STATED in [Avellaneda and Zhang (2009)](https://math.nyu.edu/inmemoriam/avellaneda/LeveragedETF20090515.pdf): "Formula (10) suggests that an investor who is long a leveraged ETF has a 'time-decay' associated with the realized variance of the underlying ETF."
- What forces the flow: STATED: Reverse splits on leveraged or inverse ETFs (such as UVXY, SQQQ, or SOXS) are purely cosmetic share-count resets triggered after prior volatility decay pushes share prices low; they do not create a new forced-flow rebalance beyond the fund's normal daily reset.
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: Shorting reverse-split leveraged/inverse ETFs on $500 incurs continuous borrow fees, margin maintenance risk, and unbounded gap-up squeeze risk (e.g., shorting UVXY or SQQQ into a market crash). No published evidence shows an abnormal return specific to the reverse-split clock on leveraged ETFs.
- Trades a year: STATED: Roughly 15 to 40 leveraged/inverse ETF reverse splits per year.
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Alpaca SIP daily and minute bars from 2016.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: Yes (exchange split notices and Alpaca SIP).
- Verdict: DROP. Reverse splits on leveraged ETFs are cosmetic resets without an event-specific forced-flow edge, and shorting them on $500 carries severe borrow and tail-squeeze risk.

Removing these exceptions leaves us with 112 listing events on Coinbase." STATED in [Li et al.](https://www.loyola.edu/_media/sellinger-business/documents/academics/departments/finance/wealth-management-conference/speakers/J_Li.pdf): "We find significant first day announcement returns of 32.8% and 22.7% for Coinbase and Binance listings, respectively." STATED in [Li et al.](https://www.loyola.edu/_media/sellinger-business/documents/academics/departments/finance/wealth-management-conference/speakers/J_Li.pdf): "For years, Coinbase has followed the practice of first announcing a short-list of cryptocurrencies it intends to research about and then deciding on whether to list or not several months later. Some of the researched coins, but not all of them, may then be listed on Coinbase later." STATED in [Li et al.](https://www.loyola.edu/_media/sellinger-business/documents/academics/departments/finance/wealth-management-conference/speakers/J_Li.pdf): "As Panel A further shows, the day 0 returns do not revert in longer periods, ranging from 5, 10, 30, and 180 days."
- What forces the flow: STATED: Addition to the Coinbase listing roadmap signals imminent US retail spot access, triggering immediate speculative buying on existing venues before Coinbase spot trading opens.
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: Structural execution trap for a $500 US retail account. Moreover, Li et al. found day-0 listing returns did NOT revert over 5 to 180 days.
- Trades a year: STATED: Roughly 25 to 50 Coinbase roadmap additions per year.
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Binance archives (offshore) and Coinbase CDE hourly candles (only 5 to 10 major coins); no Coinbase roadmap announcement archive.
- Free and lawful history: STATED: Coinbase blog/X roadmap posts are public, but exact minute-level US-venue tradability history at the moment of each roadmap post is sparse.
- Verdict: DROP. Cannot be bought fast enough on US CEXs before offshore/DEX repricing, cannot be shorted on US venues, and published evidence shows no systematic post-announcement reversal.

### Candidate 13: CME Bitcoin Futures Monthly Expiry (Last Friday, 4:00 p.m. London Time)

- Source opened: [Is there an expiration effect in the bitcoin market? (Universidad de Zaragoza / International Review of Economics and Finance, 2023)](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/125822/files/texto_completo.pdf), [Plastun, Khomutenko, and Bashlai (2022, Journal of Risk and Financial Management)](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/258815/1/jrfm-15-00092.pdf), and [CME Group Bitcoin Futures Contract Specs](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/cryptocurrencies/bitcoin/bitcoin/specs).
- Published evidence: STATED clock in [CME Group Specs](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/cryptocurrencies/bitcoin/bitcoin/specs): "Outright: Trading terminates at 4:00 p.m. London time on the last Friday of the contract month." STATED intraday result in [IREF (2023)](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/125822/files/texto_completo.pdf): "The total period covers the beginning of the futures contracts in December 2017 (we take December 31, 2017 as the initial date) until November 20, 2020." STATED in [IREF (2023)](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/125822/files/texto_completo.pdf): "When we estimate the effect using the expiration dates for the CME bitcoin futures, we appreciate a clear effect on prices at the maturity time and 5 h prior to the expiration time in all the exchanges under analysis. Returns are significantly higher than the mean return at the 1% significance level. The strongest effect is reflected in D2 and D1, that is, at the time of the expiration effect and 1 h before." Table 4 reports D2 (expiration and 1 hour prior) coefficients of 0.4637 (Coinbase) and 0.4717 (Kraken) at 1% significance (multiplied by 100 in table note), and D1 (expiration hour) of 0.2989 (Coinbase) and 0.3124 (Kraken). STATED counter-evidence on daily bars in [Plastun et al. (2022)](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/258815/1/jrfm-15-00092.pdf): "The results suggest the absence of statistically significant price effects related to expiration days in daily returns. Some minor evidence in favor of anomaly were found only in the case of weekly data: in 65% of cases, returns during such weeks were positive, and they were more than 5 times greater than the usual ones."
- What forces the flow: STATED in [IREF (2023)](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/125822/files/texto_completo.pdf): "Typically, this effect is attributed to the unwinding of short arbitrage positions. In the case of bitcoin markets, the unwinding of short arbitrage positions would be possible if the available arbitrage opportunities consisted of selling bitcoin short and buying under-priced futures and, consequently, prices rose at expiration as short arbitrageurs move to unwind their positions and provoke a buy order imbalance."
- Size after US retail costs: STATED: On Coinbase/Kraken retail taker tiers (80 to 120 bps round-trip), a +30 to +75 bps intraday window is largely eaten by fees. However, a $500 US retail account can trade spot Bitcoin ETF IBIT (zero commission, ~1 to 3 bps spread) during the Friday US morning window leading into 4:00 p.m. London time (11:00 a.m. ET).
- Trades a year: STATED: 12 monthly expirations per year.
- What data we hold: MEASURED: Binance BTC hourly/minute archives (multi-year), Alpaca SIP IBIT minute bars (since Jan 2024), and Coinbase CDE BTC hourly candles (since July 2025).
- Free and lawful history: STATED: Yes. CME expiry dates follow a deterministic calendar (last Friday of each month) and price history is already held.
- Verdict: KEEP WITH CHANGES. Testable immediately on held data with zero new collection; must test post-2021 out-of-sample (the IREF paper ended in Nov 2020) and specifically verify whether the 5-hour pre-expiry window survives on IBIT after the 2024 spot ETF launch and CME's May 2026 shift to 24/7 trading.
