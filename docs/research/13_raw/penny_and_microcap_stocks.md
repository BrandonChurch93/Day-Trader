## Bottom line

- Buying penny stocks and OTC stocks as a group has a negative expected return. This is the honest answer, and it is one of the better-measured facts in this area. Several independent datasets agree: the value-weighted OTC market portfolio returned about -0.74% a month less than Treasury bills from 1977 to 2008, before trading costs, which the authors round to -0.8% (Corrected 2026-11-25: this is an excess return on a value-weighted portfolio, not a raw index return) ([Ang, Shtauber, Tetlock, NBER w19309, Aug 2013](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19309/w19309.pdf)). In a 2014 sample of retail OTC investors, the median investor's holding-period return was -13.4% and the median investor held for 16 days, after commissions (Corrected 2026-11-25: these are investor-level medians in a sample the SEC requested, not a random sample; the median per investor and company was -11.92%) ([SEC DERA, White, 2016-12-16](https://www.sec.gov/files/White_OutcomesOTCinvesting.pdf)). MEASURED.
- The stocks most like lottery tickets (the biggest one-day jump last month) earn about 1% a month less than the calmest stocks ([Bali, Cakici, Whitelaw, NBER w14804](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w14804/w14804.pdf)). The "reason" people buy penny stocks is the very feature that is overpriced.
- Most of the documented edge in this area is on the short side: overpriced lottery stocks, pumps, de-SPACs, reverse-split names, death-spiral issuers. The no-shorting house rule does give up those paper profits. But shorting these names is costly or impossible for a small account (hard-to-borrow fees, locate fees, squeezes, and a minimum of $2.50 a share or 100% margin on shorts under $5 at Alpaca). I found no evidence that a small retail account can capture that short-side edge after costs. So the evidence does not show that the house rule costs him a realistic opportunity.
- The better-supported "moonshot with a reason" ideas are not penny stocks. They are event trades in listed stocks: opportunistic insider buying, activist 13D filings, and possibly post-bankruptcy equities. Each has published positive abnormal returns, gross of costs, in older samples. Several classic events (S&P 500 inclusion, post-earnings drift in liquid stocks) have mostly faded or are eaten by costs.

## 4. Costs and frictions specific to these names

- Spreads: average OTC spread is 0.15 against 0.08 for comparable listed stocks, 1977 to 2008. MEASURED ([Ang et al.](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w19309/w19309.pdf)). Eraker and Ready report an average OTC quoted spread of 1,316 bps, as cited by FINRA ([FINRA filing 2018](https://www.sec.gov/rules/sro/finra/2018/34-83129-ex3b.pdf)). Pink Sheets stocks in Brüggemann et al. traded zero volume about every other day, and a quarter had zero volume on 90% of days, as reported by [SEC DERA 2016](https://www.sec.gov/files/White_OutcomesOTCinvesting.pdf).
- Price bands (LULD). Tier 2 is every NMS stock not in the S&P 500 or Russell 1000. Above $3 the band is 10%. From $0.75 to $3 it is 20%. Under $0.75 it is the lesser of $0.15 or 75%. Bands double at the open and in the last 25 minutes for Tier 2 stocks under $3. MEASURED ([LULD Plan site](https://www.luldplan.com/); [LULD 2023 annual report](https://cdn.luldplan.com/reports/LULD-2023-Annual-Report.pdf); [SEC DERA LULD white paper](https://www.sec.gov/dera/staff-papers/white-papers/dera-luld-white-paper.pdf)). This means a sub-$3 small cap can move 20% before a pause, and more at the open. Stop orders do not protect you through halts.
- SEC trading suspensions last up to 10 trading days ([SEC trading suspensions page](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/trading-suspensions)). A suspended stock can come back on the OTC market or be delisted (see Nasdaq IM-5101-4 above). A holder can be locked in for 10 days and then face a collapsed price.
- Settlement: T+1 since 2024-05-28. STATED. In a cash account at Alpaca, settled-funds and good-faith rules apply. Not checked today.
- Borrow: Alpaca charges no fees on its 5,000+ easy-to-borrow names. Hard-to-borrow names need a non-refundable locate fee plus a daily fee, charged in round lots and for three days over a weekend. Shorts under $5 need the greater of $2.50 a share or 100%. MEASURED ([Alpaca margin doc](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/margin-and-short-selling)). Ang et al. say "short selling is more difficult in OTC markets". Webull does not allow OTC shorts. This is why the documented overpricing persists, and why a small account cannot easily harvest it.
- Tick size and access fees. In Sept 2024 the SEC adopted a $0.005 tick for some stocks at $1 and above. It also cut the access fee cap to $0.001 a share (0.1% of price under $1). On 2026-06-11 the SEC extended compliance relief to the first business day of November 2027. So these changes are not yet live on 2026-11-24. MEASURED ([SEC Chairman Atkins statement, 2026-06-11](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-statement-minimum-pricing-increments-access-fee-caps-061126); [Federal Register order, 2026-06-15](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/html/2026/06/15/2026-11997.html)).
- Commissions: Schwab charges $6.95 per OTC trade. On a $100 position that is 7% each way before the spread. MEASURED ([Schwab](https://www.schwab.com/legal/schwab-pricing-guide-for-individual-investors)).

## 6. Rule-based approaches: ranked by strength of evidence (small long-only account)

| Rank | Approach | Evidence for | Evidence against | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (strongest, negative) | Buying OTC or penny stocks, promoted names, top Robinhood buys, high-MAX stocks, de-SPACs, tiny-sales IPOs, reverse-split or death-spiral issuers | None for long positions | Many independent samples (1962 to 2025), real-money and backtest, all negative (section 3) | Negative expected return. Use these as exclusion filters, not buy signals. |
| 2 | Opportunistic insider purchases | 82 bps a month value-weighted five-factor alpha for a long-short portfolio (opportunistic buys minus opportunistic sells), 1989 to 2007. The buys alone were 72 bps. Routine trades: -20 bps, not different from zero. Corrected 2026-11-25: the earlier text said 1986 to 2007 and did not say long-short ([Cohen, Malloy, Pomorski, NBER w16454](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w16454/w16454.pdf)) | Gross, in-sample. Post-2007 decay not checked. Costs in small names not netted. | Best candidate to test |
| 3 | Activist 13D filings | 7% to 8% abnormal return in the (-20,+20) window. No reversal over the next year. 2001 to 2006 ([Brav, Jiang, Partnoy, Thomas, FDIC WP 2008-06](https://www.fdic.gov/analysis/cfr/working-papers/2008/2008-06.pdf)) | Price and volume start rising 1 to 10 days before the filing. A buyer after filing gets only part of the move. Gross. | Test the post-filing window only |
| 4 | Post-bankruptcy equities | "Large, positive excess returns in 200 days" after emergence, 131 firms ([Eberhart, Altman, Aggarwal, JF 1999](https://afajof.org/issue/volume-54-issue-5/)) | Small, old sample. Abstract only read. | Weak and old. Test if data exists. |
| 5 | Spinoffs | Positive abnormal returns for up to 3 years, 1965 to 1988 ([Cusatis, Miles, Woolridge, JFE 1993](https://longrunplan.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/restructuring-through-spinoffs.pdf)) | The gains are "limited to firms involved in takeover activity". Old sample. | Weak |
| 6 | Post-earnings drift in micro-caps | 2.43% a month in the most illiquid stocks | Costs take 70% to 100% (Chordia et al.). Long-short design. | Probably no net edge for long-only |
| 7 | Index inclusion | Was 7.6% in the 1990s | Down to 0.8% in 2010 to 2020 ([Greenwood and Sammon, NBER w30748](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30748/w30748.pdf)) | Largely gone |
| 8 | FDA or trial catalysts | Run-ups before positive trial news (+13.7%, wide CI) | Small samples. Pre-decision moves do not separate approvals from rejections. Single events range from -100% to +485%. | A coin flip with fat tails. No proven edge. |
| 9 | Small-cap momentum, gap-and-go, low-float day trading | None audited | Costs (Lesmond et al.). Day traders lose (Brazil 97%, Taiwan under 1% profitable). | Rule out |

On the house rules: the overpricing results (MAX, OTC, Robinhood herding, pumps, de-SPACs, death spirals) are short-side edges in the papers. The no-shorting rule does exclude them. But the same papers point to short-sale limits as the reason the mispricing survives. Alpaca's own rules ($2.50 a share minimum on sub-$5 shorts, HTB fees, locates) make these trades costly. No primary source I read shows a small retail account earning that edge net. Options are outside this report.

## 7. A "moonshot with a reason" in stocks, if not penny stocks

The better-supported bets are small, capped positions in listed stocks (above $5, exchange-listed, not OTC) on a documented event:

- Cluster or opportunistic insider buys (Cohen et al.: 72 bps a month gross for the buys alone, 82 bps for buys minus sells, 1989 to 2007. Corrected 2026-11-25).
- New activist 13D filings, bought after the filing date (Brav et al., 7% to 8% around filing, no reversal).
- Possibly post-Chapter 11 relisted equities (Eberhart et al.).

Each is a gross, in-sample, mostly pre-2008 result. None is a "10x" lottery. They are modest positive abnormal returns. A real moonshot payoff (an FDA approval, a squeeze) has no measured positive expected value for a rule-based buyer.

## What to test first in a historical replay

1. A negative-control test: buy the top daily gainers under $5 (and the Robinhood-style "top movers") and hold 5 and 20 days. Net of the real spread. Including delisted tickers. The expected result from the literature is negative. This checks the replay engine.
2. Opportunistic insider purchases (Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski classification) in listed stocks above $5, 2008 to 2026, long-only. Net of the real spread and slippage.
3. Post-filing 13D entries (day +1 and later) in listed stocks, held 1 to 12 months.
4. Exclusion filters: high MAX, recent reverse split, sub-$1, de-SPAC under 3 years old, IPO with under $10M sales, SEC-suspended or Asia-linked micro-cap IPOs. Measure how much they improve the core.

Data needed:

- A survivorship-free daily price file with delisted tickers and delisting returns. CRSP-style data, or a vendor that keeps dead tickers. This matters most here, because many of these names go to zero or move to OTC.
- Quote-level spreads (TAQ or similar) for small caps.
- SEC Form 4 and Schedule 13D filing timestamps from EDGAR.
- The SEC trading suspension list.
- Nasdaq and NYSE deficiency and delisting notices.
- Reverse split dates.
- LULD halt history.

Rule out:

- Buying OTC or Pink stocks. Alpaca mostly cannot do it anyway.
- Gap-and-go or low-float day trading.
- Promoted or chat-room tips.
- de-SPACs.
- Holding through FDA binary dates as a strategy.
- Shorting pumps (also against the house rules).
