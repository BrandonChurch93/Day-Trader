# Round 58: Thin Short-Horizon Edges (1 to 30 Days) Under Low-Cost US Execution

Date: 2026-11-26 21:45 UTC. Second checked November 26, 2026 MT.

No new rule qualifies for a scored trial or forward trading card. STATED: [Binance.US spot fees](https://www.binance.us/fees) are 0 bp maker and generally 2 bp taker, 1 bp taker on BNB/USD. Those are fees, not spread, depth or achievable fills. The question is which 1 to 30 day rules have positive gross returns and could plausibly survive 10 to 50 bp round trip but not 100 to 150 bp. Neither a long-short factor mean nor a benchmark-adjusted event return answers that question for a US cash account.

### Rank 2: Stablecoin issuance and 24-hour drift

- STATED, [Ante, Fiedler and Strehle, BRL Working Paper 11](https://www.blockchainresearchlab.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/The_Influence_of_Stablecoin_Issuances_on_Cryptocurrency_Markets_BRL_Working_Paper_No_11.pdf), June 2020, journal issue 2021: event study, not a buy order at the mint. 565 events of at least $1 million, seven stablecoins on four chains (Bitcoin/Omni, Ethereum, TRON and EOS), April 2019 to March 2020. Excluded one mistaken 5 billion USDT mint that was burned without entering circulation, a classification based on what happened later. Hour zero uses the close of the first full hour after issuance, not a fill at broadcast or confirmation.
- STATED: Table A.2 with the −150 to −30 hour baseline gives 0 to +24 hour abnormal log returns of +57.0/+66.8/+47.4/+69.4 bp (BTC/ETH/XRP/LTC, each significant in that model), versus roughly +31 to +47 bp before issuance from −12 to −1 hour and no significant abnormal moves in hours 0, 1 or 2 individually. Table 2's actual mean hourly log returns in the 0 to +24 window are **+0.47/+0.07/−0.35/−0.06 bp**, none significant. A positive abnormal return against the negative pre-event baseline is not a positive raw return that would pay fees. No trading costs are simulated. INFERENCE: subtracting 100 to 150 bp from the published CAARs gives a negative number, but subtracting 10 to 50 bp cannot establish tradability.
- STATED: [Hammerl and Beyschlag](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09429) simulate reserve management; their prior-literature discussion cites possible reverse causality, not a new test of 24-hour mint signals. [Qadi, Gong and Medda](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.26180) use January 2021 to March 2025 daily changes in estimated units outstanding, and their null average and IV results concern **Treasury yields**, not BTC/ETH returns after individual mints.
- STATED: A dated **raw on-chain record** exists and can in principle be reconstructed without a paid event feed. [Ethereum JSON-RPC](https://ethereum.org/en/developers/docs/apis/json-rpc/) describes historical receipt logs, block timestamps and safe/finalized block references; a history-retaining node or explorer is required. [Etherscan](https://docs.etherscan.io/api-reference/endpoint/getlogs) documents paginated contract logs, and [Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blockchain-analytics/docs/schema) lists Ethereum mainnet logs partitioned by block time, with a [limited free query allowance](https://cloud.google.com/bigquery/pricing). Completeness and cost of a full free backfill were not tested. [Circle's contract list](https://developers.circle.com/stablecoins/usdc-contract-addresses) identifies Ethereum USDC, and [its contract code](https://github.com/circlefin/stablecoin-evm/blob/master/contracts/v1/FiatTokenV1.sol) emits a Mint and zero-address Transfer for the same mint, not two signals. The paper says its compiled dataset is by request. No free off-the-shelf, frozen, cross-chain **circulation-issuance** feed with historical first-seen times was verified. [Tether](https://tether.io/news/tether-issuance-primer/) distinguishes treasury authorizations from customer issuance and says chain swaps are net neutral.
- INFERENCE: A defensible count would start with one known contract and event type, stable token units and one log ID (chain, transaction hash and log index). Freeze historical contract versions before the count, count after a predefined confirmation rule, never delete later-reversed events using hindsight, and log actual first-seen time forward. An archived block time is not historical receipt time, and classifying flows as fresh circulation using later treasury movements would leak. Separate authorization, actual issuance and bridging; do not treat a later burn as a filter available at entry. No data pull was done here.
- MEASURED repo overlap: No scored mint rule found on the scorecard. But [doc 43](43_crypto_events_evidence_checked.md), [Round 15](85_round_15_crypto_scheduled_events.md) and [Round 30](119_round_30_high_count_crypto_power_screen.md) already dropped the family; this is not a new clean lead merely because some raw logs are public.
- CHANGE to a metadata-only feasibility note, no trading card or test now. Reconsider only with a point-in-time event definition and updated evidence for delayed raw returns after actual costs. Do not re-propose the original launch-time trade.

### Rank 3: One-day reversal conditional on low volume

- STATED, [Bianchi, Babiak and Dickerson, Riksbank WP 413](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/rapporter/working-papers/2022/no.-413-trading-volume-and-liquidity-provision-in-cryptocurreny-markets.pdf) (2022): March 2017 to March 2022, 179,023 USD pair-days, over 300 pairs across more than 80 exchanges. Portfolio sorts use each month's 100 most liquid available pairs by the prior month's Amihud measure, with at least 365 days traded. Each UTC day, sort by lagged return, then by a standardized shock in log volume relative to the preceding 30-day volume trend. Hold to the next day. Not just crossing an absolute volume threshold.
- STATED: Table 3 low-volume long minus short +122.6 bp equal-weighted and +62.5 bp value-weighted; loser-only gross +72.2 and +29.8 bp. Table 5 reports long-short net +72.6 and +12.5 bp (latter insignificant), mid/high value-weighted net −57.0/−68.7 bp. Table 5 caption says 20 bp on the long leg and 30 on the short leg each daily rebalance; prose elsewhere says 30/40. The 50 bp difference between printed gross and net cells matches the caption; those are leg deductions, not a 25 bp a side fill simulation. Abstract rounds or differs from Table 3; use cells. No net loser-only row or isolated liquidity ranks 21 to 100.
- STATED: Han's tested subweek long-short reversal rows are negative even before fees; these are other strategies without this volume filter. [Zaremba, Bilgin, Long, Mercik and Szczygielski (2021)](https://www.wir.ue.wroc.pl/docstore/download/UEWR135bdc5e34ad49868fe8a5e77fa021c7/Mercik_Up_or_down_Short-term_reversal.pdf), January 2015 to March 2021, finds reversal mainly in smaller/less-liquid coins and continuation in the largest or most liquid groups; it does not cost or replicate the Riksbank sort. The exact daily factor differs from our hourly shock and dip rules.
- DROP. The plausible long-only leg was not costed, and none of the related studies confirms the exact daily rule on a dated US spot universe. Do not treat a conditional long-short spread as small-account net income.
