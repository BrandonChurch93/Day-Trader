# Idea Round 55: Language Model Signals That a Simple Rule Cannot Compute

Date: 2026-11-26 21:03 UTC
Scope: Published evidence for signals that require a language model to compute (and that a simple rule cannot), for holds of one day to a few weeks, in US stocks or the 20 large coins. No price data opened; no return calculations run.

## Bottom Line

Nothing in the published post-cutoff evidence clears the bar for a new build or backtest right now. Out of eight ranked leads across US stocks and the 20 large coins, zero qualify for a count stage, one earns a narrow forward-log verdict (prospective clinical-trial public dossiers, where stock returns are still UNKNOWN), two earn a watch verdict (live web-search nowcasting and news-filtered momentum), and five are dropped on lookahead risk, paid or forbidden text feeds, trading costs, or low event counts.

The owner's premise is that an agent able to read far more text than a person should have an edge. Four pieces of 2026 evidence bear directly on that premise; the three outside papers are unrefereed preprints that show pre-cutoff backtests are heavily inflated by memorization and that post-cutoff after-cost profit remains unproved (rather than proving an edge is impossible):

- Lookahead memorization drives headline LLM trading profits. In [The Only Edge Was Memorized in Training (arXiv:2611.22177, preprint, Nov 2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22177), across five models from four vendors on 598 earnings announcements and 409 bad-news drops, STATED: "Given only the ticker and the dates, a GPT-based model called the direction of 59.2% of the announcements before its training cutoff and 53.3% after it, and no agent made reliable money on the events after every cutoff." For Gemini 3.5 Flash's named V3 agent, STATED: earnings alpha fell from +32.6 bp per announcement (t = 3.71) before its cutoff to -25.5 bp (t = -1.65) after it. STATED: "Across five models from four vendors, no agent beat the simple bets." STATED: "The best earnings call in our runs was a memory." Caveat: the clean post-cutoff sample after every model's cutoff (July 2026 onward) is small (75 earnings announcements and 53 bad-news drops), which the authors note cannot rule out a small effect.
- Memorization amplification drops to zero after a model's cutoff, though a gross predictive signal can remain. In [Detecting Lookahead Bias in LLM Forecasts (arXiv:2512.23847, preprint v2, Jun 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23847), testing Llama-3.3-70B (December 2023 cutoff) on 91,357 pre-cutoff and 7,568 post-cutoff news headlines plus 106,994 pre-cutoff and 6,744 post-cutoff earnings calls, STATED: "The mean of LAP^{P(known)} collapses sharply in the year that immediately follows Llama-3.3-70B's December 2023 training-data cutoff, and the LLM x LAP interaction is statistically zero on the post-cutoff sample, exactly as the lookahead-bias hypothesis predicts." Caveat: the paper still reports positive, statistically significant post-cutoff predictive regressions for the LLM signal itself (0.436, t = 6.47 on 2024 news headlines; 0.230, t = 3.44 on capex), before any trading costs.
- Cheap language models may have lowered reading costs, though the drift-narrowing test is underpowered and pre-cost. In [Generative Artificial Intelligence and the Pricing of Complex Disclosures (Research Square rs-10584858, preprint, Sep 2026)](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10584858/v1), across January 2021 to December 2024 (1,506 announcements across 149 firms), STATED: "Within the extreme surprise deciles, high-processing-cost firms earned about 2.47% more continuation drift than low-processing-cost firms before the release; after the release the premium narrows to about 0.41%." STATED: "In high-complexity firms, the strategy earns 0.31% per announcement before the release and - 1.59% after, a difference-in-differences of 3.80% relative to low-complexity firms, with a clustered t-statistic of -1.51." Caveat: both comparisons come from only N = 98 extreme-surprise announcements (N = 17 pre and N = 21 post for high-complexity), are gross before costs, and are not statistically significant (t = -0.47 on the drift narrowing; t = -1.51 on the strategy); the authors describe the strategy evidence as directional rather than decisive.
- Our own measured ledger already points the same way. MEASURED in `docs/research/95_where_we_stand_after_32_tests.md`, our nine AI-discretionary and text tests produced zero counted passes, and Plan 11's already-built held-back score on 2,146 earnings releases is still waiting to run.

## Ranked Lead Reviews

### Rank 1: Live Agentic Web-Search Daily Nowcasting on Russell 1000

- Verdict: WATCH.
- What is new vs repo: Unlike Replay 11 (single 8-K release) or TM04 (8 fixed stocks), an agent autonomously searches the live web overnight for Russell 1000 stocks and ranks a daily Top-20 portfolio entered at the opening auction and exited at the next open.
- Source and text feed: [Agentic AI Nowcasting Predicts Stock Returns (arXiv:2601.11958v1)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11958v1). STATED: "Third, our framework is 100% agentic: we do not feed the model news, disclosures, or curated text; it autonomously searches the web, filters sources, and synthesises information into quantitative predictions." Live web search via API is cheap per query, though running 1,000 stocks nightly adds nontrivial API cost.
- Post-cutoff status: STATED post-cutoff by construction (April 2025 through January 2026, 9 months). STATED: "First, the nowcasting framework is completely out-of-sample and free of look-ahead bias by construction: predictions are collected at the current edge of time, ensuring the AI has no knowledge of future outcomes."
- Returns, costs, and event count: STATED in the abstract and main discussion: Top-20 value-weighted long-only daily Fama-French six-factor alpha is 18.4 bp per day gross before transaction costs (t = 2.46, annualized Sharpe 2.43, cumulative ~50% vs ~26% for Russell 1000 over 9 months), using an unnamed leading US AI model, with opening-auction spreads averaging 1.63 bp (authors do not report a net-of-cost Top-20 alpha table). However, the paper contains material internal discrepancies (Table 4 and Table 5 report daily Top-20 FF6 alpha of 33.4 bp per day with t = 2.96, and Table 5 reports Sharpe 3.23 vs 2.43 in the prose). Against our MEASURED doc 136 single-stock opening median half-spread of 3.787 bp (7.57 bp round-trip if opening-to-opening), a genuine 18.4 bp per day gross alpha would survive costs, with ~252 daily rebalances per year.
- Why it is WATCH and not count stage: First, it cannot be backtested historically (the authors state the temporal design is irreproducible once the information environment passes). Second, 9 months in a single bull market with an unnamed model and internal table inconsistencies is not enough to fund a new live 1,000-stock nightly pipeline while TM09 is staged for its 5-week price-only noise stage.
- Cheapest honest next step: Wait for TM09's 5-week noise stage to finish; if TM09 justifies a live news-reading desk, log a small timestamped open-to-open top-ranked paper slate alongside it rather than building a separate 1,000-stock nightly web pipeline.

### Rank 2: Prospective Clinical-Trial Public-Dossier Forecasting (TrialOutlook)

- Verdict: FORWARD LOG.
- What is new vs repo: This is the one lead in Round 55 where a language model clearly does multi-document technical synthesis that a simple rule cannot do (reading ClinicalTrials.gov protocols, PubMed biology, Open Targets, openFDA, SEC EDGAR, and company press releases together) and where the test is genuinely prospective and pre-registered. It is distinct from Round 32's skipped FDA PDUFA calendar run-up (which is a simple calendar-date rule) and from generic healthcare news polarity (Rank 7).
- Source and text feed: [Prospective prediction of clinical trial outcomes from public evidence (medRxiv 2026.11.17.26368242v1, preprint, Nov 2026)](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.17.26368242v1). STATED: "We introduce TrialDossier, which assembles standardized evidence dossiers, and TrialOutlook, which uses these dossiers with GPT-5 to estimate the probability that a trial meets its prespecified primary endpoint." STATED: "The study used ONLY openly available human data that were originally located at: ClinicalTrials.gov (https://clinicaltrials.gov), PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov), Open Targets (https://www.opentargets.org), openFDA (https://open.fda.gov), SEC EDGAR (https://www.sec.gov/edgar) and public company press releases." Caveat: STATED: "A subscription database (BioMedTracker, Citeline) was used only to identify trials and their expected readout dates and contains no participant-level data."
- Post-cutoff status: STATED prospective and pre-registered. STATED: "Among 694 ongoing trials with results expected within six months of cohort assembly, 69 outcomes first disclosed after forecast recording had been adjudicated by 20 October 2026." STATED: "TrialOutlook achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.819 (95% confidence interval, 0.708–0.914) and an average precision of 0.860. TrialOutlook correctly identified 27 of 29 failures (93.1%) and incorrectly classified 17 of 40 successes as failures."
- Returns, costs, and event count: Stock returns and trading costs are UNKNOWN because the paper tests clinical endpoint prediction, not equity returns or options-implied odds. The authors also recorded forecasts for a large US-listed universe. STATED: "We further recorded forecasts for 5,809 ongoing trials associated with US-listed companies and established a public benchmark linking archived evidence and fixed forecasts to adjudicated outcomes as they become available." Biotech readout stocks are often small caps where doc 136's opening half-spreads and our 20 bp SmallCap 600 assumption (plus binary gap risk and short-borrow limits on failure calls) can eat thin edges, and a market-implied probability may already price what public dossiers say.
- Why it is FORWARD LOG: The predictive accuracy on newly disclosed outcomes is real and post-cutoff, the underlying documents are free, and a public benchmark of 5,809 US-listed trials already exists. Because stock returns vs market expectations are UNKNOWN, a zero-capital forward log is the only honest next step.
- Cheapest honest next step: Check whether the authors' public benchmark table of 5,809 US-listed trials is downloadable and whether free ClinicalTrials.gov primary completion dates can replace BioMedTracker; if yes, log timestamped pre-readout forecasts and record post-readout returns on paper before spending any build budget.
