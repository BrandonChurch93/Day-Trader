By contrast, the SEC publishes free, public-domain, annual point-in-time CSV and XML rosters of every active closed-end fund from 2012 through 2026 on its [Closed-End Fund Information](https://www.sec.gov/data-research/sec-markets-data/closed-end-fund-information) page: "This report provides basic identification information for all entities with an 'active' filing status that are organized as closed-end investment companies (closed-end funds or CEFs)."
- Provable event count: MEASURED on 2026-11-26 from [closed-end-investment-company-2026.csv](https://www.sec.gov/files/investment/data/other/closed-end-fund-information/closed-end-investment-company-2026.csv): 973 active CEF registrants total, of which 130 carry municipal or tax-free names (for example MFS Municipal Income Trust, Nuveen Municipal Value Fund, Nuveen AMT-Free Quality Municipal Income Fund). Annual point-in-time files exist for 2016 through 2026 (CSV for 2016 to 2020 and 2022 to 2026; XML for 2021). Critical sample-size caveat: 2016 to 2026 contains only 10 independent December-to-January calendar turns (~800 to 1,200 correlated fund-years), so any historical test must treat each December as one cluster and pair the 10-turn check with a cross-sectional worst-YTD-decile versus best-YTD-decile test and a December 2026 live forward watch.
- Published evidence for direction: STATED from [Starks, Yong, and Zheng (2006, Journal of Finance)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2006.01011.x): "Examining turn-of-the-year return and volume patterns for municipal bond closed-end funds, which are held mostly by tax-sensitive individual investors, we document a January effect for these funds, but not for their underlying assets." As summarized in [First Trust commentary on Starks et al. (2006)](https://www.ftportfolios.com/Commentary/Insights/2006/11/20/Week_of_November_20th): "They researched 168 tax-free funds from 1990 to 2000 and found they averaged a 2.21% return in January, vs. an average gain of 0.19% per month for the remaining 11 months." Crucially, [Carrion (2024, Journal of Financial Research)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jfir.12384) extended the sample by 19 additional years and confirmed the effect has not decayed: "The January effect in municipal bond CEFs has become stronger in recent years and is consistent with the tax-loss hypothesis. The January effect in municipal bond ETFs is smaller and cannot be explained by tax-loss selling."
- Cost hurdle for a $500 US account: STATED and UNKNOWN. Most NYSE-listed municipal bond CEFs trade between $7 and $16 per share, allowing a $500 commission-free Alpaca account to hold 30 to 70 whole shares without fractional-share restrictions. Exact bid-ask spreads across the bottom-decile YTD muni CEFs in December are UNKNOWN until measured on Alpaca SIP quotes in the count-first step; typical liquid muni CEF spreads of 5 to 20 bps per side (10 to 40 bps round trip) sit well below the ~200 bps published January excess return.
- Bear-market survival: STATED. Counter-cyclical and bear-market-friendly. Tax-loss selling pressure is strongest in bond or equity bear markets (such as rising-rate years) when retail investors hold large unrealized capital losses to harvest before December 31; in strong bull years with few losers, the worst-YTD-decile filter naturally shrinks or stands aside.

Buying non-migrating index deletions at the forced-selling reconstitution close (for the overnight or 20-day post-reconstitution bounce) has not been queued.
- Provable event count: STATED from [Hendrix, Liu, and Roberts (July 2024, Dimensional Research)](https://www.dimensional.com/chmedia/383623/source/measuring-the-costs-of-index-reconstitution-a-10-year-perspective.pdf): "We measure the costs of index reconstitution from 2014 to 2023 for 10 US indices. In our analysis, we restrict adds and deletes to nonmigrating securities, i.e., stocks that are added to (or deleted from) an index and are not also deleted from (or added to) another index from the same index family on the same reconstitution date." Their 10-year table reports 56 non-migrating S&P 500, 223 S&P 400, and 424 S&P 600 events (703 S&P 1500 events, alongside 2,517 total deletions across the 10 indices). For the Nasdaq-100, [Nasdaq's 2025 Reconstitution Highlights](https://beta.indexes.nasdaq.com/docs/202601%20NDX%20Reconstitution%20and%20Performance%20Highlights.pdf) states: "Over the past decade, December has typically seen about six additions and deletions" and "Over the past 10 years, the index has averaged roughly three off-cycle inclusions and deletions annually" (~90 Nasdaq-100 deletions per decade). Lawful free historical coverage of exact S&P 400/600 and Nasdaq-100 press-release dates must be audited count-first before reserving a ledger row.
- Published evidence for direction (with critical counter-evidence): STATED from [Alpha Architect's summary of Hendrix, Liu, and Roberts (2024)](https://alphaarchitect.com/cost-of-index-rebalancing/): "On average, the price for deletions fell relative to nonrebalanced stocks by 30 bps from 4 pm to market close on reconstitution day, just before they were 'sold' from the index, with a reversal of a relative 63 bps by market open the following day." Over the 20 trading days after reconstitution, [Hendrix, Liu, and Roberts (2024)](https://www.dimensional.com/chmedia/383623/source/measuring-the-costs-of-index-reconstitution-a-10-year-perspective.pdf) report: "The pattern unwinds in the next month, with the excess return being -5.7% on average in the 20 trading days following reconstitution" (for additions and sign-flipped deletions combined). Counter-evidence that must be respected: [Greenwood and Sammon (2025, Journal of Finance)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13410) found that S&P 500 deletion announcement-to-effective abnormal returns fell to "+0.1% between 2010 and 2020" and pooled S&P 400/600/Nasdaq-100 deletion returns shrank by 10.3 percentage points from the 2000s to the 2010s. Moreover, [Arnott and Wu (CFA Institute Research Foundation update)](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/sites/default/files/docs/research-reports/rf_arnott-wu_active-indexing_online.pdf) report that while S&P 500 discretionary deletions beat the market by +20.43% over the next year in 1989 to 2021, "the results from 2021-2024 show that although discretionary deletions underperformed by 43.4% (-41.3% + -2.1%) relative to the market ahead of deletion, they continued to lag the market after their deletion by another 11.9% on average."
- Cost hurdle for a $500 US account: STATED and UNKNOWN. Deletions are beaten-down stocks, so almost all S&P 400/600 and most S&P 500/Nasdaq-100 deletions trade well below $500 per share (whole-share eligible for closing-cross or 15:59 ET execution). Against a 63 bps overnight reversal, round-trip spread plus closing/opening auction slippage of 10 to 30 bps in mid/small-cap deletions leaves thin net margin and requires direct Alpaca SIP quote measurement before any test.
- Bear-market survival: WEAK / MIXED. Discretionary deletions are distressed or shrinking companies that carry high drawdown and delisting risk during bear markets; the 2021 to 2024 underperformance (-11.9%) shows longer holding windows fail when macro or fundamental deterioration persists. Only the short mechanical close-to-open or short post-reconstitution bounce is worth auditing.
