## Ranked leads

### Rank 1: Fallen-angel corporate bond month-end in ANGL or FALN

- STATED: the [FTSE Russell report](https://www.lseg.com/content/dam/ftse-russell/en_us/documents/research/fallen-angels-us-credit-market.pdf) describes monthly rebalancing of **its own** index, not the benchmark of either fund. ANGL's [SEC prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1137360/000113736024000585/vefallenangelhighyieldbond.htm) identifies the ICE 10%-constrained index, with a different predecessor until February 2020. The [ICE rules](https://www.vaneck.com/globalassets/home/ucits/etf/income/usfa/h0cf.pdf) specify last calendar day using information through the third business day before the last business day. [FALN](https://www.ishares.com/us/products/283855/) uses a different index; [Bloomberg's rules](https://assets.bwbx.io/documents/users/iqjWHBFdfxIU/r46ltFMm9mpo/v0) put changes into the returns universe on the last business day. A downgrade after a cutoff can miss the current month's change.
- STATED: [DNB Asset Management](https://dnbam.com/en/news-and-insights/fallen-angels-the-last-free-lunch) says IG index deletions normally occur at the next monthly rebalance, while mandate and tracking pressures can cause sales within a short period. The two clocks are distinct: the downgrade can trigger action or be anticipated, and index trackers adjust around the deletion. Neither says that all managers sell together at the close. ANGL's prospectus says it rebalances to reflect index changes, not that it buys every downgrade at a specific print. Exact trade dates for either ETF are UNKNOWN. [Morningstar's case](https://www.morningstar.com/etfs/what-fallen-angels-have-offer) describes a deferred 2020 rebalance where the indexes did not add Ford until May, after much of the bond recovery.
- STATED: [VanEck's June 2026 material](https://www.vaneck.com/angl-value-proposition.pdf) reports fallen-angel bonds beating broad US high yield in 15 of 22 full calendar years. That is a benchmark comparison, not the fund's short-window after-cost mean. The [CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2015/01/the-impact-of-fallen-angels-on-investment-grade-corporate-bonds-portfolios-evidence-from-the-eu) reports a post-rebalance bounce in 85 liquid European bonds from 48 issuers, 2001 to 2009, with no ETF-level net test. The FTSE paper and Round 32's bond study are further evidence of bond-level downgrade and recovery, not after-cost fund shares around month-end. A US 3- to 10-session same-clock ANGL/FALN net edge against HYG is UNKNOWN; no matching post-2015 result was found.
- Price-free count check: three normal public VanEck pages opened. Their [2024 review](https://www.vaneck.com/corp/en/news-and-insights/blogs/income-investing/elevated-yields-spark-fallen-angels-appeal/) lists five entrants in **four months**, all four with at least one entrant at 1% or more of index market value. The [2025 review](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/blogs/income-investing/fallen-angels-deliver-again-with-an-eye-towards-2026/) lists ten entrants across **six months**, of which **five** met the same weight threshold. That threshold is an illustration, not a formal rule or measured rebalance demand. Its table lists ten entrants, matching the annual total, but does not separately certify each zero month. The [first half of 2026 update](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/blogs/income-investing/fallen-angels-reclaim-the-ytd-lead-as-2026-new-entrants-deliver/) identifies additions in **two of six months**, January and March, both with a name above 1%, and expressly says none in Q2. These are counts of reported index cohorts, not purchases by either fund. Twelve scheduled windows are not twelve forced-buy months.

The public ICE file gives rules, not the archive; Bloomberg describes constituent files for subscribers. Selected VanEck retrospectives make a partial zero-price count possible, not an order-time full census. No issuer prices or weights inferred from market moves were used.

### Rank 8: IPO lockup expiration

- STATED: the [2019 MIT-hosted draft](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Short-sales%20Constraints%20and%20Aftermarket%20IPO%20Pricing.pdf) studies 709 US IPOs from 2007 to 2015.
