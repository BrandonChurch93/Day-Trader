# 205h. Deep dive: agents selling work or a service

Second check, 2026-11-27: 5 claims corrected and 1 not supported. The corrections are listed in [doc 205](205_deep_dive_beyond_trading.md#corrections-from-the-second-check) and stand over this page.

What this is: a deep-dive lane (dated 2026-11-27 UTC) on legal ways one person in Nampa, Idaho, with software, design, 3D, and film skills plus an around-the-clock agent team, can earn money by selling work, services, tools, or data products rather than trading.

Labels used here: MEASURED (read directly on an opened primary filing, statute, agency rule, platform policy, peer-reviewed paper, or empirical audit), STATED (vendor claim or unverified report), INFERENCE (reasoning from those facts), UNKNOWN (no verified figure published).

## Bottom line

The two paths most likely to produce real dollars within weeks for the owner are (1) direct productized technical, design, 3D, or video work sold off-platform to businesses that care about the delivered artifact, and (2) narrowly targeted, human-submitted freelance contracts on Upwork in software, data cleaning, or visual production where his real skills differentiate him from raw AI output. Local small-business web, listings, and review-response work in Nampa and the Boise metro is a third viable option if sold face to face or by compliant email without illegal cold AI calling.

The clear traps are (1) custom assistant stores (OpenAI GPT Store has no verified public payout program, and Poe publishes no payout medians), (2) automated bidding bots on freelance sites (explicitly banned by Upwork and grounds for permanent account closure), (3) raw AI-generated 3D asset packs (CGTrader's 2026 data shows AI uploads make up one in six listings but only $1 of every $90 in revenue, and TurboSquid bans them), and (4) cold AI voice calling or fake-review services (both carry steep federal statutory penalties under the TCPA and FTC 16 CFR Part 465).

## Employment question to check first

Before selling any side work, software, 3D assets, or agent-built services, the owner needs to check his own employment agreement and company policies: whether outside consulting or side products are permitted, whether any work done with an employer's paid AI tools, hardware, or accounts triggers employer IP ownership, and whether any client category creates a conflict of interest. This page flags that question for him and does not answer it.

## Candidate 1: Productized technical, design, 3D, and film services (direct B2B)

What it is and who pays: Fixed-scope deliverables (landing pages, web apps, data cleaning pipelines, research briefs, product demos, 3D product renders, or short video edits) sold directly to startups, agencies, or small companies at a flat price or monthly retainer. Businesses pay to save staff hours or buy skills (engineering plus design, 3D, and film) they do not have in-house.

Evidence people really earn from it: Independent macro evidence shows skilled solo service providers earn real income, though almost all public case studies of $10,000+ monthly productized agencies are self-reported STATED claims rather than audited accounts. An Upwork-funded survey of 3,000 skilled US workers reported a median annual income of $85,000 for full-time exclusive freelancers versus $80,000 for full-time employees, though that sample excluded low earners before taking the median (MEASURED from [Does It Pay freelancing research](https://doesitpay.org/freelancing/research/)). Across all US adults doing any side work, a 2025 Bankrate survey of 2,616 adults found a median of $200 per month and an average of $885 per month (MEASURED from [Does It Pay freelancing research](https://doesitpay.org/freelancing/research/)).

Base rate: UNKNOWN for direct productized services specifically because no central registry tracks solo direct-billing shops; the Bankrate $200/month median across all US side hustles shows that most part-time attempts stay small (MEASURED from [Does It Pay freelancing research](https://doesitpay.org/freelancing/research/)).

Typical dollars per month at small scale and first three months: Verified solo-service medians are UNKNOWN for the first 90 days; landing one to two fixed-scope builds ($500 to $2,500 each) yields roughly $500 to $3,000 per month once a buyer pipeline exists, while months 1 to 3 commonly earn $0 to $1,500 while finding the first buyer (INFERENCE).

Money and time needed before first dollar: Under $100 (domain, basic portfolio page, Stripe or invoice link). First customer is usually found through direct outreach to existing professional contacts, targeted B2B email to businesses with visible problems (broken sites, weak product visuals), or showing a finished sample built in advance; realistic time to first dollar is 14 to 45 days (INFERENCE).

Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus taxes: Legal as a sole proprietorship or LLC in Idaho. Under Idaho State Tax Commission rules, ordinary consulting, design, and custom computer software are not subject to Idaho's 6% sales tax, while digital videos, games, music, or books sold with a permanent right to use are taxable tangible personal property (MEASURED from [Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/retailers/collecting/)). Cold commercial email to businesses has no B2B exemption under the federal CAN-SPAM Act and must include accurate headers, honest subject lines, ad identification, a physical postal address, and a working opt-out honored within 10 business days, with civil penalties up to $53,088 per violating email (MEASURED from [FTC CAN-SPAM Compliance Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)).

Platform rules on AI and automation: Selling directly avoids third-party marketplace take rates and bot bans, though client contracts and NDAs govern whether AI tools may be used on client data (INFERENCE).

What an agent adds and what needs the owner: Agents can research prospect websites overnight, build tailored prototype demos, draft code, clean datasets, and assemble research briefs in hours instead of days. His software, design, 3D, and film skills let him ship polished, hybrid human-reviewed deliverables that pure prompt operators cannot match. The owner must still handle client calls, contracts, quality sign-off, billing, and accountability.

What kills it: Cold outreach gets low reply rates; buyers ignore generic AI pitches; scope creep eats margins if deliverables are not tightly boxed; client acquisition stops whenever delivery gets busy.

Cheapest honest test: Pick one narrow offer combining his skills (for example, a 48-hour interactive product landing page with a custom 3D or motion hero), build two public sample demos in 3 days, and send 30 personalized, CAN-SPAM-compliant emails or direct messages to real prospects over 10 days. Measure replies, booked calls, and paid deposits; spend under $50.

## Candidate 2: Freelance marketplaces (Upwork and Fiverr) in result-judged categories

What it is and who pays: Bidding on or listing fixed-price jobs (code fixes, data cleaning, document processing, research reports, translation, transcription, video editing, design, and custom 3D) on Upwork or Fiverr. Small businesses and teams pay for quick, one-off execution without hiring an employee.

Evidence people really earn from it: Public SEC filings confirm real spending volume, alongside shrinking buyer counts and rising platform cuts. Fiverr's Q3 2026 Form 6-K (period ended September 30, 2026) reported $55.411 million in quarterly marketplace revenue, 2.447 million annual active buyers (down 25.8% year over year), $373 annual spend per buyer, and a 28.2% marketplace take rate (MEASURED from [Fiverr Q3 2026 Form 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1762301/000117891326004897/exhibit_99-1.htm)). A peer-reviewed NDSS 2026 study measuring Fiverr sellers with at least one sale found heavily skewed earnings with Gini coefficients of 0.71 to 0.76: Logo Design median $185 (mean $593), AI Artists median $120 (mean $452), AI Avatar median $80 (mean $387), and AI Image Editing median $65 (mean $312), with median AI gig prices of $10 to $25 (MEASURED from [NDSS 2026 Fiverr study](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/usec26-68.pdf)). An older study of 21,767 Fiverr sellers found over 40% earned $5 to $100 over nine months while 0.4% exceeded $50,000, and an academic study by Hui et al. found freelancers in AI-exposed categories saw a 2% drop in contracts and a 5% drop in earnings (MEASURED from [Does It Pay freelancing research](https://doesitpay.org/freelancing/research/)).

Base rate: Neither Upwork nor Fiverr publishes seller-wide medians or the share of accounts earning anything; a 2021 count cited by Does It Pay found only about 12 in 100 profiles won a job, and NDSS 2026 confirms that even among Fiverr sellers with at least one sale, median total category revenue sits between $65 and $185 (MEASURED from [Does It Pay freelancing research](https://doesitpay.org/freelancing/research/) and [NDSS 2026 Fiverr study](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/usec26-68.pdf)).

Typical dollars per month at small scale and first three months: For a new profile with zero reviews, realistic first-three-month earnings are $0 to $800 per month while building initial reviews, rising to $500 to $2,500 per month only if the seller breaks into $500+ technical or visual contracts rather than $25 gigs (INFERENCE).

Money and time needed before first dollar: $20 to $100 for Upwork Connects (bidding credits) plus 10 to 30 days of manual proposals before landing a first reviewed job (INFERENCE).

Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus taxes: Legal for a US resident; platforms issue Form 1099-K or 1099-NEC as required by IRS thresholds, and Idaho does not levy sales tax on pure software or consulting services (MEASURED from [Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/retailers/collecting/)).

Platform rules on AI and automation: Automated bidding bots are OUT. Upwork defines any script, browser extension, or third-party tool that sends requests, collects data, or acts faster than a human as a prohibited bot, punishable by temporary restriction or permanent block, and even approved API keys forbid proposal spamming or data extraction (MEASURED from [Upwork automation policy](https://support.upwork.com/hc/en-us/articles/43342677368467-Use-bots-and-other-automation-properly)). Upwork's API and MCP Terms of Use (Section 5.2) ban headless browsing, UI automation, multi-account fan-out, and using Upwork data for AI training (Section 5.3), and Section 9.7 requires upfront AI disclosure whenever an agent generates content a user would reasonably believe is human-authored (MEASURED from [Upwork API and MCP Terms of Use](https://upwork.pactsafe.io/versions/6a7de807acdc47dbc84c4892.pdf)). Fiverr allows AI tools as assistance across all categories but explicitly prohibits delivering generic, unmodified, or reused AI output, requires honoring client requests for non-AI work, and warns that deception or raw AI delivery triggers order cancellation, full refund, and permanent account suspension (MEASURED from [Fiverr AI guidelines](https://help.fiverr.com/hc/en-us/articles/37554976380177-Using-AI-on-Fiverr-Guidelines-for-freelancers-and-clients)).

What an agent adds and what needs the owner: Agents can legally do back-office execution after the owner wins the contract (writing first-pass code fixes, cleaning CSVs, transcribing, drafting reports, rendering 3D variations) and help draft custom proposals offline. The owner must personally browse jobs, decide where to bid, click submit, verify identity, review all output, and communicate honestly with clients.

What kills it: Shrinking buyer pool on Fiverr (-25.8% active buyers YoY), 18% to 28% platform take rates, global price competition driving simple writing/translation/image tasks toward $10 to $25, and instant permanent bans if any auto-bidder or UI bot touches the account.

Cheapest honest test: Spend $30 on Upwork Connects over 14 days submitting 15 hand-reviewed proposals strictly in technical/visual niches (bug fixes, web dashboards, 3D/motion assets) where the buyer evaluates the final artifact. Measure view rate, reply rate, and won dollars.

## Candidate 3: Local and small-business digital operations (Nampa, Boise metro, and US SMBs)

What it is and who pays: Running practical digital upkeep for local trades, clinics, restaurants, and small firms (website fixes, Google Business Profile updates, drafting polite responses to real customer reviews, organizing bookkeeping receipts, or building lawful B2B prospect lists from public business registries). Local owners pay monthly retainers ($200 to $1,000) because they lack time and technical staff.

Evidence people really earn from it: Local digital agencies and freelance bookkeepers/web maintainers are a massive, established US market, though verified solo-operator medians for AI-assisted local agencies are UNKNOWN; most published $10k/month "AI agency" stories are STATED claims by course sellers.

Base rate: UNKNOWN for solo AI-assisted local service operators; the Bankrate 2025 median of $200/month across all US side hustles remains the closest broad benchmark (MEASURED from [Does It Pay freelancing research](https://doesitpay.org/freelancing/research/)).

Typical dollars per month at small scale and first three months: UNKNOWN from verified registries; practically, signing 2 to 5 local businesses at $300 to $600/month equals $600 to $3,000/month, with $0 to $1,000/month realistic in months 1 to 3 (INFERENCE).

Money and time needed before first dollar: Under $100 (basic site, printed one-pager or email domain). First customer is found by walking into local Nampa/Caldwell/Boise businesses with a concrete fix already mocked up, referrals, or compliant B2B email; realistic time to first dollar is 14 to 30 days (INFERENCE).

Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus taxes: Legal if marketing and review handling obey strict federal and Idaho statutes. Fake reviews and AI-generated fake testimonials are illegal: FTC 16 CFR Part 465 (effective October 21, 2024) prohibits writing, creating, selling, or buying consumer reviews that misrepresent that the reviewer exists or used the business, bars buying sentiment-conditioned reviews, and bars undisclosed insider reviews, while allowing generalized review solicitations to real buyers (MEASURED from [16 CFR Part 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465)). Outbound AI voice calling without consent is OUT: on February 8, 2024, the FCC confirmed that AI-generated voices are "artificial or prerecorded voice" under the TCPA, requiring prior express consent before calling (MEASURED from [FCC Declaratory Ruling 24-17](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-confirms-tcpa-applies-ai-technologies-generate-human-voices)). In Idaho, the Idaho Telephone Solicitation Act (Idaho Code 48-1004) requires covered telephone solicitors to register with the Idaho Attorney General at least 10 days before conducting business and send a written 3-business-day cancellation notice unless exempt (MEASURED from [Idaho Code 48-1004](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title48/t48ch10/sect48-1004/)). Commercial emails must follow CAN-SPAM ($53,088 max penalty per email, no B2B exemption) (MEASURED from [FTC CAN-SPAM Compliance Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)). Bookkeeping help must avoid unauthorized CPA attestation or tax-filing claims. Ordinary web, listings, and bookkeeping services are not subject to Idaho sales tax (MEASURED from [Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/retailers/collecting/)).

Platform rules on AI and automation: Drafting polite replies for a business owner to approve and post on Google Business Profile is lawful; generating fake reviews or operating fake customer accounts violates both FTC 16 CFR Part 465 and Google policies and is OUT.

What an agent adds and what needs the owner: Agents can monitor local business listings daily, draft review responses for owner approval, audit broken mobile pages, reconcile exported receipts, and prepare monthly status reports around the clock. The owner must sell in person or by phone/email, hold client trust, verify accuracy, and avoid automated cold calling.

What kills it: Local small businesses have high churn, small budgets, and strong distrust of cold marketers; one compliance slip (TCPA or fake reviews) creates severe legal exposure.

Cheapest honest test: Pick 15 Nampa or Boise-area local businesses with visibly broken websites or unanswered reviews, build a free 1-page mock fix or 30-day review-reply draft pack, and pitch them in person or by CAN-SPAM-compliant email over 14 days. Measure paid retainer conversions; spend under $25.

## Candidate 4: Selling small paid developer tools, APIs, and public-data products (Apify Store and direct micro-APIs)

What it is and who pays: Publishing small automated tools ("Actors"), public-data feeds, monitoring alerts, or paid micro-APIs where developers, analysts, or other AI agents pay per run or per month. Buyers pay to avoid building and maintaining brittle data pipelines themselves.

Evidence people really earn from it: Apify Store is the clearest operating pay-per-run marketplace for agent-callable tools. Apify states it paid out $1.6 million to community developers in October 2026 (up from $563,000 in September 2025), though those totals are unaudited STATED vendor figures with no published median (MEASURED from [Does It Pay agent-store audit](https://www.doesitpay.org/selling-ai-agents-and-gpts/)). Apify's official documentation confirms creators receive 80% of Actor revenue minus platform usage costs (Apify keeps 20%), and AI agents can even pay per event via prepaid tokens (MEASURED from [Apify Monetization Docs](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/publishing/monetize)).

Base rate: Strongly top-heavy. An independent audit downloading 63,617 of roughly 83,000 Apify listings found the median paid listing had 1 user in 30 days (about 92% had 10 or fewer users), the median developer had 5 users in 30 days, and the top 10% of developers captured roughly 95% of counted users; only about 6% of developers (roughly 200 accounts) had enough users (~450 in 30 days) to suggest $1,000/month under an untested user-to-payout assumption (MEASURED from [Does It Pay agent-store audit](https://www.doesitpay.org/selling-ai-agents-and-gpts/)). Developers with more than 40 paid tools had a median of 227 users in 30 days versus 1 user for single-tool developers (MEASURED from [Does It Pay agent-store audit](https://www.doesitpay.org/selling-ai-agents-and-gpts/)).

Typical dollars per month at small scale and first three months: For one to three new tools, median realistic revenue is near $0 to $50 per month in the first 3 months, with $1,000+/month largely limited to developers maintaining large catalogs or hitting a high-demand niche (MEASURED/INFERENCE from [Does It Pay agent-store audit](https://www.doesitpay.org/selling-ai-agents-and-gpts/)).

Money and time needed before first dollar: $0 to $50 in cloud/API hosting; 1 to 2 weeks to build and publish a clean tool, but weeks to months to gain organic store traffic (INFERENCE).

Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus taxes: Reselling data has sharp legal limits. In `hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn`, the Ninth Circuit held that accessing publicly available web pages without bypassing a login likely does not violate the federal CFAA, while explicitly warning that breach of contract (Terms of Service), copyright infringement, trespass to chattels, misappropriation, and privacy claims remain available (MEASURED from [Ninth Circuit opinion in hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2022/04/18/17-16783.pdf)). Therefore, safe public-data products must stick to genuinely open government filings (SEC EDGAR, federal/state public records, FDA/FAA/procurement feeds) or sources whose terms allow commercial reuse, and must avoid gated/logged-in sites, copyrighted text/images, and personal contact data regulated by state privacy laws. Under Idaho rules, remotely accessed software (SaaS/APIs) and electronically delivered software are not subject to Idaho sales tax (MEASURED from [Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/retailers/collecting/)).

Platform rules on AI and automation: Apify explicitly supports automated Actors and agent-paid runs under pay-per-event pricing, provided free-plan limits are disclosed upfront and handled gracefully (MEASURED from [Apify Monetization Docs](https://docs.apify.com/platform/actors/publishing/monetize)). Building tools that violate a target website's terms or bypass blocks is OUT under this project's rules.

What an agent adds and what needs the owner: Agents can monitor government/public feeds 24/7, detect schema changes, write tests, and maintain multiple narrow tools at low labor cost. The owner must choose legally clean sources, design pricing that clears platform compute costs (since Apify deducts usage costs from the creator's 80% share), and handle support.

What kills it: 92% of Apify listings get 10 or fewer monthly users; single-tool creators rarely get discovered; source-site changes break pipelines; collecting from private/ToS-barred sites triggers cease-and-desist letters or contract lawsuits.

Cheapest honest test: Publish 2 narrow, legally clean public-government-data Actors on Apify Store (for example, structured SEC/federal procurement or Idaho public-record alert feeds) over 14 days for under $20 in compute, and measure unique paying users and net payout after 30 days.

## Candidate 5: Selling 3D assets and creative packs on digital marketplaces (Fab, Unity Asset Store, CGTrader, TurboSquid)

What it is and who pays: Selling reusable 3D models, textures, shaders, motion-graphics templates, or Unreal/Unity/Blender packs on digital asset stores. Game studios, indie developers, and filmmakers pay per license to save modeling and look-dev hours.

Evidence people really earn from it: Real money flows to human-crafted, production-ready assets, but raw AI-generated 3D assets barely sell. CGTrader's 2026 market-trends findings (covering June 2025 to May 2026, reported by 404 Media) showed that while 1 in 6 uploaded models was AI-generated, AI assets accounted for only $1 of every $90 in revenue (MEASURED from [PromptToProp marketplace audit](https://prompttoprop.com/licensing/sell-ai-3d-models-on-marketplaces/)).

Base rate: UNKNOWN for seller medians on Fab, Unity Asset Store, or CGTrader (none publishes seller-wide medians). The CGTrader $1-in-$90 figure shows the base rate for AI-generated 3D assets is extremely poor (MEASURED from [PromptToProp marketplace audit](https://prompttoprop.com/licensing/sell-ai-3d-models-on-marketplaces/)).

Typical dollars per month at small scale and first three months: UNKNOWN from verified seller-wide data; a single new 3D pack typically earns $0 to $200/month in the first 90 days unless it hits a specific game-engine gap (INFERENCE).

Money and time needed before first dollar: $0 to $50 (using Blender/Unreal/existing software); 1 to 3 weeks to build, retopologize, UV-unwrap, and clear store curation, plus 30 to 60 days for first payouts (INFERENCE).

Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus taxes: Legal for original human-authored or properly licensed hybrid assets. Under Idaho tax rules, digital videos, games, music, and books sold with a permanent right to use are taxable tangible personal property, though major marketplaces handle marketplace-facilitator sales tax collection on platform sales (MEASURED from [Idaho State Tax Commission](https://tax.idaho.gov/taxes/sales-use/guides-for-certain-groups/retailers/collecting/)).

Platform rules on AI and automation: Strict disclosure or outright bans apply. TurboSquid explicitly prohibits AI-developed models ("Models developed using AI tools are not permitted and will be removed") unless a workflow is explicitly pre-approved (MEASURED from [PromptToProp marketplace audit](https://prompttoprop.com/licensing/sell-ai-3d-models-on-marketplaces/)). Epic's Fab requires a mandatory "Created with AI" label on AI-generated content (mislabeling violates Fab terms) and allows buyers to filter out AI products; Sketchfab's store closed on October 22, 2024; Unity Asset Store requires transparent AI disclosure, retopology/optimization, and reserves the right to reject mass-produced AI submissions (MEASURED from [PromptToProp marketplace audit](https://prompttoprop.com/licensing/sell-ai-3d-models-on-marketplaces/)). Hiding AI origin is OUT.

What an agent adds and what needs the owner: Agents can generate reference concepts, write Blender/Unreal automation scripts, build documentation, and create store marketing copy. The owner's real 3D/design skill is essential to produce clean topology, LODs, rigs, and shaders that buyers actually pay for rather than filter out.

What kills it: Buyers actively filter out "Created with AI" tags; raw AI meshes have unusable topology; curation delays on Unity/Fab slow time to first dollar; slow passive ramp fails the "reliably quick within weeks" goal.

Cheapest honest test: Skip raw AI 3D uploads. If tested at all, publish one human-finished, engine-ready Blender/Unreal utility or 3D asset pack (with honest labeling) on Fab in 7 days for $0 cash and measure 30-day unit sales.

## Candidate 6: Custom assistant and AI agent stores (OpenAI GPT Store, Poe, template stores)

What it is and who pays: Publishing custom GPTs, Poe bots, or workflow templates on consumer AI stores expecting platform-paid usage royalties. In theory, the platform shares subscription or per-message fees with creators.

Evidence people really earn from it: Weak to nonexistent. OpenAI promised US builder payouts in 2024, ran only a small invited test, and has published no payout rate or verified creator payout totals; furthermore, custom GPTs face platform deprecation risk (MEASURED from [Does It Pay agent-store audit](https://www.doesitpay.org/selling-ai-agents-and-gpts/)). Poe's creator monetization documentation page was blocked when checked directly (not bypassed), and independent audits found no published Poe payout totals or creator medians (MEASURED from [Does It Pay agent-store audit](https://www.doesitpay.org/selling-ai-agents-and-gpts/)).

Base rate: Near zero on OpenAI GPT Store (no open payout program confirmed); UNKNOWN on Poe (MEASURED from [Does It Pay agent-store audit](https://www.doesitpay.org/selling-ai-agents-and-gpts/)).

Typical dollars per month at small scale and first three months: $0 per month on OpenAI GPT Store; UNKNOWN (likely near $0 without an outside audience) on Poe (INFERENCE).

Money and time needed before first dollar: $0 to $20/month in model subscriptions, but no reliable path to a first dollar within weeks.

Legal status in Idaho and the US, plus taxes: Legal; standard US self-employment income reporting applies.

Platform rules on AI and automation: You do not own the customer, pricing, or distribution; the platform can change monetization or retire the format at any time (MEASURED from [Does It Pay agent-store audit](https://www.doesitpay.org/selling-ai-agents-and-gpts/)).

What an agent adds and what needs the owner: Minimal advantage because thousands of builders can clone any prompt-wrapper bot in minutes.

What kills it: No open payout rail on GPT Store, opaque or unverified payouts on Poe, zero moat against clones, and total platform lock-in.

Cheapest honest test: Do not run a test; treat consumer custom-GPT stores as a trap for direct cash flow.
