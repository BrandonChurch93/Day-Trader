## Legal corrections

- STATED: [SEC SAB 99](https://www.sec.gov/interps/account/sab99.htm) quotes the reasonable investor's “total mix” materiality test, in financial-reporting context; [SEC Investor.gov](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary/insider-trading) says liability can include tipping or misappropriation. It does not say everyone holding material nonpublic information automatically violates a securities law.
- STATED: [O'Hagan](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/521/642/) distinguishes fraud on the source in securities misappropriation from the separate tender-offer rule, which can apply without proving fiduciary breach. [Rule 10b5-2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-240/subject-group-ECFR4bb6d4a0ab76c75/section-240.10b5-2) lists nonexclusive duties: confidentiality agreement, pattern/practice with reasonable expectations, or certain family relationships subject to a rebuttal. [Dirks](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/463/646/) ties classic tippee duty to insider breach and what the recipient knew or should have known; personal benefit matters.
- STATED: [DOJ Wahi sentencing](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/former-coinbase-insider-sentenced-first-ever-cryptocurrency-insider-trading-case) is a plea to two wire-fraud conspiracy counts tied to confidential listings; [SEC's parallel release](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-98) describes a civil settlement for alleged trading in crypto asset securities, not a ruling classifying all tokens. [Second Circuit Chastain opinion](https://ww3.ca2.uscourts.gov/decisions/OPN/23-7038_complete_opn.pdf) vacated and remanded wire-fraud and derivative money-laundering convictions for instructional error, requiring commercial value to the holder for confidential information to qualify as property; it did not decide OpenSea's information in fact had none.
- STATED: [17 CFR 180.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-I/part-180/section-180.1) reaches intentional or reckless fraud/deception connected with listed commodity transactions, including specified swaps, spot sales in interstate commerce and registered-entity derivatives. It does not generally require disclosure except to keep a statement materially non-misleading.
- STATED: [SEC EDGAR case](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2019-1) announced charges against nine people in a hacking scheme and alleged trading ahead of at least 157 earnings releases, not that all nine traded or were convicted. [Expert-network case](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2011-38-sec-brings-expert-network-insider-trading-charges) alleges specific consultants passed confidential information; it does not ban all expert networks. This desk excludes confidential tips, stolen data and unapproved expert sourcing regardless.
