# Horse racing and other pari-mutuel pools

Second check, 2026-11-27: 8 of 8 claims confirmed. Ranked summary: [doc 205](205_deep_dive_beyond_trading.md).

INFERENCE: **No reliable quick-money route for a few hundred dollars in Idaho.** The best thing to test on paper is a mandatory-payout pool with a real carryover and unusually little new money. Next is a manual model in pools that limit high-speed teams. Neither has verified small-account income. Betting every card, paying for a full data stack, chasing rebates, and trying to copy a professional syndicate are traps for this bankroll.

## Idaho access and platform rules

MEASURED: The Idaho Constitution excepts pari-mutuel betting only under enabling legislation ([Art. III §20](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idconst/ArtIII/Sect20/)). Idaho allows licensed advance-deposit wagering (ADW) on live or simulcast horse racing, with funds on deposit and no betting by minors. Electronic wagering with an out-of-state provider is a felony unless the provider is licensed by the Idaho State Racing Commission and remits at least 10% of handle as a source-market fee ([Idaho Code §54-2512](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title54/T54CH25/SECT54-2512/)). Federal interstate horse-race rules preserve state legality and require host racing association, host commission and off-track commission consent; a U.S. address alone grants no access ([15 U.S.C. §§3002–3004](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2024-title15/html/USCODE-2024-title15-chap57.htm)).

## What the pool charges, and who has the advantage

- **Takeout and breakage:** MEASURED: Bettors fund the pool and winners split the net payout; Keeneland advertises 16% for off-track win/place/show, 19.5% exacta, 15% daily double and Pick 5, and 22% for ordinary Pick 4, Pick 6, trifecta and superfecta. On-track show is 10% only at the specified locations ([Keeneland wager menu](https://www.keeneland.com/racing/wagering/details)). Breakage is the payout residue from rounding, not the pool commission; current Kentucky statutes distinguish break calculations from rounding of cash payments ([KRS 230.3615](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/LAW/STATUTES/statute.aspx?id=58137), [KRS 230.295](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/statutes/statute.aspx?id=58139)).
- **Computer-assisted wagering, or CAW:** STATED: Elite told California regulators in September 2024 it had 12–20 customers, a $20 million annual turnover threshold with ramp-up, required an AmTote-connected API, no consumer GUI, a ban on cancellations, and host approval. Hub capacity is 2,000 bets per second per client, not a retail access offer ([Elite letter to CHRB](https://www.chrb.ca.gov/misc_docs/Computer_Assisted_Wagering.pdf)). Elite sampled 17% CAW share of Santa Anita win pools but 43% of the money in the final 60 seconds. It says teams receive the same underlying feed and cannot bet after pools close; its stated 13–17 second post-close merge and calculation window explains why the displayed price may still move after the break ([Elite letter](https://www.chrb.ca.gov/misc_docs/Computer_Assisted_Wagering.pdf)).
- **Restrictions and returns:** MEASURED: From February 5, 2026, NYRA excludes high-speed CAW in the last minute in pools not already more restricted, retains a two-minute win cutoff, and keeps its Late Pick 5 and Pick 6 retail-only ([NYRA](https://www.nyra.com/aqueduct/news/nyra-to-implement-new-guardrails-for-caw-activity/)). STATED: NYRA figures reported by trade press show Belmont festival CAW share of 11.0% ($22.1 million) in 2026, down from 20.2% ($40.0 million) in 2025 ([Belmont festival report](https://www.thoroughbreddailynews.com/caw-play-plummets-at-belmont-festival-but-total-handle-still-rises/)). California testimony reports two-minute win cutoffs at Del Mar and Santa Anita, and the Churchill Downs 10-K identifies the ADW hub structure without disclosing team ROI ([CHRB transcript](https://chrb.ca.gov/DocumentRequestor2.aspx?Category=BOARDMTGTRANS&SubCategory=&DocumentID=00053108), [2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/20212/000002021226000025/chdn-20251231.htm)). Elite says recent annual Santa Anita CAW play was near its 20% share and approximately 90% gross payback parameters; 90¢ returned per dollar is a stake loss before rebates, not verified post-rebate profit ([Elite letter](https://www.chrb.ca.gov/misc_docs/Computer_Assisted_Wagering.pdf)).
- **Court claims and rebates:** STATED: The Dickey plaintiffs allege 33% of U.S. handle in 2023 and special links and rebate deals; their 92% gross payback plus 13 percentage points rebate is a hypothetical +5%, not a team ledger ([complaint](https://ustrottingnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/10-24-25-CAW-Lawsuit.pdf)). MEASURED: The magistrate recommended dismissal on October 23, 2026; that is not a final judgment or proof of after-close betting ([Dickey v. Stronach, ECF 86](https://pastthewire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/2026.10.23-00086-000-REPORT-AND-RECOMMENDATION-re-77-78-79-80-and.pdf)). STATED: Ziemba's review discusses negotiated high-volume rebates and illustrates 5 points against 15% take, not an Idaho retail quote ([Ziemba review](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/120846/1/Ziemba_Pari_mutuel_betting_markets_published.pdf)). AmWager's entry-level 4 points per dollar divided by 1,600 points per $1 of credit is 0.25% before conditions; Idaho gets none ([AmWager rewards](https://www.amwager.com/rewards/)). HorsePlayersBet advertises a 1% monthly bonus starting at $100 handle, requires re-wagering before withdrawal, and excludes Idaho from its new-player bonus; current Idaho license and rebate eligibility UNKNOWN ([HorsePlayersBet promotion rules](https://bet.horseplayersbet.com/promotions)). INFERENCE: Assume no Idaho rebates until verified. Audited contemporary team returns after rebates remain UNKNOWN.

## What the model evidence establishes

- **Historical model betting:** MEASURED: Bolton and Chapman (1986) used 200 races and holdout evaluation and found promising expected returns despite takeout, not a live bankroll record ([Management Science](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/2772602.2772613)). STATED: Benter combined his model with public odds on 1986–1993 Hong Kong data and reported four profitable seasons of five, roughly 19% takeout, approximately 20% starting-capital loss in the losing season, and about ten person-years to build and scale. His modeled maximum profit as a percentage of total pool turnover was a simulation, not stake ROI or actual profit ([Benter report](https://datagolf.com/static/blogs/benter_paper.pdf)). Bloomberg reports his and Woods's first-season $120,000 loss out of $150,000 and Woods's $10 million gain in 1994–95; not audited and not a U.S. retail record ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2018-05-03/the-gambler-who-cracked-the-horse-racing-code)).
- **Favorites and exotics:** MEASURED: Snowberg and Wolfers used win and compound bets to show probability misperceptions explain favorite–longshot bias, not a blanket post-takeout edge ([NBER / JPE, 2010](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15923)). Ziemba describes about +3% net on very short favorites historically, but says favorites were no longer underbet enough after 1997 in the data reviewed; jackpot-type exotic wagers can overbet favorites instead ([Ziemba review](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/120846/1/Ziemba_Pari_mutuel_betting_markets_published.pdf)).
- **Late money and newer models:** MEASURED: In 14,854 Australian races in 2006, late money generally improved calibration, but the flat favorite return was still −12.35%; the final pool was not known ahead of the race ([Gramm and McKinney](https://economics.ecu.edu/wp-content/pv-uploads/sites/165/2019/07/ecu1310.pdf)). A 2025 Wharton thesis on 7,728 Hong Kong races found positive gradient-boosting backtests for some selections; its main three-selection forward trial finished flat because no signals qualified. The exploratory eight-selection result is not independently verified cash profit ([Wharton thesis](https://repository.upenn.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9b580267-c2ab-45f5-a3e0-cace231c4fa4/content)). INFERENCE: Nothing here proves a current net edge available at a pre-close Idaho retail clock.
- **Carryovers:** INFERENCE: If fresh handle is H, takeout fraction t and previously netted carryover C is distributed in full, aggregate payout per fresh dollar before rounding is 1 minus t plus C/H. It is above one only if C exceeds t times H; this is a group average, not a guarantee on any ticket. STATED: Keeneland's April 24, 2026 Pick 6 had $44,695 carryover and 22% take, so required under about $203,159 fresh handle to clear takeout ([mandatory-payout notice](https://www.xpressbet.com/component/content/article/21691-1-st-post-why-play-today-friday-april-24-2026), [takeout menu](https://www.keeneland.com/racing/wagering/details)). The result labels $671,860 “Total Pool” without defining whether the carryover is included; even subtracting $44,695 leaves much more than the break-even handle ([April 24 results](https://entries.horseracingnation.com/entries-results/keeneland/2026-04-24)). MEASURED: Some ordinary jackpot days require a unique correct ticket for full payout, unlike designated closing-day distribution; read the exact pool rules ([Keeneland rules](https://www.keeneland.com/sites/default/files/PayoutRules.pdf)).

STATED: Ziemba models the ordinary Rainbow Pick 6 separately because a jackpot or consolation share depends on whether a ticket is a unique winner. He also reports one historical jai alai mandatory-day success and a Santa Anita Pick 6 syndicate win; neither is an independently audited series or a repeatable small-player result ([Ziemba review](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/120846/1/Ziemba_Pari_mutuel_betting_markets_published.pdf)). INFERENCE: Don't confuse a guaranteed minimum pool with extra payout money when new handle exceeds the guarantee.

AmTote markets commercial APIs including Gateway Web Services, not a self-serve retail feed ([AmTote products](https://www.amtote.com/products-and-services)). UNKNOWN: An Idaho-eligible, affordable authorized retail API for timestamped odds, exotic probables, or submissions. Settlement files cannot recreate the live opportunity.

## Candidate methods

- **Mandatory-payout screening:** Other current bettors supply today's payout and earlier bettors supply the carryover. Evidence for the mechanism is strong; verified small-account success rate, median, monthly net and time to a cash profit are UNKNOWN. Money/time: $0 and 7–14 days to paper-test. Legal/terms: Idaho-licensed ADW, no scraping, manual owner submission only ([Idaho Code §54-2512](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title54/T54CH25/SECT54-2512/), [Equibase terms](https://www.equibase.com/about/terms.cfm)). Agent adds breadth and checks pool rules and arithmetic on permitted inputs; owner checks the specific venue and pool. New handle, jackpot eligibility, scratches and hit drought kill it. Cheapest test: log C, t, actual and pre-close upper estimate of H from permissible observations, reject if the upper estimate makes C/H too small, and timestamp paper tickets before results.
- **Retail-only horse pools and a manual model:** Payouts are funded by other bettors; a model must estimate probabilities better than the executable public line so the edge survives the cut. Historical and backtest evidence exists, but base rate, median, small monthly net and time to profit UNKNOWN. Money/time: $0, 7–14 days to screen, no subscriptions needed for a first small paper sample. Legal/terms: permitted data only, licensed Idaho ADW, no undisclosed automation; NYRA's retail-only pools and six-per-second terms are not API permission ([NYRA guardrails](https://www.nyra.com/aqueduct/news/nyra-to-implement-new-guardrails-for-caw-activity/), [account terms](https://racing.nyrabets.com/terms-and-conditions)). Agent adds calibration and chronological testing; owner logs a manual decision clock. Take, post-decision odds movement and exotic variance kill it. Cheapest test: freeze the model, clock and selection rule before recording prospective paper bets; compare with the live public baseline, scrape nothing, and treat two weeks as an implementation check, not proof of durable profit.
- **Small rebates and one-off promotions:** ADWs give eligible customers wagering credits or a fee share to gain volume; the pool still determines returns. Posted rewards exist, but no verified repeatable Idaho profit, success rate, median, or small-account monthly dollar estimate. Money/time: $0 to check, first cash gain UNKNOWN. Legal/terms: authorized ADW only; household, rollover and location conditions apply; no multi-accounting or undisclosed bots ([Idaho Code §54-2512](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title54/T54CH25/SECT54-2512/), [AmWager terms](https://www.amwager.com/terms-and-conditions/), [HorsePlayersBet offer rules](https://bet.horseplayersbet.com/promotions)). Agent can reconcile eligible bets and effective value; owner verifies eligibility. Exclusions, rollover and a rebate below take kill it. Cheapest test: ask for written Idaho and pool-specific eligibility; do not deposit in reliance on a headline.
- **Harness and international horse pools:** The host pool still pays in pari-mutuel wagering; a local separate pool and a globally commingled pool can settle differently. No verified small-account profit, base rate, median, monthly net or time to cash gain. Money/time: $0 for 7–14 days of one-circuit paper testing. Legal/terms: use Idaho's licensed horse ADW route, not an offshore account. TwinSpires lists harness and international races; HKJC explains that only approved partners and events commingle ([Idaho Code](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title54/T54CH25/SECT54-2512/), [TwinSpires tracks](https://www.twinspires.com/race-tracks/), [HKJC World Pool](https://racing.hkjc.com/en-us/local/page/worldpool)). TwinSpires prohibits bot interactions ([terms](https://www.twinspires.com/terms/)). Agent adds time-zone handling and permitted data harmonization; owner confirms the exact pool menu and settlement source. Fragmented coverage, access and take kill it. Cheapest test: one meet and one pool on paper before buying data.
- **Greyhound, jai alai and exchanges:** Dog and jai alai pool winners are paid by losing bettors; exchange bets match counterparties and are not pari-mutuel. No verified small-scale advantage, base rate, median, monthly net or time to cash. Money/time: $0 for rule review; no cash test. Idaho prohibits live dog racing and wagering on it, preserving grandfathered simulcast access; the Commission lists Greyhound Park in Post Falls, not an online Idaho greyhound route ([Idaho Code §54-2514A](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title54/T54CH25/SECT54-2514A/), [Commission](https://isp.idaho.gov/racing/)). Licensed Florida jai alai, harness and simulcast greyhounds are lawful examples for people who can legally access them there, not Idaho authorization ([Florida regulator](https://flgaming.gov/pmw/)). No lawful Idaho jai alai or horse exchange was established; Betfair says it is “not accepting US customers or bets from the US” ([Betfair FAQ](https://content.betfair.com/faqs/)). Agent reads rules; owner would have to verify and attend a lawful location. Lack of remote access, travel and thin pools kill it. OUT as remote Idaho income; no geolocation or terms workaround.
- **Industrial CAW replication:** Other bettors' stakes and negotiated fee economics support this approach. The access and substantial pool shares above are documented; after-rebate ROI, participant base rate and median still UNKNOWN. Time and money to profit not feasible here: Elite requires approved integration and $20 million annual turnover subject to ramp-up ([Elite letter](https://www.chrb.ca.gov/misc_docs/Computer_Assisted_Wagering.pdf)). Legal/terms: approved interface and track conditions only, plus Oregon human intervention ([Oregon rules](https://secure.sos.state.or.us/oard/viewCompDocument.action?compDocRsn=820)). Agent brings computation, not capital, permissions, data contracts or negotiated rates. Cheapest test is to read eligibility and stop; no submitter.

MEASURED: For tax years beginning after 2025, IRS guidance describes the 90% wagering-loss deduction limit capped at wagering gains and the lack of a wagering-loss deduction for non-itemizers ([IRS Bulletin 2026-19](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2026-19_IRB)). INFERENCE: Even a hypothetical 2% net betting edge on $300–$1,000 monthly handle makes $6–$20 before data and tax; at a hypothetical −16% the same volume loses $48–$160. Turnover is not bankroll, and these scenarios are not evidence of typical monthly returns.
