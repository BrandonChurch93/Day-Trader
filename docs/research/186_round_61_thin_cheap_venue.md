## Question 1: Stale Quotes (Cross-Venue Lead-Lag)

### What Published Work Says

- STATED: In liquid Bitcoin markets, [Fragmentation, Price Formation, and Cross-Impact in Bitcoin Markets (arXiv:2108.09750)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.09750) studied 14 Bitcoin markets (2 quarterly futures on Huobi and OKEx, 11 perpetual swaps across Huobi, BitMEX, Deribit, Bybit, OKEx, FTX, and Binance, and 1 spot market, huobipro_BTC/USDT; zero US spot venues and zero altcoins; Feb 22 to Mar 1, 2021 model sample, resampled at 50 ms). Its principal future-return prediction targets were 500 ms and 1,000 ms (with 100 to 2,000 ms flow/return lookbacks, 5 to 600 s mean-divergence lookbacks, and 0.5 to 4,800 s adverse-selection horizons); the phrase "milliseconds to low seconds" is a summary of those horizons, not the paper's own phrasing. The paper states: "Perpetual swaps and quarterly futures on Binance and Huobi were found to be particularly strong leading markets, while the Bybit and FTX perpetuals were found to be strong laggards." and "It demonstrates that it is possible and indeed quite feasible to anticipate future price moves in Bitcoin markets on sub-1s time scales" (average R-squared 9.6% at 500 ms and 10.4% at 1,000 ms).
- STATED: Under default taker fees, all single-market and full-feature taker strategies in [arXiv:2108.09750](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.09750) lost money (smallest single-market loss -2,819 bp, average -18,899 bp), while heavy VIP fee discounts yielded positive PnL on a few markets (such as FTX perpetual, Huobi spot, and Binance perpetuals). The paper states: "Evidence in support of this assertion was provided in the previous subsection, where we observed that a single market's features are insufficient to produce \"alpha\" in excess of execution cost on another market (with rare exceptions for VIP traders that receive heavy fee discounts)." and "Only when a market exhibits lagging behaviour in excess of the laggardness to which it is naturally predisposed by its fee structure do we obtain trading \"alpha\" that can be exploited to generate positive PnL values on the market in question."
- UNKNOWN: Whether Binance.US's thin USD altcoin books (outside BTC and ETH) lag Coinbase Exchange or Binance global by **multiple seconds** with enough $250 depth to clear Binance.US's 2 bp taker fee plus spread is not measured in published literature and **cannot be tested on history**, because neither Binance.US nor Coinbase publishes free historical order-book archives (`docs/research/185_round_60_using_the_cost_finding.md`).

### What a No-Account Forward Measurement Looks Like (and What It Cannot Prove)

- INFERENCE: Using only public WebSocket or REST book endpoints (no account, no API key), log paired top-of-book and $250 depth snapshots on Coinbase Exchange and Binance.US every 1 to 2 seconds on the 20 Plan 70 USD coins, stamped on one local collector clock. When Coinbase's midpoint jumps by at least 30 bp over 5 seconds, check whether Binance.US's $250 ask (or bid) 2 seconds later is still at least 15 bp behind Coinbase's new midpoint and whether Binance.US catches up within 10 to 60 seconds.
- INFERENCE: What this public measurement **cannot prove**: (1) whether a real taker order sent from Idaho or a cloud box would actually fill before the market maker cancels or a faster colocated taker sweeps the level (latency-induced adverse selection), (2) true matching-engine timestamp alignment across two separate venues, and (3) whether the resting size was real or already in flight.

### Verdict: DROP (Overruled from scout WATCH in Second check)

INFERENCE: **Drop** as a standalone retail strategy. On BTC and ETH, sub-second competition makes a home-latency stale-quote taker hopeless and default-fee taker rules lose in `arXiv:2108.09750`. On thin Binance.US USD altcoins, multi-second lag is UNKNOWN, untestable on history, and unprovable from public snapshots (cannot prove a retail order fills before market-maker cancels or faster takers).

## Question 2: Price Gaps Held With Inventory on Both Venues (No Transfers)

### What Published Work Says

- STATED: [Makarov and Schoar (2020, Journal of Financial Economics)](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/130495/SSRN-id3171204.pdf) studied 34 exchanges across 19 countries over **January 1, 2017 to February 28, 2018** (with main Kaiko analyses on 14 to 15 liquid exchanges) and found large, multi-day gaps mostly **across** countries with capital controls (for example, US-Korea > 15% to 20%, US-Japan ~10%), not within the US: "Second, price deviations are much larger across countries (or regions) than within the same country." and "In contrast, the price deviations between exchanges in the same country typically do not exceed 1%, on average." Specifically for US exchanges, their within-US arbitrage index stayed below 2% for most of the year (reaching about 1.04 during several weeks in March, June, and December 2017). The paper also states: "While, in theory, prices across exchanges may not converge for a long time, Fig. 4 shows that, in practice, arbitrage opportunities in any given market open for less than two days, on average, and even in the extreme never existed for more than a month."
- UNKNOWN: Exact 2025 to 2026 median gap size and half-life between Coinbase Exchange USD and Binance.US USD on our 20 coins is not published; Measurement 132 will show hourly snapshots over 30 days.

### What Kills It for a $500 to $2,000 Account

- STATED (busy-venue fees): Even if Binance.US costs 2 bp taker, the second leg on a busy US venue destroys the trade at small-account volume tiers. [Kraken Support](https://support.kraken.com/articles/cross-platform-fee-tier-changes) states Tier 1 ($0+ spot volume) is 0.40% maker and 0.80% taker (40 to 80 bp per side); `docs/research/180_round_57_cost_as_the_binding_limit.md` documents Coinbase retail taker fees at 60 bp+ (and Alpaca at 25 bp taker + spread). A round-trip rebalancing across Binance.US and Coinbase or Kraken starts in an 80 to 160 bp hole before spreads.
- STATED (withdrawal holds and wire fees): Once inventory builds up on one side, moving cash back is slow and costly. [Binance.US Help Center](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/9842927-why-can-t-i-withdraw) states: "Binance.US applies a 7-day withdrawal hold (equal to 168 hours) to all ACH deposits to ensure financial security." and "Additionally, this hold applies to both the USD deposit and any cryptocurrency purchased with those funds, including stablecoins such as USDT." and "No, the 7-day hold period cannot be waived, shortened, or bypassed under any circumstances." [Binance.US Wire Help Center](https://support.binance.us/en/articles/10658835-how-to-deposit-withdraw-via-wire) states wire deposits are $0 from Binance.US and wire withdrawals cost $25 with a $100 minimum balance.
- MEASURED (minimum order size and depth): Binance.US `minNotional` is $1.00 across all 20 Plan 70 USD pairs (`exchangeInfo`, 2026-11-26), so minimum size is not the blocker; thin altcoin top-of-book depth during fast moves is.
- INFERENCE (USD vs stablecoin basis): If one venue holds USDT or USDC while the other holds USD, even a 10 to 30 bp stablecoin basis wobble swamps a typical within-US price gap.
- STATED (US per-wallet/account basis rules): Under final IRS digital-asset regulations effective for acquisitions and dispositions on or after January 1, 2025, [IRS Rev. Proc. 2024-28](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-24-28.pdf) (which provides a safe harbor under Section 1012(c)(1) to allocate unused basis across wallets or accounts as of January 1, 2025) states: "Section 1.1012-1(h) and (j) of the 2024 final regulations will apply to all acquisitions and dispositions of digital assets on or after January 1, 2025." and that Section 1.1012-1(j) rules "apply the specific identification or FIFO rules to units held within a single wallet or account." Holding the same coin on two exchanges splits basis tracking across separate accounts and creates taxable short-term dispositions on every arbitrage sell leg.

### Verdict: DROP

INFERENCE: **Drop**. Two-venue inventory arbitrage between US dollar exchanges is structurally unworkable at $500 to $2,000 because the dear venue's entry-tier fee (40 to 90 bp/side) is larger than typical within-US USD price gaps, and rebalancing hits 7-day ACH holds, $25 wire fees, and per-account IRS tax-lot fragmentation.

## Question 3: Resting Orders at a Zero Maker Fee

### What Published Work Says About Adverse Selection

- STATED: Live experiments show that passive maker orders suffer severe adverse selection even on the deepest crypto book in the world and even with a negative maker rebate. [Albers, Cucuringu, Howison, and Shestopaloff (2025, arXiv:2502.18625)](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.18625v1) ran two live experiments totaling 405,697 minimum-sized maker orders on Binance's linear USDT-margined Bitcoin perpetual (Continuous Quoting Mode: 232,897 orders, 127,051 fills, Feb 12 to 19, 2024; Periodic Quoting Mode: 172,800 orders, 95,998 fills, Aug 21 to 31, 2024; NOT spot and NOT US altcoins) and reported: "For maker orders, we find a negative correlation between fill likelihood and subsequent short-term returns, posing a significant challenge for maker order-based strategies, while the main hurdle with taker orders is overcoming the taker fee."
- STATED: Same paper on the mechanical cause and bottom line: "If the next price move is against a maker order in the top-of-book queue, that order automatically fills, with probability 1, a phenomenon sometimes referred to as adverse selection." Even assuming Binance's best VIP tier (-0.5 bp maker rebate, 1.5 bp taker), the naive market-making strategy lost -0.4307 bp per trade net of rebates (almost 60% over about 3 days and 7 hours, annualized Sharpe -109.0), and the authors conclude: "The naive market making strategy is thus a recipe for poverty - heaven help the starry-eyed novice traders, fresh from academia, who, seduced by maker rebates, attempt to implement this strategy in practice."
- INFERENCE: In thin equity or thin altcoin books, wider spreads look tempting to a small passive trader, but adverse selection is worse when informed flow hits a thin book and the passive trader cannot cancel in milliseconds.

### US Maker Rebate Programs for a Small Idaho Account

- STATED: **No US spot venue offers a maker rebate (negative fee) to a $500 to $2,000 retail account.**
- STATED: On [Kraken Pro](https://support.kraken.com/articles/cross-platform-fee-tier-changes), Tier 1 ($0+ 30-day spot volume) pays **0.40% maker / 0.80% taker**; reaching 0.0% spot maker requires $10M+ 30-day spot volume (or $10M Assets on Platform), and Kraken's spot table shows no negative spot maker fee (negative futures maker rebates of -0.0030% start at Tier 11: $5M+ spot volume, $250M+ futures volume, or $5M AoP).
- STATED: On Binance.US (`docs/research/180_round_57_cost_as_the_binding_limit.md`), the lowest tier is **0.00% maker / 0.02% taker** (0.01% on BNBUSD) for Idaho accounts, which is zero fee rather than a negative rebate.

### Binance.US Order Rules (STATED and MEASURED)

STATED from the [Binance.US API Documentation](https://docs.binance.us/):

- Post-only orders (`LIMIT_MAKER`): "This is a LIMIT order that will be rejected if the order immediately matches and trades as a taker. This is also known as a POST-ONLY order" (rejection message: "Order would immediately match and take").
- Tick size (`PRICE_FILTER`): "tickSize defines the intervals that a price/stopPrice can be increased/decreased by" and orders must satisfy `price % tickSize == 0`.
- Minimum notional (`NOTIONAL`): `price * quantity >= minNotional`.
- Self-trade prevention (`selfTradePreventionMode`): "STP is a measure to prevent users from trading against their own account or other accounts that share the same tradeGroupId" with default `EXPIRE_MAKER` and supported modes `EXPIRE_TAKER`, `EXPIRE_MAKER`, `EXPIRE_BOTH`, `DECREMENT`, and `TRANSFER`.

MEASURED directly from `api.binance.us` `exchangeInfo` on 2026-11-26 across all 20 Plan 70 USD pairs:

- All 20 pairs (`AAVEUSD`, `ADAUSD`, `AVAXUSD`, `BCHUSD`, `BNBUSD`, `BTCUSD`, `DOGEUSD`, `DOTUSD`, `ETHUSD`, `HBARUSD`, `LINKUSD`, `LTCUSD`, `NEARUSD`, `PEPEUSD`, `SHIBUSD`, `SOLUSD`, `SUIUSD`, `XLMUSD`, `XRPUSD`, `ZECUSD`) have `status = TRADING`, `LIMIT_MAKER` enabled, `minNotional = $1.00`, and `defaultSelfTradePreventionMode = EXPIRE_MAKER`.
- Tick sizes are fine enough for tight quoting: `$0.1` on `BCHUSD`; `$0.01` on `BTCUSD`, `ETHUSD`, `LTCUSD`, `BNBUSD`, `ZECUSD`, `SOLUSD`, `AAVEUSD`; `$0.001` on `LINKUSD`, `AVAXUSD`, `DOTUSD`, `NEARUSD`; `$0.0001` on `SUIUSD`, `XRPUSD`; `$0.00001` on `ADAUSD`, `XLMUSD`, `HBARUSD`, `DOGEUSD`; and `$0.00000001` on `SHIBUSD`, `PEPEUSD`.

### Verdict: WATCH (Overruled from scout KEEP in Second check; DROP Standalone Market Making)

- DROP standalone passive market making: Albers et al. (2025) proves naive passive quoting loses to adverse selection even with a -0.5 bp rebate, and no US venue pays a rebate to a small Idaho account.
- WATCH resting-order execution on Binance.US USD books via `practice/traders/E11_dip_buy_cheap_venue.md` before building a new E10 overlay: E11 already runs a 300-second $250 resting-bid line on Binance.US USD pairs using strict trade-through only (explicitly rejecting same-price fills and bounding public `aggTrades` pagination), while `practice/traders/E10_coin_trend_ratchet.md` is frozen for 365 days without maker fills.

## Question 5: Single-Venue Idiosyncratic Dislocation Mean Reversion

### The Mechanism (Why It Fits a Thin, Cheap Venue)

- STATED: [Makarov and Schoar (2020, JFE)](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/130495/SSRN-id3171204.pdf) documented that single-exchange dislocations predict local mean reversion: "When the price on any exchange deviates above (below) from the average price on other exchanges, the subsequent returns on this exchange are predicted to be lower (higher) than the returns on other exchanges."
- INFERENCE: Unlike Question 2 (which requires inventory on two exchanges and pays Coinbase's 60 to 90 bp fee), a **single-venue dislocation fade** holds USD only on Binance.US. When an uninformed local market sell pushes Binance.US's $250 USD ask far below Coinbase Exchange's live midpoint on one of our 20 coins (for example, >= 40 bp cheap and persisting for at least 10 seconds so it is not a single-tick glitch; both 40 bp and 10 s are scout-chosen round numbers, not from a published source), a trader buys on Binance.US alone (2 bp taker fee) and exits back on Binance.US once the local bid reverts within 10 bp of Coinbase's midpoint or after a fixed time stop (for example, 4 hours). Total round-trip exchange fee is 4 bp, zero transfers, single-venue tax lots, and hold time is minutes to a few hours.

### Verdict: WATCH (Overruled from scout KEEP in Second check; Wait for Measurement 132 First)

- Exact thing measured: Using paired live public books on Coinbase Exchange and Binance.US across the 20 Plan 70 USD coins, detect every event where Binance.US's $250 executable ask sits at least **40 bp below** Coinbase's live midpoint across two consecutive observations 10 seconds apart (both 40 bp and 10 s are scout-chosen thresholds, not from a published source). Mark a hypothetical $250 taker buy on Binance.US at the second observation (+2 bp fee), and mark the exit at Binance.US's $250 executable bid (+2 bp fee) at the first observation where Binance.US's bid returns within 10 bp of Coinbase's midpoint, or at a hard 4-hour time stop, whichever comes first. Compare against the matched Coinbase midpoint return over the exact same entry-to-exit timestamps to separate local basis reversion from general market drift.
- Data needed and availability: Requires free public **WebSocket** book streams on Coinbase Exchange and Binance.US (with clock-skew logging), NOT 5 to 10 second REST polling (polling 20 coins every 5 to 10 seconds across two hosts is 2 to 4 req/sec per host, exceeding the repo's 2.5 req/sec host pacing in `E10`, `E11`, and `Measurement 132`, and fails to align timestamps). First wait for Measurement 132's 30-day hourly paired-book readout (`docs/research/182_measurement_132_venue_cost.md`) to see if >= 40 bp Binance.US-below-Coinbase gaps even occur with usable frequency.
- Testable on history? **No.** Historical Binance.US klines cannot distinguish an executable $250 ask discount from a single stale print, lack paired synchronized Coinbase books, and are missing 18 months of USD history (2023-08 through 2025-01). Only a live forward measurement is honest.
- Pass mark critique (scout-chosen, underpowered): The scout proposed at least 30 distinct dislocation events across at least 15 separate UTC days over 60 calendar days, net return at least +15 bp, positive mean after removing the top 5% of events, and day-clustered t >= 2.0 on paired basis-reversion return. Thirty events across 15 days has weak power against crypto volatility, overlapping multi-coin crash events collapse effective day count, and observing a 40 bp discount on public snapshots does not prove an Idaho retail taker could fill before a faster participant sweeps the level.
- Strongest reason it could be false: Persistent venue-specific discounts or toxic local flow. If Binance.US trades at a discount to Coinbase because of a Binance.US banking or withdrawal rumor (as in June 2023) or because an informed seller is dumping on Binance.US first, the local price does not revert to Coinbase; instead Coinbase falls to meet Binance.US, or Binance.US stays discounted past the 4-hour stop, turning every fade into a losing catch of a falling knife.
