# 50. Copying other public filers: the reading, checked

Status: one helper wrote the reading on 2026-11-25 and a second opened every source: 29 claims confirmed, 9 wrong as first written, 1 not found. The table below gives the corrected version of each. Nothing here was measured on our own data. Written by the builder, 2026-11-25 18:39 UTC.

Why: the owner's idea of following members of Congress (`docs/research/48_congress_trades_evidence.md`) is one case of a wider one, which is to copy people whose trades or holdings must be made public, after the public date.

## In plain words

- Copying famous fund managers from their quarterly holdings: funds that did it with real money mostly trailed the market. GURU made 12.3% a year against SPY's 15.5% from 2012 to 2026, ALFA 9.3% against 13.5% before it closed in 2022, and GVIP 17.3% against 15.9% from 2016 to 2026. Across nine such funds, two beat SPY. One 2026 study finds a small gain from buying two days after a well-known manager's new holding is filed; in its buy-and-hold form the gain is not clear of chance and costs are not taken off.
- Large-stake filings (13D and 13G): the price jump comes on the filing day. After that day, later samples show no drift left to collect.
- ARK's daily trade list: a simulation of copying it since 2020 did no better than random matched stocks, and there is no free dated history to test on.
- Short-seller reports: prices fall hard on the day and keep falling for a year in the published samples, but we only buy, and there is no free dated feed of the reports. The exchange record of how heavily a stock is shorted is free and dated; stocks shorted most lagged by 1.1 to 2.3% a year, not clear of chance. Useful at most as a "do not buy" filter.
- Lobbying and government contracts: the real-money lobbying fund trailed the world index (+9.92% a year against 11.4%, 2022 to 2025), and the bump after a defense contract is a few hundredths of a percent.
- Analyst upgrades and target changes: the move is in the opening price. Nothing left after it.
- Drift after earnings, entered the next day: the one idea here with live support and free, exactly dated data. From 2023 to 2026 stocks with the best surprises beat those with the worst by 1.1 to 2.0 points over 60 days. Two warnings: in 2025 and 2026 that gap shrank to 0.5 points overall and held only in large companies, and in a 2000 to 2025 sample the gain was all on the losing side (bad surprises kept falling 1.46%; good ones did nothing, -0.04%). An account that only buys gets the weak half.

The data is in hand from replay 11 (dated releases, the company's own past figures, daily prices), and replay 11's plain yardstick already points the same way. Stated before any run: the published work says the buying side is the weak side, so the expectation is that it fails.
