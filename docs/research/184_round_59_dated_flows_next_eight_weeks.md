- STATED from [Dimensional (July 2024), Measuring the Costs of Index Reconstitution: A 10-Year Perspective](https://www.dimensional.com/chmedia/383623/source/measuring-the-costs-of-index-reconstitution-a-10-year-perspective.pdf) (2014 to 2023, 10 US indices, N = 2,123 Russell 2000 non-migrating adds and deletes): "Across the 10 US indices examined, nonmigrating index additions outperformed and index deletions underperformed their respective indices by 4.1%, on average, in the month leading up to index reconstitution. This price pressure reverses by 5.7% in the 20 trading days following reconstitution, on average." STATED from the same [Dimensional study](https://www.dimensional.com/chmedia/383623/source/measuring-the-costs-of-index-reconstitution-a-10-year-perspective.pdf): "On average, the price for deletions falls relative to nonrebalanced stocks by 30 bps from 4 pm to market close on reconstitution day, just before they are 'sold' from the index, with a reversal of a relative 63 bps by market open the following day." Crucially, these are benchmark-relative excess returns before transaction costs (NOT raw long-only net returns), and in Table 1 Russell 2000's own pooled overnight reversal was only -3.4 bp despite closing-auction volume 29,336% above the prior 30-day average.
- STATED from [Dimensional (migrating Russell 2000 study)](https://www.dimensional.com/us-en/insights/even-migrating-stocks-face-index-reconstitution-costs) (2014 to 2023): "As shown in Panel A, the cumulative excess return to R2 Migrating Deletions is -1.1%, on average, in the 20 days leading up to reconstitution. Over the 20 trading days following reconstitution, these stocks see an upward reversal of around 2%, on average." Benchmark-relative, gross of costs.
- STATED from [Greenwood and Sammon (2024/2025, Journal of Finance 80(2), HBS PDF)](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/ssrn-4294297_7112837f-ac0f-4b90-a4d0-802c3f79b462.pdf) (Table 8, N = 7,769 Russell 2000 additions, 1990 to 2020, cumulative market-adjusted returns, gross, no costs modeled): "For stocks added to the 2000, addition cumulative market-adjusted returns increase from an average of 2.2% in the 1990s, to 8.3% in the 2000s and thereafter declined to 3.1% in the 2010s." Exact Table 8 figures: +2.215% (1990s), +8.284% (2000s), +3.148% (2010s, p < 0.05; 2000s-to-2010s decline not statistically significant).
- Post-publication decay and contrary evidence: STATED from [Onayev and Zdorovtsov (2007, SSRN 960727)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=960727): "Price pressure-induced reversal effects are found to be much smaller to nonexistent in the recent years." STATED from [QREF (2020, ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1062976920301265): "Conversely, for firms removed from the index, we observe negative median wealth effects during the surrounding months, with no evidence of reversal as noted with index additions."

- Main reason it would fail: retail half-spreads on small-cap deletions and additions (UNKNOWN for sub-$10 names; `doc 136` measured 1.668 bp median half-spread on 38 large stocks and 3.787 bp in the first 30 minutes, with small illiquid deletions far wider), adverse selection from distressed deletions, and splitting annual forced volume across two semi-annual dates.
