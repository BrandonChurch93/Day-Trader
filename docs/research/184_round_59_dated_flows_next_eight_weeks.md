- STATED from [Dimensional (July 2024), Measuring the Costs of Index Reconstitution: A 10-Year Perspective](https://www.dimensional.com/chmedia/383623/source/measuring-the-costs-of-index-reconstitution-a-10-year-perspective.pdf) (2014 to 2023 full sample across 10 US indices: 1,822 non-migrating Russell 2000 additions and 1,638 deletions, or 3,460 total): "Across the 10 US indices examined, nonmigrating index additions outperformed and index deletions underperformed their respective indices by 4.1%, on average, in the month leading up to index reconstitution. Over the 20 trading days following reconstitution, these stocks see an upward reversal of around 2%, on average." Benchmark-relative, gross of costs.
- STATED from [Greenwood and Sammon (2024/2025, Journal of Finance 80(2), HBS PDF)](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/ssrn-4294297_7112837f-ac0f-4b90-a4d0-802c3f79b462.pdf) (Table 8, N = 7,769 direct Russell 2000 additions from outside Russell 3000, 1990 to 2020, cumulative market-adjusted returns from day before May rank date through day after June implementation, gross, no costs modeled): "For stocks added to the 2000, addition cumulative market-adjusted returns increase from an average of 2.2% in the 1990s, to 8.3% in the 2000s and thereafter declined to 3.1% in the 2010s." STATED from [Martin et al. (2022, QREF, ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1062976920301265): "Conversely, for firms removed from the index, we observe negative median wealth effects during the surrounding months, with no evidence of reversal as noted with index additions."
