## Decisions

- 1. SKIP fast stale-quote taking. The measured CME lead is a correlation lag, not time a stale Binance.US offer stays executable. The cross-exchange paper also assumes transfers and a long-short trade. Neither settles a slower one-way rule. The distinct executable version, buying a persistent local discount and exiting locally, belongs under 3 rather than becoming a second test. Do not infer fills from minute bars.
- 2. WATCH resting bids as execution only; SKIP standalone spread capture. Neither fast perpetual quotes nor the closed-end fund result rules out deeper spot bids held for hours after a washout. But a touch is not a fill, and a post-only order still has missed fills, adverse selection and an exit cost. E12 already carries the dip-buy execution comparison; E13 is Plan 75 trend, not a washout trial. Check E12's prewritten strict-fill line against taking immediately, keeping misses in the comparison. If a deeper, longer-lived bid ever warrants separate work, first show added value over the existing overlay without using lows known only afterward. Plan 76 tests the 24-hour rebound, not resting fills; it has not passed.
- 3. WATCH a clean Binance.US-only discount; SKIP betting on a broken bank rail. The May premium and the later USD pair removals do not test an executable local discount while both venues are healthy. This reopens the existing Round 61 watch, not a new alpha family. Round 61's original Coinbase comparator is barred; Measurement 133 cannot replace it. If rights and timing are cleared, count prospectively when a $250 local USD ask sits materially below a synchronized healthy same-coin USD reference and stays there long enough for a slow desk. Exclude announced or observed deposit, withdrawal or book impairment and stablecoin conversions. No second trade or transfer; exit on the Binance.US bid. Fix the gap, wait, time stop and minimum independent days before logging. Compare the marked local trade with the same-time reference move to separate basis recovery from a broad bounce. Cluster by day, and do not call a quote a fill. No count started.
- 4a. WATCH a volume-conditioned long-only add-on. The original daily long-short rule is not a cash-spot trade. The paper's Binance returns do not tell us how much came from the loser leg, and its value-weighted result is not a large-coin long-only test. A feasible question is whether a low standardized volume shock known at the close improves a next-day long-only loser rule versus the same rule without the filter, with the same coins, capital and exit and no short leg. This is distinct from Plan 76's price-only hourly washout and the E12 dip cards, but overlaps their reversal family; require incremental value, not another version of a successful-looking dip. Wait for the existing reversal work and a cleared feed with adequate full-day volume coverage. No new replay.
- 4b. SKIP the published long-short CTREND and small-coin size rules. Weekly top-rank-only rotation with a no-trade band is implementable in a cash account and is not ruled out by these papers. But it is not established as an edge specific to Binance.US. A generic trend variation goes behind Plan 75 and E13; reopen only for a prewritten rank signal distinct from their trend rule, with enough point-in-time eligible USD coins and a matched long-only, turnover-controlled baseline. Do not use hindsight exclusions.
- 4c. WATCH low-volatility or minimum-variance weights as a risk benchmark only. Weekly long-only sizing is plausible, subject to pair access, minimums, and turnover in dollars. Compare equal weighting and a simple BTC-heavy baseline over the same dates; a higher reported Sharpe does not establish new alpha or protection from a crypto drawdown.
- 5. SKIP a new single-coin ML build. A slow no-trade band can reduce churn and is feasible, but the cited selected BTC/USDT model does not show a Binance.US-specific advantage. Give the existing Plan 75/E13 trend and Plan 76/E12 reversal work priority. Treat thresholding as an execution choice, not a new profit claim.
