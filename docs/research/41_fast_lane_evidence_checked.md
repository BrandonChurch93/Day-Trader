# Round 5 checked: Short holds in small coins with leverage

Checked November 25, 2026. Counts: **11 confirmed, 6 wrong, 2 not found**. Each bullet is one verdict; repeated or closely linked numbers count together. **MEASURED (by its authors)** means a source reports results from data, not that we ran the test. **MEASURED** means a fee schedule or exact arithmetic was checked directly. **STATED** means a proposal, description or unaudited statement. **UNKNOWN** means the claimed result was not established. A basis point is 0.01 percentage point. Sharpe compares return with how much returns vary; it is not a return.

## Short-horizon patterns in small and mid coins

- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: 15-minute directional mean reversion and cost failure.** arXiv 2608.21888v1 (posted August 22, 2026) tests 183 Binance USDT spot pairs against 187 US stocks and ETFs over 2025-01-01 to 2026-02-11, a frozen holdout from 2026-02-12 to 2026-08-08, and four focal coins back to 2021. After joint false-discovery control, 90% of crypto pairs (164 of 183) show significant 15-minute reversal versus 2.7% of US stocks (5 of 187), and the pattern replicates on Binance USDT-M perpetuals with AUC 0.531 to 0.538 ([arXiv 2608.21888v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)). Costs kill the trade: gross edge peaks near 1.3 basis points per trade against a 5 basis point round-trip maker band and 10 to 20 basis point taker band, and zero of 183 pairs clear 5 basis points; reversal is stronger after aggressive flow-driven bars (AUC 0.540 vs 0.519) ([arXiv 2608.21888v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)). Caveats: offshore Binance data, two-way signal, and pre-cost detection only.
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: Zaremba et al. (2021) top-2% daily momentum is not statistically significant.** The journal paper covers 3,607 cryptocurrencies from January 1, 2015 to March 1, 2021 (2,700,925 daily return observations) and confirms strong daily reversal in the bottom 98% by market cap (value-weighted High-Low spread -3.84% per day; equal-weighted -13.19% per day) with no transaction costs or spreads deducted ([Zaremba et al., IRFA 2021](https://www.wir.ue.wroc.pl/docstore/download/UEWR135bdc5e34ad49868fe8a5e77fa021c7/Mercik_Up_or_down_Short-term_reversal.pdf)). However, the draft presents the top-2% (~23 coins, over 90% of market cap) daily momentum spread of +0.22% without noting that it is not statistically significant (p = 0.171 value-weighted, p = 0.115 equal-weighted; eight-factor alpha p = 0.169 value-weighted and 0.076 equal-weighted) ([Zaremba et al., IRFA 2021](https://www.wir.ue.wroc.pl/docstore/download/UEWR135bdc5e34ad49868fe8a5e77fa021c7/Mercik_Up_or_down_Short-term_reversal.pdf)). The sample also ends in March 2021, omits all trading costs, and uses long-short portfolios.
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: Small-coin premium at the $10,000 volume screen.** arXiv 2610.23283v2 (October 19, 2026) covers 8,970 capitalization-reporting crypto assets across 690 weekly snapshots (April 28, 2013 to July 12, 2026), assigns -100% to delistings, and reports 27.5% per week on paper for the bottom decile versus 0.01% per week (t = 0.04) when deciles are re-formed inside the tradable universe in 2021-07 to 2026-07 ([arXiv 2610.23283v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23283)). Weekly turnover is 17% to 47%, and Consolidation-era net returns after 2% one-way costs are -0.08% per week at >$100,000 daily volume and -3.69% per week at >$1,000,000 ([arXiv 2610.23283v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23283)). Where the draft errs: it says requiring >$10,000 daily volume and 2% one-way costs destroys the premium, whereas Table 7 shows the >$10,000 screen still returned +1.28% per week net in 2021 to 2026 on $292,700 (rounded $293,000) capacity; the premium disappears only when deciles are re-formed inside the tradable set or at $100,000+ volume ([arXiv 2610.23283v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23283)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Bitcoin-to-altcoin regime lead-lag fails after costs.** Khan (SSRN 7485359, posted September 19, 2026) uses two years (2024 to 2026) of hourly and 4-hour Binance data for Bitcoin and nine major altcoins, reporting 0.772 contemporaneous correlation, ~0.006 one-hour-ahead correlation, +15.1 percentage points excess 24-hour convergence after a Bitcoin regime flip, and -0.343% per event after realistic costs ([Khan, SSRN 7485359](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7485359)). A four-model ensemble (LSTM, XGBoost, Transformer, KAN) reaches 81% regime F1 but only 52% directional accuracy and cost-negative signals ([Khan, SSRN 7485359](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7485359)). Only the SSRN abstract is open on this page; Coinbase is named in the ethics statement rather than explicitly in the replication sentence.
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: Guo, Sang, Tu and Wang publication venue, horizon and cost details.** The linked SMU PDF is a December 31, 2021 working paper, not a 2024 JEDC publication ([Guo et al., SMU working paper](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=7900&context=lkcsb_research)). It confirms 30 Binance USDT spot coins (March 25, 2019 to April 30, 2021), 3.55 basis points per minute gross long-short at t+1, and 0.15 basis points per minute (2.16% per day) net on Binance futures with 13-minute rebalancing at VIP0 taker fees of 4 basis points ([Guo et al., SMU working paper](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=7900&context=lkcsb_research)). However, the paper prints a backwards futures date typo ("29 July 2021 to 30 April 2021"), reports significant predictability only through minute 5 (insignificant after minute 6, not 10), does not label whether 4 basis points is one-way or round-trip, models no bid-ask spread or slippage, uses 2019 to 2021 data, and relies on offshore Binance futures long-short across 30 coins ([Guo et al., SMU working paper](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=7900&context=lkcsb_research)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: Quarter-hour 4-to-12-hour figures are regression slopes, not raw returns.** arXiv 2607.09426v2 (dated August 24, 2026; arXiv stamp July 16, 2026) studies six Binance USDT-M perpetuals (BTC, ETH, XRP, SOL, DOGE, ADA) from January 1, 2021 to October 31, 2024 (out-of-sample July 1, 2021 to October 31, 2024, plus Bybit replication for BTC and ETH) ([arXiv 2607.09426v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)). First-10-second out-of-sample R-squared averages 3.37%, AUC averages 0.6011, and the gross pre-fee edge averages 0.510 basis points per boundary (~0.5 basis points, far below fees) ([arXiv 2607.09426v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)). However, the 2.07 to 11.33 basis points range in Table A.4 is predictive slope in basis points per unit of order imbalance (ADA 4h to DOGE 12h), not raw trade returns, and no net-of-cost strategy is tested ([arXiv 2607.09426v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)).

## Perpetual futures mechanics

- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: He et al. paper date and fee tier.** The draft dates arXiv 2212.06888 to 2024, but the opened HTML is version 7 dated September 17, 2026, while the Binance hourly sample (BTC, ETH, BNB, DOGE, ADA) runs from early 2020 through March 11, 2024 ([He et al., arXiv 2212.06888v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888)). Table 8 confirms net annualized returns of 12.55% (BTC), 18.18% (ETH), 38.39% (BNB), 35.45% (DOGE) and 23.05% (ADA), zero-cost DOGE fell from 409.29% (2020) to 62.89% (2023) and 35.36% (early 2024), deviations shrank ~22 percentage points per year, and price convergence drove far more return than funding ([He et al., arXiv 2212.06888v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888)). Crucially, the paper's "high fee" tier (6.75 basis points spot and 1.44 basis points perpetual maker one-way, plus half effective spread) corresponds to over $1 million 30-day spot volume and over $15 million 30-day perpetual volume on offshore Binance, which is far below US retail fees on a $300 to $500 account ([He et al., arXiv 2212.06888v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Funding rate predictability and carry breakeven.** The August 7, 2026 GitHub repository covers 6.1 million funding settlements across three venues, with its main Binance sample spanning 2020-01 to 2026-06 (791 contracts, 2.43 million events, 671 contracts passing >=$5 million trailing 90-day volume) ([OctopusTakopi funding-rate-alpha](https://github.com/OctopusTakopi/funding-rate-alpha)). Pooled raw funding IC is +1.89% at 1 hour (t = 6.26), +1.04% at 8 hours and +0.23% at 24 hours (t = 0.79), while the 90-day z-score predicts nothing (-0.0007 at 24 hours, t = -0.3) ([OctopusTakopi funding-rate-alpha](https://github.com/OctopusTakopi/funding-rate-alpha)). The 1-hour raw-rate spread was +8.6 basis points in 2024 to 2026 (t = 9.3, covering 4.3 basis points one-way) and +11.4 basis points in 2025 to 2026 (t = 8.8, covering 5.7 basis points one-way), while the 3-day swing carry breakeven is 23.4 basis points one-way; the repo does not explicitly print 46.8 basis points round-trip, models zero slippage or spread crossing, and uses offshore long-short deciles across hundreds of contracts ([OctopusTakopi funding-rate-alpha](https://github.com/OctopusTakopi/funding-rate-alpha)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Liquidation cascade early-warning failure.** arXiv 2607.27070 (dated August 11, 2026; arXiv v1 July 29, 2026) examines seven Binance BTCUSDT cascades from May 2022 through October 10, 2025 (over $19 billion force-closed across ~1.6 million accounts) using 1-minute prices and 5-minute open interest, top-trader and global long-short ratios, and taker buy-sell ratio ([arXiv 2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27070)). No variable is event-invariant, the October 2025 leverage pattern is an outlier that inverts out of sample, and while taker buy-sell ratio variance fell before all six events with data (median tau -0.13 to -0.44), two of six overlapped the null individually ([arXiv 2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27070)).
- **UNKNOWN · NOT FOUND: US-venue post-liquidation bounce net of cascade spreads.** Neither arXiv 2608.21888v1 nor any other cited paper isolates post-liquidation bounces on US-regulated perpetuals after cascade spread widening and 50 basis point round-trip fees ([arXiv 2608.21888v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)).

## What leverage does to small retail accounts

- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Hyperliquid wallet-level losses.** The AFT 2026 study of Hyperliquid's top 10 perpetuals from March 27, 2025 to June 30, 2026 (729,540 active wallets, marked against 120-minute midprice net of fees) shows front-end retail traders lost $1.11 billion ($154 million voluntary trading, $226 million net forced exits, $725 million fees), algorithmic market makers gained $208 million, and algorithmic takers approximately broke even ([AFT 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol395-aft2026/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36.pdf)). Front-end traders accounted for 96.0% of liquidation volume (97.6% excluding October 10, 2025), crossed the spread on 85% of volume versus 20% for market makers, and paid 2.43 basis points taker execution cost versus 0.70 basis points for market makers ([AFT 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol395-aft2026/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36.pdf)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · WRONG: ASIC Report 828 does not print the 2:1 crypto leverage cap.** ASIC Report 828 (January 20, 2026) confirms that in Australian FY 2023 to 2024, 133,674 retail CFD clients (68.42%) lost A$458.01 million including A$73.29 million in fees, and 69.22% of 61,424 retail cryptocurrency CFD clients lost money ([ASIC Report 828](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/tq0he35c/rep828-published-20-january-2026.pdf)). However, Report 828 references ASIC's CFD product intervention order (Instrument 2020/986) without stating the 2:1 retail crypto leverage ratio in its text ([ASIC Report 828](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/tq0he35c/rep828-published-20-january-2026.pdf)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Brazil mini-Ibovespa day traders.** Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti (FGV Working Paper 525, February 2020; paper dated November 21, 2019) track 19,646 individuals who started day trading mini-Ibovespa futures in 2013 to 2015 (records 2012 to 2017) ([FGV Working Paper 525](https://repositorio.fgv.br/bitstreams/0f00dd78-6909-4f91-84d5-aef8803e6436/download)). Among 1,551 who persisted over 300 days, 97.0% lost money net of fees (mean -$48.81 per day, median -$23.21 per day), 17 (1.1%) beat minimum wage ($16 per day), 8 (0.5%; abstract rounds to 0.4%) beat a bank teller wage ($54 per day), the top earner averaged $310 per day (SD $2,560), and the authors find no evidence of learning by day trading ([FGV Working Paper 525](https://repositorio.fgv.br/bitstreams/0f00dd78-6909-4f91-84d5-aef8803e6436/download)).
- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: BitMEX optimal margin and liquidation odds.** Cheng et al. (arXiv 2102.04591, 2021) analyze BitMEX BTC perpetual liquidations from January 29, 2020 to February 3, 2021 (372 days) and 5-minute prices from January 1, 2017 to February 6, 2021 ([Cheng et al., arXiv 2102.04591](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.04591)). Daily forced liquidations averaged 3.51% of open interest for longs and 1.89% for shorts, liquidated traders averaged 58.13x (long) and 59.94x (short) leverage, and holding daily margin-call probability to 1% on BTC required 32.56% margin (~3x) long and 21.01% (~5x) short ([Cheng et al., arXiv 2102.04591](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.04591)).
- **MEASURED · CONFIRMED: Isolated leverage liquidation and volatility-drag arithmetic.** At 5% maintenance margin on initial notional, an adverse move of (1 divided by L) minus m triggers liquidation near 28.3% at 3x, 15.0% at 5x and 5.0% at 10x before fees and funding; daily rebalanced compounding drag (-0.5 times L times (L minus 1) times variance) at 5x and 8% daily volatility equals -6.4% per day (exact arithmetic, no external source needed).

## On-chain leaderboards and US-usable venues

- **MEASURED (by its authors) · CONFIRMED: Hyperliquid copy-trading blow-ups.** arXiv 2611.27988v1 (submitted June 1, 2026) freezes 37,895 Hyperliquid leaderboard addresses on June 1, 2025 through April 30, 2026, retains 10,395 masters with >=$25,000 peak equity, and documents 2,621 loss-confirmed blow-ups (>70% drawdown with no recovery to 80% in 30 days and PnL drop >=50% of equity drop) across 48 crash-days, holding out 3 market-event days ([arXiv 2611.27988v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.27988v1), [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27988)). Fully 69% (1,706 of 2,477) blew up with no gradual behavioral signal, behavior led equity for only 216 of 2,477 (~9%, 215 via leverage), 65% of naive equity craters were withdrawals, and the mixture-SPRT detector scored AUC 0.49 on the sealed test set ([arXiv 2611.27988v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.27988v1)).
- **MEASURED · CONFIRMED: Kraken Derivatives US contracts and 50 bps round-trip fees.** Operated by NinjaTrader Clearing, LLC doing business as Kraken Derivatives US (CFTC-registered FCM, NFA ID 0309379), available in all US states except Maine and New York (so Idaho is eligible), listing 17 fractional 24/7 crypto perpetuals (smallest sizes include 0.0001 BTC, 100 DOGE and 1,000 SHIB) with 8-hour funding at 00:00, 08:00 and 16:00 UTC ([Kraken Pro perps](https://www.kraken.com/pro/perps/crypto-perpetuals), [Kraken blog, Nov 23, 2026](https://blog.kraken.com/product/kraken-derivatives/us-fractional-crypto-perps)). The fee is 0.25% of notional to open and 0.25% to close, which equals 50 basis points round-trip before spread and funding ([Kraken Pro perps](https://www.kraken.com/pro/perps/crypto-perpetuals)).
- **MEASURED · CONFIRMED: Coinbase Derivatives Exchange CFTC Fee Filing 2026-46.** Effective August 17, 2026, the schedule lists 22 crypto perpetual futures plus 3 crypto or gold perp-style contracts (Coin50, Pax Gold, 1k PEPE) at a $0.10 per side per contract electronic exchange fee for both Non-Professional and Professional traders ([CFTC Filing 2026-46](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules08032613651.pdf)). One detail missed by the draft: page 4 excludes anyone "using a fully automated order generating computer system" from Non-Professional status, though the $0.10 electronic fee is identical for Professional accounts; retail FCM brokerage commissions and spreads are UNKNOWN from this filing ([CFTC Filing 2026-46](https://www.cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rules08032613651.pdf)).
- **UNKNOWN · NOT FOUND: Free historical data feeds and absence of audited $300 to $500 winners.** The draft cites Binance, Kraken, Coinbase and Tardis historical data availability without opening or linking those endpoint docs, and the claim that no audited $300 to $500 US retail perpetual winner exists is an unverified negative.

## Six proposed tests: verdict on the evidence

- **Test 1 (Multi-day spot-perp basis convergence on US altcoins): WEAK SUPPORT, likely dead after US fees.** He et al. reported 23.05% to 38.39% annualized net returns on ADA, DOGE and BNB only through March 2024 on offshore Binance using institutional volume maker fees (6.75 bps spot and 1.44 bps perp one-way, requiring >$1M spot and >$15M perp 30-day volume), while deviations were shrinking ~22 percentage points per year ([He et al., arXiv 2212.06888v7](https://arxiv.org/html/2212.06888)). On Kraken Derivatives US, the perpetual leg alone costs 50 basis points round-trip before spot fees and spread ([Kraken Pro perps](https://www.kraken.com/pro/perps/crypto-perpetuals)), and splitting $300 to $500 across spot plus short perp leaves tiny capital per leg.
- **Test 2 (Cross-sectional funding-carry tilt, 3-day hold): WEAK SUPPORT, fee-marginal.** The GitHub study showed funding fails as a directional forecast and found a 23.4 basis point one-way fee breakeven only for an offshore 671-coin long-short decile basket with zero slippage or spread crossing modeled ([OctopusTakopi funding-rate-alpha](https://github.com/OctopusTakopi/funding-rate-alpha)). Kraken US charges 25 basis points per side (50 basis points round-trip) before spread on just 17 contracts ([Kraken Pro perps](https://www.kraken.com/pro/perps/crypto-perpetuals)), so the cited breakeven is already below US fees before slippage and universe shrinkage.
- **Test 3 (Post-cascade maker bounce on liquid altcoin perps): VERY WEAK SUPPORT.** No cited source measures a post-liquidation bounce return after cascade spreads and US fees. arXiv 2608.21888v1 found 15-minute flow-driven reversal peaked at 1.3 basis points gross (zero of 183 pairs cleared 5 basis points) ([arXiv 2608.21888v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)), AFT 2026 showed high-volume market makers profited on 0.16 basis point fees rather than 50 basis point retail fees ([AFT 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol395-aft2026/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36/LIPIcs.AFT.2026.36.pdf)), and arXiv 2607.27070 showed cascades have no reliable trigger ([arXiv 2607.27070](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27070)).
- **Test 4 (Liquid large-to-mid-cap daily momentum with 1x to 3x leverage): NOT SUPPORTED by cited evidence.** Zaremba et al.'s top-2% daily momentum spread (+0.22% gross, 2015 to 2021) was not statistically significant (p = 0.171 value-weighted, p = 0.115 equal-weighted) and deducted zero costs ([Zaremba et al., IRFA 2021](https://www.wir.ue.wroc.pl/docstore/download/UEWR135bdc5e34ad49868fe8a5e77fa021c7/Mercik_Up_or_down_Short-term_reversal.pdf)). Adding 3x leverage on altcoins (when Cheng et al. put 3x at a 1% daily margin-call risk even on less volatile BTC) and 50 basis point round-trip fees has no net-of-cost backing here ([Cheng et al., arXiv 2102.04591](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.04591), [Kraken Pro perps](https://www.kraken.com/pro/perps/crypto-perpetuals)).
- **Test 5 (1-to-4-hour volume-spike exhaustion fade, short-only or long-short): NOT SUPPORTED.** Zaremba et al.'s daily reversal was pre-cost and concentrated in the illiquid bottom 98% of coins ([Zaremba et al., IRFA 2021](https://www.wir.ue.wroc.pl/docstore/download/UEWR135bdc5e34ad49868fe8a5e77fa021c7/Mercik_Up_or_down_Short-term_reversal.pdf)), while arXiv 2610.23283v2 showed tradable-universe small-coin spreads vanish (0.01% per week, t = 0.04) and turn negative after 2% costs at $100,000+ volume ([arXiv 2610.23283v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23283)). No source supports a 1-to-4-hour exhaustion short on liquid US perpetuals after 50 basis point fees.
- **Test 6 (Negative control: hourly BTC lead-lag and 15-minute reversal at 3x to 5x): SUPPORTED AS A NEGATIVE CONTROL ONLY.** Khan measured -0.343% per event after costs on BTC-altcoin regime convergence ([Khan, SSRN 7485359](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7485359)), arXiv 2608.21888v1 measured 1.3 basis points gross versus >=5 basis points cost on 15-minute reversal ([arXiv 2608.21888v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)), and Guo et al.'s 2.16% per day relied on 2019 to 2021 offshore Binance futures at 4 basis point fees with no spread or slippage ([Guo et al., SMU working paper](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=7900&context=lkcsb_research)). Running this only to verify that the simulator reproduces fee and liquidation drag is consistent with the record.

## What this changes for our queue

Decided by the builder, 2026-11-25 17:45 UTC.

- No published short-hold price pattern in coins is shown here to clear the fees a small US account pays. The 15-minute and one-hour patterns earn a few basis points before costs. So the fee per side of the venue decides more than the rule does, and the venue page (`docs/research/40_leverage_venues_checked.md`) comes before any fast-lane plan is fixed.
- Tests 4 and 5 (daily momentum with leverage, fading a volume spike) are dropped as proposed: the evidence cited for them did not hold up when checked.
- Test 6 is kept as the first run of the new futures book, as a check of the machine and not as a hope. The record says it loses after fees. If our book shows a gain, we look for a fault before anything else.
- Tests 1 and 2 (price gap between a coin and its future, and holding the side that is paid funding) need two positions at once and fees far below 25 basis points a side. They wait in the ideas list until a venue with such fees is confirmed for a small account.
- The studies of leveraged retail accounts set a working rule for our own tests: every leveraged result is printed beside the same answers at 1x, and nothing above 3x is run until a method has shown something at 1x. Our own replay agrees (`docs/research/39_leverage_replay_tm03.md`).
- What this reading does not cover is the owner's own method, as he described it on 2026-11-25: find the coins that attention is moving into, get in early, trade the swings over a few hours. Moves there are large against fees, and reading what people are talking about is a thing a model can do at scale. That is where the next test goes (queue row 9g).
