## Frozen rules and scoring

STATED: [GRID_70.json](GRID_70.json) freezes 188 rules with signals, thresholds, sides, holds, entry and exit clocks, forced-flow hypotheses and source URLs. Its SHA is pinned in [COVERAGE_70.json](COVERAGE_70.json). The ten families are session-specific fades after a move (64), hourly shock fades with 3x volume (12), BTC after the London BRR fixing (12), midnight UTC stop runs with a high/low break and volume (12), alt overshoots during a sharp BTC move (16), round-level rejection (8), failed hourly range sweeps (16), hourly BTC lead (16), hourly volume shock fade (16), and hourly hedged relative continuation (16). The first two hourly fades need an explicit move at a specific clock or a volume-confirmed shock, not daily raw reversal. Hourly rejection fades a failed breach; it does not buy breakouts. BTC overshoot is the opposite setup from the lagging-alt lead rule: enter against a same-direction move bigger than BTC's by the fixed multiple. The cited findings motivate the mechanisms; they do not prove the tested rules work.

STATED: Rank simultaneous signals by descending signed trigger strength (BTC lead by largest same-side lag), then alphabetical coin. Choose one 1x gross ticket, with no overlap and same-clock exit and next entry allowed. Split a hedged ticket evenly between opposed legs and count it once. If pre-entry required history is missing, no signal may use it. If a selected ticket lacks any required hold-window bar on either leg, report it unresolved separately, omit it from return statistics, keep its scheduled slot blocked and never backfill with a different coin. Missing fills are UNKNOWN, so a finalist with missing tickets is not evidence all its signals could have been traded. Same-clock exit/entry does not imply netted costs. For shorts, use the negative simple directional return, not an inverse-price return.

STATED: For each coin, hold and UTC entry hour, the uncharged reference is the plain arithmetic average of the same spot-open to spot-open returns on all eligible dates in that block. The full search and each half have their own references; a ticket crossing the split is counted only in the full-block statistics. Give reference legs the same weights and signs as the rule. No reference enters the signal or position sizing. The scored series for each t is the rule's net return less its matched reference, clustered by UTC entry day using day-summed trade residuals with the cluster-count correction. The t is undefined when there are too few independent day clusters or effectively no residual variation. Plain net return is also checked and shown; it is not used for the t.

STATED: To be eligible, a rule needs at least 200 resolved tickets on at least 100 UTC entry dates across the search and at least 40 entry dates in each half. The output flags thin rules; they cannot be finalists. Other search marks are positive plain net mean for the full search and both halves, matched t of at least 3.5 in full and 1.5 in each half, positive plain net after removing the top ceiling of 1% of tickets ranked by plain net return, and simple plain net of at least 12% per year at 1x, computed as the sum divided by four. Rank passing rules by full matched t then rule ID, and take at most three. Treat a candidate as a duplicate of an already selected finalist if Pearson correlation of their plain net UTC-day ticket sums, including zero for idle days, is greater than 0.7. The script emits one summary line per rule, not timestamps, trades, instrument-level payoffs or bars.

### Made-up-data verification

STATED: The synthetic bar fixtures use four made-up coin paths with common and independent Gaussian hourly moves, random wicks and volume, 100 fixed null seeds and the full grid. The null requirement is zero finalists in at least 95 seeds at judged costs, using the complete search selection. A separate cost-free raw t diagnostic ignores the marks and may count very thin rules. It is not the gate.

MEASURED on made-up bars only: The completed search gate chose no finalist in 100 of 100 null seeds. The cost-free diagnostic had no raw t crossing in 84 of 100 seeds. Its 18 crossings had only 2 to 137 trades and 2 to 134 entry days, and every crossing failed the eligibility floors. The crossings were nine London fixes, six midnight stop runs, two volume shocks and one hourly shock. Sparse signals can give a large t on a small-sample standard error even when the data generator has no edge.

MEASURED on made-up bars only: The sole planted finalist was the intended rule 70-017. An always-long rule in a rising market averaged +103.30 bp net per ticket before the reference, but its matched day t was -72.16 and it failed selection. Future in-search price changes left all 188 earlier signal prefixes intact. Post-cutoff poison rows and a large post-cutoff plant left the complete summary output byte-for-byte identical.

MEASURED on made-up schedules only: The separate stand-alone power screen used 2,048 worlds, 1,393 tickets over 1,045 entry days, zero reference drift, 45 bp common day noise and 65 bp independent per-ticket noise (same-day correlation 0.324). On a 0.25 bp edge grid, 8.50 bp true net per ticket gave 54.00% and 10.25 bp gave 81.98% probability of clearing all marks for one fixed rule. This design calculation excludes the competition for three places and estimation error in the matched reference. Real signal frequency, dependence and power are UNKNOWN.
