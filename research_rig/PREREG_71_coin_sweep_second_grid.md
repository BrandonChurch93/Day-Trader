## What changes

STATED: A sharp BTC move can lead traders to make even more one-sided trades in another coin. If that coin drops much farther than BTC, test buying it for a short rebound; if it rises much farther than BTC, test shorting it. This is the same mechanism as 70-102, which led the first grid and did not clear carry. The [frozen first grid](GRID_70.json) documents the starting hypothesis and its source. Whether impatient orders or forced sales explain an hourly result is UNKNOWN.

STATED: At each possible entry, compare the latest completed close with the close that many hours earlier. BTC must reach its signed threshold. The other coin must move strictly more than the stated multiple in the same direction. Rank by its signed excess move, break ties alphabetically and pick without looking at entry or exit fills. Long fades a drop; short fades a rise. The normal entry is the hourly open immediately after the last signal close. A delayed rule keeps that signal and ranking, waits one more hour and uses that hour's open; it does not refresh the signal. Exit at the open exactly the stated hold after actual entry, with no stop or target.

STATED: Allow one gross-1x ticket at a time. On a top-two rule, put equal weights on the strongest two available signals; use full size on the first only if no second qualifies as of the signal. It is one ticket for counts, trim, ranking and the matched day t. If any selected leg is unobservable, the whole ticket is unresolved; never replace it with the third coin or resize after looking at fills. The BTC-hedged form puts half gross in the selected non-BTC coin and half in BTC the other way, on the same open times. BTC cannot also be the signal trade. The original maintenance hour, overlap, fence and missing-history constraints still apply.
