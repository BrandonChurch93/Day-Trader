## Rule from the paper

- STATED. Printed pages 6–7, §4.1, [Catching Crypto Trends](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Catching-Crypto-Trends.pdf#page=7): “Donchian Channels comprise three primary components: an upper boundary marking the highest high, a lower boundary marking the lowest low, and a middle boundary calculated as the midpoint between these extremes.” The p. 7 equations use the maximum and minimum of the last n **closes, including today's completed close**, not intraday highs and lows.
- STATED. P. 7, §4.1: “A trend-following system is constructed such that as soon as the closing price hits the upper boundary of the Donchian Channel, a new long position is established.” The next sentence says “Each position is then maintained until the first closure below a trailing stop which is updated daily according to the following formula”. P. 10, §4.3, instead prints an ordered piecewise rule: 1 if `Close = DonchianUp`, 0 if `Close ≤ TrailingStop`, otherwise prior position ([paper, p. 10](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Catching-Crypto-Trends.pdf#page=10)). Lead ruling: the equation controls. Test the upper-channel branch first even when both conditions hold. Otherwise an active leg exits at equality or below; no short.
- STATED. P. 7, §4.1: “In line with trend-following habits, we never allow the trailing stop to be updated downward.” The next sentence: “In practice, the prevailing Trailing Stop used in day t+1 is the maximum between the Trailing Stop used in day t and the middle line of the Donchain channel computed at the end of day t.” Footnote 3: “For newly opened positions, the initial trailing stop is set equal to the midpoint of the Donchian Channel at the time of entry.” Use the prior stop to judge today's close, then update the stop for tomorrow if the signal remains long, or clear it on exit. A later fresh position starts with its own midpoint.
- STATED. Pp. 8–9, §4.2: “We thus size a trend position such as the target annualized volatility is 25%.” P. 9: “To prevent excessive leverage, we do not allow the allocation to exceed 200%.” The preceding definition uses the 90-day annualized volatility of underlying returns. Set an active leg to 25% divided by that volatility, capped at 100% within each primary leg before blending. An inactive leg is cash. No borrowing or shorts; do not renormalize the remaining legs.
- STATED. P. 10, §4.3: “Conceptually, the final portfolio, referred to as the Combo portfolio, is constructed as an equal-weighted aggregation of N sub-portfolios, each driven by a different trend signal length.” The equation lists 5, 10, 20, 30, 60, 90, 150, 250 and 360 days. Primary averages only the 10-, 20- and 30-day legs. The lead chose that shorter adaptation to match the owner's horizon. The scout picked those legs after reading Table 2; their paper results are not independent support. Besides the three-leg blend, the 100% cap is an explicit departure.
- STATED. Pp. 14–15, §5.1: “Specifically, we implement a threshold-based rebalancing rule: the portfolio is only rebalanced if the difference between the current and target allocation exceeds 20%.” P. 15: “The threshold mechanism is applied only to the second case (i.e. volatility-induced rebalancing) while any signal-based adjustments, such as a fresh breakout or trailing stop event, result in an immediate position update regardless of the threshold.” Review each daily close, not weekly. Use no band on signal changes, listing exits or the monthly capital reset.
- STATED. P. 18, §7.2: “Capital is allocated equally across all selected assets, with each receiving one-Bth of the portfolio’s total capital.” “The cross-sectional trend portfolio is rebalanced at the end of each month.” Use equal coin envelopes, not daily equal capital resets or a volume rank. Start with all eligible and warmed names at the first investible open; later coins can join at the monthly reset only. Choose monthly members as of the last completed close, apply the reset at the next in-fence open, and let coin envelopes drift between resets. Remove a lost listing promptly. This price-blind roster does not copy the paper's one-year listing screen, liquidity thresholds, liquidity exits or dynamically ranked top B (pp. 18–19).
- STATED. Shown only: each of the three single lookbacks at the primary cap; the same three-leg blend with the paper's 200% maximum instead of our 100%; and the nine-leg 200% Combo, labeled “the paper's rule as printed” for its signal, blend and cap, still on our venue proxy, costs and roster. The latter is not independently judged. Keep a fixed one-ninth for each leg; a warming leg is cash. Use only completed contiguous closes **inside each stretch**. No use of repeat prices to warm vault legs. At the best-covered repeat start (first completed close of January 1, 2020), the 150-, 250- and 360-day legs cannot even signal before May 29, September 6 and December 25 respectively, and trade only at the next open. The 5- through 90-day legs also wait for 90 valid returns, at earliest the March 31 close; later listings/gaps delay any leg. No attempt to fill missing warm-up months.
