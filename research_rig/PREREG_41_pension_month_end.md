## What this tests

STATED. [Harvey, Mazzoleni and Melone, NBER Working Paper 33554, January 2026 revision](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33554/w33554.pdf), model calendar rebalancers by resetting a simulated 60/40 stock/bond allocation at the last business day of each month. The sign of the resulting equity-weight drift is the sign of stock-minus-bond performance since the reset. S&P 500 outperformance points to stock sales and bond buys; the opposite sign points to bond sales and stock buys. Their calendar return-predictability regressions target next-day futures returns, use the final five business days, and find the strongest predictability in the final four. Their trading strategy uses both long and short futures, and a separate signal for a first-session reversal. Their daily sample ran from September 10, 1997 to March 17, 2023. This plan takes the published direction and a window overlapping those final sessions, not a replication of the futures strategy, threshold rule, or its performance.

STATED. Calendar-based pensions and balanced funds may have monthly or quarterly schedules, while others trade at tolerance-band crossings. The paper gives Seattle City Employees' Retirement System's overlay memo as evidence of month-end execution practice and the Sacramento County Employees' Retirement System memo as a quarterly/band example, not as a universal rule. [Parker, Schoar and Sun, Journal of Finance, 2023](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28028/revisions/w28028.rev1.pdf) document contrarian flows from target-date funds whose mandates specify stock/bond allocations, but say practitioners do not follow fixed trade schedules; some passive funds trade daily and some active funds take weeks. Their principal TDF holdings sample was 2008Q3–2018Q4, and quarterly reports do not identify the day on which the orders traded. The SPY/TLT experiment cannot identify who traded or why. TLT holds long Treasuries, not the source paper's ten-year futures. The overlapping part of the published sample is not out-of-sample relative to the market evidence.

## Decision, fill and comparator

STATED. At the fifth-last NYSE session close, cumulate each fund's close-to-close total return from the anchor to that close, multiplying each daily ratio of raw close plus that session's ex-date cash to previous raw close. This models reinvestment in the signal only. When SPY is ahead, choose TLT; when TLT is ahead, choose SPY; on exact tie choose cash. The fourth-last session close is the buy proxy, and the last session close is the sell proxy. Do not update the signal on the entry day. These are three close-to-close intervals. No threshold, no substitute if the chosen fund is unaffordable, and no later signal.

Each month starts independently with the fixed cash budget. Buy the largest whole-share count whose modeled entry cost including the buy fee fits, and leave unused cash idle. If zero shares fit, that arm is in cash with zero return and the month still counts. Exit at the close less the sell fee; credit cash on ex-dates strictly after entry and through exit, without reinvestment. Divide net dollars by the original budget, not invested dollars; there is no idle cash yield. Raw daily close is a fill proxy, not proof an order could obtain that price or exact close-based size in practice. No fractional shares, margin, or shorts.

The primary monthly difference is net conditional return less net always-TLT in the identical window, under the identical whole-share budget and fee convention. This uses Replay 25's clock but not its continuously invested denominator; when the rule chooses TLT the paired difference is exactly zero. Ties and unaffordable selections stay in the sample. This compares to always-TLT; it is not by itself a comparison to every fixed asset mix. To judge the published sign separately, calculate net selected-laggard return minus net other-fund return across the same windows, with both priced as independent same-budget long-only arms and ties contributing zero. If the selected ETF or the other ETF is unaffordable its respective return is zero. This is a shadow spread, not a short leg.
